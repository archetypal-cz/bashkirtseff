# cz sweep brief — „madam“ + no „šik“ (owner rulings 2026-10-03)

Owner rulings (LOCKED, see content/cz/TranslationMemory.md → "OWNER rulings 2026-10-03"):

1. **Direct address „Madame“ / „milostpaní“ / „milostivá paní“ / „paní!“ (vocative, said TO a woman, no name) → „madam“.**
   - Inside speech only: „Ano, madam.“, „Madame, musíte mě provdat!“ → „Madam, musíte mě provdat!“
   - Capitalise only at sentence start.
   - NOT in scope (leave as they are): Madame + surname in narration or speech („Madame Gaviniová“, „paní Gaviniová“) — owner question pending; titles of works (*Madame l'Archiduc*, „Madame X“, *La fille de Madame Angot*); nicknames („Madame Šelma“); French quoted in footnotes; „Madame“ used as a noun for the lady of the house in narration; „Monsieur“/„milostpán“ (no ruling).
2. **chic → no „šik“** (owner: too colloquial for Marie). Every visible „šik“ (any form: šik, šiku, šikovní? — careful: „šikovný“ = skilful is a DIFFERENT word, never touch it) and any leftover French *chic* gets a plain Czech rendering chosen by context, no == and no footnote:
   - elegance / elegantní / elegantně (dress, people, carriages)
   - vkus / vkusný (taste)
   - noblesa / noblesní (high society)
   - šarm / půvab (charm of a person)
   - „to je ono“, „báječné“ in exclamations („C'est chic!“)
   - *de chic* (painting from imagination) stays as TM rules it (==z hlavy== / ==od ruky==) — not in scope.
   - Servants' speech (Rosalie etc.) may stay a touch colloquial — but still not „šik“.
   - Outfit notes („(šedé šaty, šik)“): if the French is *bien* → „sluší“ (or „pěkné!“), per the outfit palette; if *chic* → „elegantní“. Keep italics as they are.
   Compare with the French in content/_original/NNN/<same file> to pick the sense.
