%% 2026-02-09T20:09:00 RSR: Enormous entry culminating in one of Marie's most anguished letters to her mother. At Mme Gavini's dressmaker, Marie's aunt encounters Baroness Linsingen, whom Marie contemptuously describes as "la complaisante des Souvaroff, Doubelt" -- a social parasite supported by these families in exchange for performing introductions. Yet this "delicieuse creature" is received everywhere: at Princesse Mathilde's, at the Orleans princes', at embassies and government receptions. This triggers Marie's social despair: she contemplates suicide but rejects it rationally ("je suis aussi bien morte ici que je le serais la-bas"). Then the explosive letter to her mother in Nice -- a manifesto of frustration at her social isolation ("enterree a l'age heureux de vingt ans"). She demands that her mother write to the Anitchkoffs, Princesse Galitzine, and Pelikan for letters of introduction to Parisian society. The letter is both calculating and desperate, ending with a literary allusion to Paul et Virginie (her tears literally watering the paper). Georges (her brother) is cursed with characteristic venom: "que la peste etrangle, etouffe et defigure pour l'eternite." %%
