We met Torlonia as we were going up the Pincio; he was coming down in a closed carriage, for it had begun to snow at just that moment — but the hood of his *victoria*[^1] did not prevent him from raising his hat with as much style as possible. He still hopes to sell his horses. Fie! What misery. For today I adore him all the more — because he is distant and does not look at me.

We met Torlonia on 13 March; on the 14th he paid his call. On the 16th we lunched at Spillmann's and spent half the day together. But let us go on: Antonelli came for the second time in the evening on 5 March — between the two evenings there are five days. Let us keep going; these calculations console me. The next day, 6 March, we went riding with Plowden, Loëbecke and Antonelli. On the 8th another cavalcade, but *tête-à-tête*. On the 9th Antonelli again spent the evening with us; after that there is no more counting. But the main thing is the beginning — look: 26 Feb. — 28 — 5 March!

I listen to these speeches laughing from the height of my cold indifference, and at the same time I let him take my hand, take his hand in turn with an almost maternal air, and if he is not yet quite stupefied by his passion for me, as he puts it, he must see that while I drive him away with my words, I hold him back with my eyes, and while I tell him that I do not love him and never shall, I love him, or at least behave as though I loved him. I say all sorts of nonsense to him and treat him as at the ball at the Capitol. Another man would be content, an older one, but Pietro tears a napkin, breaks two brushes, puts his fist through a canvas. All these manoeuvres allow me to take him by the hand and tell him he is mad.
