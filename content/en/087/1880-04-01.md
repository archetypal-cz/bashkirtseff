We pay visits and then go to the Horse Show. I send to the atelier a crate of oranges emptied and filled with paper. I send word to Tony that Julian is waiting for him at four o'clock at the Cafe Veron. I send word to Amelie that her dog is lost, and I write to Mme de Mouzay that a very great trouble has befallen us and that she should come at once. As for Berthe, I invited her to the Odeon to see "The Wedding of Attila," the new drama by M. de Barnier. She was ready when I had her told it was an April fool.

Mme de Mouzay, who was ill, got up to come, accompanied by her daughter; great alarm.

%% 087.0763 %%
%% [#Religion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/RELIGION.md) %%
%% Qui y a-t-il mon Dieu ? dit-elle à ma tante qui ne savait rien. %%
"What is it, my God?" she said to my aunt, who knew nothing.

%% 087.0764 %%
%% Mais rien, répond celle-ci. %%
"Why, nothing," she replied.

%% 087.0765 %%
%% Comment rien ! %%
"What do you mean, nothing!"

%% 087.0766 %%
%% [#Theater_theme](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/THEATER.md) %%
%% Là-dessus une petite scène mais ça m'amuse et nous allons voir les Etrangleurs de Paris mais je dors presque tout le temps car je dois vous dire que depuis plus de quinze jours déjà j'ai de tels bourdonements dans les oreilles que je suis assez sourde pour perdre presque tout ce que disent les acteurs, de ma loge de face. %%
Thereupon a little scene, but it amuses me, and we go to see *The Stranglers of Paris*, but I sleep almost the whole time, for I must tell you that for more than a fortnight now I have had such ringing in my ears that I am deaf enough to miss almost everything the actors say, from my box facing the stage.
