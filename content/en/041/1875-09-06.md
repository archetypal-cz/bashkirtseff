I need not state the cause of my eternal grief. I have wept and I am weeping still; I am disheartened, crushed, wretched. I no longer hope for anything — yes, I do: I hope either for death or for a change. The tears and the fits of despair grow more frequent day by day; such a state cannot last. A great change will come to me, or at least a few days of forgetting.

%% 2026-09-29T01:28:41 RSR: Restored from tome05.docx ¶4755 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
Tall trees are to be planted in a very wide amphitheatre near the wall on the rue de France, so as to form a kind of great green wall, and my statue placed on this rise so that the green wall serves as its background; but the statue must stand three metres away from the trees. The garden will be planted and well kept, and this will depend on the day of my death: every week, on the Monday or the Tuesday — on whichever day I die — the municipal band of Nice will play in the garden and people will come as to a public garden, only they will be admitted solely on invitations issued by the members of the Cercle de la Méditerranée, so as to keep out nursemaids and soldiers. There will be dancing: dancing matinées. And every year, on the anniversary of my passing, there will be a night fête and my statue will be crowned with roses. This is to be done at the expense of my mother or my aunt, who are to set aside a sum whose interest will pay these costs — that is, half the interest, the other half to be distributed to the poor on the anniversary every year. In this way it will be in perpetuity, and in this way at least I immortalise myself.

%% 2026-09-29T01:28:41 RSR: Restored from tome05.docx ¶4757–4759 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
In this dejection and this incessant, dreadful pain I do not curse life. On the contrary, I love it and find it good. Would you believe it, I find everything good and pleasant, even tears, even pain. I love to weep, I love to despair, I love to be grieved and sad. I regard all that as so many amusements, and I love life in spite of everything, and I want to live. It would be cruel to make me die when I am so accommodating. I weep, I complain, and at the same time I like it — no, not that, I don't know how to put it... In short, everything in life pleases me, I find everything pleasant. And while asking for happiness I find myself happy to be wretched — it is no longer I who find myself so; my body weeps and cries out, but something in me that is above me rejoices at everything.
