# Tuesday, 1 June 1880

%% 088.0446 %%

I had the dream that always presages something disagreeable for me.

So it is reluctantly that I go to the mass the Prince is having said at Saint-Philippe du Roule for the repose of the soul of the little one who was killed.

Needless to say, the newspaper articles made me weep, and this death decidedly has a strange influence on me. All our Bonapartist friends at the exit. But my dream made me expect some horror at every moment.

Géry tells us that Gabriel will not be done until the end of this week. Poor boy. Anyway, I come home; Mme Larivière (née Lubomirska) comes to sit for me. I had thought those ladies would forget their charity portraits. And while I am having my harp lesson, a letter is brought to me. It gave me a jolt — the dream was still on my mind.

The letter is from Mme de Mouzay, who is mad, as you shall see. Smallpox is in her neighbourhood, and she begs us to take in her daughter for a few days.

%% 088.0451 %%

%% Pour qu'elle apporte la maladie alors. Je savais bien qu'il y avait cette épidémie mais à Paris on ne pense pas à ces choses- là. Mais cet avis direct, brutal et cette idée de risquer une fille de vingt ans pour une femme de trente-quatre, me met en frayeur. %%
So that she can bring us the disease, then! I knew perfectly well there was this epidemic, but in Paris one does not think of such things. But this direct, brutal notice, and this idea of risking a girl of twenty for a woman of thirty-four, fills me with terror.

%% 088.0452 %%

My hands were trembling and my cheeks grew hot — they are burning still. How can anyone ask such favours and write such things? Imagination, fear alone can give one the disease, and for me it would be death.

I would kill myself. Anyway, she is coming to dinner this evening — yet another present to be made.
