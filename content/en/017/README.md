## Thematic Summary

Carnet 017 covers Marie's life in Nice during the tail end of the social season (late February to late March 1874). Major themes:

1. **Hamilton obsession**: Marie's love for the Duke of Hamilton intensifies. She hallucinates seeing him, dreams about him repeatedly, and declares "My God, how I love him!" She blames his absence for ruining the entire season.

2. **Lambertye (Fedus)**: The Comte de Lambertye becomes a comic obsession -- she watches him, gives him Latin nicknames, makes wagers about accosting him, and compares him to a poodle.

3. **Villa 55**: The family's new villa is blessed, visited by dozens of guests, and becomes the focus of Marie's social ambitions.

4. **Nice despair**: Marie increasingly despises Nice, comparing it to a hat worn too often. Her apostrophe to the palm trees and Mediterranean is a rhetorical tour de force.

5. **Paul**: Her 14-year-old brother becomes increasingly violent and crude -- throwing tea in her face, stealing her cucumbers, refusing to ride with her out of spite.

6. **Superstition**: Broken mirrors, burning ears, prophetic dreams, the mirror-avoidance challenge (to become Princess Wittgenstein).

7. **Blushing pathology**: Marie's uncontrollable blushing extends to fearing she will blush at the mere name of any servant.
