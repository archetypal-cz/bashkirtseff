# Sunday, 5 December 1880
I have neither worked nor gone out. Madame de Brimont has come every evening; I have been to Madame Gavini's, and yesterday, Saturday, we went together to the first night of *Jean Beaudry* at the Français. In proscenium box B, M. Trebinsky, whom I call "Trèsbien," was with us. He is a tall, dark man, shy and rather awkward.

%% Le public ordinaire des premières, le gouvernement, la mère Adam etc. A côté de nous la Rattazzi avec son troisième mari, le sieur de Rute. Elle est décatie mais on comprend qu'on ait pu se damner pour cette femme. Des yeux voilés et à se mettre à genoux devant, quant au sourire, à l'expression du visage je n'ai jamais rien vu de pareil. Cette expression est la conséquence de l'ascendant qu'elle savait exercer. Mais le buste est en ruine... %%
The usual first-night crowd, the government, old Mother Adam,[^1] etc. Beside us was the Rattazzi[^2] with her third husband, one de Rute. She is faded, but one understands that men could have damned themselves for this woman. Veiled eyes one could fall on one's knees before; as for the smile, the expression of the face — I have never seen anything like it. That expression comes from the sway she knew how to hold. But the bust is in ruins...

%% 090.0308 %%
%% Aujourd'hui je fais toilette. Jupe de peluche grenat fané, tablier de satin meveilleux froncé sur le ventre; habit Robespierre en drap du même ton avec revers de peluche et de satin, col blanc et cravate à jabot. Chapeau de peluche. %%
Today I dress up. Skirt of faded garnet plush, apron-front of marvellous satin gathered at the waist; Robespierre-style jacket in cloth of the same tone with lapels of plush and satin, white collar and cravat with jabot. Plush hat.

%% 090.0309 %%
%% Des pieds... mes pieds me rappellent toujours Randouin et Lahirle se mettant à genoux et faisant semblant de ne pas les trouver tellement ils sont petits. Nous allons chez la Brimont, puis chez les Gavini et chez les Moreau. %%
Such feet... my feet always remind me of Randouin and Lahirle getting down on their knees and pretending not to find them because they are so small. We go to Madame de Brimont's, then to the Gavinis' and the Moreaus'.

%% 2026-10-01T08:33:22 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
Without that I shall soon be unable to breathe or move from my bed. Splendid. For four years now I have done everything the celebrities prescribe and I go from bad to worse... I have even gone so far as to lay hands on my beauty — I have daubed my right collarbone with iodine. And it is no better. Could my ordinary troubles perhaps have an influence on my health? Yet the larynx and the bronchi [Crossed out: and colds] are not generally affected by emotional troubles...

I have no idea; I do what I am told, avoid all imprudence, wash only in hot water now, and am ill all the same.

Villevieille told me yesterday that Tony, who came on Saturday to correct their work, asked to see our competition paintings, and that he found my eyes oddly drawn but said there was good in it, some very pretty things and charming tones. All the same, he is not pleased with the competition in general. If I do not get the medal, I shall still have done a fairly good study.

[^1]: Juliette Adam (1836-1936), prominent French writer, salon hostess, and founder of the literary journal *La Nouvelle Revue*.

[^2]: Marie Laetitia Bonaparte-Wyse (1831-1902), known as the Princesse Rattazzi after her second marriage, a celebrated salon hostess married three times.
