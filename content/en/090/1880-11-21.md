# Sunday, 21 November 1880
Yesterday I spent the day running about with Magdeleine Delsarte through the most outlying neighbourhoods in search of a model for the competition. We lunched on cakes in the carriage. In the evening, a small party at Madame Engelhardt's. I go so that people will not say I am ill. We make the acquaintance of several Russian ladies but invite no one except Thomas de Barbarin, who arrived very late. We talked and had tea.

Let us talk about me. I am reading Emile de Girardin's pamphlet *L'égale de l'homme* [Man's Equal]. It is long-winded despite a certain brusqueness, and its logic is not logical at all. But for all that it is good, since it is in favour of equality. My Sundays must be spent preparing to take examinations; I must be at least, at least a bachelor of arts when woman emerges from her humiliation. And then I shall go to see Hubertine Auclert[^1] next Sunday, at 12 rue du Cail.

[^1]: Hubertine Auclert (1848-1914), leading French suffragist and feminist activist.
