# Sunday, 9 January 1881
%% 2026-10-03T18:05:20 TR: Reading-conventions wave (KRR 2026-10-03): pun on pendre la crémaillère kept in French, explanatory footnote added. %%
%% 2026-10-03T18:32:00 CON: Reading-conventions review: conversion checked against _original (footnote quotes exact, titles rule, highlights); approved as is. %%
%% Ce soir on pend *la crème ailleurs* chez moi au 37. Une douzaine de camarades sont là réunies. (Les Zillardht, Amélie, Amixe, de Daillens, de Villevielle, Richards, Simonidès, Magdeleine etc..). Les professeurs manquent. %%
%% 2026-09-28T21:01:18 RSR: Restored from tome14.docx ¶774, Mon Journal t.14 p.60 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
This evening we hang *la crème ailleurs*[^1] — that is, we give the housewarming — at my place, at number 37. A dozen of my fellow students are gathered there. (The Zillardhts, Amélie, Amixe, de Daillens, de Villevielle, Richards, Simonidès, Magdeleine, etc.) The teachers are absent.
%% 2026-10-01T08:32:52 TR: «on pend la crème ailleurs» is Marie's pun on «pendre la crémaillère» (to hold a housewarming); kept the italic French pun with the plain gloss beside it. %%

[^1]: Metalinguistic, left in the original: Marie's pun on *pendre la crémaillère* ("to hang the pot-hook"), the French idiom for giving a housewarming; *la crème ailleurs* is "the cream elsewhere".

The atelier is hung all over with fabrics of every sort, odd lengths of backdrop, Oriental silks and paintings; two large tapestries! On the whole it is charming, plenty of refreshments, punch. Bojidar, who has been helping me arrange everything since ten o'clock this morning, is at the buffet. De Daillens calls him and Alexis my team of well-trained ponies. They are indeed very kind. The princess gives a length of Turkish fabric. As for Mme Nachet, she sent me this morning an exquisite vase with enamelled roses in relief, holding a beautiful plant.
