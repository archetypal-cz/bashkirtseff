# Wednesday, 20 October 1880
Little Mademoiselle Juvisy came to see me play the guitar; she is going to take lessons.

%% 090.0140 %%
%% [#Music_theme](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/MUSIC_THEME.md) %%
%% Tout en jouant je me mis à fredonner et elle resta ébahie de la douceur de ma voix... il ne m'en reste que la douceur. While playing I began to hum, and she was astonished at the sweetness of my voice... I have only the sweetness left. But it is all the more remarkable since in speaking I have a [word blacked out: deep] and strong voice, unless I am trying to be pleasing — then I speak a little as I sing.

A few hundred men gathered at the Cirque Fernando, gave each other blows with their fists, and decided that twelve delegates would go and ask Plon-Plon[^1] to submit or resign in favour of Victor.

[^1]: Nickname for Prince Napoleon (Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte), the unpopular Bonapartist pretender.

It is quite comical. You see that I am fair, and that despite my weakness for Cassagnac I find his politics absurd and ridiculous. If Plon-Plon dies and if Victor allows himself to be swayed by that great idiot of an imperialist-at-any-price, Cassagnac, well, he may perhaps have a few more good days... But it is very chancy, and besides, the Prince is only fifty-eight.

I sometimes think that this great Cassagnac will not be my great passion — he has so diminished himself that I am humiliated through him, and for me to adore a man, he must always hold his ground or else fall as a great man falls; the misfortune of an illustrious one is glorious, but this shrivelling in the manner of Cassagnac... no... and yet he has occupied me for so long that I am quite obliged to grant him the place he has taken... Well, we shall see. I dreamed that he was showing my letters and that his wife was so thin and so ugly.
