# Thursday, 6 January 1881
%% L’Odyssée du tableau continue. Amélie de triste et larmoyante étant devenue gaie, [Rayé: il y a de cela] j’ai bien soupçonné qu’il y avait quelque chose mais sans deviner. Voilà qu’on me dit tout à coup: demandez donc à Julian si vous serez bien seule et tranquille dans le petit salon, c’est bien le moins puisque vous payez la cloison et que le tableau est pour Julian. L’héroïne qui tient ce langage est la mère Magnan et quoique mère elle n’a que vingt-sept ans, est maigre, pointue, la voix rauque, un vrai singe, pas laide; mais bonne fille, mais bonne femme ! Adorant sa petite fille qui est très jolie et son mari le Dr. Magnan, médecin à Sainte Anne, ami de Charcot et sept fois couronné à l’Académie. là-dessus vous devinez bien que je la blague sur l’asile, les aliénés et son époux Valentin. Elle me nettoie mes brosses, ma palette, est gentille au possible et tout à fait maternelle pour moi; la complaisance même en général. Elle tint donc ce langage et moi ne soupçonnant rien malgré son air révélateur, je réponds que c’est bien entendu et qu’il est inutile de le demander mais comme formalité j’envoie la bonne à Julian qui me renvoie dire à ma grande stupéfaction qu’il entend rester absolument maître chez lui (sa marotte qu’Amélie exploite par l’intermédiaire de la bonne en lui faisant dire que je veux dominer etc. Comme c’est assez ma nature et que la bonne le lui transmet avec son air bienveillant pour moi, il a raison de croire, et c’est avec cette ficelle qu’on le fait danser) et que si je fais des conditions il aime mieux qu’on ne fasse pas les travaux. Remarquez que pour qu’il répondît cela il faut que la bonne lui ait dit autre chose que ce que j’ai fait dire. %%

The odyssey of the picture continues. When Amélie went from sad and tearful to cheerful, [Crossed out: some time ago] I did suspect something was up, but without guessing what. And then suddenly someone says to me: "Do ask Julian whether you will be quite alone and undisturbed in the little room; it is the least he can do, since you are paying for the partition and the picture is for Julian." The heroine who speaks thus is Mother Magnan, and though a mother she is only twenty-seven, thin, sharp-featured, with a hoarse voice, a real monkey, not ugly; but a good sort, a good woman! She adores her little girl, who is very pretty, and her husband, Dr Magnan, physician at Sainte-Anne, a friend of Charcot's and seven times crowned by the Académie. You can well guess that I tease her on that score about the asylum, the lunatics and her spouse Valentin. She cleans my brushes and my palette for me, is as kind as can be and quite motherly towards me; obligingness itself, in general. So she spoke thus, and I, suspecting nothing in spite of her telltale air, reply that of course it is understood and there is no need to ask, but as a formality I send the maid to Julian, who sends back word, to my utter astonishment, that he means to remain absolutely master in his own house (his hobby-horse, which Amélie exploits through the maid by having her tell him that I want to dominate, etc. As that is rather my nature, and the maid passes it on to him with her air of goodwill towards me, he is right to believe it, and that is the string by which they make him dance) and that if I make conditions he would rather the work were not done. Note that for him to answer that, the maid must have told him something other than what I had her say.

%% Il monte et je vais au confessional lui demander ce que tout cela signifie, lui expliquant tout. Alors il me fait des réponses qui ne sont pas des réponses, je voyais bien qu’il était préparé et ne comprenait rien toujours, lorsqu’il me dit qu’Amélie travaillera peut-être dans le même salon que moi, qu’il a voulu être agréable à deux personnes avec ce tableau et que s’il veut mettre Amélie là il veut en avoir le droit. Pensez donc, je tombe des nues, voilà que j’ai l’air d’avoir soulevé la question sachant qu’il est question d’Amélie. Je suis si surprise que je reste là à causer d’autre chose et comme si j’avais besoin de me livrer je raconte mes affaires avec Hubertine. Je suis très bête décidément. En sortant de là je ne dis ni oui ni non et Amélie pensant que j’accepte la chose m’explique qu’elle ne me gênera pas, se placera après moi et de façon très discrète. Je réponds que cela me paraît difficile et surtout ridicule de faire le même tableau bien que de grandeurs nature différentes, le même point de vue, le même effet de lumières. Enfin, c’est inouï, là-dessus je la blague sur ses amours avec Julian et le rideau baisse. Je rentre avec les mâchoires serrées et tellement révoltée de la duplicité de ce Julian et des intrigues de cette fille que j’en suis malade. Il est heureux encore que cette bonne Magnan m’ait prévenue, elle pensait que je devinais tout, on en avait parlé du reste mais j’ai pas entendu. %%

He comes up, and I go to the confessional to ask him what all this means, explaining everything to him. Then he gives me answers that are no answers; I could see he was prepared, and I still understood nothing, until he tells me that Amélie will perhaps work in the same room as I, that he wanted to oblige two people with this picture, and that if he wants to put Amélie there he means to have the right to do so. Just think — I am thunderstruck; now I look as though I had raised the question knowing that Amélie was involved. I am so taken aback that I stay there talking of other things, and as if I needed to unburden myself I tell him about my affairs with Hubertine. I am decidedly very stupid. On coming out I say neither yes nor no, and Amélie, thinking I accept the arrangement, explains to me that she will not be in my way, will set herself up after me, and very discreetly. I reply that it seems to me difficult, and above all ridiculous, to paint the same picture, even at different sizes, from the same point of view, with the same effect of light. Well, it is unheard of; whereupon I tease her about her love affair with Julian, and the curtain falls. I come home with my jaws clenched and so revolted by this Julian's duplicity and that girl's intrigues that I am ill with it. It is lucky, still, that good Mme Magnan warned me; she thought I had guessed everything; it had been talked about, moreover, but I had not heard.

In the evening, a meeting of the board of our future newspaper; they discuss the title, which has already been discussed. I was there yesterday, and this evening I take the Cerny girl there; I felt so dull-witted that I needed all that. I am already on very good terms with the *citoyens* and *citoyennes.* I do not hide from myself how little strength we have and how little all these discussions are worth, but everything must have a beginning; I am taking it up at the very start, I shall share all the hardships, I shall help to raise the edifice.

%% Connaissez-vous “la corneille qui abat des noix” ? %% 2026-09-28T21:01:18 RSR: Restored from tome14.docx ¶763, Mon Journal t.14 p.59 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
Do you know *La Corneille qui abat des noix*? A provincial arrives at the house of a family of Parisian friends and fancies he has penetrated all their secrets: the mother is in love with her future son-in-law, the daughter... In short, they demonstrate to him in vain that there is nothing in it, that the mother is marrying her daughter to this man — "Poor woman, she is sacrificing herself!" he cries — and that the father has dishonoured no one. But our man keeps his ideas and sets off home swearing that these family secrets will die with him. One could have oneself cut into little pieces to prove to Mme Mouzay that her inventions are fancies; she would be like the aforesaid provincial. I do not know how you take it; as for me, it drives me to distraction!
