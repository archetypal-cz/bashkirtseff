> The Chamber of Deputies shows unprecedented generosity, except, however, when it is a question of a few thousand francs to support the men maimed on our battlefields at the Invalides, or to pay the naval chaplains. In that regard it is intractable, and it is a safe bet that it will not accept the restoration of the credits voted by the Senate for this purpose. But the four billion of M. de Freycinet, the friend of M. Gambetta — those, you may be sure, will be procured at any cost. They want to do things on a grand scale. The memories of the Empire keep our republicans awake at night. They pretend to crush it with their contempt, but they imitate it.

> It does not appear, unfortunately, that they have managed thus far to revive business. It is in vain that they have organised the Exposition; in vain that they hold out the prospect of an unprecedented boost to public works. In vain that they have imprisoned the Marshal, subdued the conservative opposition in the Senate. In vain that everything smiles upon them: the Chambers, the ministers, the electors, and even M. de Bismarck (?) who sends them his finest paintings for the Trocadéro palace. Business does not revive.
