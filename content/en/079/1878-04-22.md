# Monday, 22 April 1878
%% Il y a trois nouvelles Suédoises à l’atelier dont une très forte. Schaeppi l’a mise au courant de moi, elle et ses deux amies sont donc avec moi. Puis j’ai posé pour mon portrait que peint Schaeppi, cette semaine j’aurai donc fini ces corvées. Puis, habillée de noir, je vais à pied avec Schaeppi depuis le rond-point jusqu’à la place de la Concorde. Quoique très simplement mise, je l’ai entendu *quinze fois* que j’ai une taille divine. Quant à ceux qui se sont retournés... je ne les ai pas comptés. J’en avais prévenu Schaeppi et nous nous en sommes beaucoup amusées. Mais à quoi bon tout cela puisqu’à la maison je trouve Blanc qui dit que l’on dit que Cassagnac épouse la fille de la baronne Acard. Nous ne nous gênons pas pour dire que Cassagnac marié serait une chose monstrueuse, affreuse, ignoble, grotesque. Elle n’a que de 20 à 30.000 livres de rente. Moi j’en aurai de 40 à 50 le jour du mariage. Mais avant Blanc, maman et Dîna ont rencontré le grand homme qui leur a demandé à venir dîner jeudi. Serait-ce la gratification que j’attendais du ciel ? %%

There are three new Swedish girls at the atelier, one of them very good. Schaeppi has told her all about me, so she and her two friends are on my side. Then I sat for my portrait, which Schaeppi is painting; so this week I shall be done with these chores. Then, dressed in black, I walk with Schaeppi from the Rond-Point to the Place de la Concorde. Though very simply dressed, I heard it said *fifteen times* that I have a divine figure. As for those who turned round... I did not count them. I had told Schaeppi to watch for it, and we were much amused. But what is the use of all that, since at home I find Blanc, who says people are saying that Cassagnac is marrying the daughter of Baroness Acard. We make no bones about saying that Cassagnac married would be something monstrous, frightful, ignoble, grotesque. She has only 20,000 to 30,000 livres a year. I shall have 40,000 to 50,000 on my wedding day. But before Blanc came, Maman and Dina met the great man, who asked them if he might come to dinner on Thursday. Could this be the recompense I was expecting from heaven?

Dina plays the madcap and I the sage; [words blacked out: I say things] that are very virtuous — which are, for that matter, only my innermost convictions, but which I am not in the habit of telling. Blanc treats me absolutely as an extraordinary woman: I am the most intelligent, the sweetest, the most everything that is best; I shall be famous; I laugh at everyone. But you know, he seems to believe it, though he says it half laughing. And what if I did not laugh at everything, what if I had other concerns than cutting out muscles and sticking them on the skeleton!... He seems not to admit the possibility. So much the better, but it annoys me all the same!

Blanc is not the man to put in a good word for me with anyone, though he himself is worse than a dog where I am concerned.

If only this week would end sooner! I waste my afternoons sitting; it would bore me to death if I were not reading the *History of Germany* meanwhile and taking notes, which I can do without looking. I mean to become famous all the same; if it is not to paint the portrait of Paul de Cassagnac, who, once married, will be utterly lost, it will be to paint someone else's.
