# Friday, 5 April 1878
In the wake of the misunderstandings and April Fools' jests we had to invite several people to dinner, among them Multedo, whom

%% 079.0234 %%
%% [#Reading](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/READING.md) %%
%% avait invité avant et qui s'était excusé et auquel maman a écrit hier et lequel croyant que c'est moi qui écrit me répond ce que vous pouvez voir en lisant cette lettre. %%
we had previously invited and who had excused himself, and to whom Maman wrote yesterday — and he, believing it was I who wrote, replies as you may see by reading this letter.

> That is settled. But allow me to return to you what you sent me in error and which I received this morning, at the hour when the lark greets the dawn: a note intended for M. de Morgan. M. de Morgan, whose address I do not know, must have received another that was intended for me. In that case, be advised that it has not reached me.

%% 079.0240 %%
%% kind: letter source="Lettre d'A. Multedo, Paris, 5 avril 1878" %%
%% > Peut-être qu'il n'a rien reçu et qu'en écrivant l'adresse vous avez confondu son nom avec le mien ou sa personne avec moi. Je ne savais pas que je lui ressemblais et je suis flatté de la similitude qui pourrait exister entre nous. %%
> Perhaps he received nothing, and in writing the address you confused his name with mine, or his person with me. I did not know that I resembled him, and I am flattered by whatever similitude may exist between us.

How it pains me that everything is broken off with Cassagnac. I do not know what has happened or what has broken, but what I know is that I shall not know how to approach him, and that the old understanding has melted like last year's snow...

All my life I have sought to place myself, *voluntarily*, under some illusory domination or other, but all those people I have tried were so ordinary compared with me that I have had nothing from them but disgust.

Bad inclinations still drive me to seek this domination by another, but I do not want it, since I seriously desire to think of nothing but my future, for which an almost complete freedom of mind is indispensable.
