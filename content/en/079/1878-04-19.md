# Friday, 19 April 1878
We go to church, where I sin a great deal [Words blacked out: because I] cannot help thinking that the evening spent at the Opéra with Multedo might have been [Crossed out: what would it have been, then, if it had been] with Cassagnac! I kept telling myself, instead of praying. He had whispered the number of his box in my ear, and I had promised to go there and to sup with him at Voisin's... without any sort of Mme de Dailleurs, I assure you. And nothing would have prevented it if Mme Berthe Lancaster had not taken off her mask! Only think! That evening, that night, and then everything that might have followed.

And you think, you lugubrious cretins, you think it is something one forgets! And thereupon there I am, praying to the good Lord to arrange this matter for me. On the way out Maman talks with Princess Karageorges and her son, whom we met in Nice and in Ostend and who seem to wish to keep up the acquaintance. It is understood, of course, that I am posing; otherwise I should not be tearing at your ears while tearing at my soul over this mask, which will remain one of the great rages of my life. Wretched woman!

Blanc dined with us, and then, as Maman was busy elsewhere, Dina, he and I chat in the drawing room, and Dina keeps up her part as the woman in love with Cassagnac — whom, for that matter, we talk about almost all the time. Now they are playing piquet; I had fallen asleep on a sofa after this interesting conversation. It seems Cassagnac is a man who weeps on occasion. And Blanc adds that such men are generally a tremendous success with women. I know it. Dina kept on telling her fancies, so Blanc said she was working herself up for a joke, and that, besides, Cassagnac too has the sort of character to imagine any love whatever and to cultivate it until he makes it a real one.

%% 079.0493 %%
%% Dites-donc ce serait amusant de s’imaginer un grand amour... je l’étudierais aussi bien que le mieux réussi de mes dessins, je chercherais les plans, la construction les demi-teintes, l’effet général enfin ! Ce serait serré comme un Holbein auquel on me compare. Demain je vais communier, eh bien il me semble qu’en ce moment suprême si je demandais quelque chose à Dieu Il me l’accorderait. Au premier abord j’eus l’idée de demander que	je ne le dirai pas, vous le savez. %%
%% 2026-09-29T06:52:53 RSR: Restored from tome11.docx ¶3445, Mon Journal t.11 p.214 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). OCR slip «Dieu II me» corrected to «Dieu Il me» (Mon Journal t.11 p.214). %%
I say, it would be amusing to dream up a great love... I would study it as carefully as the most successful of my drawings; I would look for the planes, the construction, the half-tones — the general effect, in short! It would be as tightly worked as a Holbein, to whom I am compared. Tomorrow I am to take Communion; well, it seems to me that at that supreme moment, if I asked God for something, He would grant it. My first idea was to ask that — I shall not say it; you know what.

%% 079.0494 %%
%% Puis ayant pensé que ce n’était pas assez sérieux je voulais demander de devenir un grand peintre... et la dernière idée c'est de demander de me marier magnifiquement et au plus vite. Eh bien, non, je lui demanderais la première chose, parce que j’en serais fière devant tous ceux qui m’entourent et que je suis assez vaine pour préférer cette misère, à une gloire universelle... Attendez ! Pas du tout, cette chose n’empêchera pas la gloire, et remarquez que je ne choisis pas entre les deux, je désire l’une et je m’attends à l’autre. S’il s’agissait de choisir entre les deux je n’hésiterais pas, vous le savez bien et donnerais tout pour mon orgueil, pour ma gloire enfin ! C’est entendu, je le Lui demanderai demain et en un moment pareil Il ne pourra faire autrement que de me l’accorder. *1880. D’épouser Cassagnac.* %%
%% 2026-09-29T06:52:53 RSR: Restored from tome11.docx ¶3446–3449, Mon Journal t.11 pp.214–215 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). OCR slip «pareil II ne» corrected to «pareil Il ne» (Mon Journal t.11 p.214). %%
Then, thinking this was not serious enough, I meant to ask to become a great painter... and the last idea is to ask to be married magnificently and as soon as possible. Well, no — I would ask Him for the first thing, because I would be proud of it before everyone around me, and because I am vain enough to prefer that paltry thing to universal glory... Wait! Not at all — that thing will not prevent glory, and note that I am not choosing between the two: I desire the one and I expect the other. If it were a matter of choosing between them I would not hesitate, as you well know, and would give everything for my pride — for my glory, in short! It is settled: I shall ask Him tomorrow, and at such a moment He cannot do otherwise than grant it. *1880. To marry Cassagnac.*

Mais il y a toujours moyen d’arranger les choses, ajouta-t-il comme je me récriais, on se rattrappe en faisant voir que la déesse descend de son piédestal pour un simple mortel... qui en est tout naturellement flatté. %%

Blanc told me a hard truth. Men, he said, are more cowardly than you think. They are afraid of a woman who is too strong. But there is always a way of setting things right, he added, as I protested: one makes up for it by showing that the goddess steps down from her pedestal for a mere mortal... who is, quite naturally, flattered.
