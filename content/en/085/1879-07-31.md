Madame de Lesseps came to invite us to her house this evening. Monsieur de Lesseps has come back to Paris to dine and will leave again tomorrow to give a lecture at Nancy for the festivities. He is a truly prodigious man. This [words blacked out: This evening he] waltzed... Berthe was with us; the poor child stayed to dinner, and when I jokingly told her to come to the Lesseps' she seemed to want to, and kept asking whether people would not think it eccentric. Her husband all but shuts her up, so she did not think of going home to dress; I lent her everything from the dress down to the stockings, and off we went. That woman is not happy. Take it from me. Her husband has bewitched Madame Boyd, who always takes his side; they are always going out and spending their time together, and Berthe is always left out.

There was not a great deal of company; the hosts were absolutely charming to us, but knowing almost no one, I felt ill at ease. We made the acquaintance of Madame de Givry and her daughter. But the season is not favourable for making acquaintances — everyone is leaving. I did not have much success, and the heat! Hardly anyone danced, despite Monsieur de Lesseps doing his best to set things in motion.
