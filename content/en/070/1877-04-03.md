# Tuesday, 3 April 1877

— Not too much Larderel, eh? said Doenhoff as he left.

Today is the day of the famous excursion to Sorrento, which I had opposed several times.

From Castellammare to Sorrento we arrange ourselves in a carriage: Maman, Dina and Melissano on the forward seats, Marcuard and I on the bench facing them, Bijou[^bijou] up on the box, a ==street idler==[^lazzarone] clinging to the back of the carriage, and our considerable luggage of eight pieces — and off we go.

All of this passed in teasing, general and particular. We snatched handkerchiefs, hats and gloves from one another. For the rest, I was fairly calm — Larderel could talk to no one but the coachman — but soon I grew jealous of the coachman, and I made the sublime reprobate hold my parasol, and on his arm I leant my head, as if on account of the jolts, with a quite particular pleasure.

By the time we reached Massa it was dark. I was frozen and in a foul mood; we sat down in a miserable tavern yard, where a drunken ==street idler== came and sang a song that seemed composed for Larderel and... Silène!! He listened, pale and distracted; I was seething.

On the way back we were alone together almost the whole time. I cannot think of that hour now without shivers of pleasure.

%% [#Love](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/LOVE.md) %%

%% Nous marchions côte à côte par cette route superbe, dans la nuit on ne se voyait presque pas, je m'étais voilée et nous parlions le plus naturellement du monde du bal masqué, des lettres, de son duel. We walked side by side along that magnificent road; in the dark we could barely see each other; I had veiled myself, and we talked in the most natural way in the world about the masked ball, about letters, about his duel. From time to time he took my hand, and I lost myself in sensations new and adorable. I told him he had interested me, at the same time as he was saying that I was charming and that he loved me madly. I would rather not have heard it, because I deserve something better than this passing declaration, this sort of amusement.

[^bijou]: *Bijou* ("jewel") is Marie's private codename for Count Larderel throughout this period.
[^lazzarone]: In Italian in the original: *lazzarone* (pl. *lazzaroni*) — Neapolitan term for a street idler or rascal, often hired for odd jobs.
[^silene]: *Silène*: Silenus, the drunken foster-father of Dionysus in Greek mythology, also associated with wine and revelry. The joke replaces the *r* of *Sirène* (the hotel's name) with *l* — making the hotel's name a coded reference to Larderel's mistress, La Righi.
[^horns]: *Une paire de cornes*: a pair of horns — the cuckold's sign in French idiom. Larderel is joking about La Righi's infidelity to him.
[^thule]: "There was a king of Thule": the Ballad of the King of Thule (*Il était un roi de Thulé*), aria from Act III of Gounod's *Faust* (1859). Marguerite sings this ballad about a king faithful to his beloved's memory — just before Faust seduces her.
[^faust]: In Gounod's *Faust* (1859), Marguerite is seduced and abandoned by Faust; she kills her child and dies in prison. The opera was everywhere in Marie's cultural world.
[^lustucru]: *Lustucru*: a comic stock figure from French nursery rhymes and popular culture, embodiment of gullible naivety (from *l'eusses-tu cru* — "would you have believed it").
[^swiss]: *Vieux Suisse des treize cantons*: "old Swiss of the thirteen cantons" — the Swiss Confederation had thirteen cantons until 1798. Marcuard is Swiss.
[^faun]: *Le faune*: the faun — a lustful woodland spirit from classical mythology.
[^gris]: *Gris* — French colloquial for tipsy or mildly drunk (literally "grey"); the term Marie uses in the original.
