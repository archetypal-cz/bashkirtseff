%% Après tout qu'est-ce qui me désespère ? Larderel a trois cent mille francs de rente et il est comte. Oui, mais *ici,* ça avait l'air de prendre, mais ça n'a pas pris et de la plus étrange façon. Passons à la seconde considération, l'amour. L'amour ? Est ridicule lorsqu'il n'est pas partagé. Donc, l'amour n'existe plus ! Mais il a existé et ça a été le seul, le premier après l'adoration mêlée d'intérêt et d'extase pour le Duc. %%
%% 2026-09-28T20:52:24 RSR: Restored from tome10.docx ¶2070, Mon Journal t.10 p.121 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
After all, what is it that drives me to despair? Larderel has an income of three hundred thousand francs a year and he is a count. He has no social position, you will say; he has everything he needs to establish himself wherever he pleases, I shall reply, adding that I prefer that to a comfortably settled mediocrity. So is that what I regret? But there are plenty of others with the same advantages who do not want me either. Must one torment oneself over every *suitable* match? Certainly not. Yes, but *here* it seemed to be taking, and it did not take — and in the strangest way. Let us pass to the second consideration: love. Love? Is ridiculous when it is not returned. So love no longer exists! But it did exist, and it was the only one, the first since the adoration, mingled with self-interest and ecstasy, that I felt for the Duke.

I have hidden nothing in this business with Larderel, and they insist there is more to it than I say! Since she says it, she must think a devilish lot of it; and if she thinks so much of it, why does she not say more?
