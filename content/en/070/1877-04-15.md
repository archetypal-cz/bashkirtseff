# Sunday, 15 April 1877
%% Il y a des courses au trot et au chronomètre à ce qu'il paraît aujourd'hui. Nous y allons et je me mets en toilette de batiste brodée et chapeau de paille. Un certain effet. Il y a peu de monde mais il y a la Cour et les Prussiens. Aussitôt arrivèrent les deux petits, Zunica et le fils de la Piedimonte qui est sur la tribune avec la princesse Marguerite. Mais j'étais très absorbée par le duc de Sarno, Larderel et plusieurs autres sur la tribune des hommes. Sous prétexte de lorgner les princesses, j'ai lorgné cet animal, cet ivrogne, ce sapajou de Florence. Melissano aperçoit ma lorgnette et salue. %%
%% 2026-09-28T20:52:24 RSR: Restored from tome10.docx ¶1973, Mon Journal t.10 p.115 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). Typesetting error of the printed edition corrected: «priincesses»→«princesses» (Mon Journal t.10 p.115). %%
There are trotting races against the clock today, it appears. We go, and I put on an embroidered batiste dress and a straw hat. It made something of an impression. There are few people, but the Court is there, and the Prussians. At once the two little ones arrived, Zunica and the son of La Piedimonte, who is in the stand with Princess Margherita. But I was very much taken up with the Duke of Sarno, Larderel and several others in the men's stand. On the pretext of looking at the princesses through my glass, I looked at that animal, that drunkard, that little monkey from Florence. Melissano notices my lorgnette and bows.

I understood almost everything. He liked me, but since his first return I have been so plain — no, let us tell the truth: I am short of dresses, and a heap of these little details hamper me and rob me of whatever grace and naturalness I had. And then I was too afraid of looking bad to look well. It was a remark of his that confirmed the suspicions I had had before. The points of all our shoes were being admired, Altamura's too — he was there as well, also in a carriage.
