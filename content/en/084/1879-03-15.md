%% Si Robert-Fleury, nommé Tony en son absence, me gronde aujourd'hui je ne fais plus de peinture ! Vous savez ce que mes progrès m'ont valu d'envie, et surtout de dénigrements. Chaque fois que cela cloche on a l'air de s'écrier: je vous le disais bien, ça ne pouvait pas durer. Mes premières toiles m'ont valu des compliments et puis je suis entrée dans une passe difficile et je sentais trop de satisfactions autour de moi pour ne pas en souffrir assez fort. Ce matin j'attendais cette leçon comme quelque chose d'épouvantable et pendant que cet animal de Tony corrigeait les autres et se rapprochait peu à peu de ma place, je récitais des prières avec une ferveur que le ciel a appréciée car on a été content de moi. Grand Dieu quel poids m'est tombé du coeur ! Vous n'avez peut-être pas idée de ces émotions-là ? Figurez-vous ce silence où je sentais la joie qu'on aurait de me voir encore aplatie - cette fois ce serait pour de bon - car amis ou ennemis sont les mêmes dans ces choses-là. %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:52:33 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
If Robert-Fleury — known as Tony in his absence — scolds me today, I give up painting! You know how much envy, and above all how much disparagement, my progress has brought me. Every time something goes wrong, they seem to cry: I told you so, it could not last. My first canvases brought me compliments, and then I entered a difficult phase, and I sensed too much gloating around me not to suffer from it keenly. This morning I awaited this lesson as something dreadful, and while that brute Tony corrected the others and came little by little nearer my place, I recited prayers with a fervour that heaven appreciated — for he was pleased with me. Great heavens, what a weight fell from my heart! Perhaps you have no idea of such emotions? Picture that silence in which I felt the joy they would have had in seeing me crushed again — and this time for good — for friends and enemies are the same in such things.

I am not afraid of madness, but I should like to rid myself of an obsession that is becoming unbearable. It dates from the famous paragraph in the *Figaro*, the Nice races. You do not know what a torment it is.

It seems to me that everywhere I am taken for a *cocotte*. And my aunt — an aunt — who is so indifferent, so passive, who looks so much as though she were only accompanying me! And I, who am decided and lively. In shops this idea makes me utterly wretched.

%% 2026-10-01T07:52:33 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
This evening we dine at the Karageorgevitches'. The prince served in the Guard after renouncing the throne of Serbia for himself and his family; he is a sickly, ordinary man. The princess is simple too; she will never understand that one can be bored at evenings like this one. Two very simple American women, the two sons of the house and two French friends, the Comtes de Deuil (?). It all smelt of the baccalauréat or the Law Faculty, and so we talked of philosophy and a heap of schoolroom things.
