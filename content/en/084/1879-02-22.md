# Saturday, 22 February 1879

What a difference from Paris! Here I wake of my own accord; the windows are open all night. The room I occupy is the one where we used to take our drawing lessons with Bensa seven years ago. I see the sun gradually lighting the trees near the little basin in the middle of the garden, as I saw it every morning then. My little study has the same wallpaper — the one I chose myself. It is no doubt occupied by some savage Englishman. I recognised it by the paper, for a corridor has been installed that confuses me. The room I am in was once a glass-fronted display room.

But I did something foolish. I went to the charity bazaar at the Square Masséna. I should have kept well clear of it all... but it was to see Paul buying a flower from the young Eristoff girl, who is here with Madame Tutcheff and her brother. Paul has been in love with her since she left school. Two years ago she came to Gavronzi, but as soon as it was seen how matters stood, the beauty was sent away. Paul wrote to her, and Constantin Eristoff, intercepting the letter, made his goose of a sister believe that they were not asking for her hand but rather... the opposite. Madame Tutcheff took care of the rest; her lover wrote Paul an insulting letter [words blacked out: and avoided] meeting him at every turn.

%% Il y a un mois Paul fit tout au monde pour le rencontrer mais en vain. Cette fois il a vu la sœur, ce qui est un évènement de genre tout différent mais qui le jette dans le plus grand trouble. Inconnu il lui serait facile de parvenir à voir l'infante, mais dans l'état actuel des choses, il lui est impossible de se justifier car on lui a *fait* croire qu'il avait les intentions les plus basses, poussé par nous pour se venger de la Tutcheff. Vous voyez ça d'ici. %%
A month ago Paul did everything possible to meet him, but in vain. This time he has seen the sister — an event of a quite different kind but one that throws him into the greatest agitation. Were he a stranger to them, it would be easy enough for him to manage to see the infanta — but in the present state of affairs it is impossible for him to vindicate himself, for she has been *made* to believe he had the basest intentions, put up to it by us to take revenge on the Tutcheff woman. You can imagine it.

%% Mon désormais unique frère est resté jusqu'au matin chez moi mais nous n'avons rien trouvé. En effet il n'y a rien à faire tant que nous ne savons pas la pensée de la jeune fille. Deux années changent un femme. De timide et d'innocente brebis on peut devenir une petite demoiselle fort dégourdie et tomber en admiration devant les misérables gommeux qui daignent, rarement, orner les salons de Nice de leur présence. %%
My now only brother stayed with me until morning but we found no solution. Indeed there is nothing to be done as long as we do not know the young woman's thoughts. Two years can change a woman. From a timid and innocent lamb one may become a very sharp young miss and be smitten with the wretched dandies who condescend — rarely — to grace the salons of Nice with their presence.
