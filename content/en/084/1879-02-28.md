# Friday, 28 February 1879

%% 084.0344 %%
%% [#Nice](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/NICE.md) [#Paul_de_Cassagnac](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PAUL_DE_CASSAGNAC.md) [#Paul_frere](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PAUL_FRERE.md) [#Eristoff](../../_original/_glossary/people/aristocracy/ERISTOFF.md) %%
%% [#Politics](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/POLITICS.md) %%
%% J'ai rêvé que Cassagnac venu en visite chez nous s'était brûlé tout entier comme la poupée du carnaval mardi dernier. J'étais toute perplexe et ne savais comment dissimuler qu'il était venu, pour qu'on ne pensât pas que nous l'avions exterminé. %%
I dreamed that Cassagnac, having come to visit us, burned up entirely like the carnival effigy last Tuesday. I was quite perplexed and did not know how to conceal that he had come, so that no one would think we had exterminated him.

> *I am keenly and deeply grieved that you should have been compelled to write the letter you wrote me. But at the same time I am glad of it, for it gives me the means of proving to you that I am a gentleman. In the hands of another, that letter might perhaps have gravely compromised you, despite your sacred title of orphan. As for the successes I may have had thanks to my "snout", as you put it with such charming originality, I respect you too much to enumerate them to you.*

Je suis très fatiguée et j'ai la fièvre, car je crois que c'est la fièvre quand on a froid toute la journée et quand on se sent toute faible. Des Perrières n'a encore rien répondu, il n'était peut- être pas rentré; et Scotti s'est excusé en écrivant qu'il était vraiment désolé, qu'il ne pouvait aller chez lui et qu'il lui dirait pourquoi. Je ne comprends pas bien. Paul m'a assuré à plusieurs reprises que ces messieurs ont été très aimables et ont accepté avec empressement de l'aider s'il en avait besoin. A présent. Comment faire et que faire ? Les bras me tombent. Si c'était moi, je serais allée partout et puis j'aurais vu, compris, parlé. Mais c'est Paul ! Il a su commencer et il ne sait plus rien. %% 2026-09-29T01:01:26 RSR: Restored from tome12.docx ¶2558, Mon Journal t.12 pp.226–227 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). Printer's typo corrected: «Fhéâtre» → «Théâtre» (Mon Journal t.12 p.226). %%
%% 2026-10-02T15:28:10 RSR: The four LAN notes above (empressement, Les bras me tombent, je fais tout au monde, abattu) were moved here from 084.0345, where the quoted words do not occur. %%
We dined at the London House with Bihovetz and then we go to the Théâtre Français, where there is yesterday's audience, the whole of travelling Paris. I hide behind Dina; I feel ugly. I am very tired and I have a fever — for I think it is fever when one is cold all day and feels quite weak. Paul has written to Scotti and to Des Pierrière asking them to come to his rooms. Des Perrières has not yet replied — perhaps he had not come home; and Scotti excused himself, writing that he was truly sorry, that he could not go to him and that he would tell him why. I do not quite understand. Paul assured me several times that these gentlemen had been very kind and had eagerly agreed to help him if he needed it. Now. How to proceed, what to do? I am at my wits' end. If it were me, I would have gone everywhere, and then I would have seen, understood, spoken. But it is Paul! He knew how to begin, and now he is quite at a loss. Now that he has thrown himself into this, he must go on — and I do everything in the world towards that end. But the poor fellow is dejected.
