I stop in front of Gioia's; as she is not here and everything is shut, I pick some daisies by putting my arm through the railings, and we solemnly pluck their petals. O flower, I thought, you who come from her garden, from the garden where he has been, where he has walked, tell me once and for all: shall I ever be, at least once and in whatever circumstances, to him what she has been to him? I pull off all the petals on my side and the two girls on theirs, and one flower told me *yes*, and I raised my head with joy, vexation, shame and anger, but I raised it, for I was strangely satisfied.
