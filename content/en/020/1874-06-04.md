The first night spent at the Hotel des Iles Britanniques was not bad. I had a dream all streaked with Wittgenstein; incidentally, since I spoke of him, long ago, I have not seen him again and have scarcely thought of him. I recalled him only to ask myself why I no longer think of him.

%% Une fête comme la vente du square Masséna de Nice. Les billets de la tombola étaient vendus par des chevaliers du pince-nez. De très jolies toilettes, les plus belles; duchesse de Mouchy, une dame qui lui ressemble, une dame en blanc, le cou couvert de perles extraordinaires, qu'on disait la duchesse de Montmorency, et la princesse Souvoroff. Cette dernière ne vendait rien du tout. Elle et sa fille restèrent quelque temps avec nous puis nous nous séparâmes. Nous restâmes assis, ensuite je fis avec maman le tour du jardin. La duchesse de Mouchy me coûte dix francs, elle était si belle aujourd'hui. Le baron de Rothschild était près de la dame aux perles, je priai maman de retourner pour la voir encore, maman retourna avec hésitation, elle pensait que je voulais voir Rothschild parce que j'ai rougi plusieurs fois pour lui depuis que nous sommes à Paris. A Nice il me regardait beaucoup et un jour c'est-à-dire presque toujours, il s'arrêtait net et regardait lorsque je passais. Et même un jour, comme j'étais en voiture avec maman et ma tante, il s'arrêta durement et regarda, alors ma tante dit: %%
A fete like the sale at the square Massena in Nice. The raffle tickets were sold by knights of the pince-nez. Very pretty dresses, the most beautiful: the duchesse de Mouchy, a lady who resembles her, a lady in white with her neck covered in extraordinary pearls, said to be the duchesse de Montmorency, and Princess Souvoroff. This last was selling nothing at all. She and her daughter stayed some time with us, then we parted. We remained seated; then I took a turn of the garden with Maman. The duchesse de Mouchy cost me ten francs -- she was so beautiful today. Baron de Rothschild was near the lady with the pearls; I begged Maman to go back so I could see her again. Maman turned back with hesitation, thinking I wanted to see Rothschild, because I have blushed several times on his account since we arrived in Paris. In Nice he looked at me a great deal, and one day -- that is to say, nearly always -- he would stop short and stare when I passed. And even one day, when I was in the carriage with Maman and my aunt, he stopped abruptly and stared, and my aunt said:

Maman interrupts me every minute reading me *Le Figaro*; I am irritated and beg her not to share this happiness with me any longer. I was rather bored at this sale; I know no one, no one, no one, and no one! It is true that someone remarked on my boots, which were very pretty and dainty, but that is a mediocre consolation. In pleasures I find my boredom -- am I to repeat my eternal complaints yet again! No, that would be boring; however, I have nothing against it: the pen writes well, the ink is good, the paper too.
