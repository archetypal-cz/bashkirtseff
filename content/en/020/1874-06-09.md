# Tuesday, 9 June 1874

%% 020.0179 %%
%% [#Paul](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PAUL.md) %%
%% [#Religion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/RELIGION.md) %%
%% 2026-01-30T12:10:59 LAN: "s'est imposé" - imposed himself; uninvited %%
%% 2026-01-30T12:11:00 LAN: "enivré de lui-même" - drunk on himself %%
%% 2026-01-30T12:11:01 LAN: "fastidieux" - tedious, tiresome %%
%% Le diacre est déjà ici, il **s'est imposé** pour aller entendre la messe. Ce misérable prêtre est enivré de lui-même; il prend des airs d'enfant gâté, de tout ce qu'il y a de plus adorable au monde et avec cela est un des êtres les plus fastidieux que j'ai jamais rencontré. Il s'oublie même souvent, par exemple, il envoie Paul lui acheter des cigarettes. J'ai rougi dix fois de colère. %%
The deacon is already here; he has **invited himself** to come and hear Mass. This wretched priest is intoxicated with himself; he assumes airs of a spoiled child, of all that is most adorable in the world, and for all that he is one of the most tedious beings I have ever met. He even forgets himself often -- for example, he sends Paul to buy him cigarettes. I blushed ten times with anger.

%% 020.0180 %%

This wretched *deacon,* with tarred boots! We are all ready at half past one.

%% Que dirai-je pauvre petite ignorante, de la messe de requiem ? Je ne me sens pas de force pour critiquer. C'est très beau, extrêmement beau, admirable, mais cela aurait pu être encore plus beau. What shall I say, poor little ignorant creature, about the Requiem Mass? I do not feel strong enough to criticise. It is very beautiful, extremely beautiful, admirable, but it could have been still more beautiful. I should like to hear it several more times; a piece of music so serious is not understood or judged in a single hearing. There are several passages that escaped me and that I rediscover only now.

%% Verdi en entrant fut accueilli par des applaudissements frénétiques. Cela m'excite comme les courses (pas une face passable dans le chœur). J'écoutais attentivement la Woldmann et la Stoltz car on sait que jadis j'eus quelques prétentions. Depuis presque un an ma voix fait des voyages comme "Les trois mousquetaires", mais je crois qu'en ce moment elle commence à revenir. La Stoltz fait de trop grands efforts pour chanter, elle a l'air de labourer la terre et non de chanter. %%
Verdi on entering was greeted by frantic applause. It excites me like the races (not one passable face in the choir). I listened attentively to Woldmann and Stoltz, for it is known that once I had some pretensions of my own. For nearly a year my voice has been travelling like "The Three Musketeers," but I believe that at this moment it is beginning to return. Stoltz makes too great an effort to sing; she looks as if she were ploughing a field rather than singing.

%% C'est une mémorable matinée, peut-être pourrai-je me vanter un jour d'avoir entendu la première fois à Paris chanter cette messe de Verdi. Il faisait une chaleur excessive et aussitôt rentrées nous nous *spogliamo.* Un télégramme. Qu'est-ce ? Mille francs qu'envoie ma tante, c'est tout de même quelque chose quand on n'a pas trop "Requiem... requiem... Requiem eterno" !! It was a memorable matinee; perhaps I shall be able to boast one day of having heard this mass of Verdi's sung for the first time in Paris. The heat was excessive, and as soon as we returned we ==*spogliamo.*==[^1] A telegram. What is it? A thousand francs sent by my aunt -- it is still something when one does not have too much. "==*Requiem... requiem... Requiem eterno*==[^2]!!" Maman was looking out the window, then turning: "Suppose we went to the Bois." I was about to propose it.

So we go. I am pretty at this moment; I put on Maman's white muslin dress -- it suits me; in the carriage the length does not show. My straw hat, hair half pinned up, some short curls on the nape (natural). I am pleased with myself, very grateful to my aunt.

As soon as we reach the level of the infantry post, the devil drives me; I turn, I catch sight of Fedus -- or merely his grey hat -- and I begin to blush, more from anger that I am blushing than from anything else. To avoid seeing that face I turn my eyes to the side:

It is exasperating! So I promise myself revenge at the next outing. We meet Princess Souvoroff, and her daughter on horseback with the obliging gentleman, M. Zveguinzoff. We stop our carriages. She called on us at the Hotel Scribe when we were no longer there. It is a pity; she leaves the day after tomorrow, but this charming person has promised to send a little note to say when she is leaving for certain. She also recommended a tailor for riding habits, next door to us -- Alfred.
