# Saturday, 12 January 1884

M. Franceschi came this morning — Jules Franceschi the sculptor, naturally.[^1] He treated my attempts at modelling with a well-founded disdain, but paid me great compliments on my painting. He says a medal is certain, and that he is speaking of my picture to his intimate friend Wolff.[^2]

[^1]: Jules-Elias Franceschi (1825–1893): French sculptor, member of the Institut de France, known for decorative and monumental work including pieces at the Paris Opéra. His mention of Wolff would have been a significant promise.

[^2]: Albert Wolff (1835–1891): the most powerful art critic in Paris, writing for *Le Figaro*. His support could make or break a Salon career; he had famously attacked the Impressionists but championed academic realists.

The Maréchale comes to collect Claire and me, and we go to Lefèvre's and then to Tony's. Franceschi and Boulanger are two men of the old school, so I ought to be delighted by their approval; both of them said: "I detest this kind of thing — but in this kind of thing, it is very good."

%% 2026-10-01T12:33:27 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%

As for Lefèvre, I must confess I had not been doing his talent sufficient justice. I saw at his studio things of an ideal form. Exactness, firmness, grace — carried to the utmost. I prefer this form to that of M. Ingres; in Lefèvre there are delicacies of line that literally enchant the eye. And nothing vague, nothing irresolute — an implacable and ravishing precision. No one paints the nude as he does... It is not, granted, a rich and painterly beauty — but there are refinements and exquisite delicacies combined with great power.
