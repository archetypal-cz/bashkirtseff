# Sunday, 4 November 1883
*(continued from Saturday)* ...and I would drop you with the greatest ecstasy for the Grand Duke I saw this morning at church. There is at present in Paris a whole string of Grand Dukes and Grand Duchesses.

[^1]: Nihilism: Russian revolutionary movement of the 1870s–80s, culminating in the assassination of Tsar Alexander II in 1881.
[^2]: *Secrétaire d'ambassade*: diplomatic secretary, a post of modest prestige — beneath Marie's ambitions.
[^3]: Edmond: a chiromancer (palm-reader) consulted by Marie in 1877, whose prophecies she took seriously and returned to throughout the diary.
[^4]: *La grande majorité*: legal majority in France was reached at twenty-five; Marie uses it with ironic grandeur, dreading the milestone as a sign of ageing.
[^5]: The exact parallel Marie has in mind is unclear — possibly Rousseau's ailments (he suffered from a urinary complaint and hypochondria) as a model of creative suffering.
[^6]: *Huit-ressorts*: a luxurious eight-spring carriage, associated with expensive kept women (*cocottes*).
[^7]: *Bergères*: literally "shepherdesses," used euphemistically for women of low or obscure birth.
