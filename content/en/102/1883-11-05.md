# Monday, 5 November 1883
The leaves have fallen and I do not know how to finish my painting. I have no luck. Luck! What a formidable thing! An inexplicable and frightening power.








[^1]: Marie credits herself with influencing Parisian fashion through her persistent demands at the leading couturiers.
[^2]: *Paupières en cabriolet*: hooded eyelids, like the curved hood of a cabriolet carriage — a period expression for heavy, overhanging upper lids.
[^3]: Tony: Tony Robert-Fleury (1837–1912), painter and teacher at the Académie Julian, one of Marie's instructors.
[^4]: Julian: Rodolphe Julian (1839–1907), founder of the Académie Julian, the progressive art school that admitted women on equal terms with men.

Since I began dressing myself in Paris I have waged war against stupid, unbecoming fashions. For five or six years I have been asking for draperies, gathered bodices, loosely arranged, mythological or Louis XV; skirts in the antique style, Judaic robes. I seemed very eccentric. But by dint of spending hours at Laferrière's, Worth's, or Doucet's, the fashion has caught on, and for two years now one sees nothing but draperies, jabots, fichus, negligently worn sashes and so forth. The most sought-after cuts at Doucet are of my invention.[^1] And none of it bears my name.
