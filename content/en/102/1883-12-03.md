# Monday, 3 December 1883
Well then — I am intelligent; I credit myself with wit, with penetration... with all the cerebral qualities, in short. And I am just. Well, in these circumstances, why not judge oneself?... It ought to be possible, since I see clearly — so let us see.

But can one judge oneself wrongly?... Thus I am in despair before what I do; each time a thing is finished I want to begin it all over again, I find it all bad because I always compare it with what I should wish it to be... But one's surroundings console: one sees people who do worse and who are admired... And so... And then it depends on the moment... All in all, at bottom, I do not think much of my artist self — I prefer to say so (in the hope of being mistaken). To begin with, if I believed I had genius [words blacked out: I should never complain] of anything... But that word genius is so formidable that I laugh as I write it about myself, and only to say that I lack it!... If I believed I had it, I should be mad.
