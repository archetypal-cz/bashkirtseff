There was to have been a great gathering of neighbours, but the rain has been falling since morning, and these good lords and ladies think twice before undertaking a journey of ten to thirty kilometres in such weather — so in the event there was no one at all save Dina, who had not yet left, the two Hamalias (to speak in the Little Russian fashion), and toward evening Mme and Mlle Tarnovsky.

%% 2026-03-06T10:05:00 TR: "lourds" = heavy, slow-witted in this context (not physically heavy). "s'enrouer" = to go hoarse. "sellette" = the hot seat (the position of being questioned/judged in the game). "papiers" game: players write phrases on slips of paper which are then read aloud and attributed — kept the French name as a proper game title. %%
It is quite extraordinary how much more ponderous Russians are than the French when it comes to parlour games requiring wit. One practically went hoarse explaining a single round of "papiers."[^3] And no one could ever understand why they were in the hot seat. My aunt Marie left everything entirely to me and I had the pleasure of entertaining the drawing room. We improvised a cotillion with a candle, an umbrella, a cushion, and a little bell.

[^3]: A popular 19th-century parlour game in which players each write a word or phrase on a slip of paper; the slips are collected, read aloud, and players must guess the author — requiring quick wit and social acuity.

%% 065.0022 %%
%% A une heure on se mit pour la cinquième fois à table. %%
At one in the morning we sat down to table for the fifth time.
