# Monday, 15 March 1875
%% Le printemps, le printemps, le printemps ! Le monde s'en est allé, la Promenade est moins peuplée, le soleil plus chaud, les matinées plus belles, les nuits plus odoriférantes et le crépuscule, ah voilà ce que j'aime. Le départ ne m'attriste pas cette année au contraire je voudrais plus vite être seule, seule entendons-nous; je veux Audiffret ou quelque chose dans ce genre autrement je m'ennuierais. Le printemps me rappelle nos promenades avec la princesse, nos conversations du duc, la villa Baquis, le chant des grenouilles, Blackprince sur son petit cheval, London House puis Audiffret tout en blanc que je ne voyais pas, étant tout occupée d'autre chose. %%
%% 2026-09-28T20:55:11 RSR: Restored from tome04.docx ¶4775 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
Spring, spring, spring! Society has gone, the Promenade is less crowded, the sun warmer, the mornings lovelier, the nights more fragrant, and the twilight — ah, that is what I love. The departures do not sadden me this year; on the contrary, I should like to be alone sooner — alone, mind you: I want Audiffret or something of that kind, otherwise I should be bored. Spring reminds me of our walks with the princess, our talks about the duke, the Villa Baquis, the croaking of the frogs, Blackprince on his little horse, London House, and then Audiffret all in white, whom I did not see, being wholly taken up with something else.

At two o'clock we set off: Pâris, Sacha and Walitsky in the first carriage; in the second, Olga, Nadinka and Julie; and lastly, in the third, a rather pretty pony-basket drawn by two lively, sweet little white horses, with bells like the duke's — myself, Marie and Stiopa. It had been arranged yesterday: we are going to visit the Russian frigate *Prince Pojarski* (grey dress and hat — good).

%% Après avoir tout visité même la machine, et les endroits les plus noirs et les salissants nous remontâmes dans la salle à manger où étaient déjà, Walitsky, Sacha, et Pâris en compagnie du baron Dungen Hern-Berg aussi officier de marine, d'un autre et du prêtre. Ce dernier nous proposa de voir son chronomètre, très extraordinaire à ce qu'il paraît, on apporte une belle boîte de bois, tout le monde s'avance pour voir, moi aussi pensant trouver quelque machine curieuse, mais au lieu de cela il en tire quatre flacons de liqueurs différentes, - c'est mon chronomètre, dit-il, et je le remonte tous les jours, - c'est-à-dire que tous les jours il vide et remplit de nouveau ces quatre flacons ou carafes. %% 2026-09-28T20:55:11 RSR: Restored from tome04.docx ¶4781 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
After seeing everything, even the engine and the blackest and dirtiest corners, we went back up to the dining room, where Walitsky, Sacha and Pâris already were, in the company of Baron Dungen Hern-Berg, also a naval officer, of another one, and of the priest. The priest is a curious character: he started out as a cornet of dragoons, then became a police commissioner, then a monk and finally a priest. A great drunkard, like that sorry excuse for a baron. The latter offered to show us his chronometer, most extraordinary, it seems; a handsome wooden box is brought in, everyone steps forward to look, I too, expecting some curious mechanism — but instead he takes out of it four flasks of different liqueurs. "This is my chronometer," he says, "and I wind it up every day" — that is to say, every day he empties these four flasks, or decanters, and fills them again. The tone in which it was said, and then his gesture and his face, so enamoured of those liqueurs, made [?] *tutti quanti*. I felt myself turning pale and, fearing *I should be seasick*, I said we must leave.
