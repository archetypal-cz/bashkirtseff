## People

### Maman
- Translation: Maman (keep French)
- Context: Marie's mother. Use "Maman" throughout — this is how Marie refers to her.
- Notes: Do NOT translate to "Mother" or "Mama" — the French form is part of Marie's voice

### Duke of Hamilton
- Translation: Duke of Hamilton
- Context: Object of Marie's romantic interest in Nice

### Prince Gagarine

- Context: Russian aristocrat in Nice society
- Notes: Owner ruling 2026-10-03 (names option B): private persons keep Marie's French spelling. Was "Gagarin" until 2026-10-03.

### Russian and other foreign names (owner ruling 2026-10-03, option B)
- Translation: private persons keep Marie's French spelling per occurrence (Tchoumakoff, Walitsky, Sapogenikoff, Miloradovitch, Romanoff for Marie's private relatives/acquaintances such as Nadine Romanoff, Karageorgevitch, Moussia, Gagarine, Larderel); famous public figures known to the English reader take the standard English form (Turgenev, Pushkin, Lermontov, Gorchakov the chancellor, Skobelev, Ogarev the poet, Tolstoy; the imperial house: Romanov).
- Notes: Same split as Kernberger (2013). Applies to editorial footnotes too (e.g. "a morganatic branch of the Romanov family" for the imperial house). Research: ~/rebuild-state/names-research.md.

## Key Terms

### toilette
- Translation: dress / outfit / ensemble
- Context: 1870s usage = the complete outfit, NOT "toilet"
- Notes: "Change one's toilette" → "change one's dress"

### en amazone
- Translation: in a riding habit / sidesaddle
- Context: Women's riding costume with long skirt for sidesaddle
- Notes: NOT "like an Amazon"

### canaille
- Translation: scoundrel / rascal / rogue
- Context: Pejorative for lower classes; Marie uses it dramatically
- Notes: Context-dependent — sometimes keep French in italics

### promenade
- Translation: promenade / walk
- Context: Both the place and the social activity of walking
- Notes: Keep "promenade" when referring to the specific location

## Foreign Language Handling

**Owner ruling 2026-10-03 (KRR): everything that is not English is translated; the original goes into the footnote.** Applies to Italian, Latin, German, Russian, Spanish, Niçois AND French phrases/tics left untranslated (*Bigre*, *Pardi*, *comme il faut*, *Ventre-Saint-Gris*, *Mort de ma vie*, *secousse*, *Deo juvante*…). Full text: `content/CLAUDE.md` "Foreign-language passages: everything translated" and `content/en/CLAUDE.md` (code-switch section).

When Marie writes in English in the French original:
- Keep the English AS-IS (it's already in the target language)
- Mark with ==highlight== if the surrounding text is translated
- Add footnote: "*In English in the original*"

When Marie writes in Italian/Latin/German/Russian/Spanish, or leaves a French phrase untranslated:
- Translate to English in the text, marked ==…== (marker right after the closing ==, before punctuation)
- Footnote with her exact original: "*In Italian in the original: …*" / "*In French in the original: *Bigre!**"; a tic repeated within one entry is footnoted at its first occurrence only; one rendering per tree (recorded in this file)
- Exceptions: metalinguistic runs (she discusses the foreign word, a pun, her own mistake — foreign in italics + explanatory footnote), titles of works, proper names and name-like codenames (Moussia, Bijou, Belle-de-jour, Désiré), naturalised words (*chic*, *ennui*, *soirée*, *atelier*, *cocottes*, *demi-monde*…)
