> In my last article I spoke of two young ladies in white, in a white *panier* drawn by white horses; up to that point, then, I was scrupulously accurate. It was in saying that these young ladies belonged to the amiable world that I was in [!'] error. What I did not know is that these young ladies belong to the best Russian society (which does not in the least prevent it from being amiable) and that they were followed by their parents in a landau. None of this detracts from the oddity of the turnout, which I continue to compare, if not to a cream puff, at least to a vanilla parfait.

Then, as the Princess launched into an intimate conversation, my aunt, with a frankness worthy of me, said that she did not care a fig for Nice society, that she had never known anyone but the consul, who was always ready to oblige, and through him the Howards; as for the rest, she had never set eyes on them. In short, she spoke very well and, outwardly at least, kept up a most becoming dignity. Besides, my aunt knows how to answer, and her stern, bold manner makes people respect her.
