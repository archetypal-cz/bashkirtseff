## Monday, 12 February 1877

%% 068.0977 %%
%% Larderel n'a pas été à ce bal. Ce qui suit est difficile à raconter. Sachez seulement que dans fort peu de temps je serai folle à lier. Mais il y a quelqu'un pour vous surveiller, direz-vous. Larderel did not go to that ball. What follows is difficult to recount. Know only that in very short order I shall be raving mad. But there is someone to watch over you, you will say. No — no one can do anything about it. If I were prevented from doing what the evil spirit commands I should become furious and disasters would follow.

%% 068.0978 %%

So today, hearing my neighbour go out, then his coachman closing the trunks, I felt seized by a kind of sadness... What, I said, he is going off like that — and whom shall I watch? And the *veglione* tonight? And a host of things. But I shall die of boredom!

He is leaving. One might leave too. But one must also see the gala Corso on the Chiaja, and our balcony is at the best spot, since we are staying at the Hôtel du Louvre. Hmm! I shall go and watch him leave anyway. It is no use, but perhaps it will give me pleasure. I am so unhappy that I try to lose myself in distraction, to kill time. So I set off with Maman, and the first person we find at the station is him, with Merisano and an officer. To get *admission to the waiting room* I go and buy two tickets to the first station — namely, Cancello.

Larderel and his two friends enter after us. I wanted to laugh; I set about perusing a newspaper. There was nothing else for it: we had to go back shamefully, without otherwise explaining our presence at the station. Larderel went through to the platform, casting me a glance. I went up to the man who takes the tickets at the gate and asked him whether there was a train back from Cancello straight away.
