Then I take Collignon back to my rooms and we talk about my affairs... at length... and after that I no longer want to see anything but Rome.
So, returning from the theatre where they were giving *La Créole* and where we had with us Bihovetz, Ricardo, and Lubimoff, my mind is made up.
**Proclamation.**
I must make use of my father for Rome while he is still here — Nice will come again. Once I am... how to put it?... established in Rome, I shall be able to go wherever I please and to meet M. Paul de Cassagnac again, since it gave me pleasure. Therefore, let us resolve what follows.

%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) [#Religion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/RELIGION.md) [#San_Remo](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/SAN_REMO.md) [#Marie](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/MARIE.md) [#Rome](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/ROME.md) %%
We write to Maman, to avoid explanations and... useless speeches, the following note:
"Go to San Remo, or elsewhere, [where necessary in the end, and arrange for M. Bashkirteff to come with us to Rome. It is definitively decided.] I want nothing else.
I give you five days.
Marie."
And now... My God, calm me.

Is it not a pity that so honourable a man as Bruschetti should displease me so greatly.
I always recall with pleasure his noble words when, in response to his proposal, I told him he did not know me. Words to which I paid no attention at the time.
— Mademoiselle, he said, it is for you to make inquiries about me, for it is the husband who gives the woman her name and her standing. I love you, and that is enough for me.
