# Friday, 10 May 1878
%% Les Suédoises m'ont raconté que Mme Boje s'est liée avec son professeur de chant et va aborder l'opéra. Son mari et ses enfants retournent en Suède et sa mère Mme Indebetten vient habiter avec elle. %%
The Swedish ladies told me that Mme Boje has taken up with her singing teacher and is going to try opera. Her husband and children are returning to Sweden, and her mother Mme Indebetten is coming to live with her.

Julian, who is a Bonapartist, came to talk with me about the pit in which Paul de Cassagnac finds himself. Defections everywhere. I have followed the appearances of the pamphlets, the letters, the articles. As for him, he has burned his bridges too often to be able to turn back.

%% Il restera tout seul si cela continue. %%
He will be left all alone if this goes on.

%% Il n'en aura que plus de charme pour les femmes, de grandeur pour les hommes. Je sais que l'on dit... Le docteur Michel le disait encore ce soir lorsque je lui ai annoncé le fameux mariage, il disait après bien des réticences qu'il ne se mariera jamais parce qu'il a au cou une chaîne d'or. C'est une grande saleté et tout à fait invraisemblable. Son journal tire à 4.000, 4.500, mais avec ses gages de député il a en tout de 20 à 24.000 francs par an. Il ne paraît pas plus. L'hôtel coûte 4.000 francs par an. En fait de domestique il y a le valet de chambre, sa fille et sa femme une grosse cuisinière qui ouvre la porte et, si ce sont des femmes, détourne la tête et s'enfuit avec une grande discrétion qui peint les mœurs du logis. Et il y a de sales serpents qui sifflent qu'il est payé par une femme et que l'impératrice lui donne de l'argent... Ce dernier est très naturel mais pour le parti et pas pour lui. %%
He will only have the more charm for women, the more greatness for men, because of it. I know what they say... Dr Michel said it again this evening when I told him of the famous marriage — he said after much hesitation that he will never marry because he has a golden chain about his neck. This is a vile slander and entirely improbable. His newspaper has a print run of four thousand, four thousand five hundred, but with his deputy's salary he has in all twenty to twenty-four thousand francs a year. He appears no more prosperous than that. The house costs four thousand francs a year. As for servants, there is the valet, his daughter and his wife — a stout cook — who opens the door and, if the visitors are women, turns her head away and flees with a great discretion that speaks volumes about the ways of the household. And there are foul serpents who hiss that he is kept by a woman, and that the Empress gives him money... The latter is very natural — but for the party, not for himself.

%% 080.0076 %%
%% Ou mettrait-il cet argent puisqu'il paraît à peine ses 24.000 francs ! %%
Where would he put such money, since he barely appears to have his twenty-four thousand francs!

%% 2026-10-01T17:52:03 RED: RESOLVED seam checked (080.DROPPED-0082 joined to 080.0079) — English reads as one continuous paragraph against _original; nothing duplicated or lost at the join. %%
That ignoble frigate captain tells us that his friend has been enquiring about us in general and about my intellectual faculties in particular. It is perhaps the first time he has spoken of it without our bringing it up first. I pretend to be angry, put on a veil, and speak of marrying Gambetta, which always makes him genuinely angry. If you knew how grotesque that man seems to me when he tries to play the Cassagnac — prostrating himself, remaining on his knees, then kissing my hand. He thinks himself full of grace; he wishes to be amusing, and is only ridiculous.

It was said with such conviction that, coming from this intelligent girl — morally crippled though she is — I am flattered by it.

%% Hélas ! ce n'est pas étonnant. Moi, comme dit ce faquin de Blanc, je suis un phénomène. Alas! It is not surprising. I, as that scoundrel Blanc puts it, am a phenomenon. I know everything, I can learn anything I wish, I am "astonishing, stupefying, strange, amusing" — but all that frightens people. I am "too strong, too clever" — men do not like to be inferior to a woman.

%% Les bêtes ne devraient pourtant pas avoir peur, quand je leur parle je fais comme avec les gens qui ne parlent pas français et au lieu de dire: voulez-vous boire ? je dis : %%
Fools, though, ought not to be afraid — when I speak to them I do as with people who don't speak French: instead of saying, will you drink? I say:

%% 080.0088 %%
%% Vous vouloir boire eau, vous avoir soif ? Je le fais bien involontairement je vous assure. %%
You wanting to drink water, you having thirst? I do it quite involuntarily, I assure you.

%% 080.0089 %%
%% Et plus j'ai de bons sentiments plus je les refoule au-dedans et plus je jette feu et flamme en dehors. %%
And the more good feelings I have, the more I suppress them inwardly, and the more fire and flame I throw outward.

%% 080.0090 %%
%% - Pour les autres vous avez du jugement, dit l'éternel capitaine, mais quand vous parlez de vous, vous ne savez ce que vous dites. %%
— About others you show judgement, said the eternal captain, but when you speak of yourself you don't know what you are saying.

%% 080.0091 %%
%% - Vous n'êtes pas bien malin Blanc, lui ai-je répondu, si vous pensez que je me profanerai jamais au point de parler sincèrement de ma personne. %%
— You are not very clever, Blanc, I replied, if you think I would ever debase myself so far as to speak sincerely about myself.

%% O Blanc ! tu m'as laissé entendre que si je n'étais pas si phénomène tu oserais me regarder comme une femme. %%
O Blanc! You let me understand that if I were not such a phenomenon, you would dare look at me as a woman.

O Blanc! O my captain.

%% 080.0094 %%
