# Friday, 16 June 1876

%% J'ai oublié de dire que hier a été célébré le mariage de Lacroix avec Saetone, qui avait pour témoins M. Guiglia et M. Émile d'Audiffret [sic]. %%
I forgot to say that yesterday the marriage of Lacroix to Saetone[^1] was celebrated, with M. Guiglia and M. Émile d'Audiffret [*sic*] as witnesses.
%% 2026-10-01T12:52:36 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-10-01 source footnote correction) — footnote [^1] updated: Saetone is Audiffret's companion; the wedding did not bring Audiffret to Nice. %%

[^1]: Saetone (Alphonse Andriot Saëtone, as the edition's index names him): a Niçois of Audiffret's set and his usual companion, mocked by Marie in earlier carnets; his bride is Mlle Lacroix. Audiffret is one of his wedding witnesses.

And people believe easily. Monsieur Tutcheff is marshal of nobility and a great lord himself. And my mother is nothing.

I said first in a fairly cold tone that I had no use for her reproaches, seeing that it was not I who had married her to my father. But as she went on weeping I went to embrace her — and beneath that kiss lay an ugly, selfish thought: for by showing myself resigned and kind I was doubling her grief and might perhaps bring her to do something.

Maman confuses the two and exasperates me. "Everything is written in advance"[^2] — that is the phrase with which she drives me out of my mind. If everything were written in advance there would have been only virtuous men — since God, being the principle of Good, would not write lives full of crimes.

[^2]: *Tout est écrit d'avance*: "everything is preordained" — a form of hard fatalism that Marie is attacking here in favour of a free-will interpretation of Providence.

I would not have gone to fetch it, had not Amalia — flattering me in every way and saying that no doubt there were many gentlemen who were asking for my hand — begged me to show her some portraits. [^5]: *Cardinalino*: "little cardinal" — Marie's nickname for Pietro Antonelli, nephew of Cardinal Giacomo Antonelli.

And yet he did not deceive me. Still, even in his tenderness there was enough nonchalance to make me dream. I would have wished for more respect, more timidity, more anxiety above all. I worried about where he was — he never did. Never did he... never, in short, was he as one ought to be when one truly loves. He seemed merely to be going through the motions. Instead of being indignant at the kisses on my hand, I smiled; instead of sending him away on the last evening, I... I almost went to meet him.
