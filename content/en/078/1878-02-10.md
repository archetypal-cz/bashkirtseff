# Sunday, 10 February 1878
Paul de Cassagnac was expected to dine; he had sent word that he would come late, but at around eight o'clock we receive a second letter in which he says that his sister has just given birth and he is going with his mother, who is leaving at once; his father was to have gone with her but has been prevented, so it is he who goes.

%% [#Berthe](../../_original/_glossary/people/recurring/BERTHE.md) [#Blanc](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/BLANC.md) %%
%% C'est dommage. Berthe était déjà là et comme nous avions commencé à rire avant la lettre, nous avons continué après, peut-être même trop pour masquer notre dépit devant Blanc. Et tout d'abord je me mis à crier tout haut en termes excessifs ce que je pensais tout bas en termes beaucoup moins violents. C'est Blanc qui reçoit tout; je l'ai prévenu d'ailleurs qu'étant trois femmes désappointées nous serions violentes et en même temps avions peur qu'il ne fût violent avec nous comme avec la femme hongroise. Cette chanson avec des variations infinies se chante jusqu'à minuit. %%
A pity. Berthe was already there and, since we had begun laughing before the letter arrived, we continued laughing after — perhaps even too much, to disguise our vexation before Blanc. And straightaway I began shouting out loud, in the most extravagant terms, what I had been thinking to myself in far less violent ones. It is Blanc who bears the brunt of it all; I warned him moreover that, being three disappointed women, we would be violent — and that at the same time we feared he might be violent with us, as he had been with the Hungarian woman. This tune, with endless variations, goes on until midnight.

*"Old Father and Mother Badinguet, two sous for the lot"* — and then[^1]
[^1]: "Badinguet": derogatory nickname for Napoleon III, derived from the workman whose clothes he wore while escaping from Ham prison in 1846. The song was a popular republican taunt.
