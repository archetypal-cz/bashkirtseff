# Thursday, 29 May 1884
Having had a fever all night I am in a furious state of irritation, aggravated by Mme Zarondny reading me Coppée's verses, and then by Jacques refusing to pose. In short, a state of agitation that could drive one mad. One could hit the people who say good morning to one; everything irritates, annoys, enrages. It is not the medal — it is the sleepless night and not being able to hear. I am too wretched; I want to believe in God. Is it not natural to seek some miraculous power when everything is misery and suffering and there is no salvation?

The doctors are powerless; one asks for a miracle — which does not come — but during the instant when one asks for it, one is consoled. It is very little.

God can only be just — and if He is just, how is it then that... One second of reflection and one no longer believes, alas!
