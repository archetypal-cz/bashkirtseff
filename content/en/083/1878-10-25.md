# Friday, 25 October 1878

%% 083.0026 %%
%% [#Academie_Julian](../../_original/_glossary/places/schools/JULIAN.md) %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
%% Nous avons fait notre petit concours de places. Tête grandeur nature en une heure. Tout le monde a fait à peu près bien, ce sera difficile à juger. %%
We had our little placement competition. A life-size head in one hour. Everyone did reasonably well; it will be difficult to judge.

%% 083.0027 %%
%% [#MME_Belletti](../../_original/_glossary/people/aristocracy/MME_BELLETTI.md) [#Belletti](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/BELLETTI.md) [#Randouin](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/RANDOUIN.md) [#Nice](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/NICE.md) [#Bordeaux](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/BORDEAUX.md) %%

%% [#Gavini](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/GAVINI.md) [#Exposition_Universelle](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/EXPOSITION_UNIVERSELLE.md) %%
%% J'ai passé chez les Gavini, Madame m'a fait cadeau d'une verrerie anglaise de l'Exposition, mais elle ne vient pas demain avec moi, Mme Belletti de Nice est ici pour quinze jours et elle est accaparée. Belletti et Randouin étaient à la tête de l'élégance préfectorale de Nice il y a trois ans. Pourvu qu'on ne m'enlève pas mes vieux. En attendant Mme Gavini me donne des petits cadeaux, m'embrasse et me dit qu'elle m'aime beaucoup. J'ai dit mes vieux, ils ne le sont pas, Gavini est très vert et sa femme est une de ces parisiennes aux cheveux encore noirs, à la peau rude mais assez fraîche et à la taille menaçant de grossir beaucoup; élégante d'ailleurs; un parler dur, provenance Bordeaux. Un air commun... cette femme-là va partout et on l'aime partout. %%
%% 2026-03-08T10:54:00 TR: "mes vieux" = "my old ones" — affectionate/possessive term for older social protectors; "très vert" = still very vigorous; "élégance préfectorale" = provincial high-society elegance; "un air commun" = a common/vulgar air. %%
I called on the Gavinis; Madame gave me a piece of English glassware from the Exposition, but she cannot come with me tomorrow — Mme Belletti from Nice is here for a fortnight and has monopolised her. Belletti and Randouin led the prefectoral set in Nice three years ago. As long as no one steals my old dears away from me. In the meantime Mme Gavini gives me little presents, kisses me, and tells me she is very fond of me. I said "my old dears" — but they are not old at all. Gavini is still very vigorous, and his wife is one of those Parisiennes with hair still black, skin roughened but fairly fresh, and a figure threatening to expand considerably; elegant, all the same; a harsh way of speaking — Bordeaux-bred. A common look... and yet this woman is received everywhere and liked everywhere.

I am bored... we have no money.

%% Mouzay vient le soir et raconte qu'elle a rêvé de Cassagnac. Il était commis-voyageur en corsets. Monsieur, lui dit-elle, est-il possible d'avoir fait un aussi sale mariage que le vôtre ! Savez-vous que ce honteux mariage vous coûte dans la société et que tout le monde pense comme je parle ! %% 2026-03-08T10:54:00 TR: "commis-voyageur en corsets" = traveling salesman in corsets; devastating comic degradation. "Moussia" is Marie's Russian family diminutive; kept as-is. "pour l'enrager" = to enrage her/spite her. %%
Mouzay comes in the evening and recounts a dream she had about Cassagnac. He was a travelling salesman in corsets. "Monsieur," she told him in the dream, "is it possible to have made such a vile marriage as yours! Do you know that this disgraceful marriage costs you dearly in society, and that everyone thinks as I say!" "What do you expect, Madame," he replied, "I did it because Mademoiselle Moussia mocked me — I married to spite her."

%% 083.0030 %%
%% [#Cassagnac](../../_original/_glossary/people/recurring/CASSAGNAC.md) %%
%% Oh ! pour m'avoir contrariée, si non enragée, il m'a contrariée non seulement en rêve, mais même en réalité... Saleté ! %%
Oh! Whether or not he enraged me — he has certainly vexed me, and not only in a dream but in reality as well... The wretch!

The little semi-official note in *Le Figaro* came afterwards like an excuse, an explanation. [Crossed out: if this silence were] If the young and pretty comtesse Stephano-Acard-Julia-Clément-Laurier were a respectable match, his friends would have said something; they certainly trumpeted the death of the abbé de Cassagnac, Paul's uncle, and printed extracts from other papers.

Daughter of a priest and [Crossed out: of a cocotte or] of an adventuress, a household that received only men, bold manners, dubious acquaintances, and the mother a... private kept woman. A bad reputation! And this is where he went to find a wife! Good Lord — after all, it seems Paul de Cassagnac is entertaining at the rostrum, but elsewhere he was simply a gentleman, a journalist, son of a writer, of a poor family, who — thanks to the adventurous life of his whole family — would find it hard to marry. Such is the word of Mme Gavini, who is the echo of all the world, of all Paris. So be it.
