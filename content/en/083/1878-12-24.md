# Tuesday, 24 December 1878

%% 083.0427 %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%

%% Madame Gavini m'invite à aller au Français mais Nadine part ce soir. %%
Madame Gavini invites me to the Théâtre-Français, but Nadine is leaving this evening.

%% 2026-10-01T07:27:19 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
So that on my return I find him here. He is in Paris with Kapitan — you remember that good fellow from Gavronzi? We dine *en famille*. Out of regard for Paul's entreaties I am civil — only just. Without Paul I would not even greet this man. He talks conciliation to Maman; it is always the same thing, and then he spoils everything. You know, it is impossible to live with him unless one is an actress drawing a fixed salary "to amuse him", like the woman who lives with him.

Je le crois incapable de faire, subitement quoique ça soit mais il assassine les gens à coups d'épingles, ce qui est d'autant plus odieux qu'il a de l'esprit et sait se conduire. %%
%% 2026-03-08T12:02:00 TR: "puéril" = childish/puerile; the quotation marks around "générosité" drip with the father's contempt. %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:27:19 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
It is incredible how wicked and childish this man is — he was furious that Paul had fine furs from here; he could not sneer at our "generosity". Paul never speaks ill of him, but yesterday, in a moment of exasperation, he admitted that it was the collar of his overcoat that had caused his anger; he knows him well, and his rages too. I believe him incapable of doing anything at all on the spur of the moment, but he murders people with pinpricks, which is all the more odious because he has wit and knows how to behave.

%% 083.0431 %%
%% Il est affreusement soupçonneux et despote au point de s'embêter lui-même pourvu de contrarier les autres. Il a l'air d'un homme exaspéré et s'il n'avait pas été toujours tel on attribuerait cette disposition à des causes romanesques, à l'amour qu'il prétend porter dans son cœur pour ma mère. Je crois tout de même que les parents ont été pour beaucoup dans la séparation. Ma mère ne l'aurait jamais inventée toute seule, j'en suis convaincue. %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:01:22 RSR: Restored from tome12.docx ¶2016, Mon Journal t.12 p.179 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
He is frightfully suspicious, and despotic to the point of making himself miserable, so long as he can thwart others. He has the air of an exasperated man, and if he had not always been like that, one would put this disposition down to romantic causes — to the love he claims to bear in his heart for my mother. All the same, I believe the relatives had a great deal to do with the separation. My mother would never have thought of it all by herself, I am convinced of that.

You know that I have an admirer. A sort of Hungarian who has two rather fine horses and who looks at me and follows me everywhere. This evening he came across us at Klein's, where I was buying some pocket-books. He asked all the shop assistants who I was and followed us to the cash desk, where I paid without asking for anything to be sent and without giving the address.

%% Après avoir reconduit Nadine à la gare nous sommes allés chez Siraudin où j'ai choisi une bonbonnière pour la Gavini. Alors M. Bashkirtseff a prié maman de s'en choisir une et cette femme incohérente est restée interdite et choisit une bêtise de onze francs. %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:01:22 RSR: Restored from tome12.docx ¶2024, Mon Journal t.12 p.179 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
After seeing Nadine off at the station we went to Siraudin's, where I chose a *bonbonnière* for Mme Gavini. Then M. Bashkirtseff begged Maman to choose one for herself, and that incoherent woman stood there dumbfounded and chose some eleven-franc trifle.

Then Dina and I go to take a few little presents to the Mouzays. I visit those women only to chat, to deliver speeches; I have taken such pains that de Daillens has offered to paint me a large screen. She paints those little fan pictures very prettily. And this large screen will look well in front of the fireplace. It cost me a 20-franc glove box: I gave the box and went into raptures over her screen, the one she has just finished.

%% 083.0444 %%
%% Je rentre et je trouve de nouveau mon père. Ils étaient allés au théâtre ensemble, ma tante, Paul, maman, son époux et Kapitan. Ce dernier sert de Bouffon, on l'appelle mon prince ce qui ébahit les ouvreuses, concierges etc. etc. %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:01:22 RSR: Restored from tome12.docx ¶2030, Mon Journal t.12 p.180 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
I come home and find my father here again. They had gone to the theatre together: my aunt, Paul, Maman, her spouse and Kapitan. The last-named plays the Buffoon; they call him "my prince", which astounds the usherettes, the concierges, etc. etc.

%% 083.0445 %%

I no longer remember what I had to tell you about Berthe or her husband, who dined here on Sunday. The three of us talked a great deal of nonsense... I forget, so it cannot be important.

%% 083.0446 %%
%% Maintenant j'ai un cahier où j'écris les lettres que j'aurais envie d'écrire et les idées... les choses que je ne pense pas enfin... je ne sais comment dire, des choses dans le genre de mes dissertations sur les Antonelli etc. Ce qui est embêtant c'est qu'en le lisant on ne le prendra pas pour des variations mais on croira que c'est arrivé. Now I have a notebook in which I write the letters I should like to write, and ideas... things I do not think, in short... I do not know how to put it — things in the manner of my dissertations on the Antonellis, etc. What is annoying is that anyone reading it will not take it for variations but will believe it really happened. No, I swear to you, it is not because it is over, but I swear to you that I did not *think* what I was writing. They were simply... how shall I put it? A kind of novel I made up by exaggerating... his love had rubbed off on me, and *then* I rambled, letting myself go in spite of myself, *working myself up* — yes, that is the right word.

We have taken a magnificent entresol at 34 avenue de Montaigne, which we shall furnish. I am bored all on my own — I mean morally, my friends. I should like to set up a sort of inn at our house for people of wit. Just to think that I might see people who understand me, who amuse me, who talk about what interests and charms me... Ah! I am wild about the idea.
