Robert-Fleury spoke to me again "from the point of view of a serious artistic future, a future as a talented painter"! I do not remember the expressions he used, but he spoke to me of my work as a whole, of my evening work, and Breslau, who heard, looked at me with consideration and with that benevolent air one puts on when one does not wish to seem jealous. It is not this week's head that is in question — my painting is still so weak that there is not much to say about it — but my studies as a whole.

C'est curieux à remarquer, chaque fois que vous vous encanaillez les gens ne pensent pas que vous leur faites l'honneur de les croire vos égaux, mais que vous êtes pis qu'eux. Ils n'admettent pas que des gens comme il faut veuillent les connaître. Bien que cela implique un manque absolu de vanité c'est parfaitement vrai. A présent je me suis posée à ravir. %% 2026-10-01T07:34:10 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
That Breslau — I should like to start talking to her again, for she is the only one with whom I can exchange ideas, on the Arts at least. She would be especially precious to me for going to the museums. At the beginning I was her friend, I defended her; then for a while I was on very bad terms in that atelier, [One line crossed out: but now I have established myself admirably] on bad terms with everyone, and it had unsettled me. And then I was not speaking to Amélie, which lowered me to the point of treating her as an equal. And Amélie, who had set me at odds with the Swiss girls, had made them believe they would gain by keeping away from me. It is curious to note: every time you mix with your inferiors, people do not think you are doing them the honour of believing them your equals, but that you are worse than they are. They do not admit that well-bred people should want to know them. Although that implies an absolute lack of vanity, it is perfectly true. By now I have established myself to perfection. I told them what I have just written, and much else besides; I was a Cassagnac once and for all, and I still am from time to time — rarely, though. And now, I tell you, I conduct myself as well as can be, and I am respected.

%% 2026-10-01T07:34:10 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
This evening I am going to see *Les Amants de Vérone* again with Nadine and Paul. We are inviting Filippini. I am not pretty, though well dressed. Capoul and Mlle Heilbronn sang and acted adorably. I am reconciled with Capoul; as for his face, he is good-looking enough for a mummer. The score opens like a flower on second hearing; one should go again — the flower would bloom and spread a quite delightful perfume. There are delicious phrases, but, mind you, it takes patience and delicacy. This music does not impose itself; one must go in search of its charm, which is subtle, almost faint, but which is there.
