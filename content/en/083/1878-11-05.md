# Tuesday, 5 November 1878

%% 083.0110 %%
%% [#M_Turquan](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/M_TURQUAN.md) %%
%% A huit heures j'étais en ville pour chercher les accessoires et à dix heures j'étais habillée en vieille dame élégante quand on apporta des billets de chez mon sauveur Turquan. Voilà la deuxième fois qu'il me rend service et si jamais je me trouve en situation de l'obliger je le ferai sans qu'il me le demande et avec un vrai plaisir. %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:32:31 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
At eight o'clock I was in town looking for the accessories, and at ten I was dressed as an elegant old lady when tickets arrived from my saviour Turquan. That is the second time he has done me a service, and if ever I find myself in a position to oblige him I shall do it without his asking, and with real pleasure.

%% 083.0111 %%
%% [#Mlle_Oelsnitz](../../_original/_glossary/people/aristocracy/MLLE_OELSNITZ.md) [#Gambetta](../../_original/_glossary/people/politicians/GAMBETTA.md) [#Princesse_Eristoff](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PRINCESSE_ERISTOFF.md) [#princesse_Troubetzkoy](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PRINCESSE_TROUBETZKOY.md) %%
%% Mais avant la réception de ces billets j'avais envoyé Mlle Oelsnitz chez Gambetta avec une lettre de la princesse Eristoff (je m'en sers toujours) qui est une amie de la princesse Troubetzkoy, et qui demande des billets. Le grand homme est fâché qu'on ne lui en ait pas parlé hier, ce matin il est trop tard, mais pour la séance suivante la princesse aura des billets. Oelsnitz a répondu que la princesse resterait encore deux jours exprès pour cela... Si jamais on m'attrape à un de ces jolis faux. %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:01:22 RSR: Restored from tome12.docx ¶1693, Mon Journal t.12 p.150 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
But before these tickets arrived I had sent Mlle Oelsnitz to Gambetta's with a letter from Princess Eristoff (I always make use of her), who is a friend of Princess Troubetzkoy and who asks for tickets. The great man is annoyed that no one spoke to him of it yesterday; this morning it is too late, but for the next session the princess shall have tickets. Oelsnitz replied that the princess would stay two more days expressly for that... If ever I am caught out in one of these pretty forgeries.

%% 083.0112 %%
%% [#Bescherelle](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/BESCHERELLE.md) %%
%% [#Appearance](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/APPEARANCE.md) %%
%% [#Julia_Acard](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/JULIA_ACARD.md) %%
%% Nous avions des billets d'officier, j'attrapai Bescherelle, le féroce Bescherelle et lui enlevai l'autorisation de changer les billets. On nous plaça dans une tribune, ce qui n'est pas bien chic, enfin ! D'autant plus que Mlle Stephano-Julia etc. était dans une belle loge, un étage au-dessous, à côté des galeries bonnes. Mlle Julia était avec sa respectable mère et l'époux de sa respectable mère. De loin elle m'a paru pas mal mise, quoique très ordinaire. Elle a un petit air heureux qui lui donne quelque chose de jeune et d'adorable à sa figure. %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:32:31 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
We had officers' tickets; I caught hold of Bescherelle — the fierce Bescherelle — and wrested from him permission to exchange the tickets. We were put in a public gallery, which is not very smart — oh well! All the more so as Mlle Stephano-Julia etc. was in a fine box one floor below, next to the good galleries. Mlle Julia was with her respectable mother and her respectable mother's husband. From a distance she seemed to me not badly dressed, though very ordinary. She has a happy little air that gives her face something young and adorable.

%% 083.0113 %%

It must also be said that he stayed *four hours* at the rostrum defending himself, or rather attacking all the French Republicans, while keeping a good-natured, submissive air. This constant submissiveness allowed him not to look crushed when he was, on two or three occasions. Besides, read it yourself.

Those vile women had a strange effect on me, sitting there as Cassagnac's wife and mother-in-law. It seemed stupid to me, and he looked as though he were working for their money.

Oh, good Lord, do not cry out! Do you really think that if I were steadily convinced that it would give me pleasure — do you think that all your miserable considerations would stop me?

Which would not turn me from my prussic acid — on the contrary; it seems to me that I would approach smiling, examine the subject and carry it out as if it were the simplest thing in the world.

%% 2026-03-08T11:08:00 TR: "l'assassinat" = "the assassination" — Marie's melodramatic label for Cassagnac's marriage, as though he murdered her hopes. Preserved as-is. %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:32:31 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
After the assassination, there came a feeling of shame when I thought of my letters — and then rage and grief at seeing this stupidity accomplished, at seeing that girl in the place which, by reason, by justice, by my ambitions, I ought to have occupied. It is like a fine role spoilt by the inadequacy of the actress. My enemies themselves will understand that it is not by extravagances, drolleries, childish pranks that I claim to deserve anything whatever. The extravagances, if there were any, are over, since I have been in this Paris, which is in the process of forming me.
