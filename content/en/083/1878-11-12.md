# Tuesday, 12 November 1878

%% Hier... non mais Aujourd'hui nous apprenons une nouvelle foudroyante. Mme Yorke est morte. En pleine vie, âgée de trente-deux ans à peine, si gaie, si insouciante ! Yesterday... no, but Today we learn a thunderbolt of news. Mme Yorke is dead. In the full bloom of life, barely thirty-two years old, so gay, so carefree! There is one of those empty-headed creatures whose disappearance makes no more impression than the fall of a leaf. She had slandered us to her family, making them believe that we had had a hand in Berthe's detestable marriage.

%% 083.0173 %%
%% Cette délicieuse Berthe est enceinte et couchée malade chez sa mère. Je ne la crois pas heureuse. %%

That delightful Berthe is with child and lies ill at her mother's. I do not believe she is happy.

%% 083.0174 %%
%% [#Health](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/HEALTH.md) %%

%% Cette pauvre Yorke est morte sans maladie, elle n'a jamais souffert que des migraines, ce sont ces migraines je crois qui ont déterminé la congestion au cerveau qui l'a emportée. %%
Poor Yorke died without illness; she never suffered from anything but migraines — it is those migraines, I believe, that caused the brain congestion that carried her off.

This evening, beside my academy figure, I drew in two strokes of the pencil the most extraordinary head in France. Julian came to correct, but instead began slapping his knees with delight; I thought he did not recognise him and was taking him for someone else, which vexed me — but not at all: the likeness is striking, it seems.

[^1]: Jacques Collin (alias Vautrin, alias Abbé Carlos Herrera) — the great criminal mastermind of Balzac's *Comédie humaine*.

At this remark, whose aptness I give myself credit for, old Julian looked at me with respect. The academy figure was set aside, and I had to give him the head for his album. The painter who made a picture of the Jewish girl's garret, with the Jewish girl bearing Heilbronn's features and Abbé Herrera modelled on Cassagnac's father, will have more than talent: he will have genius. It is impossible to dream of a more perfect incarnation of Jacques Collin...

It would be a fine trick to play on them; it would be a dreadful thing, and one I shall do if I have talent... Besides, that noble old man is very intelligent, but all the same I do not think he would be flattered.
