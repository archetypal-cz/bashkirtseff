# Sunday, 24 November 1878

%% 083.0257 %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%
%% [#Paul](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PAUL.md) [#Nadine](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/NADINE.md) %%
%% Nadine a dormi chez moi et Paul aussi, il avait peur de Juliette morte ! %%
Nadine slept in my room, and Paul too — he was afraid of the dead Juliet!

%% 083.0258 %%
%% Ma chambre était pleine de cigarettes., de pantalons... Les domestiques ont cru que tous les trois avaient découché. %%
%% 2026-03-08T11:32:00 TR: "découché" = spent the night elsewhere (euphemism for scandalous behavior). %%
My room was full of cigarettes and trousers... The servants assumed that all three of us had spent the night out.

%% 2026-03-08T11:34:00 TR: "profil tout à fait perdu" = painter's term for a three-quarter back view; "le Défunt" = the Deceased (Cassagnac). %%

Blanc was at the theatre, and knowing myself to be looking plain, I removed my gloves and posed — for the hand and a completely lost profile. That old Blanc — he reminds me of the Deceased, and before him I always find myself posing.

%% Mme Gavini m'a envoyé un coussin brodé par elle et je suis allée l'en remercier avec Paul auquel elle prête la médaille de député de son mari pour qu'il puisse visiter plus facilement les monuments. Après cette visite nous allons nous promener au Bois et le ressort casse au milieu des Champs-Elysées. Là nous descendons et rentrons à pied chez nous, ou j'offre du thé et montre mes bijoux et envoie des cigarettes pour Gavini. C'est amusant les Champs-Elysées le dimanche à pied. Nous avons rencontré Don Carlos et plusieurs messieurs qui connaissent Mme Gavini et qui m'ont passablement regardée. %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:01:22 RSR: Restored from tome12.docx ¶1840, Mon Journal t.12 pp.164–165 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). Printer's typo corrected: «plusieus» → «plusieurs» (Mon Journal t.12 p.164). %%
Mme Gavini sent me a cushion she had embroidered herself, and I went to thank her for it with Paul, to whom she is lending her husband's deputy's medal so that he can visit the monuments more easily. After this visit we go for a drive in the Bois, and the spring breaks in the middle of the Champs-Élysées. There we get out and walk home, where I offer tea, show my jewels and send some cigarettes for Gavini. The Champs-Élysées are amusing on a Sunday, on foot. We met Don Carlos and several gentlemen who know Mme Gavini and who looked at me a good deal.

%% 2026-09-29T01:01:22 RSR: Restored from tome12.docx ¶1841, Mon Journal t.12 p.165 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
Several of them know me by sight, one in particular who, whenever he is with Kolemine, always nudges him with his elbow when I pass. Kolemine has a certain admiration for me.

%% 2026-10-01T07:34:10 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
%% 2026-10-01T17:27:43 RSR: Stray italics removed from «des» in «musée des Antiques»; the print has no italics there (Mon Journal t.12 p.165, scan Tome12.pdf). The italic «de» in «être de chic» is printed so and kept. %%
We visited the Museum of Antiquities with Nadine. Ah! How banal and incredible it is — and yet how true: the moderns will never equal the ancients, and Michelangelo was right to cry out, when he wished to add legs to the antique torso: a man cannot continue the work of the gods! I tell you this impression because, coming from me, it cannot be *chic*, mere convention.
