# Thursday, 31 May 1877
%% C'est pour me donner cette lettre qu'on m'a dérangée au milieu d'une peinture fervente. Le nouveau Melissano est mieux réussi que le premier, j'ai commencé le portrait de grand-maman d'après un pastel plus petit que nature, j'agrandirai et je continue le mien qui s'améliore peu à peu. La lettre de Marcuard contenait une vilaine petite photographie d'Alexandre où il était tout souriant et banal. %%
%% 2026-09-28T20:52:24 RSR: Restored from tome10.docx ¶2972, Mon Journal t.10 p.193 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
It was to give me this letter that I was disturbed in the middle of some fervent painting. The new Melissano has come off better than the first; I have begun Grand-maman's portrait from a pastel smaller than life, which I shall enlarge, and I am going on with my own, which is improving little by little. Marcuard's letter contained a nasty little photograph of Alexandre, in which he was all smiles and commonplace.

> You have just received a useless letter from me, since here is the portrait; but listen, I am sending it back to you: it faces the wrong way, and if I placed it beside the prince it would turn its back on him. *So then* be very kind and send quickly, quickly *a picture* in costume, which I shall of course return to you, heaping blessings upon you. Maman certainly accepts the offer of a picture for herself. Does the visit stir me, you ask? I have been so stirred for nearly seventeen years and six months now (I am hiding a year) that I need not go to the trouble of getting beside myself. You speak of speech and of song, seeming unaware that plumage is the chief ornament of birds of this species.

I cherish a secret hope that my beloved mother knows something of my letters to the Swiss. I say *beloved* mother without the least irony, and with tenderness. Her kindly ways towards me have left me with deep gratitude and much affection for this woman, a stranger to me, who must be so good. For her sake I should like to go and live in Florence; she encourages me and makes me see sympathy everywhere, and hands held out to me.

%% Je ne crois pas que ce soit parce qu'elle est la mère d'Alexandre, parce que je ne crois pas à de l'amour pour lui. Je ne me croirai possédée par ce démon que lorsque je ne pourrai ni manger, ni marcher, ni m'habiller, quand je serai sur mon lit en délire, mourante, à l'agonie. %% 2026-09-28T20:52:24 RSR: Restored from tome10.docx ¶2988, Mon Journal t.10 p.195 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
I do not think it is because she is Alexandre's mother, for I do not believe I feel any love for him. What sort of love is it that leaves me so tranquil, and of which, having no reasonable hope of any kind, one does not die? I shall believe myself possessed by that demon only when I can neither eat, nor walk, nor dress, when I lie on my bed delirious, dying, in my last agony. Until one comes to that, one has felt only *preferences*, which, unreturned, are as violent as love returned.
