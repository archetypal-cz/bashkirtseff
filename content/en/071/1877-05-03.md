# Thursday, 3 May 1877
%% [Rayé : Nous avons déjeuné chez] Marcuard [Mots noircis: demeure dans] le palais de la baronne de diable, [Dans la marge : baronne d'Hoogheworst], sait comment, qui lui a cédé cet appartement par complaisance. Il n'a qu'un salon qui est un petit musée. Il est impossible au premier abord de se rendre compte de toutes les antiquités, curiosités et objets d'art, entassés pêle-mêle. Les murs sont recouverts de toutes les tapisseries imaginables, Gobelins, orientales et même des morceaux de dentelles. Des vases, des vieilles armes, des tableaux, dont plusieurs n'ayant d'autre mérite que celui de l'âge. Je me trouve toujours si *confortablement* avec cet homme que j'ai passé trois heures charmantes. %%
%% 2026-09-28T20:52:24 RSR: Restored from tome10.docx ¶2491, Mon Journal t.10 p.154 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
[Crossed out: We lunched at] Marcuard [words blacked out: lives in] the palazzo of the Baroness de Devil, [In the margin: Baroness d'Hoogheworst], knows how, who let him have this apartment as a favour. He has only one drawing room, which is a little museum. At first sight it is impossible to take in all the antiques, curios and objets d'art heaped up pell-mell. The walls are hung with every tapestry imaginable, Gobelins, Oriental, and even pieces of lace. Vases, old weapons, pictures, several of which have no merit but their age. I always feel so *comfortable* with this man that I spent three charming hours.

J'avais grande envie de voler tous ses portraits, ce serait un si grand plaisir de les avoir à présent que cela me fait encore plaisir. J'ai toujours eu à combattre les petits sentiments désagréables que faisaient naître en moi le portrait d'Antonelli, pour pouvoir me monter la tête et encore ! %% 2026-09-28T20:52:24 RSR: Restored from tome10.docx ¶2492, Mon Journal t.10 p.154 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
I rummaged everywhere, particularly among the visiting cards and the invitations from all these ladies of Florence, and above all the portraits! The portraits of Larderel. Two in costume and one plain. I am sorry I cannot convey, in describing him, the stamp of supreme distinction this man bears. His face is such a mixture of openness and subtlety that one would readily take it for something more than subtlety. By my crude definition, Mirafiore and he both look like cheats. And yet what a handsome face, yes, very handsome, and above all noble and elegant. I had a great longing to steal all his portraits; it would be such a pleasure to have them now, while it still gives me pleasure. I always had to fight down the small unpleasant feelings that Antonelli's portrait stirred in me before I could work myself up, and even then! It almost never went well; there was nothing but the idea that he loved me, and then... the rest.
