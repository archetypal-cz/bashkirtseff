# Sunday, 23 January 1876

%% 052.0391 %%
%% [#Fashion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/FASHION.md) %%
%% [#Church](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/CHURCH.md) %%
%% [#Nice](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/NICE.md) [#Rome](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/ROME.md) %%
%% Je me fais un large vêtement blanc pour la maison pour le printemps à Nice et je suis triste, je m'ennuie à Rome, il me semble qu'il y est déjà temps de retourner. Une absence de Nice ne peut pas durer plus de trois semaines. Nice, misérable ville, pourquoi n'y puis-je vivre comme j'aime: J'étais à l'église, je ne connais personne ! %%
I am making myself a large white garment for the house — for the spring in Nice — and I am sad; I am bored in Rome; it seems to me it is already time to return. An absence from Nice cannot last more than three weeks. Nice — miserable city — why can I not live there as I love to! I was at church — I know no one!

%% 052.0392 %%
%% [#Society](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/SOCIETY.md) %%
%% [#Nice](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/NICE.md) %%
%% A Nice je connais tout le monde. Mais vivre à Nice, autrement qu'en reine, ne vaut pas la peine. Nice est trop petite pour qu'on y vive simplement. Qu'est-ce que je dis donc ! A Nice je connais tout le monde ! Mais je ne connais personne à Nice, et personne ne veut de nous ! %%
In Nice I know everyone. But to live in Nice otherwise than as a queen is not worth the trouble. Nice is too small to live in simply. What am I saying! In Nice I know everyone! But I know no one in Nice, and no one wants us!

%% 052.0393 %%
%% [#Meta_diary](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/META_DIARY.md) %%
%% Je voudrais bien ne plus parler de cela, c'est connu, très connu, et c'est sous-entendu à chaque page. Ainsi, n'en parlons plus. %%
I should like to stop speaking of it — it is known, very well known, and is implied on every page. So — let us say no more of it.

%% 052.0395 %%
%% Je suis triste, je suis à l'étranger, j'ai envie de retourner chez moi, pour un jour seulement, car si j'y reste plus longtemps encore partir. Je n'ai pas une âme qui m'occupe et je ne puis rester en place. %%
I am sad — I am abroad — I want to go home, for one day only, for if I stay longer I would have to leave again. I have not a single soul to occupy me, and I cannot stay in one place.

Why are we so badly off in Nice?

%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%

%% Ah ! comme je m'ennuie ! Si au moins nous étions tous ensemble. Quelle folle idée de se séparer ainsi ! Il faut toujours être ensemble, les ennuis semblent moindres, on se sent mieux. Jamais, jamais on ne se partagera plus en deux. Nous serions cent fois mieux ensemble, grand-papa, ma tante, Walitsky, tout le monde. %%
Ah! How bored I am! If only we were all together. What a mad idea to separate like this! One must always be together — the tedium seems less, one feels better. Never, never shall we divide again into two. We should be a hundred times better off together: grandfather, my aunt, Walitsky, everyone.

%% Sait-on ce que c'est que Walitsky ? Je me suis contentée jusqu'à présent de le désigner par un W. Walitsky est le fils de M Walitsky de Kharkov, marié à la baronne Rosen. Avant ils étaient riches; mais le père, par trop de confiance et de bonté, s'est ruiné, il est mort lui et sa femme. Notre Walitsky a deux sœurs et deux frères, quant à lui il est notre ami comme son père était notre ami. Walitsky c'est la créature la plus dévouée, la plus désintéressée, la plus honnête, la meilleure du monde. Il est plus qu'un ami, plus qu'un parent, il est pour chacun de nous un second soi-même[^2]. Il est grognon, ennuyeux quelquefois, mais ce n'est rien. A présent qu'on le connaît je continuerai à le désigner par un W. %%
Does one know what Walitsky is? I have been content until now to designate him by a W. Walitsky is the son of Monsieur Walitsky of Kharkov, who married the Baroness Rosen. Formerly they were wealthy — but the father, through too much trust and goodness, ruined himself; he and his wife died. Our Walitsky has two sisters and two brothers; as for him, he is our friend as his father was our friend. Walitsky is the most devoted, the most disinterested, the most honest, the best creature in the world. He is more than a friend, more than a relation — he is for each of us a second self.[^2] He is grumpy, tedious sometimes — but that is nothing. Now that you know him, I shall continue to designate him by a W.
%% 2026-03-05T13:55:00 TR: Marie introduces Walitsky formally to the reader after using "W" throughout the journal per LAN QUIRK note. "un second soi-meme" = a second self per LAN. %%

[^2]: "Un second soi-même" — a second self: the classical philosophical formulation of true friendship (from Aristotle via Cicero); applied by Marie to Walitsky with unusual warmth and gravity.

%% 052.0400 %%
%% [#Fashion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/FASHION.md) %%
%% [#Doria](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/DORIA.md) [#Villa_borghese](../../_original/_glossary/places/villas/VILLA_BORGHESE.md) [#Pincio](../../_original/_glossary/places/landmarks/PINCIO.md) [#Corso](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/CORSO.md) %%
%% Nous avons vu deux fois le grand Doria[^3], une fois à l'entrée et une fois dans la villa. Il m'a reconnue. La princesse se promenait là avec sa livrée rouge. Je suis très regardée à cause de ma robe blanche, et je me sens gênée. Au Pincio et au Corso un monde fou. %%
We have seen the tall Doria[^3] twice — once at the entrance and once inside the villa. He recognised me. The Princess was walking there with her red livery. I am looked at a great deal on account of my white dress and feel embarrassed. At the Pincio and the Corso, an enormous crowd.

[^3]: "The tall Doria" (*le grand Doria*): a member of the Doria family distinguished by his height; apparently a recurring presence on the Roman promenade.

%% 052.0401 %%
%% [#Theater_theme](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/THEATER_THEME.md) %%
%% Le soir nous allons au teatro Apollo, on donne "La Vestale" et un ballet. %%
In the evening we go to the Teatro Apollo[^4] — they are performing *La Vestale*[^5] and a ballet.
%% 2026-10-03T17:33:12 TR: Reading-conventions wave (KRR 2026-10-03): Opera title given in its established English form, Marie's title in the footnote (no highlight). %%
%% 2026-10-03T17:45:21 CON: Reading-conventions review: La Vestale reverted to its original title in italics + gloss (opera performed under its Italian/French title in English, cf. TM La Sonnambula, Il Trovatore); not an established English title. %%
%% 2026-03-05T13:55:01 TR: "La Vestale" = Spontini's opera per LAN. "teatro Apollo" = Apollo Theater per LAN. %%

[^4]: The Teatro Apollo: the principal opera house in Rome, built in 1795 on the site of the Castel Sant'Angelo's ramparts; it was demolished in 1889 to make way for the embankment of the Tiber.
[^5]: *La Vestale* (*The Vestal Virgin*): opera by Gaspare Spontini (1807), performed in English-speaking houses under its original title; a tragedy set in ancient Rome featuring a Vestal Virgin who breaks her vow of chastity for love; one of the great works of early 19th-century opera.

I am in white with a Greek coiffure.[^6] There is a large audience and above all many men — between our box and the stage there was not a single woman.
