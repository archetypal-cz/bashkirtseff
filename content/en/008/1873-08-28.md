%% 2026-02-16T15:25:00 TR: "me décrotter" — clean myself up (colloquial: lit. remove mud). "je retourne avec vers 'Hamilton'" — unclear phrasing in original; likely "I return to the word 'Hamilton'" in the Dumas book. "parent" — here: relation/relative, not parent. %%
%% A Marseille, on m'éveille. Je vais avant tout me décrotter, je suis moins sale que je pensais. De si bonne heure je ne puis manger, une tasse de thé seulement. Je me remets à lire, je retourne avec vers "Hamilton". Je n'ai jamais rencontré un plus beau nom. Je profite de l'occasion pour dire à ma tante encore une fois que Napoléon n'est pas parent, puisque sa mère est princesse de Bade. Alors on parla de la parenté des Russes et des Anglais: %%
At Marseille they wake me. I go first of all to clean myself up — I am less dirty than I thought. So early I cannot eat; only a cup of tea. I take up my reading again; I return to the word "Hamilton." I have never encountered a finer name. I take the opportunity to tell my aunt once more that Napoleon is no relation of his, since his mother is a Princess of Baden. Then we spoke of the kinship between the Russians and the English:

Depuis Toulon je suis en proie à une grande douleur, je m'approche de Nice. Tous les endroits sont égaux pour moi excepté Paris que j'adore et Nice que j'abhorre. Il n'y a même pas moyen d'étudier. At Toulon we dine, and very well; truly, if it had been at twelve or one o'clock instead of ten, I should have eaten perfectly. Since Toulon I am in the grip of a great sorrow — I am approaching Nice. All places are the same to me except Paris, which I adore, and Nice, which I abhor. There is no way even to study. And such heat!

Georges has been detained at the Austrian border. His Anna ran off, stealing his passport. And without a passport they are holding him. I do not want to eat anything. We go up to the drawing room; Prater met us in the street. They have taken on a little Black boy, who will ride out with us in the carriage. As long as I do not look out of the window I am content, but I cannot bear this pale, scorched greenery, this burning sky, this red earth, this heavy air! Maman says, too, that we shall live in Paris. Oh, my God, how I should like it! Protect me and help me!

With winter will come society, and with society my gaiety. It will not be Nice but a little Paris for four months. And the races! And the pigeon shooting! And the Duke of Hamilton, who will come! I ought to love Nice, since it is the only town where I have the pleasure of seeing him. And sometimes as many as ten times! It is a happiness I have forgotten, that I no longer understand. Ah, everything has its advantageous side: in Nice I have the happiness of seeing the Duke, and it is so great, so beautiful, so rare, that at the mere thought of it every unpleasantness fades away, and the six or seven months I must get through seem to me a sea that I would cross without looking back and without taking my eyes off the lighthouse that guides me. I do not hope to land, no; I hope only to see that shore, and the mere sight of it will give me the character, the strength and the courage to live until next year.
