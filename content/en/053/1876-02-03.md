# Thursday, 3 February 1876

Once more, Beauty-of-the-Day.

%% 053.0329 %%
During my sleep I distinctly heard a voice repeat three times: "Bergerault! Bergerault! Bergerault!" The devil take it.
%% 2026-03-05T11:00:00 TR: "Bergerault" — unidentified name; Marie hears it in a dream, and its significance is unclear. Kept as-is. %%

%% 053.0330 %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/people/writers/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
At eleven o'clock, in dark attire, I go to the Capitoline with Lola, Dina, and Domenica. We go through the statues again — Lola's remarks make us laugh. I believe we scandalised a gentleman by our scrupulous examination of the Dying Gladiator.[^1] He entered and found two women behind and two women in front, examining the statue with the most stupefied expressions imaginable.

[^1]: The *Dying Gaul* (also called the *Dying Gladiator*): celebrated ancient Roman marble sculpture in the Capitoline Museums, depicting a defeated Gaul warrior.

%% 053.0331 %%
Fortuné is with us — he is amused.

%% 053.0332 %%
We come out a little sheepish, but really one is wrong to think ill of it — for my part, I look at the most nude statues without any ulterior thought, and for a good reason: a man completely naked is very ugly and even ridiculous. I could never tire of admiring Agrippina — she has a fine, plump chin, an admirable nose in form and expression, and a small, slightly pursed mouth that enchant me. Besides, Nero resembles her entirely in profile. That cruel expression pleases me.
%% 2026-03-05T11:00:01 TR: Agrippina — the marble bust in the Capitoline, usually identified as Agrippina the Elder or the Younger. Nero was her son (the Younger). %%

%% 053.0333 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
%% [#Italian](../../_original/_glossary/culture/languages/ITALIAN.md) %%
From there we pass to the Italian Pantheon — only a few busts are placed. The face of M. Poussin[^2] struck me by its majestic beauty — he has the air of Peter the Great. Then to the Etruscan Museum, where we enter and make great noise over little broken statuettes, a bronze bull's rear, a bronze horse's front, and a heap of little earthenware pots — we imagine it is the gods' kitchen utensils, and the ==custodi==[^3] follow us, astonished by our gaiety. Then the picture gallery, which is oppressive.
%% 2026-03-05T11:00:02 TR: "batterie de cuisine" = kitchen battery/utensils. "custodi" = Italian for museum guards. %%

[^2]: Nicolas Poussin (1594–1665): the great French classical painter who spent most of his career in Rome. The Capitoline has a bust of him.

[^3]: *Custodi*: Italian for museum guards/custodians.

%% 053.0334 %%
%% [#Religion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/RELIGION.md) %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
And there are idiots who admire these dreadful daubs — Virgins without eyebrows, real masks instead of human faces. The martyrdom of Saint Sebastian abounds — it irritates me like the head of Beatrice Cenci.[^4] In a corner I find a pretty little painting, the Rape of Europa[^5] — a canvas of 30 centimetres. Europa is on the back of a bull swimming across the sea. I must copy it.

[^4]: Beatrice Cenci (1577–1599): executed for patricide; subject of the famous portrait attributed to Guido Reni in the Barberini collection.

[^5]: *The Rape of Europa*: classical subject depicting Zeus in the form of a bull abducting Europa across the sea.

%% 053.0335 %%
There are Titians with those beautiful red-gold tresses and those enormous wrists — principally the face of his beloved, to whom all his paintings resemble.

%% 053.0336 %%
%% [#Religion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/RELIGION.md) %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
Not one canvas drew my attention — except a Magdalen who had the look of a sick Chinese woman. A dreadful muzzle. The eternal Saint Sebastian riddled with arrows, who seems not to feel them, as if one were throwing roses at him.

%% 053.0337 %%
%% [#Religion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/RELIGION.md) %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
Saint Petronilla by Guercino[^6] detained me some minutes, because Bensa had given me to copy two hands and a child's head that he himself had copied from this painting when he was twenty-two. His copies are atrocious, as I now see.

[^6]: Giovanni Francesco Barbieri, called *Il Guercino* (1591–1666): Italian Baroque painter.

%% 053.0338 %%
%% [#Philosophy](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/PHILOSOPHY.md) %%
The busts of Socrates (there are three in the museum) amuse me. What philosophy in that nose!

%% 053.0339 %%
[Written across the page: All of this I visited as a barbarian.]

%% 053.0340 %%
We return to the hotel for a few moments — I take a little glass of wine and eat a bread roll — and we set off again.

%% 053.0341 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
At the Vatican we had only an hour and a half — we merely passed through, talking loudly and making our remarks, through the Raphael Rooms, the Raphael Loggia (which disappointed me), and the picture gallery. *À la bonne heure!*[^7] These are living faces, fine paintings that are a pleasure to see.
%% 2026-03-05T11:00:03 TR: "À la bonne heure!" = "Now we're talking!" / "That's more like it!" %%

[^7]: *À la bonne heure!*: "Now we're talking!" / "That's more like it!"

%% 053.0342 %%
%% [#Italian](../../_original/_glossary/culture/languages/ITALIAN.md) %%
In the Sistine Chapel we make such a racket that the custode comes to shout ==silenzio!==

%% 053.0343 %%
After looking at Michelangelo's celebrated Last Judgment and finding that all his men were the same colour — which is not natural — I went to the far end, behind the benches.

%% 053.0344 %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/people/writers/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%
There is a door which we tried in vain to open; so I looked through the keyhole. Lola followed my example but recoiled with a cry — she intrigued me; I looked again, but had scarcely applied my eye to the hole when I too recoiled with an exclamation followed by a paroxysm of unseemly gaiety in a chapel. Dina and Lola laughed with me. For where I had first seen a room, I now saw only an immense eye and half a nose. It was so frightening that it was utterly unexpected — no, it is the contrary I mean.
%% 2026-03-05T11:00:04 TR: The famous keyhole incident in the Sistine Chapel — someone was looking back through from the other side. %%

%% 053.0345 %%
We must return — we saw very little.

%% 053.0346 %%
%% [#Religion](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/RELIGION.md) %%
Ah! — Poussin's martyrdom of Saint Erasmus. What a horror. I also saw the Raphaels — but you do not expect, do you, a *guide Diamant*.[^8]

[^8]: A *guide Diamant*: the famous French Hachette blue guidebook series, considered the standard tourist authority of the period.

%% 053.0347 %%
Yet in all conscience I ought to speak of the Transfiguration[^9] since everyone speaks of it.

[^9]: *The Transfiguration* (1520): Raphael's last painting, considered his masterpiece, now in the Vatican Pinacoteca.

%% 053.0348 %%
No, truly — it is very beautiful and very natural. But that rogue Raphael painted very little himself. He drew — but that is not enough.

%% 053.0349 %%
I was not there long enough to criticise properly — another day.

%% 053.0350 %%
As we sit down to table, M. Besnard comes — but he is not received.

%% 053.0351 %%
%% [#Health](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/HEALTH.md) %%
I spend the evening saying nonsense about the statues, and tying a ribbon around Prater's neck. Maman is ill and I am anxious about Nice — for three days now, not a letter.

%% 053.0352 %%
It is late, I hasten, I am sleepy.

%% 053.0353 %%
A heap of little anxieties — Nice, and then a period dress that is not going well — well, good night!
