"panorama de Lourdes" — a panorama painting: the massive 360-degree circular paintings were fashionable popular entertainment in the 1880s. "le fils du statuaire" — the son of the sculptor; Carrier-Belleuse père, Albert-Ernest Carrier-Belleuse (1824–1887), was one of the most celebrated French sculptors of the period; his son Henri-Anatole designed the Lourdes panorama. "l'exécution en grand" — the large-scale execution of the final panorama. "points approuvés par Tony" — the points approved by Tony Robert-Fleury; his aesthetic judgment as her teacher is the measure she applies. "dans le Midi" — to the South of France; standard prescription for lung patients (Nice, Cannes, Menton). "les dents serrées" — teeth clenched. "Aller dans le midi s'est se rendre" — "s'est" is a scribal/spelling error for "c'est" in the original: "Going to the Midi is surrendering." Preserved as-is in translation. "le triomphe de toute la vermine des Mouzay" — the triumph of all the vermin of the Mouzays; family enemies or provincial relatives she despises. "j'ai bonne mine" — I look well; I have good color. "un point de folie" — a touch of madness, an obsession. "a propos de bottes" — for no good reason, apropos of nothing (literally: apropos of boots). %%

[^2]: Pierre-Carl Potain (1825–1901), eminent French physician and specialist in cardiac and pulmonary diseases. He was among the most sought-after doctors in Paris; his presence signals that Marie's family took her condition seriously, even if she did not.

[^1]: Carrier-Belleuse: a painter, son of the sculptor Albert-Ernest Carrier-Belleuse (1824–1887), who also employed the young Rodin; Marie writes "Carrier-Belleuse, the sculptor's son". Which son is meant (Pierre Carrier-Belleuse, 1851–1932, a painter of panoramas, or another) and the earlier first name "Henri-Anatole" are not verified. The Lourdes panorama was one of the fashionable 360-degree circular paintings popular in 1880s Paris.

If I had another family, it might be done — but these people — look, [blacked out: it has been] three days that I have been telling my aunt: yes, I am ill, but if you want to care for me properly and not drive me to despair, please do not shout it from the rooftops. It is the plea of an invalid, a touch of madness if you like — but in short, do not go lamenting my health to all and sundry at every turn; care for me, but do not tell everyone.
