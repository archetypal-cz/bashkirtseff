# Sunday, 2 October 1881
%% 2026-09-28T21:01:18 RSR: Re-set from tome14.docx ¶2650, Mon Journal t.14 p.215: this text sat under «Samedi 1er octobre 1881» in the old extraction but belongs to the date line «Dimanche 2 octobre 1881» (¶2649, printed as its own line) (2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
[words blacked out: One feels] one has been dreaming on coming out of that bloody infamy. Bullfights. An abominable slaughter of nags and cows in which the men do not seem to run any danger and in which they play an ignoble part. Besides, the only interesting moments for me were seeing the men roll on the ground; one of them was trampled by the bull, a real miracle that he escaped, and so they gave him an ovation. People throw cigars and hats, which are thrown back very deftly, not the cigars, the hats. And the handkerchiefs wave and people let out savage howls... As a matter of fact the ovation was for another man, not for the one who fell, no matter. The public knows them and has its favourites; to me they are puppets of various colours. It is a cruel game, but is it amusing? That is the question! Well, no, it is neither thrilling nor interesting, it is horrible and ignoble. [words blacked out: This beast,] supposedly furious, which they goad with many-coloured capes and into whose body they plant a kind of sword; the blood flows; the more the animal shakes itself, the more it leaps, the more it wounds itself. They present to it poor horses [words blacked out: with their] eyes bandaged, [words blacked out: which it disembowels, the intes]tines come out, the blood flows, the horse nevertheless gets up again and obeys to the very last the man, who often falls with it but almost never comes to any harm. That black blood on the sand and that scarlet blood on the backs of the bulls; there was one black one especially when we arrived, it was as if wrapped in scarlet bands; at first I thought it was decked with ribbons, for those pikes planted in its hide were streaming. And when the horses are dead the fight continues with the men — that is to say a dozen Spanish idiots goad and riddle with stabs [words blacked out: the bull] which [words blacked out: leaps and pursues] but it is always the cape he meets. And when — wounded, bleeding, bellowing in distress — he stops and turns his head from the attacks, they present that red cape again and kick him… Then the crowd stamps its feet; the poor beast drops to its knees and lies down to die in the harmless pose of a cow resting in a meadow. He is killed with a blow to the nape. [words blacked out: illegible, in the end] the music plays, and three beribboned horses harnessed to a kind of hook to which the carcass is attached come to carry off at a gallop the dead horses and dead bull. And then it starts again — three men on horseback, more disembowelled horses, and then the ridiculous, bloody goading of the toreadors. [words blacked out: And when] about fifteen horses and five or six bulls have been killed, the elegant public goes to stroll in the Bueno Retiro — one of the most beautiful parks in the world, which I prefer to the Bois, to say nothing of London and Vienna, and which is more beautiful than the villas of Rome — but no, Rome has a charm such that nothing can be compared to it.

But… We left Biarritz Thursday morning and arrived in the evening at Burgos. The Pyrenees strike me with their majestic beauty — at last one escapes Biarritz's cardboard rocks. We travelled with a fat gentleman who spoke no French; none of us speaks Spanish; nonetheless he explained an illustrated newspaper to me and offered two flowers at a station. If you imagine that travelling with my mothers is a pleasure, you are quite mistaken. Besides, it is entirely natural — they have neither my youth nor my curiosities… In short, since it is past, I shall speak no more of their innocent teasing — especially since they are so fussy that I shall have a thousand occasions to return to the subject. Woeful airs, and then absurd questions, pretending to believe themselves in some wilderness, a country one never goes to. And the guide saying it is cold in Burgos — a true desolation; one should have brought the pelisses, what is to be done, what will become of us. What a country! And see what there? The cathedral — but only the English go… And the worst of it is that all this was addressed to me in the third person, or nothing was said at all, but while speaking of something else — airs! And if I protest, they say nothing was said and that I am looking for pretexts to quarrel.

So — Burgos… Ah! they are unbearable; when it is not the doleful resignation and complaints in the third person, it is so complete an indifference that one is quite taken aback.
