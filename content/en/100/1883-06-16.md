%% 2026-09-28T21:01:28 RSR: Moved out of 100.0026, which ran several manuscript days together: this day's text from tome15.docx ¶3647, Mon Journal t.15 p.286 (2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
So then, I withdraw the title of masterpiece from Bastien's paintings. Why? Because his *Amour au village* sets my teeth on edge, or because I lack the courage of my opinion? One dares deify only the dead; if Millet were alive, what would people say of him? And besides, there are only six canvases of Millet there; shall we not find six equivalent canvases in the rue Legendre? Not a hope! 1. *Jeanne d'Arc*; 2. The portrait of his brother; 3. *Le Soir au village*; 4. *Les Foins*; 5. I do not know everything, and he is not dead yet. Bastien is less Millet's son than Cazin, who resembles him a great deal, or rather more... young... Bastien is original; he is himself. One always derives a little from someone, but then the personality emerges. Besides, poetry, force, charm are always the same, and if seeking them is *imitating*, then it would be enough to make one despair. One feels a keen impression before a Millet; one finds it again before a Jules... What does that prove?
