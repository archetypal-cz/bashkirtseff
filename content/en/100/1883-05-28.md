You can imagine that the mention is the subject of conversation; I draw on everything I have said and written since that fatal event and present it to this great man as a brilliant improvisation.
The great man is altogether charming. He is not the sly one from Damvillers. He is a man of the world and a man of wit. He has said nothing but remarkable things; he is neither Bouguereau nor Manet, nor even the false naïf from Damvillers.
I call him a false naïf and Saint-Marceaux approves; indeed he has referred to him as a photographer, in the third person.
The modern school is on a wrong path, and Bastien too.
