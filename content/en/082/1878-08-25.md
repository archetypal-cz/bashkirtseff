Afterwards we paid a call on Mme de Balorre, on her daughter the Baronne de Wykerslooth, and on her other daughter, married to the Comte de Souza, an embassy attaché... The latter will not go to the Mouzay woman's evenings; her mother and her other sister do not often go there either, for that matter. Then a card left at the Boyds'.

[^82.179.1]: Grand Duke Constantine Nikolaevich (1827–1892), brother of Tsar Alexander II, was one of the architects of the Great Reforms of the 1860s, including the emancipation of the serfs. He was in Paris during the 1878 Universal Exposition.
[^82.183.1]: Raoul Nathan is an ambitious journalist and poet in Balzac's novel *Une fille d'Ève* (1838–1839) who captivates a married countess through his passionate intelligence.

One was made two years ago; the man sits astride a chair. The other [words blacked out: is quite recent]: Monsieur sits in an armchair and appears to be clinging to it. The first bears, in my own hand, the word: *Before*; the second, *After*. Like the advertisements for oils against hair loss. There are also two mottoes: *En Avant!* and *J'y suis, j'y reste.*[^82.185.1] But I have placed these images so as to hide them in shadow, behind a curve the screen makes to leave room for the candelabrum. I was already getting into the habit of looking at them all the time.

[^82.185.1]: "J'y suis, j'y reste" (I am here, I stay here) was a phrase attributed to Marshal MacMahon at Sebastopol in 1855, when ordered to abandon a captured position. Paul de Cassagnac adopted it as his political motto.
