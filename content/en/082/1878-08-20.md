# Tuesday, 20 August 1878

%% J'ai oublié de dire que mon oncle Nicolas est ici depuis trois semaines ou plus. Il est très laid et c'est un nihiliste, un nihiliste borné. Je m'en suis moquée et puis j'ai raisonné sérieusement et je l'ai prié de m'expliquer la chose et de me dire aussi clairement et brièvement que possible ce qu'ils veulent. %%
I forgot to mention that my Uncle Nicolas has been here for three weeks or more. He is very ugly, and he is a nihilist[^82.150.1] — a narrow-minded nihilist. I mocked him at first, then reasoned with him seriously and asked him to explain the matter to me and to tell me, as clearly and briefly as possible, what it is they want.

%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%

%% Il s'est embarassé dans une telle multitude de mots que j'en suis étourdie et agacée. Ils sont tous ainsi. Il paraît que la liberté n'existe encore nulle part et que la Suisse est une monstruosité ou à peu près. J'écouterai les choses les plus monstrueuses si elles sont logiquement et clairement dites. La confusion, le manque de *positif* ne se peut souffrir que dans les belles choses. %%
He tangled himself in such a multitude of words that I was left dizzy and irritated. They are all like that. It seems that liberty does not yet exist anywhere, and that Switzerland is a monstrosity, or very nearly. I can listen to the most monstrous things, provided they are stated logically and clearly. Confusion, the lack of the *concrete* — one can only tolerate that in beautiful things.

%% J'ai un chronomètre de table à réveil et répétition; une corbeille pour les journaux et papiers, j'écris avec une plume d'oie comme M. Grévy, il me manque une sonnette je l'aurai demain. %%
I have a table clock with alarm and repeater; a basket for newspapers and papers; I write with a goose-quill pen, like M. Grévy[^82.152.1] — I lack only a small bell, which I shall have tomorrow.

[^82.150.1]: Russian nihilism was a radical philosophical and political movement of the 1860s–1880s that rejected traditional moral and social authority. Inspired by writers like Nikolai Chernyshevsky and Dmitri Pisarev, nihilists advocated the destruction of existing institutions. By 1878 the movement had radicalized significantly, with Vera Zasulich's attempted assassination of the governor of St. Petersburg having occurred in January of that year.
[^82.152.1]: Jules Grévy (1807–1891) was then President of the Chamber of Deputies; he would become President of the French Republic in January 1879. His frugal, old-fashioned habits — including writing with a quill pen — were widely noted.

> *Mont de Marsan*
> 19 August 1878
> Mademoiselle,
> *Will you permit me to write to you, and would you carry your kindness so far as to give me news of yourself? I learn that you have gone to Ems to join your cousin. I also hear that your great-uncle is getting worse and worse. If misfortune were to befall him, it is better that you should not witness it, and that your kind heart should not be put to the cruel ordeal of a painful spectacle. I therefore rejoice at your departure.*
> *I hope, however, that you and your cousin will return to Paris soon, so that, on the day when I can no longer resist my sorrow at being far away, I may go and pay you a visit there, to gather strength and courage until my final return at the beginning of the winter.*
> *Would you be so obliging as to tell me when you will all leave Ems for Paris, and whether you do not intend, after your return from Germany, to make some other journey or excursion?*
> *Be kind and generous, Mademoiselle, as the natural inclinations of your heart usually lead you to be: give me news of yourself, and news of your intractable cousin. Tell me whether she holds me in horror, or whether she has for me only a hard and disdainful indifference. Tell me whether you are my friend, whether you remember the request I whispered to you on the day of my departure, to which you replied: "I promise you."*
> *Please reply quickly; I am reckoning how many days and hours I shall have to wait for your answer.*
> *Write to me at length and in detail: every word that comes from Germany will be worth a diamond to me.*
> *I lay my respectful homage at your feet, and at those of your cruel cousin and of your aunt.*
> *A. MULTEDO*
> Is it true that the famous guitar arrived broken?
