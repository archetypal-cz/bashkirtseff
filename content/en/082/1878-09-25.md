> *Who knows whether you will come back any time soon! You who hate Russia so, and flee it — perhaps you will be detained and bound there by life and happiness. Places matter for little enough in our existence. One is well where one loves; everywhere else one is ill.*

> *You ought to give your dear Maman a little distraction. Take her away from Paris if you are not leaving soon for Russia. Do not leave her in that sad apartment where everything reminds her of her Father and of your devoted Walitsky. %% 2026-10-02T16:40:28 RSR: Split: paragraph 4 of 15 of Collignon's letter (Mon Journal t.12 pp.125–126, tome12.docx ¶1381–1396; ¶1388/1389 is one paragraph broken by the page turn, joined here); the whole letter had shared one ID (082.0452). %%
> *I do not intend to stay where I am; as soon as I find something better I shall do as the ministers do — I shall resign. It is the life of a nun in a convent that I lead, and I am profoundly sick of it; one cannot always live with children, I need people of my own age. We see no one, we go to no one's house; I live between the schoolroom and my bedroom; I cannot bear it any longer, this solitude weighs on me and is killing me. If at least the pecuniary and material advantages weighed in the other pan of the scales, I would be patient and tell myself that in time I should have my independence; but I have a most uncomfortable room into which not a ray of sun comes in winter, by way of remuneration 100 francs a month without laundry, and the prospect of spending my time between Nice and the mountains two months in summer, or quite simply Nice winter and summer.*

> *Here there is not a soul yet — crowds of workmen cleaning, painting, prettifying in readiness for the foreigners who are expected in crowds. They say the Season will be brilliant; we shall see — expectations are not always realities. The weather has grown considerably cooler and may be called bearable. Immense works are going on at Monte Carlo: 600 workmen are labouring night and day without respite at the enlargement of the Casino. The night work is done by electric light; it is curious to see. Last Sunday I went all the way there. Poor M. Némikoff is worse; he walks with difficulty and looks like a skeleton.*
