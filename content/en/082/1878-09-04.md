Alas, Walitsky was among us, our best friend, and we shall never see him again. Grand-papa too has gone for ever... I refrain from naming Pincio... you know they bought me another one just like him, with the difference that this one is a female. This false Pincio has almost made me forget the first. It is not for that reason that I do not name him, but out of respect for Grand-papa... So, when I think back... It was at the Deceased's house, in the smoking room; I was looking at his portrait; he came up to me and said, in that voice and with that air which belong to him alone, and the mere memory of which overwhelms both mind and heart:

I was already enchanted, because it amused me and made me proud to play a part in a Parisian adventure like a *grown-up*. Not having been out in society, it always seems to me that I am not yet grown up... and at the same time I am afraid of looking old. It was to me that he said it... there were five of us... he paid attention only to me, while being gallant with the others... Oh! how detestable all those I listened to before are beside him, how ordinary, narrow, mediocre... Is it possible to stoop so low as to come under the influence of a Larderel, an amusing but contemptible creature... a man like all the others, in short... let us leave him to the other women... but as for me — it was truly stupid. But here, what enchantment to have all one's thoughts understood, shared; to find the delicacies of one's soul again in another soul; never to fear that a word, a gesture, a look might be not understood or misunderstood... I adore his wit, I adore his intelligence... how well he understands my vexation... what he has perhaps not understood is that I am mad but honest... no, I prefer to admit something else: I could have, and I did not know how. I do not know whether that consoles me or drives me to despair!! And if Berthe had not taken off her mask, M. Acard might still be Paul de Cassagnac and everything would have been quite different... yes, I feel it, I believe it, I am sure of it, I would swear to it!! He would have made of me whatever he wished... If I ever happened to say such a thing and to affect this submission towards another man, you must feel with me how ridiculous and impossible it all was. To let myself be dominated by a vicious little creature, by a jockey of high society, by a failed dandy — oh, la la! That is all I can say. At least, if I am mistaken here, there is some semblance of reason...

A day will come when I shall perhaps discover that all this was imagination — now that would be a paradox! What would all things be without imagination? and our feelings, [words blacked out: are they not] without imagination? Kant maintains that things exist only through our imagination; that is going too far, but I accept his system in the domain of feeling... In fact feelings are produced by the impression that objects or beings make, and since he says that objects are such or such only... that they have, in a word, no objective value and have reality only in our mind. To reconcile all that, one would have to be in no hurry to go to bed, and not be thinking of the hour at which one must start drawing in order to have finished by Saturday.

Things have [Crossed out: value] reality only in our mind? Very well — and I tell you that the object strikes the sight, or the sound the hearing, and that these... let us say *things*! produce a sensation which accords with them... Otherwise nothing would need to exist; we should *invent* everything. If nothing exists in this world, where then does anything exist? Certainly... the inhabitants of another planet perhaps see otherwise than we do, and in that case *they* are right; but we are on the earth — let us stay there, let us study what is on it or under it, and that is quite enough.
