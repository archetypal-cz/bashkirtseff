%% Depuis que j'ai ma voiture, je m'ennuie moins, aujourd'hui je suis même assez occupée. %%
Since I have had my carriage, I am less bored — today I am even rather occupied.

%% (Robe Archiduc, chapeau noir, jaquette gros bleu, ravissante). Je mène les filles de Mme Aleinikoff, une Russe qu'on a connue à l'hôtel, à Rauenthal, hauteur d'où l'on admire le Rhin. Le temps est assez sombre et je n'étais pas en train pour admirer. %%
(Archduke dress,[^39.194.1] black hat, navy blue jacket — ravishing.) I take Madame Aleinikoff's daughters — a Russian lady we met at the hotel — to Rauenthal, a height from which one admires the Rhine. The weather is rather gloomy and I was not in the mood to admire.
%% 2026-03-05T13:10:00 TR: "Robe Archiduc" — a fashionable cut named after Austrian royalty. "gros bleu" — LAN: dark/navy blue. "en train pour" — LAN: "in the mood for, disposed to." Marie's parenthetical outfit documentation is characteristic. %%

[^39.194.1]: *Robe Archiduc*: an "Archduke-style" dress — a fashionable cut of the 1870s named for its association with Austrian royalty.

%% 039.0195 %%
%% Les Allemands sont détestables ! Ils ne se dérangent pas et c'est la voiture qui doit donner passage aux piétons. Ils sont endimanchés partout dans les villages que j'eus à passer. Fi ! la sale, la brutale, la vilaine nation ! %%
The Germans are detestable! They do not move aside — it is the carriage that must yield to the pedestrians. They are all in their Sunday best in every village I passed through. Fie! The dirty, brutal, odious nation!
%% 2026-03-05T13:11:00 TR: "ne se derangent pas" — "do not move aside" (road etiquette). "endimanches" — LAN: in Sunday best, provincial/unfashionable. "Fi!" — LAN: archaic exclamation of disgust; "Fie!" is the English equivalent. %%

%% - Bon, dis-je vous allez voir qu'il y aura un "dites à Marie" etc. et en effet: "Dites à Marie, écrit-elle, que pas un de *ses* Français n'est venu me voir, pas une fois, excepté Girofla qui vend tout pour payer ses dettes et celles de son père. Il veut éviter les publications et vend et veut lui-méme payer. Il vend pour presque rien et je le plains tellement, pauvre !" -Il est venu voir ma tante. C'est bien. %%
— Very well, I say, you will see there will be a "tell Marie" etc. — and indeed: "Tell Marie," she writes, "that not one of *her* Frenchmen has come to see me, not once, except Girofla, who is selling everything to pay his debts and those of his father. He wants to avoid public announcements[^39.197.1] and is selling and wants to pay out of his own pocket. He is selling for almost nothing, and I pity him so, poor fellow!" — He came to see my aunt. That is well.
%% 2026-03-05T13:12:00 TR: "eviter les publications" — LAN: avoid public announcements of bankruptcy/debt; "avoid public announcements" explains the legal term. "vend pour presque rien" — "selling for almost nothing" (desperate sale). %%

[^39.197.1]: In French law, a debtor facing insolvency could face *publications légales* — official public notices of bankruptcy proceedings. Audiffret is selling assets privately to avoid this public humiliation.

— Why for him, *no?* Because he tormented me — perhaps without meaning to.

%% 039.0207 %%

I am truly strange — I say he tormented me because he did not prostrate himself before me.

%% 039.0208 %%

I believe I could not torment him much — he pleases me, and without noticing it I shall always have some kindness for him.

At this I laugh. No — these people will never understand me. They do not know me. Me — marry for love and neglect all the rest! Come now! as d'Audiffret would say. He amuses me, he interests me — but nothing more.
