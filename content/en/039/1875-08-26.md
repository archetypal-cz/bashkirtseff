# Thursday, 26 August 1875

%% 039.0211 %%
%% [#Mortality](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/MORTALITY.md) %%
%% Nous avons recu nos photographies en Graces et en *disgraces .* C'est a mourir de rire ! %%
We have received our photographs — some as the Graces,[^39.211.1] some as *disgraces.* One could die laughing!
%% 2026-03-05T13:45:00 TR: "en Graces et en disgraces" — LAN: wordplay on successful poses (the Three Graces) vs failed ones (disgraces). The italics on "disgraces" are Marie's own. "C'est a mourir de rire" — "one could die laughing" per TM idiom. %%

[^39.211.1]: A reference to the Three Graces of classical mythology — the personifications of beauty and charm. Marie's wordplay: *en Graces* (as the Graces, graceful) vs *en disgraces* (disgraced, unflattering).

%% 039.0221 %%
%% [#Russian](../../_original/_glossary/culture/languages/RUSSIAN.md) [#French](../../_original/_glossary/culture/languages/FRENCH.md) %%
%% Aucune langue que la petite russienne ne peut exprimer les choses si nettement et si drolement ! %%
No language but Little Russian[^39.221.1] can express things so clearly and so comically!

[^39.221.1]: *La petite russienne*: Marie uses the diminutive to refer to what was then called "Little Russian" (*малоросійська*), i.e., Ukrainian, distinct from "Great Russian" (*великоросійська*). In Marie's milieu, the two Slavic languages were often heard together; she is likely referring to Ukrainian expressions in Walitsky's verse.

%% Je vais a Schwalbach avec Machenka, maman et Dina suivent en caleche. La nous voyons *Obeziana* et son frere un garcon tres propre et gentil, ils me regardent beaucoup et restent tout le temps dans la galerie des boutiques tout pres de nous. %%
I go to Schwalbach with Machenka; Maman and Dina follow in a calèche. There we see *Obeziana* and her brother — a very well-turned-out and agreeable young man; they watch me a great deal and remain the whole time in the shopping gallery very close to us.

%% 039.0226 %%
%% [#Comtesse_Benvenuti](../../_original/_glossary/people/aristocracy/COMTESSE_BENVENUTI.md) %%
%% J'ai dine a Nassauer Hof, l'hotel a la mode. J'y ai parle a la comtesse Benvenuti, femme aimable mais presque toujours grise. Misere de monde. Non, je vois qu'il faut renoncer a toute espece de recherche. Ennuyons-nous, ennuyons-nous, et restons tranquilles. %%
I dined at the Nassauer Hof, the fashionable hotel. I spoke there with the Comtesse Benvenuti, an amiable woman but nearly always tipsy. What a sorry state of affairs. No — I see that one must renounce every kind of pursuit. Let us be bored, let us be bored, and remain quiet.
%% 2026-03-05T13:48:00 TR: "a la mode" — "fashionable" (the Nassauer Hof was Wiesbaden's premier hotel). "grise" — LAN: euphemism for tipsy, intoxicated; "tipsy" is the natural English equivalent. "Misere de monde" — "what a sorry state of affairs" (expressing disillusionment with society). "recherche" — LAN: pursuit of social contacts/eligible men. %%

%% 039.0227 %%
%% Sous nos fenetres est bien passe un etre humain le matin mais dans la journee j'ai perdu sa trace. %%
Under our windows a human being did pass this morning — but in the course of the day I lost track of him.

%% 039.0228 %%
%% [#Stiopa](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/STIOPA.md) [#Plevasko](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PLEVASKO.md) %%
%% A la porte, Stiopa nous rencontre, il vient de recevoir une depeche de ma tante, l'avocat Plevasko l'attend a Paris et elle appelle Stiopa. %%
At the door, Stiopa meets us — he has just received a telegram[^39.228.1] from my aunt; the lawyer Plevasko is awaiting him in Paris, and she is summoning Stiopa.

[^39.228.1]: *Dépêche*: a telegram.

%% 039.0229 %%
%% De nouveau nous parlons de chevaux et on ne m'irrite pas beaucoup, heureusement. %%
Once again we speak of horses — and they do not irritate me too much, fortunately.
