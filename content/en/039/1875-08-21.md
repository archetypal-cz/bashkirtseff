%% 039.0106 %%
%% [#Papa](../../_original/_glossary/people/family/PAPA.md) [#Paul](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PAUL.md) %%
%% Mon pere ecrit que, malgre tous ses soins et toute sa tendresse, Paul se conduit d'une facon impossible. %%
My father writes that, despite all his care and all his tenderness, Paul is behaving in an impossible fashion.

%% 039.0107 %%
%% [#Maman](../../_original/_glossary/people/family/MAMAN.md) %%
%% Maman avait ecrit a Paul que son pere se ruinait, pour lui oter l'espoir d'heriter et le faire etudier. Et mon pere repond a cela: %%
Maman had written to Paul that his father was ruining himself, in order to remove from him the hope of inheriting and to make him study. And my father replies to this:

%% 039.0108 %%
%% [#Reading](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/READING.md) %%
%% *J'ai lu votre lettre a notre fils et j'estime non superflu de faire sur elle quelques remarques. Je ne me ruine pas* etc. etc. puis: *Je vends les proprietes parce que votre haine et celle de votre fille envers moi me force* etc. etc. %%
*I have read your letter to our son and consider it not superfluous to make a few remarks upon it. I am not ruining myself* etc. etc., then: *I am selling the properties because your hatred and that of your daughter towards me compels me* etc. etc.
%% 2026-03-05T13:00:00 TR: "j'estime non superflu" — LAN: formal/legalistic pompous phrasing; "consider it not superfluous" preserves the stiff register exactly. The italics and "etc. etc." are Marie's own notation, indicating she is quoting selectively. %%

%% 039.0109 %%
%% Et moi de mon cote je prends une feuille de papier et ecris ceci: %%
And for my part I take a sheet of paper and write the following:

%% 039.0110 %%
%% [#Reading](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/READING.md) %%
%% [#Love](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/LOVE.md) %%
%% *J'ai lu votre lettre a ma mere et j'estime aussi non superflu de faire quelques remarques, je n'ai pas la moindre haine envers vous, au contraire je vous aime comme un pere et si je ne suis pas particulierement tendre, accusez de cela vous seul, qui n'avez rien fait pour vous attacher votre fille Maria Bachkirtseva.* %%
*I have read your letter to my mother and also consider it not superfluous to make a few remarks. I have not the slightest hatred towards you — on the contrary I love you as a father, and if I am not particularly tender, blame that on yourself alone, who have done nothing to attach your daughter Maria Bachkirtseva to you.*
%% 2026-03-05T13:01:00 TR: Marie deliberately mirrors her father's pompous style ("j'estime non superflu") in her own reply — the mirroring is the joke and the rebuke simultaneously. "Maria Bachkirtseva" — Marie signs with her formal Russian name (variant spelling), kept as-is. %%

%% 039.0111 %%
%% [#Meta_diary](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/META_DIARY.md) %%
%% Ayant ecrit cette lettre j'ecris ceci presque tout d'un trait, Godard seul m'a coute quelques corrections. D'ailleurs c'est assez laid. %%
Having written that letter I write the following almost all in one go — only Godard cost me a few corrections. Besides, it is rather poor.
%% 2026-03-05T13:02:00 TR: "tout d'un trait" — LAN: "all in one go, without stopping." "Godard" refers to Maurice Godard Decrais, featured in the verse below. "c'est assez laid" — "it is rather poor" (the verse she is about to write). %%

%% 039.0112 %%
Revue de Nice[^39.112.1]

[^39.112.1]: *Revue de Nice*: a satirical verse review — a theatrical genre in which known figures are lampooned in verse portraits, like a revue show.

%% 039.0113 %%
%% [#Saetone](../../_original/_glossary/people/recurring/SAETONE.md) %%
Fat, ruddy, slightly lame,
Patron of beginners,
Always content, always gay,
Fluttering everywhere at all times.

%% 039.0114 %%
%% [#Theater_theme](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/THEATER_THEME.md) %%
When he enters a theatre
And makes the rounds of the boxes
Displaying his alabaster hand
He deserves every eulogy.
%% 2026-03-05T13:03:00 TR: "fait le tour des loges" — "makes the rounds of the boxes" (theatre box social circuit). "main d'albatre" — "alabaster hand" (ironic feminine compliment applied to a vain man). The verse maintains the mock-heroic register. %%

%% 039.0115 %%
%% [#Love](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/LOVE.md) %%
To the beautiful ladies and the young misses
He instantly ravishes the heart
By the fire of his eyes
And by his conquering air.

%% 039.0116 %%
His name resounds everywhere:
Andriot Saetone.

%% 039.0117 %%
%% [#Danis](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/DANIS.md) %%
More timid, more lean and small,
Arthur Danis appears
With irreproachable detachable collars.[^39.117.1]
Both of them are adorable.

[^39.117.1]: *Faux cols* — detachable collars, a fashionable men's accessory in the 1870s.

%% 039.0118 %%
%% [#Maurice_Gros](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/MAURICE_GROS.md) %%
Face rosy, powdered, affected,
Moustache pomaded.
Hanging on someone's arm,
Maurice Gros, illustrious cad —
%% 2026-03-05T13:04:00 TR: "fardee" — powdered/made up (pejorative on a man). "guindee" — affected, stiff. "pommadee" — pomaded (hair). "faquin" — cad, worthless fellow; "cad" is the appropriate period register. %%

%% 039.0119 %%
What a pity one must speak
Of such a scoundrel!

%% 039.0120 %%
%% [#Comte_dAspremont](../../_original/_glossary/people/aristocracy/COMTE_DASPREMONT.md) %%
The Comte d'Aspremont,
Tall as a greasy pole[^39.120.1] —
For a nose he has a mountain,
No, a whole mountain range.

[^39.120.1]: *Mât de cocagne* — a tall greased pole from the top of which prizes are suspended; a fairground contest.

%% 039.0121 %%
%% [#Gautier](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/GAUTIER.md) %%
Shabbily dressed, distracted, stooping,
Monsieur Gautier advances —
A man of Nice not even in his Sunday best,
Aiming at elegance.

%% 039.0122 %%
%% [#Galula_Dechiar](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/GALULA_DECHIAR.md) %%
The future notary Galula-Dechiar,
A secondary personage
And cunning as a fox.

%% 039.0123 %%
He keeps his large eyes
Raised heavenward without cease.

%% 039.0124 %%
%% [#Comte_de_Laurenti](../../_original/_glossary/people/aristocracy/COMTE_DE_LAURENTI.md) %%
The Comte de Laurenti,
Royalist and gentleman,
Dark, lean, dry, small
And wrinkled like an old apple.

%% 039.0125 %%
%% [#Maurice_Godard_Decrais](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/MAURICE_GODARD_DECRAIS.md) %%
Maurice Godard Decrais,
Timid and sincere,
Who brays like a donkey —
Son of the prefect, he is tolerated.
%% 2026-03-05T13:05:00 TR: "brait" — brays (donkey); "brays like a donkey" for his voice. "prefet" — prefect (regional government administrator); his social tolerance comes from his father's position. %%

%% 039.0126 %%
He is candid and virtuous —
His posterior imitates
The tortuous coils
Of Hippolytus's hydra.[^39.126.1]

[^39.126.1]: In Greek myth, Poseidon sent a sea-monster (*hydra* or sea-serpent) at the prayer of Theseus; it terrified Hippolytus's horses and caused his death. Marie deploys the classical reference for comic effect.

%% 039.0127 %%
But already Andriot
Regards him with paternal eye
And of this little fool
Will make a great man, I hope.

%% 039.0128 %%
%% [#Emile_d_Audiffret](../../_original/_glossary/people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md) %%
Let us arrive now
At the illustrious lord of the castle,
That outlandish young man
At the head of these boors.
%% 2026-03-05T13:06:00 TR: "chatelain illustre" — "lord of the castle" (his château). "abracadabrant" — "outlandish" captures the sense of fantastic, bizarre. "rustres" — "boors," uncouth people. %%

%% 039.0129 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
His sweet name is Emile,
He is pink as a rose,
That dear imbecile
Who thinks only of his pose.
