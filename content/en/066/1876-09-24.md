I was shivering. My father lined us all up indiscriminately on either side of the road, counted us, then divided us into two sections — the armed and the unarmed. At last I made out a gunshot — the signal. Do you see that calculation, that scheme, that project? I read it in his face, in his eyes; I guessed it from what had gone before — and it struck me as so amusing, so very amusing, that I told the Princess and my father, begging them to say nothing to the little man, who, frightened, had taken refuge in the red house and returned quite confused, not knowing how my father would look at him. Had I not guessed — that is, had he not meant to say what I understood — he would not have fled and would not afterwards have told me a heap of nonsense resembling complaints, reproaches, everything and nothing.
