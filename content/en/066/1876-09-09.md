# Saturday, 9 September 1876 (28 August)
%% Les jours passent, je perds un temps précieux dans les meilleures années de ma vie. Des soirées en famille, des plaisanteries charmantes, une gaieté dont je fais tous les frais... puis on se fait monter et descendre du grand escalier, dans un fauteuil par Michel et l'autre. On regarde ses souliers dans la glace tout en descendant... Tous les jours comme ça... %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶987, Mon Journal t.9 pp.65–66 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
The days go by; I am wasting precious time in the best years of my life. Family evenings, charming jokes, a gaiety I supply entirely by myself... then one has oneself carried up and down the great staircase in an armchair by Michel and the other one. One looks at one's shoes in the mirror all the way down... Every day like that...

%% 066.0124 %%
%% Mais quel ennui ! Pas une parole d'esprit, pas une phrase instruite... je suis pédante malheureusement et j'adore entendre parler des Anciens et des sciences... cherchez-moi ça ici ! Les cartes et rien d'autre. Je m'enfermerais bien pour lire mais mon but étant de me faire aimer ce serait une étrange manière pour y parvenir. %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶988, Mon Journal t.9 p.66 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
But what boredom! Not one witty word, not one educated sentence... I am a pedant, unfortunately, and I adore hearing talk of the Ancients and of the sciences... find me that here! Cards and nothing else. I would gladly shut myself up to read, but since my aim is to make myself loved, that would be a strange way of going about it.

As soon as I am settled for the winter I shall take up my studies again as before.

Voilà de la vulgarité, mais mon journal en est plein. Enfin il y avait du mécontentement dans l'air, j'étais fâchée et, dans ma voix, on entendait ces notes tremblantes qui annoncent un orage. %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶991, Mon Journal t.9 p.66 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
Afterwards we played croquet and ball. And in the evening there was some business with the servants involving Paul; my father took the valet's side, I *reprimanded* (that is the word) my father, who *swallowed* the reprimand. That is vulgar, but my diary is full of such things. Pray believe that I am not vulgar out of ignorance or out of *vulgarity.* I have adopted this careless style for its speed and for the ease with which it lets one say a great deal. In short, there was discontent in the air; I was angry, and in my voice one could hear those trembling notes that herald a storm.

%% 066.0128 %%

I have placed myself so high, I said, that all petty miseries passed beneath my feet — and now here are some that touch me!

%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶993, Mon Journal t.9 p.66 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%

I had nothing to forgive, but I understood that he was addressing me as a power, and, not wishing to irritate the Princess or anyone else any further, I smiled and asked why this man had come to me.

Ah! if only I were at least rewarded for the life I lead. Croquet, the rubber ball, cards, and to finish, a session on the staircase: Pacha's nerves to fray and the hair of the prostrate Michel to torment, varying this exercise with tugs that his ears feel until they turn red.

Le premier soir que j'en jouai, tous les domestiques vinrent en foule effrayés et ne comprenant pas d'où pouvaient venir ces cris déchirants et ces hurlements infâmes, qui avait une arrière-ressemblance avec les cris d'agonie d'un jeune cochon à la cuisine. %%

The first evening I played it, all the servants came running in a crowd, frightened, unable to understand where they could be coming from — these heart-rending cries and infamous howls, which bore a faint resemblance to the death-cries of a young pig in the kitchen.
