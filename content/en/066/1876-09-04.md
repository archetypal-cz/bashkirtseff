# Monday, 4 September 1876 (23 August)
%% [Mots noircis : Il n’y a]vait plus personne, pas même Michel, mon père dormait, lorsqu'arriva une vieille dame à lunettes bleues, un peu bossue, plate devant, aux grands pieds et tenant une petite ombrelle noire dans ses grandes mains. Elle demanda mon père en français, s'annonçant comme la femme de l'homme qui est venu il y a deux jours vendre des moutons, un intendant français. Mon père refusa de la voir, par paresse; elle insistait, et Pacha eut à faire cinq fois le chemin du salon au lit de Monsieur. %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶883, Mon Journal t.9 p.58 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
[words blacked out: There was] no one left, not even Michel, and my father was asleep, when an old lady arrived in blue spectacles, a little hunchbacked, flat in front, with big feet, holding a small black parasol in her big hands. She asked for my father in French, announcing herself as the wife of the man who came two days ago to sell sheep, a French steward. My father refused to see her out of laziness; she insisted, and Pacha had to make the trip from the drawing room to Monsieur's bed five times.

%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶884, Mon Journal t.9 p.58 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
At last, deaf to all reason, the fury came into the library; Pacha followed her, trying to hold her back, but she, heeding nothing, went into the bedroom and, drawing aside the curtains of the alcove, found herself face to face with my father. Dumbfounded, he sat bolt upright and fixed her with bewildered eyes, whereupon she burst out laughing like a madwoman.

%% Et ce rire se répondit comme une traînée de feu par tout l'étage inférieur et eut un retentissant écho car dans chaque pièce il y avait ou la Princesse, ou Pacha ou Paul, qui se retenaient depuis trop longtemps. Ils entrèrent en furie chez mon père qui toujours assis se tenait les côtes pendant que je me débarbouillais. %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶885, Mon Journal t.9 p.58 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
And this laughter ran like a trail of fire through the whole ground floor and was answered by a resounding echo, for in every room there was the Princess, or Pacha, or Paul, who had been holding themselves in for far too long. They burst into my father's room, where he sat, still upright, holding his sides, while I was washing my face.

In the night they called me from my room out onto the balcony and set off a little firework display [words blacked out: for me]. At every rocket a cry was heard on the hillside: it was Simon, the poor idiot. People insist he is not mad but makes a trade of it. In any case, the effect was fantastic: the moon, the fire, and a madman whose cries were repeated several times over by the echo.
