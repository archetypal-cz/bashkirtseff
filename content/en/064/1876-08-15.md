# Tuesday, 15 August (3 August 1876)
%% La maison est gaie et claire comme une lanterne. Les fleurs embaument, les perroquets parlent, les canaris chantent, les domestiques courent. Vers onze heures un bruit de clochettes nous annonça mon voisin. C'était M. Hamsley, ne dirait-on pas un Anglais ? Eh bien pas du tout, une ancienne et noble famille de la Petite-Russie. Sa femme est une des Prodgers d'ici. Mon bagage n'étant pas arrivé (nous sommes descendus une station plus tôt qu'il fallait), je me suis montrée en robe de chambre blanche ; quelle immense différence moi à présent et moi il y a un an ! Il y a un an j'osais à peine parler, « je ne savais que dire » comme Marguerite. A présent je suis *grande.* Ce monsieur a déjeuné avec nous : que veut-on que je dise de lui et de ceux que je verrai ? Excellentes gens mais puant la province d'une lieue. %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:37:11 RSR: Restored from tome08.docx ¶4221, Mon Journal t.8 pp.371–372 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-29 rebuild). OCR «1 II» corrected to «! Il» from the scan (p.372). %%
The house is as cheerful and bright as a lantern. The flowers are fragrant, the parrots talk, the canaries sing, the servants run about. Towards eleven o'clock a jingling of little bells announced my neighbour. It was M. Hamsley — would you not take him for an Englishman? Well, not at all: an old and noble family of Little Russia. His wife is one of the Prodgers of these parts. As my luggage had not arrived (we got off one station too early), I appeared in a white dressing gown. What an immense difference between me now and me a year ago! A year ago I hardly dared speak — "I knew not what to say," like Marguerite. Now I am *grown up.* This gentleman lunched with us: what would you have me say of him and of the others I shall see? Excellent people, but you can smell the provinces on them a league away.

I was walking slowly, leaning on Paul's arm, my thoughts wandering heaven knows where, when, as we passed beneath branches that hung very low over our heads and formed a ceiling of interlaced leaves, I imagined what Antonelli would say if I were on his arm, walking down this path. He would say... — it is a bit steep, writing down one's fancies!... He would say, leaning slightly towards me, he would say in that languorous, penetrating voice he used only with me..., he would say... "How pleasant it is here, and how I love you."
