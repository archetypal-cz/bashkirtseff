# Saturday, 8 November 1879

%% 086.0487 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%
%% [#Paris](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/PARIS.md) %%
%% J'ai fini le portrait de la concierge. Elle est très ressemblante. C'est une joie immense dans la loge, fille, beau fils, petites filles, sœurs, tout cela est dans le ravissement. %%

I have finished the portrait of the concierge. It is a very good likeness. There is immense joy in the lodge: daughter, son-in-law, little girls, sisters, all of them in raptures.

Unfortunately Tony did not share this enthusiasm. He began by saying it is not bad, and then... It is not going as well as it should; there is no denying that I am not as gifted for painting as for drawing. Drawing, construction, form — all that comes of itself. The painterly side is not developing fast enough. He does not want me to waste my time like this.

%% 086.0489 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%
%% Il faut en sortir, il faut faire quelque chose pour cela. Vous pataugez c'est évident et comme vous êtes extrêmement bien douée, comme vous avez de très grandes dispositions, cela m'ennuie. %%
%% 2026-03-09T15:30:00 TR: "pataugez" — you're floundering/wading; vivid metaphor for artistic struggle %%

— We must get you out of this; something must be done about it. You are floundering, that is obvious, and as you are extremely gifted, as you have a very great aptitude, it bothers me.

%% 086.0490 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%

— It does not amuse me either, Monsieur, but I do not know what to do about it.

%% 086.0491 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%
%% - Il y a longtemps que je veux causer de cela avec vous. Il faut essayer de tous les moyens, il ne s'agit peut-être que d'ouvrir une porte. %%

— I have wanted to discuss this with you for a long time. One must try every means, perhaps it is only a matter of opening a door.

%% 086.0492 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%

%% 2026-10-01T07:53:46 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
— Tell me what I must do — a copy, a plaster cast, a still life — I will do whatever you tell me.

%% 086.0493 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%
%% - Vous ferez tout ce que je vous dirai de faire, eh bien alors nous sommes certains d'en sortir. Venez me voir samedi prochain et nous en causerons, d'ici-là je m'occuperai de vous, je trouverai peut-être quelque chose pour vous. %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:53:46 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
— You will do everything I tell you to? Well then, we are sure to get you out of it. Come and see me next Saturday and we will talk it over; meanwhile I shall think about you, and perhaps I shall find something for you.

%% 086.0494 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%

I should have gone there on a Saturday long ago. All his pupils do. Well, he is a good fellow.

%% 086.0495 %%

At one o'clock M. de Morgan came to fetch us and take us to M. de Saint-Marceaux's.

I must not forget to tell you that we talked with Tony in the little plaster-cast room, where there were only two good souls, almost strangers to the gossip of the establishment.

%% 086.0497 %%

C'est un véritable artiste tout en étant homme du monde (point sur lequel M. de Morgan insiste toujours comme pour *excuser* Saint-Marceaux d'être artiste, "bonne famille, grande fortune") une belle tête quoique chauve. %% 2026-03-09T15:30:00 TR: "homme du monde" — man of society/gentleman; implies aristocratic breeding %%
%% 2026-03-09T15:30:00 TR: "pieds aristocratiques" — aristocratic feet; small, elegant feet as class marker %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:53:46 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
Saint-Marceaux did not recognise me — Maman and I are in black velvet — or else he pretended not to, since I said nothing, and even when he asked me whether I painted I answered that I loved the Arts without practising them. He is a true artist while being a man of the world (a point M. de Morgan always insists on, as if to *excuse* Saint-Marceaux for being an artist: "good family, great fortune"); a fine head, though bald. Superb eyes, and in his whole person something lively, as in Gabriel, only older, with something touching about him, like a grief or a sadness. Short, but wiry, and with aristocratic feet.
