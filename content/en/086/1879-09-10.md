He was married to Grand-papa's sister, by whom he had several children but only two survive: the unmarried Count Alexandre and the Marquise Montecuculi de Merano who has a host of children and is very rich, having inherited from an old relative, the Marquis de Merano. There are even stories about this. The old uncle has hit on the idea of making Dina a young widow; the idea is charming... Dina is a beautiful girl of twenty-four, when she was nineteen I believed she was already faded, but I was only fifteen... As a very young girl she was worn, pale, sallow, ugly; now, with a little dressing up, though without rouge, she can make an impression and look very pretty. Even her complexion has become very white and faintly pink, although her body has remained a dark, earthy colour, inexplicable alongside that blondest of blonde hair, greenish blonde at the temples and golden at the end of the braids. Her hair falls below her knees but there is very little of it; on her head and down to her shoulders it is still thick but the rest of the braids is pitiful, which she makes up for by working in long false pieces, and the whole forms an astonishing forest... That is precisely what revolts my silly honesty when people admire this hair: I believe I could not bear all these compliments knowing that three-quarters of this beauty is not legitimate, for, between us, if she let only her own hair hang down people would laugh at it, it would be rats' tails. All the same, I say it only here and I would take care not to say it to anyone in the world, but I have scruples.
