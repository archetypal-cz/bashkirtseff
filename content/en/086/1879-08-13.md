Krishaber left me the choice between Mont-Dore and the sea. Mont-Dore is far, Dieppe is quite near, the Karageorgevitches are in Dieppe. And then we were about to get into the omnibus when Blanc arrived. "Blanc! Come with us!" I said. "I should be glad to, but Mont-Dore is a long way off; if it were Dieppe..."

%% Est-ce que toutes ces villes de mer sont les mêmes ? J'ai été à Ostende, à Calais, à Douvres et je suis à Dieppe. Cela sent le goudron, le bateau, les cordages, la toile cirée. Il fait du vent, on est exposé de tous côtés et l'on se sent en détresse. Cela sent le mal de mer. Quelle différence avec la Méditerranée. Là on respire, là il y a quoi admirer, là on est bien. %%
Are all these seaside towns the same? I have been to Ostend, to Calais, to Dover and I am in Dieppe. It smells of tar, of boats, of ropes, of oilcloth. It is windy, one is exposed on all sides and feels forlorn. It smells of seasickness. What a difference from the Mediterranean. There one can breathe, there is something to admire there, there one feels well.

I came here to breathe! Ah, yes, to be sure! No doubt outside the town and the port the air is better. I do not like any of these northern seas. And from all these hotels here one only sees the sea from the fifth floor. O Nice, O San Remo, O Naples!! O Sorrento!!! You are not vain words, you are neither exaggerated nor profaned by the travellers' guidebooks, you are truly beautiful and divine!
