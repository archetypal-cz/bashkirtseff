%% Je me suis crée un ennui moi-même. Quand ce ne sont pas les autres c'est moi. Ce portrait. J'ai eu la bêtise d'en parler à Tony. Maintenant j'aurai le plaisir d'essuyer toutes les taquineries de ma famille qui commence ou commencera à dire que Tony est un gueux, un va-nu-pieds, un être qui sans moi n'aurait pas de quoi manger. Ah ! il est bien heureux de voler de l'argent pour ce portrait. C'est quatre mille francs mais je leur dis que je suppose que c'est six ou huit mille. Comme cela les quatre mille francs leur paraîtront moins terribles. Je les regrette moi-même mais que voulez-vous ! Si je n'avais parlé de ce bête de portrait tout irait si bien. Cet homme est si bon. Je suis allée lui porter ma copie, "ce n'est pas encore assez large, pas encore assez de tenue". J'en ferai encore la semaine prochaine deux têtes de Rubens copiées par Robert-Fleury père, un grand artiste celui-là. Et puis un petit bout de toile du même mais original. Comme j'admire beaucoup l'esquisse qu'il a fait pour son plafond du Luxembourg (lui Tony) il me l'offre de la façon la plus gracieuse disant que cela lui fait grand plaisir de la donner à quelqu'un qui s'y connaît bien, qui apprécie... %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:01:34 RSR: Restored from tome13.docx ¶1687, Mon Journal t.13 p.124 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
I have brought a vexation on myself. When it is not other people, it is me. This portrait. I was foolish enough to mention it to Tony. Now I shall have the pleasure of enduring all the teasing of my family, who are beginning, or will begin, to say that Tony is a beggar, a ragamuffin, a creature who without me would not have enough to eat. Ah! he is only too glad to be stealing money over this portrait. It is four thousand francs, but I tell them I suppose it is six or eight thousand. That way the four thousand francs will seem less dreadful to them. I begrudge the money myself, but what would you have! If I had not mentioned this stupid portrait, everything would be going so well. This man is so kind. I went to take him my copy: "It is not yet broad enough, not yet firm enough." Next week I shall do two more: two heads after Rubens copied by Robert-Fleury the father — a great artist, that one. And then a little scrap of canvas by the same, but an original. As I greatly admire the sketch he made for his ceiling at the Luxembourg (he, Tony), he offers it to me in the most gracious manner, saying it gives him great pleasure to give it to someone who really understands, who appreciates...

%% 2026-09-29T01:01:34 RSR: Restored from tome13.docx ¶1690, Mon Journal t.13 p.124 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
I am already growing bolder and am hardly afraid of him any more. After seeing him once or twice a week for two years at the atelier, it seems very odd to be chatting with him and to have him help me on with my pelisse. A little more and we shall be a pair of friends. But for this portrait I should be very content, for my master is as good to me as can be.

I come home and find a whole club at our house — all Mme Gavini's set and ours, and Bichinsky, who has come to introduce a Count Wodzinsky, a handsome Pole, much sought after. I am in high spirits. There were Chaudordy, Multedo, Gavini, etc... but some were missing because of the weather, which is frightful. Ah! a new friend: a naval captain, Lahirle, a man who, they say, can talk all the time without ever being tedious.
