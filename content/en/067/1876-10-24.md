# Tuesday, 24 October 1876 (12 October)
%% Je n'ai pas eu d'enfance, mais la maison où j'ai vécu toute petite m'est sympathique sinon chère. Je connais tout le monde et toute chose, les serviteurs de père en fils et vieillis à notre service s'étonnent de me voir si grande. Et je jouirais de quelques doux souvenirs si mon esprit n'était empoisonné par les préoccupations présentes. %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶1995, Mon Journal t.9 p.142 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
I had no childhood, but the house where I lived when I was very small is congenial to me, if not dear. I know everyone and everything; the servants, who have served us from father to son and grown old in our service, are astonished to see me so tall. And I would enjoy a few sweet memories if my mind were not poisoned by my present preoccupations.

%% 067.0288 %%
%% On m'appelait Mouche, *Mouha* et comme je ne pouvais aspirer l'h à la russe je faisais comme les Français, je disais Moucha, ce qui veut dire: martyrisation. Une lugubre coincidence. %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶1996, Mon Journal t.9 p.142 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
They used to call me Mouche, *Mouha*, and as I could not aspirate the h the Russian way, I did as the French do and said Moucha, which means: torture. A lugubrious coincidence.

%% 067.0289 %%
%% Ce nom était si populaire qu'il est *resté* et c'est Julie que l'on nomme ainsi, je veux dire les paysans et les domestiques. %%
%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶1997, Mon Journal t.9 p.142 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
The name was so popular that it has *stuck*, and it is Julie who is called by it now — I mean by the peasants and the servants.

%% 2026-09-28T12:25:39 RSR: Restored from tome09.docx ¶2005, Mon Journal t.9 p.142 (missing from the original extraction, 2026-09-27 rebuild). %%
Grandpapa's immense library holds precious and rare volumes, and among all these books I chose one, as much because of the moral impossibility of procuring it elsewhere as to disgust myself with books... how shall I put it? Well, I have purloined “Mlle de Maupin” by Th. Gautier. Oho! you say. Is that not so?

Someone wrote to Lola about my "extraordinary" photographs; I am a great topic of interest, and the famous Dina Bedriaga turned up one fine day with her hat trimmed with crow's feathers, saying that she had filched these feathers from Michel, who had brought them from Gavronzi, where I killed the crow in question.
