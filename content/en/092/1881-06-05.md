# Sunday, 5 June 1881 – 24 May

Yesterday I telegraphed Julian for news of Tony, and am anxious... When the telegram arrives, opening it will be... Ah! it would be a great loss for me.

%% 092.0095 %%
%% [#Censored_1887](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/CENSORED_1887.md) %%
%% Je suis toute la journée dehors et fais des études, il fait très beau. Je ne peux pas me figurer que cet homme si jeune encore puisse mourir... et pourtant il était très très changé depuis six mois. %%

I am out of doors all day doing studies; the weather is very fine. I cannot imagine that this man, still so young, could die... and yet he had changed very, very much these past six months.

%% 092.0096 %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%
%% Je retrouverai toujours Tony et son atelier, écrivais-je en partant, un instant j'ai hésité de l'écrire, et si je ne le retrouvais pas pensai-je. %%

"I shall always find Tony and his atelier again," I wrote on leaving — for a moment I hesitated to write it, and what if I did not find him again, I thought.

People will say that one only tells of "presentiments" after the event; the reason is that one dare not speak of them for fear of calling down misfortune... But when I heard of Tony's illness [words blacked out: it gave me] a strange feeling, not like the other times he was ill... and all the while I was travelling I kept telling myself that it is [word blacked out: afterwards.] that he will die, without believing it myself. Well, perhaps God will let us keep him. Speaking of presentiments, I never had a worse one than for this journey. It seemed to me that I should die of grief if I let Maman go, and that God knows what would happen to me if I went myself; hence those cruel hesitations. And the impression on arriving! Now it is all right. But Tony... Oh! if God were kind... You perhaps think that I talk of God or of feelings — never in my life. When I talk, I joke, and the more reason there is to be moved, the more I joke, so as to destroy even the appearance of a heart or a soul. So, with regard to Tony, all I allow myself to say is: Dash it all, I should be jolly annoyed if poor Tony... Or else I say that nobody is to speak to me of it.

%% 092.0098 %%
%% C’est maman qui m’ennuie toujours... La voilà qui fait de l’émotion sur Tony ! Ça ne regarde que moi, ça c’est comme quand elle parle peinture ou invente mes succès, dix hommes en mourraient si on pouvait être tué par les ridicules exagérations. La voilà qui dans un moment d’expansion dit que Julian me doit sa décoration, que c’est ma présence qui lui a porté bonheur. “Il me l’a dit”. Alors la présence des cent cinquante autres élèves ? Dame, ça ne s’explique pas, affaire de sentiments, sans parler des fleurs de Mme de Peronney, la voilà qui est allée embêter Tony sans que je le sache, en lui recommander et un tas de choses gauches, j’en suis sûre. Le talent de me créer des positions d’une fausseté !... Me couvrir de ridicule enfin et plus je la supplie de ne pas me désespérer plus elle s’en cache mais le fait tout de même. %%
%% 2026-09-28T21:01:18 RSR: Restored from tome14.docx ¶1886, Mon Journal t.14 p.145 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). printed «cinquantre» (Mon Journal t.14 p.145) corrected to «cinquante», a typesetting typo. %%
It is Maman who always vexes me... There she is, making a show of emotion over Tony! That is my business alone; it is like when she talks painting or invents my successes — ten men would die of it, if one could be killed by ridiculous exaggerations. There she is, in a moment of effusiveness, saying that Julian owes his decoration to me, that it was my presence that brought him luck. "He told me so." And what of the presence of the hundred and fifty other students? Well, these things cannot be explained, a matter of feeling — not to mention Mme de Peronney's flowers. And now it turns out she went and pestered Tony without my knowing, to recommend me to him, and a heap of clumsy things besides, I am sure of it. Her talent for putting me in the falsest of positions!... Covering me with ridicule, in short; and the more I beg her not to drive me to despair, the more she hides it, but she does it all the same.
