# Monday, 13 June 1881 – 1 June

I have begun a life-size peasant woman, standing, leaning against a fence woven of dry branches like a basket. Straw and planks have been laid on the ground to protect me from the damp, and a small portable pavilion of two rooms has been put up there, so that I am admirably installed.

Je crois qu’il y a eu quelques flirtations [Mots noircis: avec Michel] cet hiver car c’est évidemment à cause de son arrivée qu’elle a pris cet air aujourd’hui. %%

Maman, Paul, Nini, Papa, Michel, Dina and Sperandio stay there part of the day. Dina has dressed up as a peasant woman (her nose powdered); it suits her, but she has that inspired, defiant, lascivious air which disgusts me greatly. I believe there were some flirtations [words blacked out: with Michel] this winter, for it is obviously because of his arrival that she has put on that air today.

%% 092.0115 %%
%% Michel est d’un froid glacial du reste. C’est égal c’est dégoûtant cette expression de visage et de corps, il y a là je ne sais quoi qui me cause comme de la honte, une sorte de souffrance, d’inquiétude. Michel a apporté des fleurs et à dessein a oublié d’en offrir à Dina. Il m’a apporté de la toile pour peindre et est aimable avec moi infiniment plus qu’avec elle parce que je suis absolument indifférente peut-être. Michel, le chat, Nini ou n’importe quoi; avec tout le monde la même bienveillance indifférente et presque dédaigneuse. [Rayé : ce n’est pas une cause] %%

Michel, for that matter, is icily cold. All the same, that expression of face and body is disgusting; there is something in it that causes me a kind of shame, a sort of pain, of unease. Michel brought flowers and deliberately forgot to offer any to Dina. He brought me canvas to paint on and is infinitely more amiable with me than with her, perhaps because I am absolutely indifferent. Michel, the cat, Nini or anything else; with everyone the same indifferent and almost disdainful benevolence. [Crossed out: that is not a reason]
