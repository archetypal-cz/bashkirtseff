%% Monsieur Robert-Fleury a été très content de moi. Il est resté une demi-heure au moins devant une paire de pieds grandeur nature que j'ai fait, m'a redemandé si j'avais jamais peint, m'a dit que je voulais donc faire sérieusement de la peinture, combien de temps je pouvais rester à Paris, m'a exprimé le désir de voir mes premières choses à la couleur, m'a demandé comment j'avais fait. Je répondis que je l'ai fait pour m'amuser. Comme cela se prolongeait, tout le monde est venu se mettre derrière lui pour écouter et au milieu de là j'ose le dire, stupéfaction générale, il a déclaré que si j'en avais envie je pouvais peindre. A cela j'ai répondu que je n'en mourais pas d'envie et que je préférais me perfectionner en dessin, à laquelle résolution il applaudit beaucoup. J'ajoutais alors que je le priais de me traiter très sévèrement, ce qu'il me promit en disant qu'il le ferait d'autant plus que cela vaut la peine. %%
# Saturday, 29 December 1877
Monsieur Robert-Fleury was very pleased with me. He spent at least half an hour before a life-size pair of feet I had done; asked me again whether I had ever painted; said that I evidently wished to take painting seriously; asked how long I could stay in Paris; expressed a desire to see my first work in colour; asked how I had done it. I replied that I had done it for amusement. As this went on, everyone came and gathered behind him to listen, and in the midst of it all — I dare say it — to general amazement, he declared that if I wished I could paint. To which I replied that I was not dying to do so and preferred to perfect my drawing, a resolution he applauded warmly. I then added that I begged him to treat me very severely, which he promised to do, saying he would do so all the more since it was worth the trouble.

This evening at tea: the Comtesse de Fayet, the Baron d'Alt, and Monseigneur. For the time being La Fayet is an almost necessary evil. She has a dreadful manner; I felt affronted at having to endure her — but I hope to be rid of her now that the prince has been introduced and has called on us. At any rate, that makes one more gentleman to escort us. He is small, dark, fresh-complexioned, with a long black moustache, a large and mobile lower jaw, and a forehead that often furrows. Good manners, unassuming, not too stupid for the present, a calm and unremarkable air. He helped me make my chocolate and seems a decent enough fellow.

But good heavens, I forgot to tell you that Paul — my little brother Paul — arrived this morning at six o'clock. He is on excellent terms with his father and will leave again in a few days. He is plump, big; beside him I look like a little princess. At eighteen, a man — and an immense man — a proper gentleman, and this gentleman went to see "La Tzigane" at the Renaissance.
