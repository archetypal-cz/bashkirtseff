> I urgently beg you to send me news of your grandfather; his condition seemed alarming when I left Paris, but I hope with all my heart that there has been no fresh catastrophe and that these attacks have not recurred. I do not think I shall be able to come back to Paris now, as I had intended, my father being ill and my grandmother also rather unwell. It is hardly surprising, for we have suddenly had a severe cold spell; everyone has a cold and is coughing.

I am irritated; I have no time to do anything, life is so short... long enough if one uses all of it... but I should like to have nothing left to learn at twenty, so as to enjoy what I would have learnt, and, while enjoying it, to go on learning, but differently, without fatigue, the way one learns the small things that complete the great ones.

Poor women! What effort, what fever, just to know what every science student learns, what all men learn, or most of them, I mean the well-bred part. They send you to school and you learn it all quite naturally, whereas I, whereas we... We squander ourselves, we squander books, we know things but without order, and I am mad about order, symmetry, regular things... even if I then upset everything myself, turn it all upside down and do it over. I cannot express myself at all tonight.
