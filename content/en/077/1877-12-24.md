%% 2026-02-09T23:00:00 RSR: Christmas Eve at the Académie Julian. Marie sends a humorous telegram to her Swiss fellow students who are absent. "Les Suissesses" refers to her group of Swiss art students — including Louise-Catherine Breslau, Schaeppi, Zeller, and Forchhammer — who form her closest circle at the atelier. The telegram is a playful pastiche comparing the women's atelier to a stable ("écurie"). %%
%% # Lundi 24 décembre 1877 %%
%% Bons modèles, les Suissesses manquent et je leur télégraphie ces quelques mots assez caractéristiques: %%
# Monday, 24 December 1877
Good models on hand, but the Swiss girls are absent, so I telegraph them these rather characteristic words:

%% 2026-02-02T12:30:00 LAN: "écurie de femmes" - Marie's playful/mocking term for the atelier, comparing it to a stable %%
%% 2026-02-02T12:30:00 LAN: "stalles" - stalls (as in stable stalls), continuing the animal/stable metaphor %%
%% 2026-02-02T12:30:00 LAN: "Fruitière" - dairywoman/cheese-maker; Marie mocking a mother who wants her daughter to draw %%
%% 2026-02-02T12:30:00 LAN: "assassiner cadavre" - playful: "to murder the corpse" - likely refers to drawing a plaster cast or model %%
%% Pourquoi ne venez-vous plus écurie de femmes ? Beaucoup de stalles vides. De la paille, des ânes, des vaches. Fruitière (une maman qui veut que sa fille dessine) changé commerce, vend foin, avis aux amateurs. Je vous attends ce soir pour assassiner cadavre. %%
%% TR: Preserved the staccato telegram style — no punctuation between clauses, comic register throughout. "Fruitière" kept in French with parenthetical gloss in original. %%
Why do you no longer come, women's stable? Many empty stalls. Straw, donkeys, cows. Cheesewoman (a maman who wants her daughter to draw) changed trade, now sells hay, buyers take note. I await you this evening to murder the corpse.
