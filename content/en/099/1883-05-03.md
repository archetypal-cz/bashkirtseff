# Thursday, 3 May 1883

%% 099.0325 %%
%% Les deux frères doivent dîner demain, à moins que... %%
The two brothers are to dine here tomorrow — unless…

%% Je sens que je serai stupide, gênée, je ne saurai rien dire. Ah! c'est ennuyeux. Et puis inviter quelqu'un d'autre ou pas ? S'il n'y a qu'eux je ne saurai que faire et maman et ma tante seront si bêtes... Mais qui inviter ? Dusautoy jouera du piano pour qu'il y ait du bruit... I feel I shall be stupid, awkward — I shall not know what to say. Ah, it is tiresome. And then, should I invite someone else or not? If it is only them I shall not know what to do, and Maman and my aunt will be so foolish… But who to invite? Dusautoy can play the piano to provide some sound… Ah — one must leave everything to God's grace…

%% 2026-03-10T10:00:00 TR: "démoli" — demolished, incapacitated (slang for injured). "vente de charité" — charity sale (society fundraising). "chic" — fashionable, stylish. "petit consul" — minor consul (dismissive of low diplomatic rank). %%

Bojidar being incapacitated, I work on my portrait. And during the rest breaks I can tell you that… Mme Mackay was at the *vernissage* with Princess Massalsky, the wife of the Russian consul in Paris. They met at the charity sale, where I saw both their names printed. Mme Mackay offered cards for the *vernissage*; the little princess — who is neither particularly fashionable nor particularly aristocratic, and who is after all only the wife of a minor consul — accepted. It is not difficult to reconstruct.

All the same I should very much like to be seen walking with the aforesaid *consulesse* [sic], which would establish me far better than being seen with Mlle Canrobert. Maman will not understand that nothing will help until we have the protection of our own people. Mme Massalsky will have told Mackay that no one from our embassy comes to us. And people suspect the worst — for we cannot very well announce that we are merely country squires, moderately well-off and nothing more. We have set ourselves up here as great lords; it is evident that everyone wonders why we know neither the embassy nor any notable Russians…

%% 099.0330 %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%

%% 2026-03-10T10:00:00 TR: "poser à l'enfant" — play the innocent, pretend naivety. "chaque feuille est faite" — every leaf is individually rendered (art criticism: over-detailed background). "se collent aux figures" — stick to the figures (no atmospheric separation). %%
%% Décidément je sais poser à l'enfant... Mais c'est que son tableau à Bastien me fait de plus en plus réfléchir. C'est malheureux qu'il ne voit pas le tort que lui fait ce fond ou chaque feuille est faite. Ça manque d'air, positivement. Et les maisons dans le fond se collent aux figures. %%
I really can play the innocent… But the fact is that Bastien's painting makes me think more and more. It is unfortunate that he does not see the harm done by that background where every leaf is individually rendered. It positively lacks air. And the houses in the background cling to the figures.

%% 099.0331 %%
%% On a reproché l'incohérence du paysage de Jeanne d'Arc, ici c'est pire. Là c'était papillotant mais la nature l'est. Ici c'est lourd. Dagnan lui a dit que les figures s'enlevassent davantage. Il me l'a répété en ajoutant qu'il ne comprenait pas bien ce que Dagnan entendait par là et que dans un sujet comme le sien on pouvait pour ainsi dire se fiche de l'effet, (textuel) à quoi j'ai lâchement répondu qu'il ne s'en était pourtant pas fichu. Il a aussi la prétention d'avoir peint la tombée du jour. Je vous défie de me dire à quelle heure ça se passe. Le ciel est gris et blanc, le clocher est noir, le fond est d'un vert impitoyable et tout cela enlève de la consistance aux figures. Les foins, les pommes de terre, Jeanne d'Arc, le père Jacques même n'accusaient pas ce défaut à ce point. La Jeanne d'Arc m'est restée dans le souvenir comme une merveille et si on est surpris au premier abord par le papillotement du fond c'est tout simple, la nature même donne cette impression en plein jour. %%
%% Mais ici... Décidément ce garçon aurait besoin d'être guidé par moi... S'il voulait seulement avoir beaucoup d'amitié pour moi, tout en gardant la Mackay... Mais enfin pourquoi, pourquoi, pourquoi ? %%
%% Est-ce qu'on sait pourquoi l'on aime ou n'aime pas. Aussi je demande pourquoi par protestation contre l'incurie du ciel. Pourquoi est-ce un autre et pas celui-là. %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%
%% 2026-09-26T01:24:09 TR: Retranslated from Marie's French, restored from tome15 by RSR on 2026-09-26; supersedes the earlier English that had been invented from an RSR summary. "papillotant / papillotement" — flickering, a dazzle of small touches. "se fiche de l'effet (textuel)" — not care a hang about the effect, his very words. "lâchement" — cravenly (she flattered him instead of disagreeing). Bastien's pictures kept under their French titles in italics. %%
People found fault with the incoherence of the landscape in *Jeanne d'Arc*; here it is worse. There it flickered, but nature flickers. Here it is heavy. Dagnan told him the figures ought to stand out more. He repeated this to me, adding that he did not quite understand what Dagnan meant by it, and that in a subject like his one could, so to speak, not care a hang about the effect (his very words) — to which I cravenly replied that he had cared a good deal about it all the same. He also claims to have painted the fall of evening. I defy you to tell me at what hour it is happening. The sky is grey and white, the steeple is black, the background is a pitiless green, and all this takes the substance out of the figures. *Les Foins*, the *Pommes de terre*, *Jeanne d'Arc*, even *Père Jacques* did not show this fault to such a degree. *Jeanne d'Arc* has stayed in my memory as a marvel, and if one is surprised at first by the flicker of the background, that is quite simple: nature itself gives that impression in broad daylight.
But here… That young man clearly needs me to guide him… If only he would be very fond of me as a friend, while keeping Mackay… But after all, why, why, why?
Does one ever know why one loves or does not love? So I ask why, as a protest against the negligence of heaven. Why is it someone else, and not that one?

Et moi sans me fâcher je lui explique pourquoi tout cela est nécessaire comme si elle ne le savait pas et que cette affaire de deux sous me nui à Paris. Et si on m'avait écoutée, il y a des années que tout serait oublié. Non, je sens que même ceux qui me lisent ne croiront pas à tant de bêtise nuisible, à tant de niaiserie enfantine, à tant de lâcheté stupide ! %%

This evening I spoke to my aunt about her journey; she raises thirty-six silly objections. And I, without losing my temper, explain to her why it is all necessary — as if she did not know — and that this twopenny affair is doing me harm in Paris. She answers that these are inventions of mine, that nothing does any harm, and that she has no wish to go and stay out there for two years. Two years, that is to say two months. That is her way of putting things: when we are going to the theatre, say, and it is eight o'clock and I am not ready, my aunt sends word that it is ten minutes to eleven. So I send back word that if it is ten minutes to eleven it is too late, and I am going to bed. And I am not caricaturing — that is how it is, exactly, and about everything. In short, she will not go. Why?
— Because they may keep me there a long time.
Observe that she had begun by denying that the affair existed at all. "And how did it end, then, without your going?" I asked.
— I have no idea; I only know that they no longer summon me.
Since that innocent little trick did not succeed, she says they will keep her a long time. That is a confession. It is like having to deal with lunatics. These women are mad, or rather stupid. But what suffering!
— What does it matter if they keep you a long time?
— But I have not the slightest wish to languish out there, and besides, at the Consulate they know that it is an affair concerning my brothers, that I have nothing to do with it, and that…
— At the Consulate they know it, perhaps we know it, but everyone imagines it is still that same lawsuit, which prevents you from going back to Russia, and that you are a criminal. Come now, think — how can anyone believe you! So I am to be harmed like this, deliberately and for nothing?
— I shall not go.
— You will not go?
— No.
— Then I shall jump out of the window!
And I burst into nervous tears, noisy, magnificent, like everything that is true, sincere, irresistible.
The truth is, it is really beyond belief. And if I did not know everything as well as anyone, one would suspect dark abysses behind it. The same objections and the same puerile, childish, idiotic scenes were made to stop Maman from settling that stupid affair. It took her two months. And if they had listened to me, it would all have been forgotten years ago. No, I feel that even those who read me will not believe in so much harmful stupidity, so much childish silliness, so much stupid cowardice!
When they see me sobbing like a lost soul, my aunt cries out that very well, she will leave tomorrow, but that it will be the end of everything, that we shall never see her again, that she never wants to come back. As if I were doing her a mortal injury by sending her there. There must be people like that at Charcot's.
And now I wager they are telling each other that it was the bad weather that set my nerves on edge and that, luckily, by tomorrow I shall have forgotten all about it!!!
