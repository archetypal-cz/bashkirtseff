We are having a little republican-artistic-poetic dinner this evening. Gaillard and his wife, Clovis Hugues and his wife, Dusautoy — that Dusautoy was one too many, he is there only to gossip, and so is a journalist friend of the Gaillards whose name I do not know. Then there are Julian and Tony. I very much wanted to invite the brothers, and this morning I wrote a line to… the architect, explaining this *impromptu* dinner: would your brother care to come. If you bring him, I shall give you a painting! [words blacked out: …you] understand, for him to come it would take… he will not come; if he was bored last time — he came for his brother's sake, and once that service was rendered, he has had enough of us too.

No — but something happened, something *happened*! Ah, it is ravishing. When one does not expect it, while expecting it all the same, and it does not keep one waiting, and yet it is unexpected. He came. At half past seven. They both came. He came at last!

The Arlequin is run down, and Gaillart improvises verses on that masterpiece, which they call the work of a man of fashion — I do not understand how anyone can understand it so badly. Gentle Tony is positively delighted with my views on art. He says it less than it shows in his eyes and in the way he listens to me; besides, there was a moment when I suddenly noticed that everyone was listening to me in silence — I was talking in an undertone to Julian about this year's sensational Salon entries. Oh! I know very well how to sing my own praises in this journal — and with such modesty.
