# Monday, 1 September 1884
%% L'architecte apporte des perdreaux et emporte la promesse d'une visite au Bois. C'est une vraie partie, nous emportons tout ce qu'il faut pour faire du chocolat. Il en prend une tasse, puis nous faisons quelques pas, il marche tout seul. %%
%% 2026-09-29T06:53:05 RSR: Restored from tome16.docx ¶4049, Mon Journal t.16 p.295 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
The architect brings partridges and carries off the promise of a visit to the Bois. It is a real excursion: we take everything needed to make chocolate. He has a cup, then we take a few steps — he walks quite unaided.

%% 2026-03-10T12:00:00 TR: "Le Juif Hecht" = the Jew Hecht — Henri Hecht, art collector; Marie uses the antisemitic epithet commonplace in her social milieu. "potins" = gossip. "surprise mêlée de joie" = surprise mingled with pleasure — the irony that friends, having been told Bastien was nearly dead, are disconcerted to find him alive and out. %%
The drive back as yesterday — but with encounters that will give rise to gossip. The Jew Hecht,[^hecht] and the painters Béraud and Tissot.[^tissot] Since Bastien's imminent death had been announced, his friends show a surprise mingled with joy.

[^hecht]: Henri Hecht (1840–1891), prominent Paris art collector and patron. Marie uses an antisemitic formulation typical of her social world.

[^tissot]: Jean Béraud (1849–1935) and James Tissot (1836–1902), fashionable painters of Parisian life. Both were acquaintances of Bastien-Lepage.

%% 105.0729 %%
%% Et on va dire que nous le promenons. C'est vrai. Mais on en déduira des bêtises. %%
And people will say that we are taking him out for drives. It is true. But they will draw foolish conclusions.
