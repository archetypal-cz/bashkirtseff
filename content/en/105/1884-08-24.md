%% # Dimanche 24 août 1884 %%
%% Nous avons été ce matin à six heures et demie refaire une autre photographie et ce pauvre Emile me raconte enfin en détail ce que c'est que cette maladie. Inflammation des intestins et des reins. Le médecin dit qu'il *craint une tumeur.* On sait ce que cela veut dire. Ça ne peut pas se guérir. Du reste il est trop faible pour subir le traitement nécessaire. Et sans l'application de ce traitement il n'y a pas de salut. %%
%% 2026-03-10T12:00:00 TR: "*craint une tumeur*" = italicised in original — the doctor fears a tumour. The cancer diagnosis made by the previous entry is now confirmed in medical detail. %%
Sunday, 24 August 1884. This morning at half past six we went to take another photograph, and poor Emile finally tells me in detail what this illness is. Inflammation of the intestines and kidneys. On one side. And since it is in an inaccessible region... it has no bearing — that is to say, he can eat what he likes. The doctor says he *fears a tumour.* One knows what that means. It cannot be cured. Besides, he is too weak to undergo the necessary treatment. And without that treatment, there is no hope.

%% 105.0698 %%
%% — C'est un cercle vicieux, dit Emile. %%
— It is a vicious circle, says Emile.

%% 105.0699 %%
%% — Mais... Sapristi ! %%
— But... Good heavens!

%% 105.0700 %%
%% Oh ! il n'y pas de doute. %%
Oh! there is no doubt.

%% 105.0701 %%
%% Ce midi nous avons été à l'enterrement de Nittis, un peintre que je n'ai pas connu mais qui avait du talent. %%
%% 2026-03-10T12:00:00 TR: "Nittis" = Giuseppe De Nittis (1846–1884), Italian Impressionist painter who died in Paris on 21 August 1884. %%
At noon we went to the funeral of De Nittis,[^nittis] a painter I did not know personally but who had talent.

[^nittis]: Giuseppe De Nittis (1846–1884), Italian Impressionist painter based in Paris, celebrated for his street scenes. He died on 21 August 1884.

Then to the Bois, and on the way back these ladies wanted to go up to Bastien's — I remained in the carriage, and what do I see! Jules's head at the window, his two arms waving for me to come up. Emile comes to fetch me and I go up.

But all that... why?

The architect comes this evening to fetch the photograph for Jules, and Dina, who plays the ingénue as always, asks him whether the lady who came in to see Jules the other day was Mme Mackay.

General consternation.

— You could not have seen Mme Mackay at my brother's! cries the architect — is it not so, Madame, that you have never seen Mme Mackay at my brother's?

Whereupon Dina persisted in playing the innocent; I tried to change the subject by exchanging knowing looks with Emile... In short... It went...

And we know nothing at all, in the end...

All that, all that, all that... What is the point, what good is it? Disgust with everything and everywhere. If he dies, a few tears from the brain; if he lives, an indifferent friend. But I want nothing any more.
