%% 2026-09-28T20:55:11 RSR: Restored from tome04.docx ¶3937 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
In a word, this is how one ought always to be in this theatre. The Opera is an elegant theatre; the Français is quite enough for those who want to be free and easy — which Galula remarked as well, that little clerk, quite superb in his big white tie.

%% 2026-09-28T20:55:11 RSR: Restored from tome04.docx ¶3938 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
I am in blue — that adorable blue silk dress, like those one sees on enamels or on ivory, the dresses of the last century — and *my* hairstyle. Blue suits me very well, and so does the dress, the bodice at least. The skirt is made like a long skirt and is short, but at the theatre one cannot make out such niceties, especially when one does not show oneself too much.

%% 2026-09-28T20:55:11 RSR: Restored from tome04.docx ¶3941 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
Dina has a long black-and-white striped silk dress, well made; she is wearing it for the first time. My aunt is in white crêpe de Chine, violet velvet and Valenciennes lace; she is in the middle, and Dina and I take the side seats. In the first box is the Prodgers woman with the Vigier woman, both in red — the first in velvet, the second in silk and some other heavy stuff I cannot name. This fine lady has bared her bosom down to the waist — covered with lace, it is true, but looking no less indecent for that.

Belona is a handsome brunette, but the absence of rouge and her hairstyle (smooth hair and two plaits) make her plain. She has a fine mezzo-soprano voice, young, fresh and supple, but not trained enough: the notes are not joined, and the voice is not very strong, not brilliant, and makes no great effect.

If one listens carefully one hears that she sings in tune, that her notes are pure and that the voice comes out easily — but no power, no brilliance!
