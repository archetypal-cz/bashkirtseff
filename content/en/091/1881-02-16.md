# Wednesday, 16 February 1881

%% [#Citoyen_Bal](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/CITOYEN_BAL.md) %%
%% Je vais au Droit des femmes ce soir. On discute si l'on va participer à la fête de Victor Hugo. Sept contre, six prononcent oui. Les six citoyens ont débité des raisons immenses. Le citoyen Bal (l'homme à la tête invraisemblable) *tombe* Victor Hugo et déclare hautement qu'avec des loisirs et de l'argent il en aurait fait peut-être autant. %%

I go to the *Droit des femmes*[^1] this evening. They debate whether to participate in the Victor Hugo celebration. Seven against, six in favour. The six citizens delivered enormous speeches. Citizen Bal (the man with the improbable head) attacks Victor Hugo and declares loudly that with leisure and money he might perhaps have done as much.

Naturally Hubertine[^2] votes for participation — so do I. I speak little, a few phrases only. Then left alone with Hubertine, we speak of the matter. She is extremely moderate [Crossed out: I cannot understand it]. And she is always cited alongside Louise Michel in the *right-thinking* newspapers. What clichés and routine can do. Ah, what misery!

[^1]: *Le Droit des femmes*: the society Hubertine Auclert founded in 1876 (renamed *Le Suffrage des femmes* in 1883), which campaigned for women's political rights including the vote (en.wikipedia «Hubertine Auclert»). Marie attended its weekly meetings as «Pauline Orell» (8 December 1880).
[^2]: Hubertine Auclert (1848–1914), French suffragist, founder of *La Citoyenne* (1881).
