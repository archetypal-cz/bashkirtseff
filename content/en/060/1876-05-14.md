# Sunday, 14 May 1876

%% 060.0163 %%
%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%

Yesterday, as I was lamenting having missed the chance to meet Larderel, Antonelli said to me:

%% 060.0166 %%

%% A midi Antonelli est chez nous, il a l'air ou il me semble tout languissant et indécis, quant à moi je parle peu et suis bien contente d'aller déjeuner chez Spillmann. Et Dieu, quelle surprise, Larderel et Massa déjeunent à une table voisine. Larderel est si beau. Je n'ai rien pris qu'une tasse de bouillon mais je parlai tout le temps, tant pour ma tante et Antonelli que pour Larderel qui me regardait beaucoup. %%
At noon Antonelli is with us — he looks, or seems to me, quite languid and undecided; as for myself I speak little and am very glad to go and lunch at Spillmann's. And God — what a surprise! — Larderel and Massa are lunching at a neighbouring table. Larderel is so handsome. I took nothing but a cup of broth, but I talked the whole time — both for my aunt and Antonelli and for Larderel, who watched me a great deal.

%% 2026-02-10T12:30:00 LAN: "Torre di Quinto" - a flat plain north of Rome used for horse racing in the 1870s (before the Capannelle hippodrome opened in 1881). %%
%% 2026-02-10T12:30:00 LAN: "presser le pied" - foot-pressing under the carriage blanket; a physical intimacy that was scandalous but deniable. Marie's rationalization that follows is a remarkable piece of self-analysis. %%
%% 2026-02-10T12:30:00 LAN: "hebetee" - stupefied, dazed. "eclipse totale d'esprit" - total eclipse of mind -- vivid metaphor. %%
%% En passant nous prenons Potechine et allons dans la plaine de Torre di Quinto où sont les courses. Antonelli est en face de moi, je suis très hébétée, je ne sais comment faire et dire et finis par me laisser, le plus vulgairement du monde, presser le pied. Il ne manquait plus que ça. Mais je me suis dit ceci : quoiqu'il arrive, ce que je fais là n'est connu de personne et ne laisse ni marque ni dommage, la vie est si courte et si rarement belle qu'il faut en prendre ce qu'on peut. D'ailleurs je suis en ce moment abrutie et il y a éclipse totale d'esprit d'intelligence et de mémoire, et de fierté. Je ne m'accuse de rien une fois dans cet état, et ne demande rien de moi. %%
On the way we collect Potechine and drive to the plain of Torre di Quinto[^5] where the races are being held. Antonelli is opposite me — I am quite stupefied, I do not know what to do or say, and I end up allowing, in the most vulgar fashion imaginable, my foot to be pressed. That was all I needed! But I said this to myself: whatever happens, what I am doing here is known to no one and leaves neither mark nor harm; life is so short and so rarely beautiful that one must take from it what one can. Besides, I am at this moment dazed — there is a total eclipse of mind, of intelligence, of memory, and of pride. I accuse myself of nothing once in that state, and demand nothing of myself.

[^5]: Torre di Quinto: a flat plain north of Rome used for horse races in the 1870s, before the Capannelle racecourse opened in 1881.

%% Les tribunes sont presque vides. La princesse Marguerite[^6] est dans la sienne, habillée en blanc, d'ailleurs à présent c'est la mode. Le jockey de Larderel tombe et Larderel court le ramasser suivi d'une foule nombreuse et de Pietro* qui revient nous rendre compte de l'accident au moment où je vois Larderel conduisant lui-même son jockey par le bras. Cela m'a semblé si gentil que je suis folle de lui. Il a couru lui-même et, vraiment, en costume de jockey si ridicule et si peu coquet, il est cent fois mieux qu'habillé. %%
The grandstands are almost empty. Princess Margherita[^6] is in hers, dressed in white — the fashion at present. Larderel's jockey falls, and Larderel himself runs to pick him up, followed by a large crowd and by Pietro*, who comes back to report the accident to us at the very moment I see Larderel leading his own jockey by the arm. That struck me as so charming that I am mad about him. He ran himself, and truly, in such a ridiculous and unbecoming jockey's costume, he is a hundred times better than in ordinary clothes.

[^6]: Princess Margherita of Savoy (1851–1926), wife of Crown Prince Humbert; she became Queen of Italy in 1878.

%% Antonelli se plaignait d'être malade, j'offrais de le laisser partir, de le déposer où il voudrait mais il resta jusqu'à la fin, et alla avec nous au Pincio. Près de la Farnesina, tout Rome était stationné à pied et en voiture, car il faisait beau et tous ceux qui n'étaient pas allés aux courses craignant la pluie, vinrent voir le misérable retour. %%
Antonelli complained of being unwell — I offered to let him go, to drop him wherever he wished, but he stayed until the end, and came with us to the Pincio. Near the Farnesina, all Rome was gathered on foot and in carriages, for the weather had cleared and all those who had not gone to the races, fearing rain, came to watch the miserable procession returning.

My right foot was subjected to a gentle pressure the whole time, and my left rested on his right boot. Nothing was said, but from time to time the pressure grew stronger.

%% 060.0172 %%

%% De sorte qu'en sortant de voiture à sept heures du soir j'eus froid aux pieds lorsque cet appui et cette caresse muette me manquèrent. Il y avait foule au Corso et Bruschetti avec des regards de fou furieux voulant paraître digne et sévère. J'étais mécontente de nous promener ainsi avec Antonelli, on en parle assez sans cela. So that stepping out of the carriage at seven in the evening my feet felt cold when that support and that silent caress were gone. There was a crowd at the Corso and Bruschetti with the look of a madman trying to appear dignified and severe. I was displeased at our promenading about with Antonelli like this — people talk enough about it as it is. He was recounting all his adventures before the convent, and ended by saying that the convent had half stupefied him in seventeen days, and that had they kept him there as long again he would have stayed forever.

Torlonia has told him of his love for the Metchersky girl — from which I conclude that Antonelli has told Torlonia of his own love for me. Besides, I no longer concern myself with Torlonia. I like Larderel very much, and I am greatly irritated and humiliated by Antonelli, all the more so as I see he is far from contemptible — in position and all the rest. And besides, one must not let oneself be carried away into the clouds — an opportunity presents itself that may put an end to my torment, so I must seize it. Yes — seize it — but that is precisely what is impossible; besides, I understand nothing of it and I irritate and torment and enrage myself!

%% 060.0174 %%
%% Mon premier soin fut de regarder mes pieds, il n'en est pas comme des lèvres et ils ont changé de couleur, car c'étaient des bottines en cuir jaune. %%
My first concern was to look at my feet — they are not like lips and they had changed colour, for they were yellow leather boots.

%% [#Kernberger](../../_original/_glossary/culture/literature/KATHERINE_KERNBERGER.md) %%

I am dazed and on edge — I am looking for an occasion to explode at Antonelli, to tell him everything that has been suffocating me so long, and, depending on his answer, either to drive him away or to throw myself into his arms.

The boots have kept no traces — it was only mud picked up on the thoroughly rain-soaked racecourse.

I was so tired this evening that… no, wait — let me lean back on the sofa for a moment and dream; it is with great difficulty that I force myself to write this.

Good — here I am ready. I was so tired this evening that I had not the courage to do my hair and remained in the sitting room with it all down my back.

Simonetti came at nine bringing tickets for a performance of the Roman musical society, along with the compliments of Cavaliere Rossi. I conversed and made tea as best I could until half past ten, when Antonelli arrived — for the first time, doubtless owing to the summer season, not in a tailcoat.

%% 2026-03-05T10:00:00 TR: "la fameuse boîte blanche" — "the famous white box," the box in which Marie keeps her diary, per LAN note. %%
Simonetti left soon after, and we three remained. We spoke of my journal — that is to say, of the questions I treat in it — and Antonelli asked me to read him something, I no longer remember what, about the soul or about God. I went into the hall and knelt down beside the famous white box and began to look for the passage in question while Pietro held the candle. But as I searched and kept coming upon passages of general interest, I would read them aloud laughing, and this lasted almost half an hour; for a moment I felt his breath in my hair, as I was reading with my head bent down. And far from moving away, I did not stir — nor did he.

Back in the sitting room, he began recounting all manner of anecdotes from the age of eighteen. How, as a soldier, wrapped in a great cloak, he would go off to "lord it over" a cobblers' dance hall. One evening, having singled out a pretty woman, he ordered her to dance with no one but him; the cobblers became furious and resolved to beat him and his companions the following day. The next day he did not go, and his companions on arriving were set upon with terrible blows by the charming cobblers, who at their entrance had knocked over all the lamps.

Il s'arrangea avec la fille qui lui laissait la sortie libre. %% 2026-03-05T10:00:00 TR: "la révolution de 70" — the seizure of Rome by Italian troops, 20 September 1870, ending the Pope's temporal power. %%
At the time of the revolution of 1870,[^7] his father took the whole family off to Monaco and Nice for three months to avoid witnessing the royal outrages. It was there that Master Pietro began to live, and as he was shut up in a little room, he discovered one day, while amusing himself by tapping his cane against the walls, a secret door giving into the room of his mother's chambermaid. He came to an arrangement with the girl, who allowed him free passage. But his brothers, who had always been spiteful and against him, tried to make his life wretched — he escaped only by threatening to report their debts to Papa.

[^7]: The capture of Rome by Italian forces on 20 September 1870 ended the Pope's temporal power. The Antonelli family, deeply bound to the Vatican, regarded this as a calamity.

At first he was very clerical and attended all demonstrations with great ardour. One day the clericals announced with great pomp that a *Te Deum* was to be sung at the church of Saint John Lateran, and the liberals duly surrounded the church to hiss them as they came out.

A general brawl ensued, and the young cockerel was carried off to the Austrian embassy, whence he was sent home with a thousand kindnesses and attentions to the ==family home==[^10]. His father was furious, and when Pietro presented himself before the Cardinal, the Cardinal scolded him for this excess of zeal — so that the young man, furious in turn, went straight off to join his regiment, to the great scandal of everyone.
