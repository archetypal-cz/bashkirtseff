The tip of my nose hurts; nothing shows yet, but the pain puts me in a bad temper. We arrive at Saint-Jean, a very pretty spot, and go round the bay by boat. I *chat* mostly with Saëtone, and Audiffret seeks me out only half-heartedly, so that I am piqued. We dine badly, and at dinner he again places himself beside me on my left; on my right I have Saëtone.

I like to hear Audiffret talk; in spite of his southern accent his speech is very pleasant.

%% 2026-09-29T01:28:41 RSR: Restored from tome05.docx ¶2018 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
By moonlight we go out onto the terrace facing the inn. Girofla and I share a philopena, the forfeit to be at the winner's discretion,[^44] and we promise each other the greatest indiscretions in the world. As for me, I know what I would make him do — what I have sworn to make him do: crawl on all fours from our house to the baths, and there jump off the pier fully dressed. I don't tell him. Of course not!

I sing, and sing well. Saëtone howls; Audiffret does not look cheerful — he sings a little, but half-heartedly; he is not as he was the other time. He plays with Olga, who is as free with him as a child would be. They call each other neither Monsieur nor Mademoiselle, but simply Girofla and Giroflé. It is very sweet to see. There is a great deal of laughter — I laugh too, but not with a full heart.

[^44]: *A philopena (French* philippine*) was a popular forfeit game: two people who share a double kernel (nut or almond) each eat one, and whoever first greets the other at their next meeting with the agreed formula (in France, "Bonjour Philippe!" / "Bonjour Philippine!") wins a forfeit. "À discrétion" means the loser must do whatever the winner demands.*

[^45]: *Mock-liturgical Latin: "Deliver me, O Lord, from the Collignon!" — a parody of the* Libera me, Domine *of the Office of the Dead, with Collignon's name given a Latin ending. The "[sic]" is the editors'.*
