# Tuesday, 25 June 1878

%% 081.0029 %%
%% [#Paris](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/PARIS.md) [#Paul_de_Cassagnac](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/PAUL_DE_CASSAGNAC.md) [#Laferriere](../../_original/_glossary/culture/fashion/LAFERRIERE.md) %%

%% J'ai passé chez Laferrière, elle croit que c'est demain à la mairie et jeudi à l'église. Ainsi ce n'est même pas samedi. J'espérais avoir le temps de respirer. Ainsi tout va être fini ? Ce qui me console... au fait je ne sais si cela me console ou me désole, c'est que si j'avais pensé à la possibilité de me marier avec Cassagnac j'aurais pu le faire. Je le prenais tout à fait au sérieux et puis je croyais qu'il coûtait plus cher. Et surtout je le voyais toujours entre cinq ou six femmes plus la Suédoise comme permanence, et je pensais que pour se marier il lui faudrait aimer et je n'osais pas désirer cela, c'est un sujet si délicat. %%
I called at Laferrière's; she believes it is tomorrow at the town hall and Thursday at the church. So it is not even Saturday. I had hoped to have time to breathe. And so it will all be over? What consoles me... though in truth I do not know whether it consoles or distresses me — is that had I considered the possibility of marrying Cassagnac, I could have done so. I took him quite seriously, and besides, I believed he cost more. And above all I always saw him surrounded by five or six women plus the Swede as a fixture, and I thought that to marry he would need to be in love — and I dared not desire that; it is such a delicate matter.

*I thought him expensive from the moment I ceased to believe he was not for sale.* No — I never thought him for sale, never thought it because I thought something else entirely of him. And then when he questioned me about my allowance, and then about my dowry:

They say the Acard girl is the Cardinal's daughter — whether she is or not, the connection exists, and Blanc had pronounced her a blot.

%% 081.0039 %%
%% Quelle immense désillusion ! Moi qui pensais que Paul de Cassagnac était différent des autres ! J'avais bien découvert qu'il mentait dans les petites choses et qu'il donnait le plus solenellement du monde des paroles d'honneur qui auraient fait crever d'envie Athos lui-même, et puis qu'il y manquait. %% 2026-10-02T19:16:43 RSR: Split (paragraph wave): print paragraph 4 of 8 of 081.0036, which held 8 printed paragraphs under one ID (Mon Journal t.12; tome12.docx; split points checked by the survey on the docx and the scan indentation). %%
What an immense disillusionment! I who had thought Paul de Cassagnac different from the rest! I had already discovered that he lied in small matters, and that he gave, with the utmost solemnity, words of honour that would have made Athos himself die of envy — and then broke them. The old father must be furious if the bride has only twenty thousand a year; I offer him seventy thousand, and then twice as much.

%% 081.0040 %%
%% J'ai craint de pleurer, crainte vaine... %%

I was afraid of crying — a groundless fear...

It is oppressively hot! When one is working, it is dreadful.

%% 081.0043 %%
%% Seulement... cela vaut peut-être mieux. Mais c'est bête de n'y avoir pas songé avant. En y mêlant une vieille femme et[en] se conduisant comme une fille qui offre son association j'aurais pu le faire aussi facilement que commander une robe chez Laferrière. Quand au reste je n'ai pas d'inquiétudes trop vives, le mariage n'y change pas grande chose, seulement je ris en pensant que je ne suis pas la seule... Oh ! mais moi quoique je fasse contre ou pour c'est écrit. Je n'y tiendrai peut-être plus... je crois que si, après tout c'est un homme qui mérite qu'on se pique au jeu, je commencerai par acquérir une certaine célébrité puis une belle position peut-être, encore dernièrement une tireuse de cartes me l'a prédit comme les autres. Edmond m'a annoncé un matamore comme ennemi. Ce sera lui qui sera furieux pour la façon dont je traite sa belle. %% 2026-10-02T19:16:43 RSR: Split (paragraph wave): print paragraph 8 of 8 of 081.0036, which held 8 printed paragraphs under one ID (Mon Journal t.12; tome12.docx; split points checked by the survey on the docx and the scan indentation). %%
Only... perhaps it is all for the best. But how stupid of me not to have thought of it sooner. By involving an older woman and conducting oneself as a girl who offers her partnership, I could have done it as easily as ordering a dress from Laferrière. As for the rest, I have no very acute anxieties; marriage changes little in that regard, and I only laugh to think I am not the only one... Oh! but whatever I do, for or against — it is written. Perhaps I shall stop caring... I think not, though; after all, he is a man worth staking one's pride on. I shall begin by acquiring a certain celebrity, then perhaps a fine position — only lately a fortune-teller foretold it once more, like all the others. Edmond predicted I would have a swaggerer for an enemy. It will be he who is furious at the way I treat his fair one. By the way — you know I wrote to the *Figaro* about a masked ball at the Trocadéro; the thing was printed.

Une fois revenue et peut-être avant elle s'intéressera, voudra savoir qui a été si attentif et fidèle et alors on s'arrange pour le mieux. %%
All the same — I am a Bonapartist, because it may lead me to a certain position, always interesting when one is in opposition, and if the Empire returns and things take their natural course, thanks to an idea I am starting on. It is simply this: every day the Empress Eugénie receives her bouquet of violets, anonymously.[^1] Once she is back — and perhaps before — she will take an interest, will wish to know who has been so attentive and faithful, and then one arranges things to the best advantage.

[^1]: Violets were the emblem of the Bonapartists; sending them anonymously to the Empress in exile was a gesture of covert dynastic allegiance.

You know that the Prince Imperial is to marry the third daughter of the King of Denmark. What a fortunate king! His second son is king of Greece; his three daughters will be empresses of Russia, England, and perhaps of the French. There is no denying it: this alliance is a good thing.
