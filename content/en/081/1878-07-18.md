Madame de Hohenlohe has departed — only the two young men remain, who dined beside me at the *table d'hôte* today. They occupy the ground floor of the right pavilion, we the first floor. Their balcony is below ours. I sing and they play the piano. According to the servants' gossip, they find me pretty and well made. They always fling my behind in my face! I who know so many things cannot understand why this particular wealth is so prized.

In Germany one sees many that are broad and flat — I have never seen one so prominent as mine, which is a perfection... of its kind. After painting I dressed and joined the company... (apropos of Madame Abaza... there is in Frankfurt a certain Monsieur Kirchenstein) before setting off we stood chatting in front of the house, so that the two princes had time to take a good look at me... They must think me very pedantic, without my noticing it. At table I spoke with Dr Tomachewsky of scientific matters; then Madame Antonsky and the others spoke of professors, of Madame Abaza who sings; they asked what lessons cost, and it was said that my anatomy professor was paid so much and another so much, etc.

%% 2026-10-01T17:17:16 RSR: OCR garble «m^ij?» corrected to «mais» (Mon Journal t.12 p.53, scan Tome12.pdf; tome12.docx ¶617 carries the garble «m^ij?», the print reads «mais c'est la vérité»). %%
I have made myself a garnet velvet bonnet like the black one I had in Paris. I wear it only indoors; it is difficult to go out walking in a red hat, although it suits me to perfection; I look like a child, and it is a superb colour. I should like to have my portrait done in it, but that is impossible... I may often be merely passable or pretty, but there are moments, days, when this bonnet, a hat, a veil, hair waving and escaping in disordered curls... knotted on top of the head, livelier eyes, a happier smile... in this way I sometimes manage to call myself *ravishing*; but to believe it one must live with me and observe me close at hand. The bourgeois who admire me do not suspect what I myself admire; they see only features, a complexion, a figure. My features drive the whole atelier mad, Julian at the head. It seems my features are capricious and picturesque... Greuze. Well, there are poses in which I become absolutely classical if a band is put across my forehead. It seems incredible, but it is the truth; I have made sure of it with mirrors and with sketches done at the atelier. I am speaking for future generations; forgive me if this is read sooner. Inquire of those who have known me otherwise than from seeing me on the promenade or at a ball. I repeat, the head is for the fanciful, who will find endless things to admire in it: the colouring, the mobility; it is fifty heads in one.
