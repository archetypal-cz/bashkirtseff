# Monday, 15 July 1878

%% Toute la société va à Francfort. Edouard Strauss joue au jardin zoologique. J'y vais aussi pour voir la fameuse Ariâne. Elle est dans une espèce de chapelle qui est éclairée en rose. C'est superbe... comme moi. %%
The whole of society goes to Frankfurt. Edouard Strauss is playing in the zoological gardens. I go as well, to see the famous Ariadne.[^1] She stands in a kind of chapel, lit in rose. It is magnificent... like me.

[^1]: The celebrated Ariadne on the Panther sculpture by Johann Heinrich Dannecker (1814), displayed in the Bethmann Museum in Frankfurt. The pinkish rose light was the traditional way the piece was displayed to enhance its marble translucency.

%% Le petit Dourassoff qui comme je crois l'avoir dit a vingt et un ans à peine et timide comme la timidité même, commence à parler un petit peu. Pourtant la société qu'il fréquente le plus jusqu'à présent est celle du petit Dimitri Rembelinsky âgé de neuf ans. Un type que ce Dourassoff (il est le neveu de Mme loukowkine, de Rome) il m'a offert une rose, mais il ne voit pas, je parie, de différence entre moi et la vieille Antonsky. %%
%% 2026-10-01T07:33:04 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
Young Dourassoff — who, as I believe I have mentioned, is barely twenty-one and shy as shyness itself — is beginning to speak a little. Yet the company he frequents most, thus far, is that of little Dimitri Rembelinsky, aged nine. A character, this Dourassoff (he is the nephew of Madame Loukowkine, of Rome); he offered me a rose, but I wager he sees no difference between me and old Madame Antonsky.

%% 2026-10-01T07:33:04 TR: RESOLVED SOURCE CHANGED (2026-09-28 rebuild) — English re-aligned to the rebuilt French. %%
Having nothing better to do at meals and at dinner, I try to guess why this lady lives abroad... She does not love her husband, who has another family, but she must love someone else... She is perhaps like Maman... it is admirable, but it is very tiresome [words blacked out: for those around her. One becomes fretful], one clings to petty things, in short one grows disagreeable and difficult in the dealings of daily life. Do you suppose that if Maman had some human interest in her heart, do you suppose she would make a point of tormenting me about the way my maid hangs my clothes in the wardrobes or arranges my bedside table — and that several times a day — or of tidying my dressing table or my desk, when I beg her ten times a day, every time she touches them, to leave things as they are, because I arrange them after my own notion and know where to find everything?

Well, with her it has become a mania, and what would you say if I told you that she insists on emptying every vessel she comes across in any room whatever: basin, jug, etc. etc. When I see that I do not know what comes over me; I very nearly swoon.
