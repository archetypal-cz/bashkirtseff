[Monday, 24 June 1878] *Your son is marrying a person who is no longer quite young, who is nearly penniless, and whom he does not love — while he need only say one word to marry someone young, pretty, well-born, and suitable in every way, with 70,000 francs a year from the very day of his marriage and nearly as much thereafter. We speak to you too late, but we could not do so sooner. You will understand that one wishes to remain anonymous at the risk of appearing suspect. Matters are very far advanced; but I implore you in the name of what you hold most dear to reflect before concluding this deplorable folly that your son is committing, which is truly not calculated to secure his happiness or serve his ambition. Once more, Monsieur, I appeal to your reason, your heart, and your charity [Crossed out: for all those who suffer]. Ask your son — who knows that this young lady exists, should you doubt it, though he himself has only known since yesterday. But all is not yet lost. Read the enclosed letter and see that it reaches him. I entrust it to your honour.*

[Monday, 24 June 1878] *When one takes a resolution of which one does not oneself approve, one will hear nothing against it — because one is afraid of listening to things that accord so well with one's own feelings.*

Je n'ai pas besoin de vous dire qu'une femme qui ne demande qu'à tout vous sacrifier et qui ne vous regarde pas comme un moyen de parvenir parce qu'elle n'en a pas besoin et parce qu'elle en a cinquante autres, est prête à un mot de vous à venir se mettre à vos pieds dévouée comme un chien, soumise comme une femme qui aime quelle que soit votre situation ou votre avenir. *One would have gone so far as to write dreadful things to the young lady, but she will not let you go for anything in the world. Not even if she were told that you detest her and have just taken a new mistress whom you adore. It is not you she wants — it is a husband who is to compensate her for the long obscurity of an unmarried daughter, a husband who will carry her somehow or other into that society which she now affects to disdain. I need not tell you that a woman who asks only to sacrifice everything for you, who does not regard you as a means of getting on — because she has no need of one and has fifty others — is ready at a word from you to come and place herself at your feet, devoted as a dog, submissive as a woman who loves, whatever your situation or your future. You know well that a truly superior creature will always be thrifty, a good housekeeper, a faithful friend — above all when she has a good heart. Not a heart that melts in a drawing room over some mishap, but one that will be open to you alone.*

*The young lady has, of course, already ordered gowns from Laferrière's (on such a dowry!) — you would do well to call there; I mean at the fitting rooms; but those gowns will serve to charm some elderly naval officer or some notary who will hasten to offer his heart, his frigate, or his practice, dazzled by the luster you will have lent her. Which will be very nice. But you! Such bourgeois mediocrity is not for you! Come, come — have some shame! Why are you in despair? Thank God you have a future. Parents are always anxious, too cautious, with too little faith in their children. A tender mother entangled in the machinations of a young woman eager to find a husband will stop at nothing to get her son married, so that he may have no more adventures and duels. And yes — a father wants the same, judging by his own experience, not realizing how very different you are from other men, and then... he is not certain of events, it will perhaps be harder later on, he thinks?*

La femme voudra des robes et vous serez dans la nécessité de voir quelques amis, de recevoir, ce dont vous pouviez vous passer étant garçon. *What anguish at each hour that brings your end nearer! Do you renounce everything, then? Do not deceive yourself! She will want gowns, and you will be obliged to see a few friends, to entertain — things you could dispense with as a bachelor. And what is more atrocious than financial wrangling, above all when there is not even love? You will convince nobody that you are in love with that determined manner of the common Parisienne and those faded cheeks. As for wit! Let us speak of it. You are not stupid enough to delude yourself. Certainly she does not resemble all young girls, no. I realize too late that it is shameful to run down someone who is not worth the trouble, but one is so afraid, one desires so, so, so much that you should come back to yourself!*

%% 2026-03-08T10:00:00 TR: Latin "quaerens quem devoret" kept in Latin with footnote per project guidelines. %%
*How one would make those bourgeois traits disappear — all those childish turns of phrase you would not forgive in yourself, constrained as you are by a young lady who for at least ten years has studied every form of dissimulation... ==*quaerens quem devoret*==.[^1] If she has been mad it is you who made her so. I beg you, there is still time! Do not indulge in false generosity — if you have scruples, it can be arranged that she was the one to break it off, which will place her on a* pedestal and you will have lost nothing. Set off on a journey — oh, there are so many means if only you would! There is still time; it is never too late to do the right thing. How miserable you will be if regret comes too late!

[^1]: *quaerens quem devoret* — Latin: "seeking whom to devour" (1 Peter 5:8, of the devil). Marie quotes it of her rival.

*Your own family would be glad to see you do something better. Come now, one noble impulse... What can one say to persuade you! I call God to my aid. God who reads hearts will forgive the one that beats so sincerely for you for wishing to take you from another woman who perhaps loves you (why not?) But in such matters an angel would be selfish.*

*Do not leave yourself with burning regrets. Say that you have read the letters and that they change nothing because you love her... if you love her? But do not let it be thought that it is too late and that you lacked the energy to brave so much.*
