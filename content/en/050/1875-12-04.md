# Saturday, 4 December 1875

%% 050.0467 %%
%% [#Emile_d_Audiffret](../../_original/_glossary/people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md) [#Maman](../../_original/_glossary/people/family/MAMAN.md) [#Monaco](../../_original/_glossary/places/cities/MONACO.md) [#Maurice_Gros](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/MAURICE_GROS.md) %%
%% Hier, à la gare, Audiffret s'est approché de maman, qui allait à Monaco. Il avait l'air le plus canaille du monde avec son long paletot jaune dégrafé et flottant, en société de Gros et de Mayer l'ex-banquier. %%
Yesterday at the station, Audiffret approached Maman, who was going to Monaco. He had the most disreputable look in the world, with his long yellow overcoat unbuttoned and flapping, in the company of Gros and Mayer, the former banker.

%% 050.0468 %%
%% [#Love](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/LOVE.md) %%
%% [#Emile_d_Audiffret](../../_original/_glossary/people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md) [#Maurice_Gros](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/MAURICE_GROS.md) %%
%% Il faut être moi pour avoir pu m'humilier jusqu'à m'occuper d'un Audiffret. Et je suis bien honteuse, mais là véritablement honteuse et confuse. Un paysan niçois qui s'encanaille avec des Gros et Cie fi ! Tant qu'il se conduisait bien, ça allait encore. Mais à présent, le théâtre, les chanteuses, les cocottes et Gros. Il en est tout sale. Vous ne croirez peut-être pas, mais je le dirai tout de même. Non seulement je ne l'aime pas, mais je ne m'en occupe même plus, je le trouve paysan, canaille, insignifiant. J'allais dire dégradant, mais c'est trop fort, cela ferait croire qu'il m'est quelque chose. Je ne veux plus le cribler d'injures, il ne le mérite pas. Un petit crevé niçois. Ecco. %%
One would have to be me to have humiliated oneself so far as to take an interest in an Audiffret. And I am truly ashamed — truly ashamed and mortified. A Nice peasant who goes slumming with the likes of Gros and company — fie! So long as he conducted himself well, it was still all right. But now — the theatre, the singers, the cocottes, and Gros. He is entirely soiled by it. You may not believe me, but I shall say it all the same: not only do I not love him — I no longer even occupy myself with him; I find him a peasant, a scoundrel, a nonentity. I was going to say "degrading" — but that is too strong; it would imply he means something to me. I no longer wish to shower him with insults — he does not deserve them. A little Nice fop. ==There you are.==[^ecco]
%% 2026-03-05T11:00:00 TR: "s'encanaille" — to lower oneself by associating with vulgar company. "crève niçois" — 1870s: affected, pretentious young dandy from Nice. *Ecco* — Italian: there you have it; code-switching for emphasis. %%

I wept at Maman's — and at luncheon I announced that I am going to study singing and become an actress. And I shall do it — if God is good enough to preserve my voice I shall do it. It is the only means for me to acquire the fame I thirst for — for which I would give ten years of my life without hesitation. I cannot rot here!

We went to the Opera to get a box for this evening — they are giving *The Barber*,[^barb] my little favourite opera. I am in a golden cloud. I aspire to something unheard-of, fabulous — I want to reveal myself to the world. I want to be famous. I shall sing.

The general laughs, Dina laughs, my aunt laughs. Every minute *père* changes the position of his hand — I imitate him like the most faithful mirror. The curtain falls; I stop a moment before leaving — *père* does the same, looks at me, and removes his hat.
