# Friday, 4 June 1875

%% 033.0490 %%
%% [#Sabatini](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/SABATINI.md) [#Emile_d_Audiffret](../../_original/_glossary/people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md) %%

At the baths with Sabatini; at the door of the cabin Audiffer — in white like me — comes to greet me.

%% Il avait mis son cheval au galop pour arriver le premier et arriva en effet le premier mais nous avons passé tout droit de sorte que tout en traversant la Promenade il se retournait et regardait et s'arrêtait. Ce ne fut qu'en retournant que nous descendîmes. %%
He had set his horse at a gallop to arrive first, and did indeed arrive first — but we drove straight past, so that as he crossed the Promenade he kept turning back to look and stopping. It was only on our return that we got down.

Much laughter on account of Prater, whom Girofla calls Girofla. Girofla promises to find me a horse for the cavalcade we are organising on Monday. He asks where we go every day after leaving Rumpelmayer's; I say: to the London House.

I go home with the Sapogenikoffs and Dina — Collignon and Pâris go to the London House and find the man there.

%% That is the question. Collignon dit que hier, a thé, lorsque maman m'appela petchouna, il a dit à demi-voix: mio bella petchouna. - J'étais étonnée et je l'ai regardé.- ajouta-t-elle. %%
==That is the question.==[^1] Collignon says that yesterday at tea, when Maman called me *petchouna*, he said under his breath: ==*mio bella petchouna*==[^2] — I was astonished and looked at him — she added.

[^1]: *That is the question*: In English in the original.
[^2]: *mio bella petchouna*: Mixed Italian and Russian — "my beautiful [little sweetness]" (*petchouna* / *печенька* is a Russian endearment, literally "little cookie"). Grammatically incorrect Italian (*mio* should be *mia* for feminine). In Italian/Russian in the original.
