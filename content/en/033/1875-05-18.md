# Tuesday, 18 May 1875

%% 033.0019 %%
%% [#Collignon](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/COLLIGNON.md) %%
%% [#Appearance](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/APPEARANCE.md) %%
%% On parlait de nos figures et Collignon dit que, si elle ne me connaissait pas, elle dirait de moi - Voilà une belle fille, car vous etes grande et bien faite - ajouta-t-elle. Elle a encore dit que j'avais une jolie bouche, des mains blanches, de jolis sourcils, un joli nez, de jolies oreilles. %%
We were speaking of our faces and Collignon said that if she did not know me, she would say of me — There is a beautiful girl, for you are tall and well made — she added. She also said that I had a pretty mouth, white hands, pretty eyebrows, a pretty nose, pretty ears.
%% 2026-03-02T10:00:00 TR: "figures" — faces/features (period usage, not English "figures"). "mains blanches" — white hands as marker of gentility, not manual work. %%

%% 033.0020 %%
%% [#Collignon](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/COLLIGNON.md) %%
%% Je suis contente, car cette personne juge droitement et exactement et n'exagère et ne ment jamais. %%
I am pleased, for this person judges honestly and precisely and never exaggerates or lies.

%% 033.0021 %%
%% [#Appearance](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/APPEARANCE.md) %%
%% Ce fut dit parce que nous étions ên train de nous dire franchement nos figures, et de nous juger. %%
It came about because we were in the midst of frankly telling one another what we thought of our faces, and judging each other.

%% 033.0022 %%
%% [#Appearance](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/APPEARANCE.md) %%
%% Je le sais bien que je suis grande et belle et c'est ce qui me fait murmurer. %%
I know perfectly well that I am tall and beautiful — and that is precisely what makes me complain.

%% 033.0023 %%
%% [#Ambition](../../_original/_glossary/culture/themes/AMBITION.md) %%
%% Tant de plus vilaines et qui sont heureuses. Mais qui sait, peut-être suis-je réservée à de grandes choses. J'en suis digne par l'audace seule, de les attendre et espérer... %%
So many plainer women who are happy. But who knows — perhaps I am destined for great things. I am worthy of them by the sheer audacity of awaiting and hoping for them...

%% 033.0024 %%
%% Les prêtres seront contents le jour où je me débarrasserai de mes chers parents ! Je ferai partout dire des messes. %%
The priests will be delighted the day I am rid of my dear parents! I shall have masses said everywhere.
%% 2026-03-02T10:00:00 TR: "me débarrasserai de mes chers parents" — ironic: become independent of her family (not wishing them harm — she means when she escapes their control). "dire des messes" — have masses said (charitable donation to the Church). %%

%% [#Sapogenikoff](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/SAPOGENIKOFF.md) [#Nina](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/NINA.md) [#Marie_Sapogenikoff](../../_original/_glossary/people/mentioned/MARIE_SAPOGENIKOFF.md) [#Ma_tante](../../_original/_glossary/people/family/MA_TANTE.md) [#Maman](../../_original/_glossary/people/family/MAMAN.md) %%
%% Voilà ce qui s'est passé aujourd'hui. A cinq heures j'étais habillée et en chapeau (robe blanche, chapeau Watteau très bien) et prête pour aller au bain comme c'était convenu hier. Maman rentre et dit que les Sapogenikoff ne vont pas, je m'étonne, elle dit que Madame craint les vagues et le vent d'aujourd'hui. Je flaire quelque chose qui ne paraît pas et vais de suite chez les Sapogenikoff. Nina qui ne sait ni se tenir ni parler laisse de suite voir que ce ne sont ni les vagues ni le vent. J'ouvre de grands yeux, enfin elle permet à ses filles d'aller et en montant Marie me dit une chose qui met tout mon sang en ébullition, c'est que c'est ma tante qui aurait prié Nina de défendre à ses filles d'aller afin que je ne me baigne pas non plus. Ceci me parut tellement abominable que j'ai de suite dit, tant pour me soulager que pour me défendre, que l'on veut me poser en fille récalcitrante, en monstre moral, en enfant désordonnée, désobéissante, capricieuse, volontaire, et qui donc ! Ma propre tante. Et qu'on aille reprocher aux autres les calomnies quand on en est soi-même la source. Il n'y a rien à dire, il paraît que je vais un de ses jours étrangler quelqu'un. %%
%% 2026-09-29T01:28:41 RSR: Restored from tome05.docx ¶1124 (missing from original extraction, 2026-09-28 rebuild). %%
Here is what happened today. At five o'clock I was dressed and in my hat (white dress, a Watteau hat, very becoming) and ready to go to the baths, as had been agreed yesterday. Maman comes in and says the Sapogenikoffs are not going; I am astonished; she says Madame is afraid of today's waves and wind. I scent something behind this and go straight to the Sapogenikoffs'. Nina, who knows neither how to behave nor what to say, at once lets it be seen that it is neither the waves nor the wind. I open my eyes wide; at last she lets her daughters go, and on the way up Marie tells me something that sets all my blood boiling: that it was my aunt who had supposedly asked Nina to forbid her daughters to go, so that I should not bathe either. This seemed to me so abominable that I said at once, as much to relieve my feelings as to defend myself, that they want to make me out a recalcitrant girl, a moral monster, a disorderly, disobedient, capricious, wilful child — and who does it? My own aunt. And then to go reproaching others for slander when one is its very source oneself! There is nothing to be said; it seems I am going to strangle somebody one of these days.

So Dina told her that this evening she would come for certain, and indeed she came.
