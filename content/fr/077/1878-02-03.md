Household chaos: Pierre has a sprained foot, Triphon is drunk, only Chocolat (a servant) is functional, and grandfather needs constant attention. During tête-à-tête, the famous "sale" dialogue: Cassagnac confesses he wanted to kiss her during the masked visit. Marie says kissing "would be... sale" (dirty). Cassagnac replies tenderly that she's safe as long as she finds kissing "sale." Marie counters: "peut-être quand... mais quand on n'aime pas, c'est sale, n'est-ce pas?" — revealing both her innocence and sophistication. He names her "Suzanne" and claims a "droit acquis" (acquired right) to see her feet. Marie puts on her monk's robe (froc); he kisses her arm under the hood. She slips her fingers through a door-cupboard and he bites them — she whispers "saligaud." M. d'Andigné visits; Marie worries he'll think Cassagnac is courting her. Carnet ends with "Gloriae Cupiditas" (Desire for Glory) — Marie's personal motto. Note: BN annotation records pages 63-68 removed, deficit noted April 13, 1938. %%
