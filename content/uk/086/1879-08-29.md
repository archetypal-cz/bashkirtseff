Immediate friendship with Hecht - beach painting, excursions to Puys, casino evenings. Hecht impressed by Marie's earlier desire to see Gambetta, sees her political intelligence. Marie feels conflict about Republican sympathies vs. attachment to Bonapartists - races scene where riderless horse reminds her of Prince Imperial, making her cry. Told she looks 20-22 (she's actually younger), causing nervous prostration. Plans to cultivate Hecht for access to Gambetta and "Petit Phare" (Arnaud). Philosophical crisis: claims she no longer loves Cassagnac despite their past connection. Suicidal thoughts, contempt for death, expects no paradise. Self-aware about posing. Considers bequeathing diary to Hecht instead of Cassagnac. Questions whether Cassagnac was her great love. Later annotation (Dec 4, 1879) finds her earlier claims about "love" impertinent. %%
