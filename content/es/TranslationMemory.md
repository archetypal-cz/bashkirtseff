## Foreign Language Handling

When Marie writes in English, Italian or Russian in the French original:
- Translate to Spanish
- Mark with ==highlight==
- Footnote: "N. de la T.: En inglés/italiano/ruso en el original: «…»" with the original text (single words: add the Spanish gloss after a dash)

When Marie writes in Spanish (rare):
- Keep AS-IS, ==highlight==, footnote "*En español en el original*"

## Stylistic Conventions

- Footnote labels: N. de la A. / N. de la T. / N. de la E. — pilot seed — confirm
- Quotation marks: « » outer, " " inner — pilot seed — confirm
- Dialogue: raya (—) — pilot seed — confirm
- Dates: "sábado 11 de enero de 1873" (lowercase day and month) — pilot seed — confirm
- Numerals: words up to thirty; figures above, for ages and money — pilot seed — confirm
- Footnote calls: the `[^...]` marker goes **before** the punctuation mark ("… *Un ballo in maschera*[^01.07.1]."), per RAE, not after it as in the en tree — OPS 2026-09-05

### Mouton
- Translation: Mouton (kept)
- Context: a servant/groom who fails to send the horse; target of "canaille"
- Notes: "la canaille Mouton" → "el canalla de Mouton" (natural Spanish insult construction, not a literal calque)

### Boreel
- Translation: Boreel (kept)
- Context: a young man Marie is losing interest in (1873-01-14); "sa démarche" (his gait) is what displeases her
- Notes: "sa démarche" → "su andar" (concise literary noun, avoids clunky periphrasis)

### bal Floréal
- Translation: el baile Floréal
- Context: major winter-season social ball in Nice
- Notes: "bal" translated, "Floréal" kept as proper name (Revolutionary calendar month); footnote added on first mention (1873-01-13) explaining the event

### Théâtre français (Nice)
- Translation: el Théâtre français (kept in French)
- Context: proper venue name in Nice, distinct from "a theatre in France"
- Notes: not translated, no italics — treated as a proper noun like madame/monsieur + surname

### La joie de la maison / Le chapeau de paille (Un chapeau de paille d'Italie)
- Translation: kept in French, italicized: *La joie de la maison*, *Le chapeau de paille*
- Context: vaudeville plays performed with actor Ravel, 1873-01-12
- Notes: footnote gives the well-known Spanish title of the Labiche play (*El sombrero de paja de Italia*) on first mention; title itself stays French per "títulos de obras → cursiva"

### Pierre-Alfred Ravel
- Translation: Ravel (kept)
- Context: famous French comic actor of the period
- Notes: brief biographical footnote added on first mention

### tiré des billets (fortune-telling game)
- Translation: sacar papeletas de la suerte
- Context: parlor game, pre-written fortunes drawn and read
- Notes: "les ai mis dans le gant" → "las guardé en el guante"

### faire sa connaissance
- Translation: conocerlo / conocerla (direct object, never "le/la conocer")
- Context: formal social introduction; recurs re: the Duke of Hamilton
- Notes: deliberately avoids leísmo; "conocer" takes a direct object in Spanish

### voiture (en voiture / la voiture)
- Translation: **carruaje**
- Context: both "riding around in the carriage" (1873-01-11) and "the Duke's carriage" (1873-01-15)
- Notes: OPS 2026-09-05 overturned the translator's "coche". Two reasons: content/es/CLAUDE.md names *coche* vs *carro* as exactly the regional flag the neutral-Spanish policy avoids, and in present-day Spanish "coche" is read first as a motor car, which mis-times a duke's equipage in 1873. "Carruaje" is panhispanic and period-correct. CON 2026-09-05 upheld the ruling: **carruaje** is final for carnet 001. The superseded TR comments in 001.0011 and 001.0018 still name "coche" and are kept as a record of the deliberation, not as instructions. Human to confirm at end of pilot.

### notre loge
- Translation: nuestro palco
- Context: the family's private box at the ball/theatre
- Notes: pilot slice, carnet 001

### toilette(s) — plural usage
- Translation: atuendo(s)
- Context: "pas de jolies toilettes" (1873-01-14) = no beautiful outfits among the crowd
- Notes: reinforces the singular usage already seeded above; "robe" stays "vestido" so the two terms don't collide in the same paragraph

### Notre nouvel an (Russian New Year)
- Translation: nuestro año nuevo
- Context: 1873-01-13, Julian-calendar Russian New Year, falls on 13 January Gregorian
- Notes: footnote (N. de la E.) explains the calendar gap; carried over from the pre-existing English footnote definition, translated

### en mettant un miroir sous l'oreiller (mirror divination)
- Translation: poniendo un espejo bajo la almohada
- Context: 1873-01-13/14, traditional Russian New Year folk divination for dreaming of one's future husband
- Notes: new footnote added (N. de la E.) — not present in the original es/ scaffold, added to match the en/cz convention of footnoting period folk customs

### si seulement
- Translation: **si al menos**
- Context: 001.0018, Marie's wish that the Duke would call on the house
- Notes: CON 2026-09-05 replaced "si tan solo", which reads as a calque (English *if only* / French *si seulement*) and sounds like modern dubbing in an 1873 sentence. "Si al menos" is the native desiderative and loses no longing. Also available: "ojalá" when the wish stands alone without a following main clause.

### se montrer avec (quelqu'un)
- Translation: **dejarse ver con**
- Context: 001.0014, Marie and Dina wanting to be seen with someone from high society at the races
- Notes: CON 2026-09-05 replaced the pasiva refleja "que se nos viera con", which was grammatical but cold and made the display accidental rather than willed. "Dejarse ver con" is the Spanish idiom for social preening and keeps the reflexive of the French.

### assister (avoir assisté) — gallicism watch confirmed
- Translation: presenciar
- Context: 001.0003, editorial commentary ("les événements auxquels Marie dit avoir assisté")
- Notes: first live catch of the "assister à" trap from the gallicism table; NOT "asistir a"
