# Papa (Constantin Bashkirtseff)

**Research Status**: Moderate
**Last Updated**: 2026-09-26
**Merged from**: FATHER_BASHKIRTSEFF (now a redirect to this entry)

> **Disambiguation — who is "papa"?** In the Nice household (1873–1877) Marie's bare "papa" is almost always her **maternal grandfather, Stepan Babanine** — see [#Grand_papa](GRAND_PAPA.md). Her father Constantin is "mon père", "mon (cher / auguste / illustre / honorable) père" or "mon pater". Marie calls him "Papa" only when she is with him: his Nice visit of Nov 1873 (013.0085, 013.0093, 013.0109), Poltava and Gavronzi in Jul–Oct 1876 (carnets 064–066), and Paris in Nov 1876 (067.0122). This entry is for Constantin only. Tag the grandfather's "papa" as #Grand_papa, not #Papa. Other "papas" of 1875–76: old M. d'Audiffret (049.0155, 050.0104–0502), operetta refrains, other people's fathers. The per-occurrence tables are in `.claude/reports/papa-referent-2026-09-26.md`.

## Overview

Constantin (Constantine) Bashkirtseff (1833–1883) was Marie's father, a Russian nobleman and landowner of Gavronzi near Poltava (Ukraine, then part of the Russian Empire). He separated from Marie's mother in 1861 and lived apart from the family, who moved to Europe in 1870.

## Family Background

- Son of General Paul Grigorievitch Bashkirtseff ([#Paul_Grigorievitch_Bashkirtseff](PAUL_GRIGORIEVITCH_BASHKIRTSEFF.md))
- Landowner at the Gavronzi estate near Poltava
- Married Marie Stepanovna Babanine ([#Maman](MAMAN.md))
- Father of Marie (1858–1884) and Paul (1859–1899)
- Marshal of the nobility: "mon père est maréchal de noblesse" (023.0208); "mon honorable père, maréchal de noblesse" (030.0128)

## In the diary, 1873–1875

Constantin is absent from the Nice household. Marie mentions him with marked forms:

- **Durocher affair** (1873-10-25, 011.0277–0284): "Mon très cher père a une actrice française Durocher…", his public liaison in Russia.
- **Announced visit** (1873-11-07, 012.0096): "Mon cher père vient à Nice. Animal !"
- **Visit to Nice / Monte-Carlo, 24–27 November 1873** (013.0082–0109): "Mon auguste père est arrivé." Only during this visit does Marie call him "Papa" / "le Papa" / "mon adorable papa" (013.0085, 013.0093, 013.0109), always next to "mon père".
- **Family quarrels**: the grandfather reproaches "ma race, mon nom, mon père" (019.0313); Marie defends him (025.0054: "Je connais peu mon père et l'aime encore moins…").
- **Letters, 1875**: Marie writes to Paul and Sacha so that they tell "mon père" she is coming to Russia, "pas pour rester chez lui" (032.0372); "Mon père écrit que … Paul se conduit d'une façon impossible" (039.0106).

## 1876–1877: Poltava, Gavronzi, Paris, Rome

- In July 1876 Marie travels to Russia and meets her father (carnets 064–066). Her plan is to bring him to Rome so that the family looks respectable.
- At Poltava and Gavronzi she calls him "Papa" and plays up to him: "Vous serez mon frère aîné et je vous nommerai Constantin" (065.0179). Prince Michel Eristoff, stepson of his sister Nathalie, "mange l'argent à papa" (065.0064).
- In November 1876 Marie and her father travel to Paris together (067.0122, "Papa et maman"), where she introduces him to Cassagnac. They go on to Rome at Christmas 1876, but he leaves before New Year without improving Marie's social standing.
- In December 1876 he makes a provocative visit to Nice (067.0291 "la carte de mon père … voici son défi"), and maman confronts him at Monaco with his mistress and Mme Tutcheff (067.0316).
- "M. Constantin Bashkirtseff a épousé la plus belle et la plus vertueuse des femmes… Il a rendu cette femme parfaitement malheureuse" (068.0240).

## Later life and death

- December 1878: he arrives in Paris, and Marie learns from him that she was a full-term baby (contradicting the family's claim of a premature birth).
- April 1881: he comes to Paris wanting to take Marie back to Gavronzi, and she refuses. He did not understand or approve of her dedication to painting.
- 1882: the parents are somewhat reconciled after twenty years of separation.
- 1883: he falls very ill and Marie's mother returns to Ukraine. He dies at Gavronzi on 11 June 1883. Marie reflected on their often troubled relations and regretted not travelling to Russia with her mother while he was dying.

## Marriage History

A story told on 1 September 1873: Marie Ivanovna Perlik warned Marie's mother against marrying Bashkirtseff. The warning went unheeded, which foreshadows the separation.
