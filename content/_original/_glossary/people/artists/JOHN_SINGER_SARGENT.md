[John Singer Sargent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Singer_Sargent) (1856–1925) was an American portrait painter trained in Paris in the atelier of [Carolus-Duran](./CAROLUS_DURAN.md), whose most celebrated pupil he became. Marie never met him in the pages of the diary; he appears only as a name at the Salon, a rival measure of what portraiture could be.

## In Marie's Diary

%% GLO_JOHN_SINGER_SARGENT.0004 %%
%% 2026-09-07T12:00:00 RSR: Salon of 1882 (095.0045). The picture is not named in the diary. %%
- **May 1882** (095.0045): at the Salon, "le portrait de jeune fille par Sargent" haunts her. She calls it ravishing, an exquisite work one would gladly hang in a museum beside the van Dycks and Velázquez.

%% GLO_JOHN_SINGER_SARGENT.0005 %%
- **May 1883** (099.0305): reviewing the Salon painter by painter, she dismisses him in two words: "Sargent est médiocre."

%% GLO_JOHN_SINGER_SARGENT.0006 %%
%% [#Portrait_de_Mme_X](../../culture/art/PORTRAIT_MME_X.md) %%
- **May–June 1884** (104.0058, 104.0557): the portrait of Mme Gautreau, the future *Madame X*, is the succès de curiosité of the Salon and generally found atrocious. Marie disagrees: for her it is perfect, masterly, true painting; Sargent simply painted what he saw, and the sitter's heavy make-up is what looks horrible in daylight. A month later she still lists it among the few good things in the show, "tant décrié et pourtant très bon".
