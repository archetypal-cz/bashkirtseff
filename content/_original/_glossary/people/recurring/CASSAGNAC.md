# Paul de Cassagnac

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0002 %%
[Paul Adolphe Marie Prosper Granier de Cassagnac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Adolphe_Marie_Prosper_Granier_de_Cassagnac) (2 December 1842, Paris -- 4 November 1904, Saint-Viatre) was a French journalist, politician, and celebrated duelist. Editor of the Bonapartist newspaper *Le Pays* and founder of *L'Autorite*, he was one of the most combative and flamboyant figures of the early Third Republic. Marie was infatuated with him from 1876 to approximately 1880, making him one of the major romantic obsessions of her diary with approximately 280 tagged references across carnets 063--105.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0003 %%
## Biography

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0004 %%
### Origins and Family

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0005 %%
Paul was the son of [Bernard-Adolphe Granier de Cassagnac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolphe_Granier_de_Cassagnac) (1806--1880), himself a prominent journalist and politician, and Rosa de Beaupin de Beauvalon, described as a Creole from a wealthy plantation-owning family. The Granier de Cassagnac family came from Averon-Bergelle in the Gers department of Gascony. Marie refers to Cassagnac as "Creole" and "Gascon" -- both accurate: Creole through his mother, Gascon through his father's family. His cousin was [Prosper-Olivier Lissagaray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosper-Olivier_Lissagaray) (1838--1901), the socialist journalist and historian of the Commune, with whom Paul fought bitter duels despite their blood relation.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0006 %%
### Education and Early Career

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0007 %%
Paul studied at Foix and Perpignan, completed his baccalaureate in Toulouse, and began law studies before abandoning them for journalism. He founded the newspaper *L'Independance parisienne* in 1862 at age twenty, then worked as a chroniqueur for *La Nation* (1863) and editor of *Diogene* (which provoked his first duel, with Aurelien Scholl). In 1866, he entered *Le Pays*, the conservative newspaper directed by his father, becoming editor-in-chief the following year.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0008 %%
### Military Service

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0009 %%
When the Franco-Prussian War broke out in 1870, Paul volunteered for service. He was captured at the [Battle of Sedan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sedan) and interned as a prisoner of war in the fortress of Cosel (Kozle) in Silesia. Marie records one of his war stories: upon returning to France, he nearly hanged a police commissioner with his own sash (carnet 064, 1876-07-25). The experience of Sedan and captivity deepened his Bonapartist conviction and his hatred of the Republic.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0010 %%
### Physical Appearance

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0011 %%
Marie's first physical description, on meeting him (1876-07-24): "Il est tres grand, brun, des cheveux noirs et des yeux italiens ; il serait beau si ce n'etait un peu d'embonpoint... et une certaine contraction de la bouche qui fausse ses traits qui sont de nature reguliers et nobles" (He is very tall, dark, with black hair and Italian eyes; he would be handsome but for a little stoutness... and a certain contraction of the mouth that distorts features which are by nature regular and noble). His colleague Jules Richard described him as "chiseled like a sixteenth-century soldier, his mustache large and turned up *en panache*, his skin lightly tanned, his step slow and haughty." Marie later elaborated: "Cassagnac est un chevalier du Moyen-age ou un seigneur de la Ligue ou de la Fronde" (1879-08-17). She also noted his "magnetic gaze" and his "lips of a child" during their intimate encounters. By 1879, his physical decline was evident: Paul (Marie's brother) found him "abruti, jaune, sale, tout a fait tombe" (stupefied, yellow, dirty, completely finished), while servant Rosalie confirmed he had lost his "regard insolent" and "air crane" (1879-03-05).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0012 %%
## Political Career

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0013 %%
For detailed coverage of Cassagnac's political positions, parliamentary career, and role in Bonapartist politics, see the comprehensive [#Politics](../../culture/themes/POLITICS.md) entry. Key points summarized here for biographical completeness.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0014 %%
Paul was first elected deputy for the Gers department (arrondissement of Condom) on 20 February 1876 as an Imperialist candidate. He adopted a policy of systematic obstruction "to discredit the republican regime." His elections were repeatedly invalidated by the republican-dominated Chamber of Deputies (1876, 1877), each invalidation triggering costly by-election campaigns. He was re-elected on 2 February 1879, and Marie tracked the results with vindictive satisfaction: "Cassagnac est elu mais il n'a que 990 voix de majorite" versus 4,131 in 1877 (1879-02-03). He was excluded from the Chamber in 1883 for calling Jules Ferry "dernier des miserables et dernier des laches" during the Tonkin debate. Not re-elected in 1902, he was defeated by Joseph Noulens.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0015 %%
He popularized the pejorative nickname ["La Gueuse"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gueuse) (the beggar-woman, or the slut) for the Republic, later adopted by Action francaise royalists. In 1877, he encouraged MacMahon to attempt what amounted to a Bonapartist coup d'etat. He founded the daily *[L'Autorite](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Autorit%C3%A9)* in 1886, with the motto "Pour Dieu, pour la France!" His sons Paul and Guy continued the newspaper until 1914.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0016 %%
Despite his antisemitic and clerical orientation, Cassagnac demonstrated intellectual independence during the [Dreyfus Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreyfus_affair): he publicly expressed doubts about Dreyfus's guilt and advocated for trial revision. Clemenceau later praised his "chevaleresque" character and "noble attitude" during the crisis.

## The Dueling Career

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0018 %%
Cassagnac was one of the most prolific duelists in French political life, fighting at least twenty-two duels over his career. His known opponents include:

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0020 %%
Marie was enthralled by his dueling reputation. She called him "le dogue, le bretteur, l'epouvantail de la canaille" (the mastiff, the swordsman, the scarecrow of rabble) (1876-11-27). She followed his newspaper controversies and "gifles" (slapping incidents) with fascination, subscribing to *L'Indiscret* by Ulysse Pic which chronicled his violent exploits (1879-01-28). His valet de chambre kept "deux pistolets charges" (two loaded pistols) on a table during visitors' calls (1878-02-14).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0021 %%
## Marriage

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0022 %%
On 27 June 1878, Cassagnac married Alexandrine Julie Acard in Paris (8th arrondissement). The marriage was devastating to Marie, who learned of it while still emotionally entangled. They had two sons, Paul and Guy, who later directed *L'Autorite*.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0023 %%
The circumstances of the marriage were scandalous. According to gossip Marie gathered from Michel, the Gavinis, and others: Cassagnac married for money after his political patron Mme de la Valette (widow of the Marquis de la Valette, Napoleon III's diplomat) could no longer fund his repeatedly invalidated election campaigns. Mlle Acard's mother, the "comtesse Acard" (later Mme Couvelet), was alleged to have been the mistress of [Cardinal Antonelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Antonelli), the Vatican Secretary of State. Clement Laurier (1832--1878), a republican lawyer, provided the bride's dowry. The ceremony was secretive -- "comme les premiers chretiens dans les caves" (like early Christians in caves) -- with protocol violations including the mother marrying a week before her daughter and Cassagnac serving as his own witness (1878-09-15). Marie called it a "sale mariage" (dirty marriage) but defended Cassagnac as a victim: "on l'a pousse dedans, il a ete force, on lui a monte la tete" (he was pushed into it, forced, they filled his head) (1878-09-05).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0024 %%
Captain Blanc later reported malicious gossip: Cassagnac called his wife "Ninette" (his former mistress's name) to avoid confusion, she was "covered in pimples" and had gotten fat while dictating his newspaper articles (1879-07-30). Marie tracked the marriage with obsessive precision: "le 27 il y aura juste sept mois que Cassagnac se sera marie et il doit etre bien ennuye s'il ne voit rien de nouveau chez sa femme" (on the 27th it will be exactly seven months since Cassagnac married and he must be quite bored if he sees nothing new in his wife) (1879-01-21).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0025 %%
## Relationship with Marie Bashkirtseff

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0026 %%
### Phase 1: The Intellectual Seduction (July 1876 -- November 1876)

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0027 %%
Marie first heard the name Cassagnac in July 1876, when her stepfather Georges offered to introduce her. She refused -- then immediately regretted it: "Brute que je suis, j'ai dit non !" The very next line reveals her political ambitions: "Paul de Cassagnac va un de ces jours etre le chef du parti de l'Empereur. Je veux le connaitre, vivre a Paris et rassembler chez nous les bonapartistes" (1876-07-21). She added a marginal note: "Remarquez que je suis a cette epoque absolument ignorante de la politique et que c'est la premiere fois que j'entends prononcer le nom de Cassagnac."

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0028 %%
The first meeting occurred on 24 July 1876 at the Mouzay residence. Marie's account is one of the great set-piece scenes of the diary. She sat down "toute haletante" (breathless) after being introduced. Cassagnac held forth magnificently: "il parla de lui comme d'un autre, exposant ses qualites, sa position, sa bravoure, son orgueil, sa vanite, son ambition, sa fatuite" (he spoke of himself as of another, laying out his qualities, his position, his bravery, his pride, his vanity, his ambition, his conceit). He declared: "La beaute ne me touche pas" (Beauty does not move me) -- and Marie believed him. He was a mesmerist and they discovered shared interests in magnetism and spiritism. Their second meeting followed on 26 November 1876 at a lunch: "Mon cher Cassagnac etait deja la, remplissant la moitie du salon" (My dear Cassagnac was already there, filling half the drawing room).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0029 %%
### Phase 2: The Masked Intrigues (January -- February 1878)

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0030 %%
The relationship intensified dramatically during the carnival season of January 1878, through an elaborate game of anonymous letters and masked visits. Marie and four companions -- Dina, Berthe, Yorke, and Mme de Daillens -- wrote to Cassagnac as "five anonymous women" via the Madeleine post office, requesting tea at his apartment. They visited him masked at the bal de l'Opera, where women in the crowd constantly asked to see him: "Tu veux donc voir ce Cassagnac ?"

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0031 %%
The second masked visit to his apartment on 28 January 1878 is one of the diary's most extraordinary scenes. The five women arrived in elaborate costumes; Marie wore "la robe du Capitole" with silver lace and a black Chantilly mantilla, stuffing her bodice with four large towels. They were recognized instantly but Cassagnac "pretended not to know them." He knelt at their feet, kissed Marie's feet, served champagne, offered cigars, and read from his unpublished book *Les femmes que l'on n'a pas* (Women One Has Not Had). He gave each woman a character reading: the "colonelle" (Dina) was serene, "Georges" (Berthe) was skeptical, the "novice" (Yorke) was "either perfectly innocent or perfectly perverse," and Marie was "the only one with whom something could happen."

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0032 %%
On 3 February 1878, during a dinner at the family home, the famous "sale" dialogue occurred. In a tete-a-tete, Cassagnac confessed he had wanted to kiss her during the masked visit. Marie said kissing "would be... sale" (dirty). He replied tenderly that she was safe as long as she found kissing "sale." Marie countered: "peut-etre quand... mais quand on n'aime pas, c'est sale, n'est-ce pas ?" -- revealing both innocence and sophistication. He named her "Suzanne," claimed a "droit acquis" (acquired right) to see her feet, kissed her arm under her monk's robe, and bit her fingers through a door-cupboard. She whispered "saligaud" (dirty pig).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0033 %%
On 14 February 1878, Marie visited Cassagnac's apartment at 10 bis rue de Boulogne for the third time, this time spontaneously. She admired his "pieds admirables" in suede boots and his blue-and-red flannel house clothes. That evening she wrote the diary's most sexually explicit line: "Si je traduisais mes pensees avec la brutalite qui me caracterise je dirais : qu'il me tarde donc de me marier pour devenir la maitresse de M. de Cassagnac" (If I translated my thoughts with the brutality that characterizes me, I would say: how impatient I am to marry so I can become M. de Cassagnac's mistress). She immediately qualified it: with another man it would be "honteux, sale et tragique," but Cassagnac had so much wit and charm that it would seem "un amusement assez naturel."

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0034 %%
### Phase 3: Engagement Rumors and Heartbreak (March -- September 1878)

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0035 %%
Rumors of a marriage between Marie and Cassagnac spread rapidly: at Monte Carlo, M. des Perrieres of *Le Figaro* told Marcuard that Marie was marrying Cassagnac (1878-02-22). At her atelier, students gossiped: "on dit que j'epouse Cassagnac" (1878-02-24). Marie understood the social dynamics: without a proper salon, Cassagnac's visits "look like courting." She worried about his approaching marriage to Mlle Acard from April 1878 onward, learning rumors from Breslau's landlady, from Schaeppi, from the newspapers. The news fell on her "comme un glacon" (like a block of ice) (1878-04-17).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0036 %%
Marie's reaction to the June 1878 marriage mixed despair, rage, and dark humor. At the Gavinis' she proclaimed herself "folle de Cassagnac" and declared she would marry him -- no one believed her (1878-06-20). The Gavinis judged that "Cassagnac ne merite qu'Acard et que je suis bien trop jolie, trop riche, trop bien pour lui." She confided to Michel that Cassagnac's letters had arrived "four days before the ceremony" when "he was in it up to his neck" -- too late (1878-09-05).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0037 %%
### Phase 4: The Haunting (1879 -- 1883)

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0038 %%
After the marriage, Marie adopted distancing tactics while remaining obsessed. She renamed him "le Defunt" (the Deceased) and used bitter irony: "Vieille bete de Cassagnac, s'etre marie" (Old fool Cassagnac, getting married) (1879-06-03). At Versailles she "turned her back vigorously" when encountering him. Yet she continued to track him: reading *Le Pays* for his articles, following his parliamentary performances, collecting newspaper clippings of his speeches in an album.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0039 %%
The street encounter of 30 October 1879 produced one of the diary's most passionate declarations: "Je ne trouve personne au monde comme Cassagnac et tres certainement je suis a ses pieds. Il est beau, il est sympathique, il est charmant, il est bon. C'est le seul etre au monde qui soit mon egal, mon superieur, aux genoux duquel je voudrais etre" (I find no one in the world like Cassagnac and most certainly I am at his feet. He is handsome, sympathetic, charming, good. He is the only being in the world who is my equal, my superior, at whose knees I would wish to be). She added a devastating comparison: with Gambetta she would feel herself before a god, but Cassagnac was "bien un homme, mon homme, ma moitie" (truly a man, my man, my other half).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0040 %%
In 1880, Soutzo's courtship temporarily displaced Cassagnac, but tellingly it was Soutzo's imitation of Cassagnac's parliamentary manner that reignited Marie's passion for the original. On 9 June 1880, re-reading her old diaries, she found her own "surprising lines" about wanting to become Cassagnac's mistress: "Abominable pas vrai ?" she asked herself, with rueful self-knowledge. On 13 June 1880, she confided in Julian about Cassagnac; the atelier director encouraged her fantasy that "when you are famous, he will return." She admitted she had written secret "in extremis" letters to Cassagnac offering him money -- letters she would never reveal.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0041 %%
By 1881, Marie's assessment became more critical. On 22 February 1881, after reading Gambetta, she judged: "Cassagnac m'apparait depourvu de sens, pale, miserable. Qu'est-ce que je puis aimer dans cette mediocrite doree ?" (Cassagnac appears to me devoid of sense, pale, miserable. What can I love in this gilded mediocrity?). Yet she continued dreaming of him: in April 1881, she dreamed he showed her his house and boasted of his domestic life "with two small children" (1881-04-17). In May 1881, Mme de Brimont told Marie her own Cassagnac story -- a masked Opera ball seduction following the same pattern Marie had experienced, proving it was Cassagnac's standard technique for women (1881-05-10).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0042 %%
The last significant references appear in 1883. On 6 June 1883, Marie saw Cassagnac walking past her garden with his wife: "j'ai vu son profil a travers la fente de la porte" (I saw his profile through the crack in the door). She wished she could have "sprayed him with the garden hose" if he had been alone (1883-06-06). On 14 July 1883, she acknowledged the passion had been retrospective: "je l'ai aime (??) de souvenir" (I loved him (??) from memory) (1883-07-14). On 15 July 1883, she used Stendhal's crystallization theory to analyze the Cassagnac attachment: "Il y avait commencement de cristallisation pour Cassagnac, je le raisonne, l'examine... Je m'accorde la permission de l'aimer. La cristallisation continue et j'y arrive" (1883-07-15).

## Nicknames in Marie's Diary

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0044 %%
Marie used an extraordinary range of names for Cassagnac, reflecting the shifting emotional register of the relationship:

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0045 %%
- **Popaul** -- affectionate/mocking diminutive (the most frequent nickname)
- **le Defunt / the Deceased** -- ironic code name adopted after his marriage
- **le grand Paul** -- "big Paul" (contrasting with her brother, "petit Paul")
- **mon Mirabeau** -- comparing him to the great revolutionary orator
- **ce turc** -- "this Turk," playful nickname implying exotic seductiveness
- **Paolo** -- Italian form of Paul, affectionate/mocking
- **mon frere / ma soeur** -- sibling language used in the flirtation
- **Suzanne** -- the name *he* gave *her* during the masked visits
- **Creole** -- reference to his Creole mother
- **Gascon** -- reference to his Gascon father's origins
- **le Balsamo** -- comparing him to Cagliostro, the 18th-century charlatan

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0046 %%
## Significance in the Diary

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0047 %%
Cassagnac represents several crucial dimensions of Marie's development:

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0048 %%
**Political awakening.** Before Cassagnac, Marie was "absolument ignorante de la politique." Through following his journalism and parliamentary career, she developed sophisticated political analysis, eventually moving from Bonapartism toward republicanism. Her comparison of Cassagnac to Gambetta -- the former "gilded mediocrity," the latter a man who "with one word dominates and enchants you" -- marks a political and emotional maturation.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0049 %%
**Intellectual seduction.** Uniquely among Marie's attachments, Cassagnac was loved through his writing before she met him -- "an intellectual seduction unique among her attachments" (as noted in [#Love](../../culture/themes/LOVE.md)). She compared him to Balzac's Raoul Nathan from *Une fille d'Eve* (1878-08-25), Bussy d'Amboise from Dumas's *La Dame de Monsoreau* (1879-05-27), and d'Artagnan from his parliamentary profile. She analyzed his prose style like a literary critic: articles that looked "translated from verse" (1878-09-03).

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0050 %%
**Art versus love.** Marie explicitly blamed Cassagnac for her poor concours results: "Si je n'ai pas monte c'est la faute de Cassagnac" (If I have not advanced it is Cassagnac's fault) (1878-01-29). She recognized that sleeping only three hours a night during competition week -- consumed by the Cassagnac social whirl -- was the real cause. The tension between romantic fascination and artistic discipline is the core conflict of the 1878 diary.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0051 %%
**Romantic pattern.** Cassagnac exemplifies Marie's pattern of attraction to powerful, established men who are ultimately unavailable. The progression from idealization through disillusionment to retrospective "crystallization" (her own Stendhalian term) recurs with Hamilton, Antonelli, and to some extent Bastien-Lepage.

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0052 %%
## Later Life and Death

%% GLO_CASSAGNAC.0053 %%
Paul de Cassagnac continued his political and journalistic career through the 1880s and 1890s. He was re-elected deputy for Gers in 1898 but defeated in 1902. He also served as mayor of Couloume-Mondebat. He received the Legion d'honneur (Chevalier) on 15 August 1868. His published works include *A Rabagas* (1872), *Histoire populaire abregee de Napoleon III* (1874), *Histoire de la troisieme Republique* (1876), and *La publicite dans les journaux* (1893). He died of acute appendicitis on 4 November 1904 at Saint-Viatre, aged 61. He is buried at the [Cimetiere de Montmartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montmartre_Cemetery).
