# Soroka

## Overview

"Soroka" is Marie Bashkirtseff's Russian-language nickname for Emile d'Audiffret, a young Niçois nobleman. The full form is "britaya soroka" (Russian: shaved magpie / pie rasée), shortened to "soroka" in daily use. The name was adopted by Marie's entire household in Nice and abroad.

See main entry: [AUDIFFRET](../recurring/AUDIFFRET.md)

## Origin

Marie explains the nickname's adoption in early 1876: "à présent tout le monde chez nous à Nice et ici donne un nom affreux à ce pauvre garçon, on le nomme britaya soroka ce qui veut dire Pie rasée et par abréviation on dit soroka simplement" (carnet 053).

## Extended Usage

Marie also uses "soroka" (plural "sorokas") generically for admirers/suitors who stare at her at the opera or on the promenade -- flashy men attracted to shiny things, like the magpie of Russian folklore. In Rome (1876), she writes of "le Soroka de Rome" who "rappelle en laid Soroka de Nice" -- a Roman admirer who resembles Audiffret, only uglier.
