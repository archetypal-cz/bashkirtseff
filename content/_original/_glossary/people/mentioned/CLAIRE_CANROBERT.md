# Claire Canrobert

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0001 %%
## Who she was

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0002 %%
Claire Canrobert was the daughter of [Marshal Canrobert](MARECHAL_CANROBERT.md) and [Leïla Flora Macdonald](MARECHALE_CANROBERT.md), and Marie's closest friend among the Paris aristocracy in the last year and a half of her life. Marie never gives her a surname beyond "Canrobert" ("Claire (Canrobert)", 099.0419; "Mlle de Canrobert").

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0003 %%
Genealogical listings give her full name as Eugénie Angélique Rose Hélène Claire Leïla Certain de Canrobert, born in 1865 and died in 1945 (Geneanet trees of "virgile81", Mathieu Nival, and "garric"; the birth year is not confirmed by a civil record seen here). She would therefore have been seventeen or eighteen when Marie met her, and nearly seven years younger than Marie, hence "la petite". She married in the summer of 1890 (27 July or 11 August, the listings disagree) the naval officer Paul Fabre-Roustand de Navacelle (1861–1939); their children were Henri (1891–1947), Napoléon Charles (1893–1915), Marcelle Flora Marie (1897–1979) and Anne Eugénie (1898–1977). Geni calls her "Claire de Navacelle (Canrobert)". Marie had been dead six years when she married.

## Marie and Claire

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0005 %%
**Winter 1883: the Julian girl.** On 3 February 1883 the Maréchale brings her daughter, "qui travaille chez Julian", to see Marie's studio (098.0279). From 28 February to April she is one of the three pupils (with Marie and Alice) of the weekly perspective lessons that the architect Émile Bastien-Lepage gives in Marie's studio (098.0431, 098.0464, 098.0484; 099.0139: the course is interrupted when "la petite Canrobert" has the mumps). Marie finds her "très intelligente" (099.0234, 28 April), paints beside her ("la petite Canrobert peint à côté de moi", 099.0126) and watches her do a still life while Marie paints the portrait of Bojidar (099.0317, 099.0419).

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0006 %%
**Spring and summer 1883: friendship.** From May she is at Marie's apartment for whole afternoons, while the Maréchale takes Marie out (099.0442). On 17 May Marie dines at the Canroberts' after Claire has worked with her on the pastel of Villevieille (099.0465). In July she spends three days at Jouy and in September ten more; the two girls lie on the salon floor "comme tous les soirs" (101.0320), and Marie draws the pastel of Claire's younger brother Louis (101.0305–101.0332). On 31 December, at the Opéra-Comique, her mother kisses Marie at midnight in the hope that "ma précieuse amitié reste à Claire" (102.0618).

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0007 %%
**Winter 1883–1884: the flower-seller.** Claire comes nearly every day. Marie takes her to the studios of painters (7 and 12 January, 102.0657, 102.0672) and notes how Claire reports back what the artists around Princess Mathilde say about her, such as Jules Lefebvre (102.0619). On 17 December Marie records that Claire "va faire un tableau, des fleurs avec une vieille marchande. Ce sera encore moi" (102.0501); the picture is largely Marie's, and on 22 January Claire finds it "presque fini, grand étonnement" (103.0099). Marie's feelings are those of a forger: "Je pleure en peignant ces odieuses fleurs. Ô lâcheté ! Ce n'est pas par amitié que je commets ce faux" (14 February, 103.0333). See [#Claire's flower-seller picture](../../culture/art/TABLEAU_DE_CLAIRE_FLEURS.md).

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0008 %%
**Salon 1884 and after.** On 24 March Claire is received at the Salon, and comes to see Marie, who has only a "n° 3" and no place on the line; Marie notes that Claire "a l'air de trouver absolument naturel" that she is not hung higher (103.0582). On 1 April, "cette sacrée petite gueuse de Claire me dit que j'ai tort de mal prendre le n° 3" (103.0655). On 18 March, however, at Jouy, they had philosophised on the grass, and Marie had told her that she had greatly irritated her, "nous sommes maintenant assez amies pour que ça n'arrive plus" (103.0553). Marie's note on 15 June is guarded: "Les Canrobert ? Si l'on veut, mais je ne me livre pas entièrement avec eux" (104.0587).

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0009 %%
**Summer 1884.** Claire's mother expects another picture for her: "Les Canrobert ne me lâcheront pas tant que je ne leur ferai un tableau pour Claire" (23 July, 105.0281). During the weeks at Jouy Marie dissects her friend with some severity: "Claire est une petite fille extraordinairement perfectionnée, sa mère lui a suggéré jusqu'à ses émotions j'en jurerais" (105.0362); she thinks Claire borrows her remarks, "elle finira par me faire sentir que je la pille" (105.0421); on 5 August, the long tirade on the girl with "sa tête ronde, ses cheveux de japonaise coiffés à la japonaise et sa myopie", the "esprit des jeunes filles intelligentes", and "C'est fatigant, c'est fatigant, c'est fatigant" (105.0455). On 4 August she had been sitting beside Claire at the Versailles Congress (105.0423); on 26 August the girl and her mother pay their last call recorded in the diary (105.0675).

## In Marie's art

%% GLO_CLAIRE_CANROBERT.0011 %%
Besides the flower-seller picture (which Marie painted for her, winter 1883–84), Marie also made a portrait of "Mlle de Canrobert". Mathilde Blind, in her introduction to the English *Journal* (1890), lists among Marie's most characteristic portraits "those of Mme. P. K., her sister-in-law, of Bojidar Karageorgevitch the Servian Prince, and of Mlle. de Canrobert", and praises the ease of the figure. The diary does not describe that portrait; it is not located. Marie's pastel of Claire's brother Louis (autumn 1883) appears in the 1885 posthumous catalogue as "Louis de Canrobert" (see [#Portrait Mme X](../../culture/art/PORTRAIT_MME_X.md)).
