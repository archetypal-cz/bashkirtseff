## Three different Louis

%% GLO_LOUIS.0002 %%
The tag Louis is used for three unrelated people, in three places in the diary. None can be identified further than the diary allows.

%% GLO_LOUIS.0003 %%
## 1. Louis Canrobert (b. about 1872), the Maréchal's youngest child

%% GLO_LOUIS.0004 %%
A boy in the household of Maréchal and Maréchale Canrobert, whom Marie befriends in 1883 (their daughter Claire works with her and Alice at her studio). He is the "petit Louis Canrobert qui a dix ans" in the Bois de Boulogne party of 17 May 1883 (099.0467), "Louis qui a onze ans" at the Canroberts' on 26 August 1883 (101.0240), where he has a brother of seventeen (Marcel, whose escapade to Paris is told on 2 September, 101.0306) and a sister Claire. In September 1883 Marie, ill with a cold, makes his portrait in pastel:

%% GLO_LOUIS.0005 %%
- **2 September 1883** (101.0305). "Je fais le portrait de Louis au pastel, ça a l'air de venir."
- **5 September** (101.0310). She works at nothing else.
- **7 September** (101.0317). "Louis ne pose pas du tout" while Marcel sings and Claire paints, "et ça s'appelle faire un portrait".
- **8 September** (101.0320). "J'ai fini le portrait de Louis."
- **14 October 1883** (101.0604). At Jouy, retouches to the portrait.

%% GLO_LOUIS.0006 %%
The pastel is not among the works catalogued on this site (no portrait of Louis Canrobert was found among Marie's known works). Marcel is presumably the son Marcel Canrobert, lieutenant in the 6th Chasseurs d'Afrique in 1895 (Journal du Lot, 5 February 1895, which names him as the Maréchal's son); the Maréchal (François Certain de Canrobert, 1809-1895) was seventy-four in 1883. The diary does not give Louis's birth date or place in the family; the identification "child of the Maréchal and the Maréchale" comes from the diary's grouping of "la maréchale, Claire, Alice et le petit Louis Canrobert" and is probable, not documented.

%% GLO_LOUIS.0007 %%
## 2. Louis-Jules-René-Marie, the Bashkirtseffs' young servant (1883)

%% GLO_LOUIS.0008 %%
A Black boy of about fifteen, in the household for nearly a year by June 1883, "très intelligent, qui me rappelle l'ancien Chocolat-Fortuné Saïd" (100.0078). The parish priest doubted the authenticity of his baptism and, to avoid administering the sacrament to a pagan, rebaptised him on 6 June 1883. Marie insisted on being his godmother; as neither her father nor Bojidar could be found in time, a church employee stood in for "M. Bashkirtseff", whom she had pompously entered as "maréchal de la noblesse et conseiller d'État". Marie baptised him "Louis-Jules-René-Marie" (100.0080), and he made his first communion on 7 June; the family had him dine with them that evening (100.0094). On 28 July she calls him "mon fils spirituel Louis-Jules-René", who sings café-concert songs in the Pitauchards' apartment; he had once been to the Ambassadeurs (100.0452). The household nicknamed him "Thibaudin", after the minister of war of the day, and Marie writes that the joke will need explaining in fifty years (100.0454). His other name and origin are not given.

%% GLO_LOUIS.0009 %%
## 3. Louis, Jeanne Suermondt's husband (1878)

%% GLO_LOUIS.0010 %%
In the letter of Jeanne Suermondt, written at Halfweg near Haarlem on 14 May 1878 and copied into the diary on 18 May (080.0173): "mon Louis et moi" are married nearly a year; "il n'a que 24 ans, moi 19", he had been a naval officer who served in the "guerre d'Atchine" (the Aceh War in Sumatra, which began in 1873) and, as Jeanne did not want a husband at sea, left the navy to become an *économe*; they live between Haarlem and Amsterdam and she expects a child in a few weeks. His surname is not given beyond "Suermondt", Jeanne's maiden name (presumably; not established).
