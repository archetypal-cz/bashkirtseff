## Two Doucets

%% GLO_DOUCET.0002 %%
The name stands for two unrelated people. Almost every mention (1875-1883) is the Parisian dress-making house of **Jacques Doucet**, where Marie had her clothes made. Two entries of 1880 and 1883 are about the painter **Lucien Doucet**, Prix de Rome, whom Marie describes as a favourite pupil of Julian. This page treats the couturier first.

%% GLO_DOUCET.0003 %%
## Jacques Doucet, couturier (1853-1929)

%% GLO_DOUCET.0004 %%
Jacques Doucet was born in Paris on 19 February 1853 and died at Neuilly-sur-Seine on 30 October 1929. His family business at 21 rue de la Paix, founded in the early nineteenth century (sources give 1816 or the 1820s), was split by the early 1860s between his mother Mathilde's lace and lingerie shop and his father Édouard's shirt shop. Jacques began working in it about 1875 and made it one of the first haute couture houses of Paris; Réjane, Sarah Bernhardt, Liane de Pougy and La Belle Otero were clients, and Paul Poiret and Madeleine Vionnet were trained there. He was also a major collector and patron (sale of much of his eighteenth-century collection in June 1912). (Fr. Wikipedia, "Jacques Doucet (couturier)"; Mimi Matthews / "The House of Doucet (1816-1928)", https://mimicofmodes.com/2014/10/15/the-house-of-doucet-1816-1928/; V&A Museum collection notes.)

%% GLO_DOUCET.0005 %%
## Marie and the house of Doucet

%% GLO_DOUCET.0006 %%
The diary does not say whether Marie dealt with Jacques himself; "Doucet" is the shop. Her references run from linen (1875) to dresses (1878-1883), when Doucet, Worth and Laferrière are her three couturiers; in 1879 she says she has left "Worth et Laferrière" for "Doucet et Savarre", and that dressing at the ordinary places makes her "embourgeoisée".

%% GLO_DOUCET.0007 %%
- **5 and 11 August 1875** (038.0148, 038.0269). Doucet makes her "du linge de la dernière élégance" and a tulle veil that costs a hundred francs: "il est vrai que ce voile est divin." (Doucet's was then the lingerie shop of Mme Doucet, which her son Jacques was beginning to turn into a couture house.)
- **3 October 1877** (075.0128). In half an hour Doucet makes her "un délicieux petit scapulaire bleu" on the day she starts at Julian's.
- **9 October 1878** (082.0577). "Felix et Doucet me feront les choses les plus ravissantes." The English footnote there already names Jacques Doucet.
- **31 October 1878** (083.0081). They pass "la boutique de Doucet" in a carriage.
- **26-27 November 1878** (083.0272-0279). While Marie is trying on a dress, Mme de Cassagnac arrives to collect her otter coat; she had her whole trousseau at Doucet's for 11,000 francs. Marie finds it "ennuyeux d'entendre annoncer Mme de Cassagnac quand on essaye une robe".
- **19 August 1879** (086.0102). The "Worth et Laferrière" refrain above.
- **15 October 1880** (090.0114). She complains that Doucet and others have copied the crossed draperies and pointed sash she has worn for two years.
- **21 March 1881** (091.0335, 091.0337). Doucet makes her a Directoire or Consulat costume in white crêpe de Chine for M. de Dalmas's fancy-dress ball.
- **27 September 1881** (093.0146). Her white Louis XV muslin dress at the seaside is so admired that everyone asks Rosalie for the dressmaker's address: "donc gloire à Doucet". The English footnote identifies him.
- **5 November 1881** (093.0337). "Je me suis achetée un chapeau et vu Doucet."
- **13 January 1882** (094.0167). A dress made "par Doucet et moi de compagnie", a near copy of Greuze's *La Cruche cassée*, with a garland of Bengal roses.
- **20 April - 20 June 1882** (094.0421, 095.0029, 095.0147, 095.0199). Fittings. On 7 May she states her dress bill: "peut-être huit mille francs par an, pas plus chez Doucet", where women spending fifty or sixty thousand dress like the cocottes. On 20 June she says she wanted to paint "le Salon d'essayage chez Doucet" but turned to a different subject (*Les Saintes Femmes*).
- **15 February and 22 May 1883** (098.0341, 099.0528). Conferences with "M. Jean" at Worth's and Doucet's, three dresses ordered; a letter from Doucet about a bodice.
- **1 May 1883** (099.0305). "Chez Doucet et Gelot pour des chapeaux", with Gervex, Julian and Carrier-Belleuse.
- **5 November 1883** (102.0133). Her claim: after years of working "pendant des heures chez Laferrière, Worth ou Doucet", draperies, jabots and negligent sashes came into fashion, and "les façons les plus courues chez Doucet sont de mon invention. Et rien de cela ne porte mon nom."

## Lucien Doucet, painter (1856-1895)

%% GLO_DOUCET.0009 %%
Henri-Lucien Doucet was born in Paris on 23 August 1856 and died at Saint-Leu-d'Esserent (Oise) on 31 December 1895. A pupil of Jules Lefebvre and Gustave Boulanger, he won the Prix de Rome in 1880 and was at the Villa Medici from 1881 to 1884; he later taught at the Académie Julian (1888). Best known for *Après le bal* (1889) and portraits (Princess Mathilde; Robert de Montesquiou, 1879), he received a first-class pastel medal in 1889 and the Légion d'honneur in 1891. (En. Wikipedia, "Henri Lucien Doucet", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Lucien_Doucet; Orsay artist index; Villa Médicis residents list.)

%% GLO_DOUCET.0010 %%
- **17 July 1880** (089.0177). The day the Prix de Rome result reaches Julian's: "C'est Doucet l'élève favori de Julian qui a le prix de Rome." Mlle de Villevieille offers a punch upstairs and Julian pays for another below; they shout "Vive la Commune!" in jest. (Her "élève favori de Julian" is her wording; Lefebvre taught at Julian's.)
- **24-25 June 1883** (100.0207, 100.0210, 100.0212, 100.0227). At the École des Beaux-Arts she sees the Rome "envois". Of the two paintings she can look at, she finds Schommer's "abominable, vide, bête, poncif" and Doucet's *Ave Maria* "exquise": a white-walled town in sunlight, the Virgin in white, an angel in a flowered yellow gown; on second view the colour is "ravissante" and the handling reminds her of Bastien-Lepage and Morot.
