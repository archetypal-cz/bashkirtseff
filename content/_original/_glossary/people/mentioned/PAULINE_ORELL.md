# Pauline Orell

**Research Status**: Basic
**Last Updated**: 2025-01-23
**Diary Coverage**: Up to 1881-02-24

A pseudonym adopted by Marie Bashkirtseff: "la citoyenne Pauline Orell", her name for attending the feminist society Le Droit des femmes (Hubertine Auclert's circle) in Dec. 1880 (1880-12-02, 1880-12-08; Mme de Daillens used "Alexandrine Norscott"), later reused as a disguise.

## Diary References
- December 2 and 8, 1880: Marie chooses the name for the feminist society and attends under it.
- February 24, 1881: Marie disguises herself as "Pauline Orell" to visit the Folies-Bergère. She wears a thick veil and black wig (which makes her face look much broader). She tries on Alexis's male clothes but can't button them, so reverts to the female Pauline Orell disguise.

## Analysis
The name is Marie's invention (RSR 2026-10-21, correcting an earlier note that treated it as a possible stage character); no external referent has been identified.

The disguise allows Marie to explore transgressive spaces (the Folies-Bergère's demi-monde) while maintaining a layer of protection and plausible deniability.

## Notes
Further research needed to determine if "Pauline Orell" refers to a real person or is purely Marie's invention.
