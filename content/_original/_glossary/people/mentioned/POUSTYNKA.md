# Poustynka

## Overview
A house, probably a country house or dacha, that Marie knew in her childhood in Russia, with walls covered in bark. The word is Russian, *пустынька*, a diminutive of *пустынь* (a hermitage, a place apart); the diary uses it as a proper name. The Mon Journal t.2 index has no entry for it.

## Relevance to Marie
It appears once, in a dream of 15 November 1873: she dreams of the villa Gioia being refurnished for Lord Hamilton, and «toute la maison était couverte d'écorces de bois comme Poustynka» (012.0221). So the house had a bark-clad exterior (or interior) that the dream borrows. The context of the dream is Marie's grief for the Duke of Hamilton ([DUKE_OF_HAMILTON](../core/DUKE_OF_HAMILTON.md)) after the Nice winter.

## Identity uncertain
We do not know where Poustynka was or whom it belonged to; the diary mentions it only in passing, as if the reader knew it. If you can tell us, we would be glad to hear from you.
