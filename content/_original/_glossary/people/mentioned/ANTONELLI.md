# Count Pietro Antonelli (1853-1901)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0002 %%
**Research Status**: Comprehensive
**Last Updated**: 2026-05-24
**Diary Coverage**: Carnets 050-103, concentrated in 054-068 (Feb 1876 - Feb 1877)
**Diary References**: ~350+ across ~130 entries spanning carnets 050-103
**Type**: Person -- romantic interest, later diplomat and explorer

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0003 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0004 %%
[Count Pietro Antonelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Antonelli) (Rome, April 29, 1853 -- at sea, January 11, 1901) was an Italian nobleman, diplomat, and African explorer who became one of Marie Bashkirtseff's most significant romantic interests during her extended stay in Rome from January to May 1876. The nephew of [Cardinal Giacomo Antonelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Antonelli) (1806-1876), the powerful Vatican Secretary of State often called the "Italian Richelieu," Pietro occupied a singular position at the intersection of papal and secular Roman society. Marie's intense, ambivalent attachment to him -- never quite love, never quite indifference -- dominates approximately six months of diary entries and produces some of her most psychologically penetrating writing.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0005 %%
After the end of his involvement with Marie, Pietro reinvented himself entirely: at age 26 he departed for the Horn of Africa as an explorer, became the Italian ambassador to Ethiopia at the court of [Menelik II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelik_II), negotiated the controversial [Treaty of Wuchale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Wuchale) (1889), served as a Deputy in the Italian Parliament (1886-1895), and died of yellow fever contracted in Rio de Janeiro at the age of 47.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0006 %%
## Biographical Information

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0007 %%
### Family and Background

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0008 %%
Pietro was the youngest of three sons of Count Luigi (Louis) Antonelli and Countess Camilla Folchi. His brothers were Domenico (Dominique), the eldest, and Paolo (Paul). The family's prominence derived entirely from Cardinal Giacomo Antonelli, Luigi's brother, who as Secretary of State to [Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX) from 1848 until his death in 1876 was effectively the temporal ruler of the Papal States and then the most powerful figure in the Vatican after the Pope himself.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0009 %%
Marie recorded the family structure in extraordinary detail after Pietro narrated it to her (059, April 29, 1876):

> Il y a d'abord et avant tout le cardinal-diacre Jacques Antonelli. Ce grand personnage avait quatre frères dont un, celui qui était directeur de la banque est mort sans enfants laissant sa fortune au père de Pietro. Il lui en reste donc trois, dont un n'a pas d'enfants et n'en aura pas, les deux autres en ont. Le premier a un fils qui, marié à la fille de la comtesse Garcia depuis huit ans déjà, n'a pas d'enfants et n'en aura pas. [...] Et enfin il y a le comte Louis Antonelli marié à la comtesse Camille Folchi dont il a eu trois fils: Dominique, Paul et Pierre.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0010 %%
The Antonelli family were [Black Nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_nobility) -- those Roman aristocratic families who remained loyal to the papacy after the [Capture of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Rome) in 1870 and refused to recognize the Italian state. This political allegiance was central to the family's identity and to the obstacles Marie faced. The Cardinal's own origins were in [Sonnino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnino), near [Terracina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracina), in the old Papal States.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0011 %%
### Physical Appearance and Character

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0012 %%
Marie's descriptions of Pietro are detailed and contradictory, reflecting her oscillation between fascination and clear-eyed critique. On first identifying him (054, Feb 15, 1876):

> Le comte Antonelli ressemble à Gautier qui est parfaitement beau comme on sait.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0013 %%
She returned repeatedly to his eyes, which she found irresistible:

> Il a des yeux adorables, surtout lorsqu'il ne les ouvre pas trop, ses paupières qui recouvrent la prunelle au quart, donnent à ses yeux une expression qui me monte la tête et me fait battre le cœur. (054, Feb 28)

> Antonelli a des yeux splendides. (060, May 13)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0014 %%
But she was equally sharp about his chin, which she analyzed with almost phrenological precision:

> Antonelli a un menton long et arrondi et qui, s'il devenait prêtre, deviendrait un de ces mentons gras, fuyants et hypocrites, comme on en voit souvent chez les prêtres catholiques. (060, May 13)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0015 %%
His character she summed up in a single contrast with [Doria](DORIA.md):

> Doria, majesté, glace et terreur, Antonelli gaieté, coquetterie et charme. (055, Mar 5)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0016 %%
Pietro was physically vigorous and impulsive -- he had run away from home at 17 to join the army, he wrestled Walitsky on the floor for sport, he ate the silk threads from Marie's dress fringe, he jumped from walls to retrieve her dropped bouquet. Rossi's nickname for him was "pazzerello" (little madman). Marie found him charming but ultimately unserious: "Antonelli n'est qu'un enfant, entièrement dépendant de ses parents" (055, Mar 10).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0017 %%
### Early Life and the Convent Episode

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0018 %%
Pietro's youth was marked by rebellion against his family's papalist expectations. In 1870, during the [Capture of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Rome), the 17-year-old Pietro attended a demonstration where Italian nationalists were whistling at papal supporters. When someone shouted an insult, Pietro punched the man, sparking a general brawl. He was taken to the Austrian embassy for safety, sent home, and scolded by the Cardinal himself. Furious at the Cardinal's rebuke, he ran away to join the Italian army -- a scandalous act for a papal family (060, May 13-14).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0019 %%
After his military service, he compounded the scandal by joining the Circolo della Caccia (Hunting Club), whose honorary president was Prince Humbert (the future [King Umberto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umberto_I)). Italian newspapers seized on the story: "le comte Pierre Antonelli, neveu du cardinal qui était entré au Caccia-Club dont le prince Humbert est le président honoraire" (060, May 13). The Vatican was furious. Pietro's brothers Domenico and Paul pressured him until he was effectively exiled to a monastery for 17 days as punishment. Marie referred to this episode extensively, nicknaming Pietro "le Moine" (the Monk).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0020 %%
### Financial Situation

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0021 %%
Pietro's financial position was a crucial factor in Marie's calculations. As the youngest of three sons, he would inherit only a quarter of the family fortune (the eldest receiving half under Italian inheritance law). Moreover, the great wealth depended on two deaths: the Cardinal's and their father Luigi's, who at 64 was robust and could "vivre facilement encore dix ou quinze" years (059, April 29).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0022 %%
In the meantime, Pietro was entirely dependent on his family. He had no carriage (riding in borrowed ones or fiacres), no watch, and crushing debts. Marie noted with humiliation:

> J'ai demandé, mais si vous saviez comme les Antonelli sont durs, je vous assure qu'ils sont durs ! (055, Mar 14)

> J'étais vexée de le voir dans un misérable fiacre, tandis que tant de canailles ont des voitures et des chevaux. J'étais fâchée, humiliée pour lui. (055, Mar 14)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0023 %%
After Cardinal Antonelli died on November 6, 1876, the inheritance proved disappointing. The Cardinal bequeathed about 623,341 gold francs to his four living brothers and two nephews -- but pointedly excluded Pietro, who "had become an anticlerical Italian nationalist." Brother Paul received 30,000 francs of annual rent. Meanwhile, the Cardinal's alleged illegitimate daughter, Countess Loretta Lambertini, launched a scandalous lawsuit against the family that dragged through the courts (068, Jan 1, 1877).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0024 %%
## The Romance with Marie (February-May 1876)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0025 %%
### First Encounter and Identification (February 15, 1876)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0026 %%
Marie first noticed Pietro at the theater in Rome but could not identify him. It was Rossi who named him from his theater box (054, Feb 15):

> Rossi vient nous voir et de suite on lui demande qui est ce monsieur. C'est le comte Antonelli, le neveu du cardinal. Bigre ! il ne pouvait pas être autre chose.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0027 %%
Marie's response was immediate and characteristically self-dramatizing:

> J'ai donc regardé Antonelli et je l'ai bien vu. Il est charmant, si charmant que je me déclare tout haut amoureuse de lui en ajoutant que je n'ai pas de chance et que ceux que je regarde ne me regardent pas !

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0028 %%
### Carnival Courtship (February 16-29, 1876)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0029 %%
The Roman Carnival provided the setting for the early phase of their flirtation. During the Corso parades, Marie and her party pelted acquaintances with confetti from their balcony and carriage. On February 22, masked in white dominos with indigo stripes, they ambushed Pietro:

> Voilà Antonelli ! Il reste tout étourdi par notre furieuse attaque et par les cris de: Pietro ! carino ! angelo !

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0030 %%
On February 24, Pietro demonstrated the aristocratic impetuousness that thrilled Marie. When a peasant seized a bouquet meant for her, Antonelli physically wrested it from the man's hands -- "c'est la manière d'agir. Personne n'aurait fait cela, qui irait se battre avec un paysan pour un bouquet ordinaire du carnaval ! Antonelli et personne d'autre."

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0031 %%
The last day of Carnival (February 28) brought Pietro to Marie's balcony and then to their hotel for an extended evening visit -- their first sustained private conversation. Marie named the problem that would define the entire relationship: "Comment nommerai-je l'homme ? Le comte Antonelli est trop long et empesé pour l'emploi journalier. Antonelli, on pourrait le confondre avec le cardinal" (054, Feb 15). She settled on "cardinalino" -- little cardinal.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0032 %%
### The Horseback Ride and Declaration (March 7-8, 1876)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0033 %%
The emotional climax of the romance came during a horseback ride in the Roman Campagna outside the Porta del Popolo. The scene is among the most cinematically vivid in the entire diary. Marie and Pietro rode out with Plowden, Loebecke, and Walitsky, but maneuvered to be alone. On a trackless mountain above the Tiber, among shepherds whom Marie compared to Faustulus and Acca Laurentia (the foster parents of Romulus), Pietro declared his love.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0034 %%
Marie threatened him with her riding whip but was deeply moved. Then her horse bolted:

> Mon cheval passa à un galop très rapide, j'essayai de le retenir, mais il prit la carrrière. La plaine était grande, je courais et mes efforts étaient vains. [...] J'entendais Antonelli derrière moi [...] j'eus envie de sauter par terre, mais le cheval allait comme un trait. C'est bête d'être tuée ainsi pour rien, pensai-je, car je n'avais plus de force, il faut qu'on me sauve. (055, Mar 8)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0035 %%
Pietro saved her. Afterwards, "the verb to love was declined on every tone." He revealed he had fallen in love at first sight at the opera (La Vestale), and confessed he thought of her at 3 AM at his club. He told her he would turn 23 in April -- making him almost exactly Marie's age (she turned 18 in November 1876, but he was actually 22, turning 23 on April 29).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0036 %%
### Piano-Side Declarations (March 5 and 16, 1876)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0037 %%
Pietro made two elaborate declarations of love at the piano in Marie's salon, positioned in a corner of the large drawing room where they could speak privately while Marie played. On March 5, he kissed her cheek -- the first physical intimacy -- which she found outrageous. On March 16, the scene escalated: he threatened to shoot himself, to jump from a window. Marie played what she called the "coquette de glace" (ice coquette):

> Plus il y mettait de sérieux et de chaleur plus je riais et plus j'étais froide. [...] Au milieu d'une phrase toute de tendresse et d'amour je disais quelque chose d'irrésistiblement drôle. (055, Mar 16)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0038 %%
### Marie's Cold Analysis

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0039 %%
Throughout the romance, Marie maintained a ruthless analytical distance. She desired Pietro but never deceived herself about the marriage prospects:

> Je ne peux pas consentir à être sa femme. (055, Mar 16)

> Si Antonelli est riche, j'aime, s'il est pauvre je n'aime pas. Je crois que je dis juste. (056, Mar 28)

> Antonelli n'est pas un mari. Il n'a même pas de montre. (055, Mar 11)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0040 %%
She ranked him precisely in her hierarchy of suitors:

> J'ai déjà eu trois passions malheureuses: Hamilton, Audiffret et Torlonia. Merjeewsky, Bruschetti et Antonelli; les trois comtes sont une compensation pour les autres trois. (056, Apr 4)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0041 %%
And she analyzed the dynamic between them with devastating clarity:

> Ce n'est pas de l'amour que j'ai pour vous, me dit Antonelli, c'est une passion. (056, Mar 28)

> Si je donnais un coup à Antonelli il ne me le rendrait pas, tandis que Torlonia me le rendrait et m'en donnerait deux en plus. (056, Mar 28)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0042 %%
### Family Opposition and Crisis (March 30 - April 1876)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0043 %%
The romance collapsed under family pressure. Baron Visconti revealed to Marie's mother that the Antonelli family -- the old Cardinal, Pietro's father -- absolutely opposed any marriage with Marie. The Pope himself would make difficulties. The reasons were religious (Marie's family was Orthodox, not Catholic) and social (the Bashkirtseffs' financial situation was opaque and their social standing uncertain).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0044 %%
Marie was devastated but characteristically defiant:

> Pourquoi s'opposer à ce mariage ? La religion. Bah ! Pourvu que les enfants soient catholiques, le reste importe peu. (056, Mar 30)

> Non, mais concevez-vous cette audace ! Le Pape ne permettra pas ! Que veulent-ils donc pour un troisième fils dévergondé, dépravé et que sais-je encore ! (056, Mar 30)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0045 %%
The crisis deepened when Pietro was sent to a monastery (the convent of San Giovanni e Paolo) for several days during Holy Week -- ostensibly for religious retreat, but Marie suspected punishment. She made the anagram of his name: "Pietro Antonelli = Il étonne l'apôtre" (He astonishes the apostle) and agonized: "Malheureux Pietro ! Qu'en a-t-on fait ! Ces maudits jésuites l'ont empoisonné ou étouffé ou noyé dans le Tibre" (056, Apr 7).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0046 %%
### Last Meetings in Rome and Naples (April-May 1876)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0047 %%
After the family's opposition became clear, the relationship entered a prolonged, ambiguous twilight. Marie left Rome for Naples in mid-April but left instructions at her hotel: "si le comte Antonelli venait on lui dira que je suis à Naples" (058). She returned to Rome briefly in May, and the final meetings (carnet 060, May 13-14) produced some of the most emotionally intense writing: an intimate foot-pressing episode in a carriage at the Torre di Quinto races, Pietro's extended narration of his family history, and a scene where Marie read from her own diary to him by candlelight:

> J'ai senti son haleine dans mes cheveux car je lisais tête baissée. Et, loin de fuir, je n'ai pas bougé, lui non plus. (060, May 13)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0048 %%
### The Aftermath (1876-1884)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0049 %%
Marie never saw Pietro again after May 1876, but he haunted her diary for years. Returning to Rome in December 1876, she searched for him obsessively at the Pincio, the opera, and on the Corso, while her mother "tient sa tête retournée, comme un damné de Dante" scanning crowds for his face (068, Dec 26). She learned the Cardinal had died on November 6, that the family was impoverished, and that gossip in Nice had distorted their relationship into a rumor that she had been engaged to the Cardinal's nephew and jilted.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0050 %%
By January 1877, she tried to close the chapter: "Eh bien voilà qui est une affaire finie, expliquée, racontée. Et que je ne me parle plus d'Antonelli. Assez" (068, Jan 7-9). But he continued to surface -- in dreams (071, May 1877: "Depuis quelques jours je rêve d'Antonelli et il me revient à l'idée"), in nostalgia triggered by reading Lamartine's Jocelyn (072, July 1877: "ce n'est pas Alexandre pour sûr, c'est plutôt Rome, notre sombre balcon, la pluie et Antonelli se sauvant exaspéré par mes taquineries"), and in her cataloguing of life's romantic episodes (072, July 1877: "Audiffret, Antonelli, Alexandre" -- the three A's). As late as 1884 (carnet 103), his name still appears in passing.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0051 %%
Her final emotional assessment, rereading her old journals in May 1877, was characteristically self-aware:

> J'ai feuilleté l'époque d'Antonelli [...] C'est vraiment surprenant comme je raisonnais. J'en suis émerveillée et remplie d'admiration. [...] j'étais assez inquiète qu'on ne crût à un amour (passé) pour le comte Antonelli. Dieu merci on ne peut pas le croire, grâce à ce cher journal. (071, May 30)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0052 %%
## Later Life and Career (1879-1901)

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0053 %%
After the Cardinal's death and the collapse of the family's Vatican-era wealth, Pietro reinvented himself dramatically. In 1879, at age 26, he left Italy for the Horn of Africa as an explorer -- a trajectory eerily consistent with Marie's observation that he was a restless, impulsive young man unsuited to the drawing rooms of Roman aristocracy.

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0054 %%
His African career was distinguished: he explored territories from 1879 to 1881, succeeded Orazio Antinori as Italian ambassador to Ethiopia at the court of Emperor Menelik II, and negotiated the [Treaty of Wuchale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Wuchale) on May 2, 1889. This treaty was intended to establish an Italian protectorate over Ethiopia, but a critical discrepancy between the Italian and Amharic versions of Article 17 -- "consente" (consents) versus a word meaning "may" -- led to the disastrous [First Italo-Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Italo-Ethiopian_War) (1895-96) and the Italian defeat at [Adwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Adwa).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0055 %%
Pietro served as a Deputy in the Italian Parliament for the Right (1886-1895), representing the Roma I constituency, and briefly held office as Undersecretary for Foreign Affairs under Prime Minister [Francesco Crispi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Crispi) (December 1893 - June 1894). He was later appointed minister plenipotentiary to Buenos Aires (1894) and transferred to Rio de Janeiro (1897), where he contracted yellow fever. He died on January 11, 1901, at age 47, attempting to return to Italy by steamship.

## Significance in the Diary

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0059 %%
The Antonelli episode is one of the diary's richest and most psychologically complex romantic narratives. It differs from Marie's other attachments in several ways:

- **Reciprocity**: Unlike Hamilton, Audiffret, or Torlonia, Pietro actively and repeatedly declared his love. Marie had a genuine suitor to refuse rather than an indifferent object to pursue.
- **Intimacy**: The horseback rides, piano-side conversations, foot-pressing in the carriage, and the candlelight diary-reading scene achieve a level of physical and emotional intimacy unmatched elsewhere in the diary.
- **Cold calculation**: Marie's simultaneous romantic involvement and mercenary analysis ("Si Antonelli est riche, j'aime, s'il est pauvre je n'aime pas") is at its most nakedly honest.
- **Social stakes**: The Vatican's involvement, the Black Nobility politics, the papal opposition, and the gossip scandal in Nice gave the episode real-world consequences that Marie's other infatuations lacked.
- **Duration**: The affair spans roughly six months of active involvement (Feb-May 1876 in carnets 054-060), with echoes persisting for years afterward (through carnet 103, 1884).

%% GLO_ANTONELLI.0060 %%
Marie's retrospective self-annotation, added marginally to one of the early entries, captures her mature judgment:

> Prière une fois pour toute de ne pas trop accorder d'importance à ce que j'écrivais d'Antonelli. Je l'embellissais pour créer un roman. (055, Mar 6)
