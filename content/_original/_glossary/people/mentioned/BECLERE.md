# Béclère ("le petit Béclère")

## Overview

A young Parisian doctor Marie calls "le petit docteur de la rue de l'Echiquier" (105.0019) and, familiarly, "le petit Béclère" (105.0021, 105.0100) — "petit" here meaning young/junior, not physical stature. He examines Marie in early July 1884, disagrees with [Potain](POTAIN.md)'s relatively reassuring reading of her condition, and personally brings her ("Il me mène"/"c'est le petit Beclère qui m'y mène") to consult the senior specialist [Grancher](GRANCHER.md).

## Identity — unresolved, flagged as ambiguous

No documentary link to a specific "Dr. Béclère" has been confirmed for this diary entry; the identification below is a plausible candidate, not a certainty.

The most prominent physician named Béclère in this period is **Antoine Béclère** (1856-1939), later famous as the founder of French radiology and radiotherapy — but radiology did not yet exist in 1884 (Röntgen's discovery of X-rays was in 1895), so any connection here would be to his early general-medical career, not his later specialty. Antoine Béclère completed his *internat des hôpitaux* in Paris 1877-1881 (at Saint-Louis and Necker, in dermatology, ophthalmology, and pediatrics) and defended his thesis in 1882, meaning that by July 1884 he would have been a 28-year-old physician a few years past his internship — plausibly consistent with Marie's "petit Béclère" (a junior doctor young enough to be so described, already active enough to be consulted and to escort a patient to a senior specialist). He did not become a titled hospital physician (*médecin des hôpitaux*) until 1893, so in 1884 he would still have counted as a junior figure in the Parisian medical world.

This identification is **not confirmed** by any secondary source found; it rests only on the plausibility of the timeline and the surname. An unrelated Béclère (a relative, e.g. Antoine's father Claude Béclère, also a physician per some sources) cannot be ruled out. Treat with appropriate caution; do not present as settled fact in translation or footnotes without flagging the uncertainty.
