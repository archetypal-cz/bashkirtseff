# Montebello

## Overview

A name of the Lannes de Montebello family (dukes of Montebello, descendants of Napoleon's marshal Lannes; en.wikipedia «Duc de Montebello»). The tag covers two people:

- **019.0373 (27 May 1874):** «cette héroïne du duel Montebello, Metternich» — a Montebello who fought, or was said to have fought, a duel with a Metternich. See «Identity uncertain» below.
- **093.0368 (15 November 1881):** «Cassagnac s'est battu en duel avec son concurrent malheureux, M. de Montebello et l'a blessé». This is **Adrien Lannes de Montebello** (1851–1935), who stood against Paul de Cassagnac in the Mirande constituency (Gers) at the election of 21 August 1881 and lost (8,793 votes to 11,016; fr.wikipedia «Adrien Lannes de Montebello»). They fought with swords on 12 November 1881 and Montebello was wounded in the right arm (Ferréus, *Annuaire du duel 1880–1889*; Karen Offen, *Paul de Cassagnac and the Authoritarian Tradition in Nineteenth-Century France*, 1991, citing *Le Pays* of 10 November 1881). Confidence 0.9. The Mon Journal t.14 index has «Montebello (M. de) fonctionnaire : 251».

«La comtesse de Montebello» of 1880–1883 (087.0777, 087.0792, 087.0810, 098.0522) now has her own entry, [COMTESSE_DE_MONTEBELLO](../aristocracy/COMTESSE_DE_MONTEBELLO.md) (split 2026-10-22, B-84).

## Identity uncertain

We are not sure who the Montebello of the 1874 duel is. At Laferrière's on 27 May 1874 Marie sees Gioia and la Soubise come out of a side salon and writes: «J'ai vu cette fée de la mode, cette héroïne du duel Montebello, Metternich, elle m'a paru très laide» (019.0373; Mon Journal t.3 p.205, print checked; the t.3 index lists «Montebello, 205» and «Metternich, 205» with no first names). The heroine may be Gioia or la Soubise (see the RSR note at 019.0373). No record of a Montebello–Metternich duel was found (web searches 2026-10-22; the duel claimed in an older RSR note, «1870, over Giulia Barucci», has no source and is withdrawn; Barucci died in 1871, while Marie's Gioia is alive in 1874). Contemporary press (Gallica, *Le Figaro*, *Gil Blas* 1870–1874) is the likely place to find it. If you can identify this duel, we would be glad to hear from you.
