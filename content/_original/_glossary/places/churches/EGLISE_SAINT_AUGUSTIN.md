## Overview

%% GLO_EGLISE_SAINT_AUGUSTIN.0002 %%
Saint-Augustin is the Paris parish church in the 8th arrondissement, at the end of the Boulevard Malesherbes, built by Victor Baltard between 1860 and 1871 (Wikipedia, «Saint-Augustin, Paris») with a pioneering metal frame behind its stone facing and a Romanesque-Byzantine design. Under the Second Empire it was a monumental landmark of the new Haussmann quarter, and for the Bonapartists of the Third Republic it was «l'église bonapartiste» (Marie, 079.0339): the church where the exiled dynasty's feasts and funerals were celebrated.

%% GLO_EGLISE_SAINT_AUGUSTIN.0003 %%
## Relevance to Marie

%% GLO_EGLISE_SAINT_AUGUSTIN.0004 %%
The church is Marie's stage for Bonapartist Paris and for [#Cassagnac](../../people/recurring/CASSAGNAC.md): on 15 August 1877 (the Emperor's feast) she goes to the one o'clock mass, where «tout le monde avait l'air d'être venu plutôt pour Paul de Cassagnac que pour Napoléon III ou IV» (073.0430); in March 1878 she tries to find out whether Mme Boje is «la femme blonde de l'église Saint-Augustin au 15 août» (079.0007); the funeral of Prince Lucien Murat is held there (079.0339, April 1878); she contrasts the quiet Cassagnac wedding of 1878 with «les bruyantes ovations de Saint-Augustin» (081.0098) and remembers his habit of slipping in by the side door (082.0245); in June 1879 she attends the memorial mass for the Prince Imperial (085.0453, 085.0490).
