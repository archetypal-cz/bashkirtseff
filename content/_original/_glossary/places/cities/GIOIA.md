## Identity

%% GLO_GIOIA.0001 %%
**Mme Amélie Gioia** (full name revealed by Marie in carnet 086, entry 1879-09-09: "la maîtresse du duc de Hamilton se nommait Amélie Gioia"). Classified in the published diary indexes as "Gioia (Mme Amélie), demi-mondaine." Italian by birth or descent (the name Gioia is Italian, meaning "joy" or "jewel"). Active in Nice and Paris society from at least the early 1870s.

%% GLO_GIOIA.0002 %%
She is one of the most frequently referenced figures in the diary across its first seven years, appearing in approximately 40 carnets (001-086) and referenced over 200 times. She served two narrative functions: first as the Duke of Hamilton's mistress and Marie's romantic rival (1873-1874), then as Emile d'Audiffret's lover and Marie's social rival (1875-1876), and finally as a figure of nostalgic reflection (1877-1879).

## Physical Description

%% GLO_GIOIA.0003 %%
Marie's physical descriptions of Gioia, accumulated across many entries:

- **Height**: Tall, half a head taller than Marie (045.1875-09-28)
- **Age**: About 35 during the Audiffret affair in autumn 1875; Marie later says she had the appearance of "une fleur épanouie et sur le point de s'effeuiller" (a fully opened flower about to lose its petals) (047.1875-10-16)
- **Hair**: Blonde, possibly dyed — "elle, elle se teint" (she dyes her hair) (007.1873-07-26); Marie copies her hairstyle "à la Gioia" (038.1875-08-04)
- **Build**: Well-made, with square, high shoulders (032.1875-05-14)
- **Bearing**: Majestic, queenly — "elle n'a plus l'air d'une reine" (she no longer looks like a queen) when Hamilton leaves (014.1873-12-23); "vraiment majestueuse" (038.1875-08-11)
- **Beauty**: Dazzling when dressed up ("éblouissante, ébouriffante, incomparable, surtout de port, de manière, de taille" — 015.1874-01-17) but "chiffonnée et médiocre" without toilette; "vieille et passable" without makeup, "jeune et belle" with it
- **Resemblance**: Strikingly resembles the Duke of Hamilton himself — "ils se ressemblent d'une façon saisissante" (072.1877-06-29)
- **Expression**: Has "l'expression de la toute-puissance" (the expression of all-powerfulness), shared only with Princess Souvoroff and the Duke among all people Marie knows (022.1874-07-25)

## Nicknames and Code Names

%% GLO_GIOIA.0004 %%
Marie and her circle use various names for Gioia:

- **La Gioia** — with the French article, used in carnet 007 frontmatter (La_Gioia)
- **Centifolia** — "the hundred-petaled rose" (Rosa centifolia), Marie's poetic code name; used in carnets 002-005 and later. Marie eventually rejects it: "Non, elle n'est pas la duchesse Centifolia, ni Laïs non plus, elle est simplement la Gioia" (carnet 002)
- **Laïs** — classical allusion comparing her to the famous Greek courtesan (014.1873-12-27); paired with Hamilton as "Alcibiade"
- **Mme Pirate** — coined during the Audiffret affair after the operetta *Girofle-Girofla*, where pirates kidnap the character Girofla; Gioia "kidnapped" Audiffret (047.1875-10-14)
- **bijou de mon âme** — "jewel of my soul," ironic endearment playing on "Gioia" = Italian for "jewel/joy" (024.1874-10-02)
- **c'tte femme** / **C'teu** — dismissive contractions of "cette femme" (this woman), used as code in the index of carnet 046
- **cocotte** — "kept woman," used contemptuously
- **femelle** — "female" (animal connotation), used in fury
- **rosse** — "worthless nag," used during the Audiffret crisis

## Chronological Arc in the Diary

### Phase 1: Hamilton's Mistress and Marie's Rival (January 1873 - December 1873)

%% GLO_GIOIA.0005 %%
**First appearance**: January 19, 1873 (001.0023) — "Il nous dépassa pour aller chez Gioia" (He overtook us to go to Gioia's). Marie sees the Duke of Hamilton's carriage heading toward Gioia's residence. From this first mention, Gioia is established as the woman who holds the Duke.

%% GLO_GIOIA.0006 %%
**The rival dynamic**: Marie observes Gioia from a distance throughout the 1873 Nice winter season, never speaking to her directly. Key moments:
- Feb 18, 1873: Gioia wears "a magnificent dark red velvet dress"
- Feb 20, 1873: Marie theorizes Gioia retains the Duke "not so much with her beauty as with habit, words, a thousand artifices!"
- Feb 24, 1873: "Mais Gioia, oh! que je la hais!" (But Gioia, oh how I hate her!)
- Mar 4, 1873: Duke's yacht departs; Marie believes Gioia has left with him; claims he keeps her as "un meuble nécessaire" (a necessary piece of furniture)
- Mar 9, 1873: "He will leave Gioia for me, I am sure" / "I will be better than Gioia! Yes! with God's help!"

%% GLO_GIOIA.0007 %%
**Gioia's departure for Russia**: April 18, 1873 (004) — "Gioia est partie, on dit pour la Russie, pour cette affaire d'héritage que Simonoff lui laissa." Gioia leaves Nice for Russia regarding an inheritance from Simonoff, a wealthy Russian protector. This reveals she had financial arrangements with multiple men. Marie's family discusses this openly.

%% GLO_GIOIA.0008 %%
**The Princess's conversation** (July 26, 1873, carnet 007): The Princess predicts Marie will marry Hamilton. Extended discussion about Gioia: "Nous tuerons Gioia, nous l'anéantirons ensemble!" (We'll kill Gioia, we'll annihilate her together!). The Princess reveals Gioia's children are not all from Hamilton — "il y en a de Simonoff, le marchand russe, et d'autres" (some from Simonoff, the Russian merchant, and others). Crucially, the Princess calls Gioia "une cocotte" — confirming her status as a courtesan, not an aristocrat.

%% GLO_GIOIA.0009 %%
**Post-Hamilton marriage** (October-November 1873): After Hamilton marries Lady Mary Montagu (confirmed by *Galignani's Messenger*, November 9, 1873), Marie's attitude toward Gioia shifts to identification and sympathy:
- Oct 15: "Comme j'étais heureuse l'hiver dernier!... je voyais Gioia... Tout allait bien" (How happy I was last winter... I would see Gioia... All was well)
- Oct 18: "Pauvre Gioia, où est-elle?" (Poor Gioia, where is she?) — genuine concern for her rival
- Oct 19: Family debates whether Gioia was Hamilton's wife; papa says he doesn't believe Hamilton left Gioia, but Marie reveals he married Lady Montagu
- Oct 31: "Gioia m'intéressait à cause du duc" — admits Gioia only interested her because of Hamilton, but acknowledges she is "remarquable dans son genre" (remarkable in her way)
- Nov 4: Gioia's villa is being redecorated — Marie hopes she'll return
- Nov 15: Elaborate dream about Gioia's villa being refurnished for "Lord Hamilton qui va revenir"
- Dec 27: Classical allusion — Gioia as "Laïs" to Hamilton's "Alcibiade"

### Phase 2: Object of Fascination (1874 - Summer 1875)

%% GLO_GIOIA.0010 %%
Marie's feelings evolve from hatred to obsessive admiration:
- Jan 17, 1874 (015): At opera — "elle était si belle, si belle, si belle que la respiration m'a manqué" (she was so beautiful that my breath stopped). Mother says: "Je suis heureuse qu'Hamilton ne l'ait pas vue aujourd'hui"
- Sep 29, 1874 (024): At Walery's photography studio, Marie sees two portraits of Gioia and begs to buy one — refused as against house rules. Obsessively seeks her photograph through intermediaries (Bouba promises to obtain one)
- Oct 2, 1874 (024): First near-encounter — Marie and Gioia pass each other on the Promenade, recognize each other, Marie blushes. She contemplates speaking to her: "c'est si commode ici, un matin, il n'y aura personne, elle passera et je tâcherai de lui parler"
- Oct 7, 1874 (024): Marie names her pet monkey "Gioia" — a significant psychological detail
- Feb 11, 1875 (029): At the opera — "J'aime Gioia parce qu'il l'a aimée et c'est un indicible bonheur pour moi que de la regarder" (I love Gioia because he loved her); "Gioia et moi = une étincelle" (Gioia and I = a spark)
- Feb 25, 1875 (030): "Gioia était incomparable aujourd'hui et Hamilton ne pouvait choisir une autre femme, celle-là seule est digne de lui" — and six consecutive nights dreaming of Gioia
- Jul 30, 1875 (037): Marie visits Gioia's empty villa — enters through the garden, describes every room in vivid detail, is deeply moved but disappointed: "Pas pour la Gioia. Elle manque de fantaisie, de grâce, de luxe."

%% GLO_GIOIA.0011 %%
**Growing desire for contact** (August 1875):
- Aug 4, 1875 (038): Gets hair styled "à la Gioia"
- Aug 10, 1875 (038): "J'ai un penchant irrésistible vers Amélie Gioia. Il faut lui parler, à la première occasion je lui parlerai. Il y aura quelque chose entre nous, mon génie me le dit." — Marie's first use of Gioia's full first name. This passage also reveals her almost romantic fascination
- Aug 11, 1875 (038): "J'ai vu Gioia, je ne veux plus partir" — delays departure from Nice to remain near her

### Phase 3: The Audiffret Affair (September - November 1875)

%% GLO_GIOIA.0012 %%
In autumn 1875 (carnets 045-051), Gioia takes Emile d'Audiffret ("Girofla") as a lover — the same young man who had been courting Marie. This creates a dramatic triangle:

**Gioia's household revealed**: She lives in a villa on or near the Promenade des Anglais (no. 77), with a garden, grille, terrace, and theater box. The household includes at least two young children, an older woman ("la vieille"), a concierge named Balestro, and a chambermaid named Louise (049.1875-11-09).

%% GLO_GIOIA.0013 %%
**Key events**:
- Sep 27, 1875 (045): Marie's aunt spots Audiffret near Villa Gioia after dinner
- Sep 28, 1875 (045): Marie and aunt see Audiffret walking with Gioia on the Promenade — "Voilà Gioia, dit ma tante, avec Girofla." Marie's devastating realization: "la réalité et le rêve" — in her fantasy, Audiffret shot himself for love of her; in reality, "j'en suis à la Gioia"
- Oct 1, 1875 (045): Marie stakes out Gioia's villa with Sabatini, deducing that Audiffret spent the night — she watches the fiacre and calèche movements at 10:30 am. "Je me suis promenée rose d'habit et d'humeur, j'ai vu Gioia à sa porte"
- Oct 6, 1875 (046): Marie and Olga write anonymous threatening letters to Audiffret's father, parade past Gioia's house
- Oct 12, 1875 (046): At performance of *Girofle-Girofla* — "Girofla est enlevée par les pirates!" triggers coded conversation about Gioia
- Oct 14, 1875 (047): "Mme Pirate" nickname coined. Marie and Gioia exchange laughing looks — their first direct visual interaction: "nous nous sommes tout racontés en une minute et nous avons ri de tout, elle de l'avoir enlevé, moi de l'avoir perdu." Marie's extraordinary declaration: "je l'aime tout en la voulant étrangler, elle m'empêche" (I love her while wanting to strangle her, she stands in my way)
- Oct 16, 1875 (047): Extended debate about beauty — mother compares Gioia (fading flower) to Marie (rosebud about to bloom)
- Oct 21, 1875 (047): "Gioia est partie" — Gioia leaves Nice

%% GLO_GIOIA.0014 %%
**Marie's self-awareness during the crisis**: "Moi, la petite Marie, hier une enfant, en face de Gioia une courtisane au commencement de son déclin! Cela me hausse!" (Me, little Marie, yesterday a child, face to face with Gioia a courtesan at the beginning of her decline! That elevates me!)

%% GLO_GIOIA.0015 %%
**The couturière connection**: Gioia dresses at Laferrière (the famous Paris couturière), as does Marie. When Gioia begins wearing white — Marie's signature color — Marie is furious: "Gioia s'est habillée en blanc, cette saleté s'habille en blanc!" (053.1876-01-26). Audiffret, who knows Gioia's wardrobe, recognizes Laferrière corsages "entre mille" (034.1875-06-19).

### Phase 4: Later References (1876-1879)

%% GLO_GIOIA.0016 %%
After the Audiffret crisis, Gioia fades from the daily narrative but resurfaces periodically:
- Mar 2, 1876 (055): "O Gioia!" — Marie uses the name as an Italian exclamation of joy in Rome (playing on the word's meaning), no longer referring to the person
- Mar 5, 1876 (055): Retrospective reflection — "pendant Hamilton j'admirais Gioia, pendant Audiffret Robenson" (during Hamilton I admired Gioia, during Audiffret [I admired] Robenson) — showing Marie's pattern of fixating on her rivals
- Jun 15, 1876 (062): Marie hires a new chambermaid and loves her "parce qu'elle se nomme Amélie, comme Gioia" — revealing Gioia's first name was Amélie
- Jul 9, 1876 (063): "J'ai vu ma chère Gioia" in Paris
- Jun 29, 1877 (072): Obtains Hamilton's photograph from Berthe's album. Sees resemblance between Hamilton and Gioia: "ils se ressemblent d'une façon saisissante." Reports that "Gioia a eu un fils de lui" (Gioia had a son by Hamilton)
- Aug 19, 1877 (073): Reading Ouida's novel *Ariadne* — the heroine is also named Gioia. Marie's extended retelling (25+ paragraphs) of the fictional Gioia's story resonates deeply: "j'accepterais à l'instant le sort de Gioia" (I would accept Gioia's fate in an instant). The real and fictional Gioias merge in Marie's imagination
- Sep 9, 1879 (086): **Final mention** — "Et si on veut remonter tout à fait haut on trouvera que la maîtresse du duc de Hamilton se nommait Amélie Gioia." Marie is reflecting on the pattern of women named Amélie in her life, and Gioia becomes simply a historical reference point

## Gioia's Residence: Villa Gioia

%% GLO_GIOIA.0017 %%
The Villa Gioia (or "chez Gioia") was located on or near the Promenade des Anglais in Nice, no. 77. It stood near the Villa Léon (024.1874-10-02). Features described by Marie when she visited the empty villa (037.1875-07-30):
- Small garden with tropical plants, entered from the Rue de France
- Bronze negroes holding cushions on the ground-floor balcony
- Elegant entrance hall, then salon with cretonne in oriental style, multitude of bronzes, statues, bibelots
- Two immense divans
- Second salon in grey and blue wool
- Dining room in old oak with fine antique porcelain
- Marble staircase to the first floor
- A terrace (which Marie later copies for her own villa — 033.1875-06-06)

The villa was occasionally put up for sale or rearranged (011.1873-10-28, 012.1873-11-04).

## Gioia's Children and Patrons

%% GLO_GIOIA.0018 %%
Gioia had at least two children. Their paternity was a matter of gossip:
- Some children were fathered by Simonoff ("le marchand russe"), a wealthy Russian protector who died and left her an inheritance (004.1873-04-18, 007.1873-07-26)
- At least one son was reportedly fathered by the Duke of Hamilton — "Gioia a eu un fils de lui" (072.1877-06-29)
- The children lived with Gioia at the villa, attended by "la vieille" (an older woman, likely a nurse or companion)

%% GLO_GIOIA.0019 %%
**Known patrons/companions** (chronological):
1. **Simonoff** — Russian merchant, died before April 1873, left her an inheritance
2. **The Duke of Hamilton** — William Alexander Louis Stephen Douglas-Hamilton, 12th Duke (1845-1895); their relationship ended when he married Lady Mary Montagu in 1873
3. **An unnamed Englishman** — a companion who departed just before Audiffret's arrival in 1875
4. **Emile d'Audiffret** ("Girofla") — a young Nicois viveur, from September 1875

## Role in Marie's Psychology

%% GLO_GIOIA.0020 %%
Gioia is among the most psychologically significant figures in the diary. Marie's relationship to her evolves through several distinct phases:

1. **Hatred** (early 1873): Pure jealousy — "oh! que je la hais!" Gioia is the obstacle to Marie's fantasy of marrying the Duke
2. **Strategic dismissal** (spring 1873): Gioia as "meuble nécessaire" — a necessary accessory the Duke will discard
3. **Identification** (autumn 1873): After Hamilton's marriage, Marie and Gioia are fellow sufferers — "Pauvre Gioia, où est-elle?"
4. **Obsessive admiration** (1874-1875): Marie wants to speak to her, obtain her photograph, copy her hairstyle, visit her villa. The fascination becomes almost romantic: "J'ai un penchant irrésistible vers Amélie Gioia"
5. **Rivalry renewed** (autumn 1875): Gioia takes Audiffret, and Marie oscillates between fury and grudging respect
6. **Ambivalent love** (late 1875): "Je l'aime tout en la voulant étrangler" — the mature articulation of competing feelings
7. **Nostalgic distance** (1876-1879): Gioia becomes a symbol of Marie's youth, referenced in passing with affection

%% GLO_GIOIA.0021 %%
Marie's fascination with Gioia also reveals her complex attitudes toward the demimonde. Despite her class prejudice, Marie is drawn to these women: she finds the Saxe (another courtesan) "sympathique" and fantasizes about becoming a courtesan herself for one year "pour écraser et anéantir toutes les autres" (045). Her observation that Mme Prodgers copies Gioia's walk "but nobody except me would guess it" shows Marie's keen social eye.

## Gioia as Catalyst

%% GLO_GIOIA.0022 %%
Marie explicitly identifies Gioia as someone who makes things happen in her life: "Cette femme me fait mouvoir" (This woman makes me move — 047.1875-10-14). Gioia's presence rouses Marie from lethargy. Her absence leaves Nice feeling empty: "Nice n'est plus Nice. Gioia n'est plus" (011.1873-10-15). When Gioia returns, "je ne suis plus endormie grâce à Dieu" (I am no longer asleep, thank God).

The transformation of jealousy into ambition is one of the diary's key themes. Marie's plan to become a celebrated singer was partly motivated by wanting to surpass Gioia: "I will be better than Gioia! Yes! with God's help!" (March 9, 1873).

## Historical Context

%% GLO_GIOIA.0023 %%
No external biographical records for Amélie Gioia have been identified beyond the diary and its published indexes. She is classified consistently as "demi-mondaine" in all published indexes (carnets 02, 03, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, 13). The world of the *demi-monde* (literally "half-world") — women who lived in luxury maintained by wealthy men but were excluded from respectable society — was a recognized feature of Second Empire and early Third Republic France. Nice, as an international winter resort, was a particular center of this milieu.

The name "Gioia" (Italian for "joy" or "jewel") may well be a *nom de guerre* rather than a birth name, as was common practice among courtesans of the period. Marie explicitly plays on the Italian meaning: "bijou de mon âme" (jewel of my soul), "O Gioia!" (Oh Joy!), and "questa cara Gioia di Schlangenbad" (this dear Joy of Schlangenbad — 040.1875-08-30).

## The Fictional Gioia (Ouida's *Ariadne*)

%% GLO_GIOIA.0024 %%
In August 1877, Marie reads *Ariadne* (1877) by Ouida (Louise de la Ramée, 1839-1908), whose heroine is also named Gioia. The fictional Gioia is a sculptor raised on Homer and Virgil who walks to Rome, is seduced and abandoned by the beautiful poet Hilarion, goes mad, creates a masterpiece statue, and dies in Hilarion's arms. Marie devotes one of the diary's longest literary critiques to this novel (073.1877-08-19, 25+ paragraphs) and identifies with the fictional Gioia: "j'accepterais à l'instant le sort de Gioia." The merging of the real Amélie Gioia with her fictional namesake shows how deeply both women resonate with Marie's self-image.
