# Stabat Mater (Rossini)

%% GLO_STABAT_MATER_ROSSINI.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_STABAT_MATER_ROSSINI.0003 %%
Rossini's *Stabat Mater* sets the medieval sequence for soloists, chorus and orchestra. He began it in Spain in 1831 on a commission from Fernández Varela and completed only part of it, leaving the rest to Giuseppe Tadolini; after Varela's death his heirs sold that partial version to a Paris publisher, Rossini disowned it, and he wrote the missing numbers himself in 1841. The complete work was first performed at the Théâtre-Italien's Salle Ventadour in Paris on 7 January 1842 (Wikipedia, «Stabat Mater (Rossini)»). It dates from the years after Rossini had stopped writing opera. It is not Pergolesi's *Stabat Mater* (1736), which is a different work for two solo voices; see [Stabat Mater (Pergolesi)](STABAT_MATER_PERGOLESI.md).

%% GLO_STABAT_MATER_ROSSINI.0004 %%
## Marie and the Stabat Mater

%% GLO_STABAT_MATER_ROSSINI.0005 %%
**24 March 1875, Nice.** "Ce soir on chante le Stabat Mater, éclairage a giorno, (robe blanche, Worth, coiffure avec faux cheveux, très bien) Nadia et les enfants sont avec moi" (030.0341). The date falls in Holy Week; Easter Sunday 1875 was 28 March. Marie does not name the composer or the venue.

%% GLO_STABAT_MATER_ROSSINI.0006 %%
**9 November 1875, Nice.** In her mock letter to Audiffret's father announcing that his son will be crowned *Rosière*, the guests include "M. Cresci, des chœurs masculins et féminins du Théâtre Italien, qui chanteront plusieurs morceaux du "Stabat Mater"" (049.0187). The passage is satire; the choruses of the Italian opera are listed among the absurd witnesses of Audiffret's "vertu". See [Cresci](../../people/mentioned/CRESCI.md) and [Émile d'Audiffret](../../people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_STABAT_MATER_ROSSINI.0008 %%
Marie never names the composer in either paragraph. Rossini's setting is the likely one for 049.0187, because it has a chorus and Italian opera companies gave it in concert, while Pergolesi's is for two solo voices; for 030.0341 the composer is open, and the diary's tag to the Pergolesi entry in that paragraph is therefore uncertain. The settings that Marie did identify as Pergolesi's are the Naples performances of March and April 1877 (see the Pergolesi entry).
