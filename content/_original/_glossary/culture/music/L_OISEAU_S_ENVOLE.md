# "L'oiseau s'envole" (Paul et Virginie)

%% GLO_L_OISEAU_S_ENVOLE.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_L_OISEAU_S_ENVOLE.0003 %%
"L'oiseau s'envole / Là-bas, là-bas / Et ne revient pas" is the refrain of a song from Victor Massé's opera *Paul et Virginie* (libretto by Jules Barbier and Michel Carré, after Bernardin de Saint-Pierre), first given in Paris in 1876; the Researcher's note on 071.0207 cites the phonobase.org record "Paul et Virginie ; l'oiseau s'envole". Marie calls it a "chanson de vieux nègre dans Paul et Virginie" (068.0215), a period label for the song of the old slave in Massé's opera. The opera has its own entry: [Paul et Virginie (Victor Massé)](../theater/PAUL_ET_VIRGINIE_MASSE.md); the novel is [Paul et Virginie](../literature/PAUL_ET_VIRGINIE.md). The earlier diary passages (below) identify the song.

%% GLO_L_OISEAU_S_ENVOLE.0004 %%
## Marie and "L'oiseau s'envole"

%% GLO_L_OISEAU_S_ENVOLE.0005 %%
**13 August 1878, Soden.** "Je chante depuis deux jours 'l'oiseau s'envole' parce qu'il n'y a pas jusqu'aux laveuses de vaisselle qui ne chantent. On dit que l'on se marie" (082.0068), with the gossip of the spa about weddings. Later the same day: "Je chante 'l'oiseau s'envole' et on profane cette céleste musique par une plaisanterie…" (082.0080); when the [Hohenlohe princes](../../people/mentioned/HOHENLOHE_PRINCES.md) ("les Gans") pass in the evening she hums instead a popular street song, "Quand les canards vont tous en tas…" (082.0081), which has not been identified.

%% GLO_L_OISEAU_S_ENVOLE.0006 %%
**Earlier uses of the same song:**

%% GLO_L_OISEAU_S_ENVOLE.0007 %%
- 18 December 1876, Nice: Marie recalls and quotes the verses from memory, "cette chanson de vieux nègre dans Paul et Virginie", and adds "Je n'ai pas de maison, moi ! Je n'ai pas de nid" (068.0215, 068.0216; the Linguistic Annotator warns that the lines are an opera lyric, rhymed, and must be rendered as verse).
- 9 May 1877, Florence: telling the story of the comb she gave to Larderel, she recalls the first sheet of notepaper she gave him her address on, painted with a pale-blue bird flying into the clouds, leaving behind a half-erased heart, "avec ces vers de 'Paul et Virginie'" (071.0206, 071.0207).
