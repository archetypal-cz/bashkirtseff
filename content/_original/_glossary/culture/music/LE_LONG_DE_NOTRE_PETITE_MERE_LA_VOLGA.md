# Le long de notre petite mère la Volga

%% GLO_LE_LONG_DE_NOTRE_PETITE_MERE_LA_VOLGA.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LE_LONG_DE_NOTRE_PETITE_MERE_LA_VOLGA.0003 %%
Marie's French rendering of the Russian folk song «Вниз по матушке по Волге» ("Down Mother Volga"), as the diary's own footnote and the earlier linguistic note identify it (034.0021). It is a well-known Russian song, sung by choirs and soloists and arranged by composers including Glazunov, whose choral arrangement was published by Belaieff (publisher's catalogue listing, ficksmusic.com). Its origin and the text current in 1875 were not established here, and no free full-text link was found; the glossary does not link a text that cannot be tied to the version Marie knew.

## Marie and the song

%% GLO_LE_LONG_DE_NOTRE_PETITE_MERE_LA_VOLGA.0005 %%
**7 June 1875, Nice.** On the ride back into town after an outing: "Ensuite Yourkoff a entonné Le long de notre petite mère, la Volga, et nous avons repris en chœur. Et enfin on est entré en ville à onze heures du soir en poussant des cris et des aboiements et des miaulements féroces" (034.0021). [Yourkoff](../../people/mentioned/YOURKOFF.md) leads and the party joins in the chorus; the paragraph also mentions the [Volga](../../places/landmarks/VOLGA.md).

%% GLO_LE_LONG_DE_NOTRE_PETITE_MERE_LA_VOLGA.0006 %%
**9 June 1875.** Marie's aunt reports that Girofla (her nickname for [Émile d'Audiffret](../../people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md)) has thought since Monday about the song Marie sang, [*Dormi pure*](DORMI_PURE.md), and its words: "J'ai cru entendre aux paroles de Le long de notre petite mère mais ce serait un non-sens" (034.0114). Marie wonders whether he had the words of the Russian song in mind and dismisses the idea.
