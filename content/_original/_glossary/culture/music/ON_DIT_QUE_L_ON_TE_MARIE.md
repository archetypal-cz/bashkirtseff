# "On dit que l'on te marie" (Ay Chiquita)

## Overview

A sentimental romance whose first line is «On dit que l'on te marie, / Tu sais que j'en vais mourir». It is the French version, by Paul Bernard, of the Spanish song *Ay, Chiquita* (also called *Las amonestaciones*) by the Spanish (Basque) composer Sebastián de Iradier (Yradier), published in Paris by Heugel. Source for this identification: a post on the Nabokov Society list (https://nabokovsociety.org/node/795), which quotes the first stanza and the refrain, "Qui voudrait m'aimer… et qui meurt, qui meurt pour toi". I have not independently confirmed the Heugel imprint or the translator; treat these details as secondary-source only. The 1878 repertoire claim below is Marie's own text. No full-text link is given because I did not find a verifiably public-domain scan.

## Marie and this work

A tune she sings or hears again and again, with guitar, from 1878 to 1884. The earlier RSR footnotes call it simply a popular sentimental song of the drawing-room guitar repertoire.

- 081.0501 (20 Jul 1878, Soden): asked to sing it with her mandolin and guitar and her Neapolitan songs; "attendrissement général".
- 081.0509 (21 Jul 1878): one of the two princes tries it on the piano and cannot find the ending, which irritates her.
- 081.0624 (5 Aug 1878): the princes play the Russian anthem, *On dit que l'on te marie* and the [Marseillaise](LA_MARSEILLAISE.md).
- 085.0054 (7 May 1879, Paris): she stops humming the song, "que j'ai toujours dans la bouche", when she sees Cassagnac on the street.
- 091.0616 (12 May 1881): sings it with guitar the day the rumour of Gambetta's marriage is denied.
- 103.0248 (2 Feb 1884): "Une banale petite chanson… et ça après coup. Pas de vers." among her first-love music.
