# Concert of sacred music, Società Filarmonica, Naples (25 March 1877)

## What it was

A "concerto vocale e strumentale di musica sacra" given by the amateur [Società Filarmonica](../../people/mentioned/SOCIETA_FILARMONICA.md) of [Naples](../../places/cities/NAPLES.md) on **Sunday 25 March 1877** (Palm Sunday in 1877; Easter fell on 1 April), with singers drawn from the Neapolitan aristocracy and a volunteer orchestra of gentlemen "dilettanti". Marie, her mother, [Dina](../../people/core/DINA.md) and Olga Hamontoff attended. She pasted the printed programme into her diary (069.0518-0520), having announced it in 069.0516 ("Ce soir concert à la Filarmonica dont voici le programme").

## The programme (as printed)

**Parte prima**
1. Haydn: the second sonata of the *Seven Last Words*, for string instruments (Haydn's quartet version of *Die sieben letzten Worte unseres Erlösers am Kreuze*).
2. Miceli: soprano solo from a *Stabat* (Sig.ra Sambon).
3. Ed. Moreaux: *Rimember!*, meditation for melodium (Baron Galiani).
4. Mozart: *Lacrimosa*, quartet "nel Requiem" for soprano, contralto, tenor and bass.
5. Pergolesi: soprano solo and soprano-contralto duet from the *Stabat Mater*.
6. Gounod: *Ave Maria* on the first Prelude of S. Bach, for soprano, violin, melodium and piano.

**Parte seconda**
1. E. Burelle: *La Penitenza di Beethoven*, transcribed for violin, melodium and piano.
2. Mozart: *Agnus Dei*, soprano solo.
3. Miceli: *Libera me Deus*, soprano solo from the *Miserere*.
4. Rossini: *La Carità*, chorus.

Followed by the orchestra list (twelve violins, three violas, five cellos, three double basses). The "melodium" is a reed-organ of the harmonium family.

## Marie and the concert

Her account of the evening is in 069.0521-0535. She arrived in fear that everyone would be wearing black; it turned out they were in colours and bare shoulders; she moved to the Fabbricatore seats; the orchestra of dilettanti received "une salve de reconnaissance et d'approbation"; M. Fabbricatore played in a Mozart quartet and the duke of San Martino the double bass. Between pieces she held court: the duke of Melito said he preferred "Les noces de Jeannette" to "cette musique de Pergolèse et Mozart"; she was introduced to the marchese di Campomarino and Count Caracciolo; the duke of San Cesario told her she was "dorée sur tranches". She ends: "Vous remarquez sans doute que je n'ai pas encore retrouvé l'art de remplir des pages."

## Notes and doubts

- **Identified**: Haydn (*Seven Last Words*, quartet version), Mozart (Requiem), Pergolesi (*Stabat Mater*), Gounod (*Ave Maria* on Bach's first Prelude from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*), Rossini (*La Charité*, one of the three *Chœurs religieux*, for women's voices). The IMSLP links point to scores of these works, not to the arrangements actually used in Naples.
- **Not identified**: "Miceli" (a Neapolitan composer; the best candidate is Giorgio Miceli, 1836-1895, born in Reggio Calabria and trained at the Naples Conservatory, per Wikipedia, but no sacred *Stabat* or *Miserere* by him has been found); "Ed. Moreaux, *Rimember!*"; "E. Burelle", transcriber of a "Penitenza di Beethoven" (presumably the Beethoven song known as the "Bußlied"); the Mozart *Agnus Dei* solo.
- The Società Filarmonica itself could not be traced in this pass; no history of the society, or of its "duca Galiani" members, has been found. The tag on 069.0516 points to the Cercle Philharmonique stub; whether that is the same society was not established.
- Marie's own wording and most surnames in the concert paragraphs are in the diary; this entry gives the programme the paragraph IDs and the context.
