# "Si j'étais reine" (la mélodie de Caen)

%% GLO_MELODIE_DE_CAEN.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_MELODIE_DE_CAEN.0003 %%
A sentimental romance beginning "Si j'étais reine au beau pays de France", which Marie calls "la mélodie de Caen" and sings "pour me calmer". The words, as she copies them, are a love-song of renunciation: if the singer were queen of France she would give her sceptre, her position and her power for a single day of the beloved's gaze; but she is neither a noblewoman nor a queen, and has "rien que l'amour de mon âme, rien ici-bas", and if the beloved will take her "toit solitaire" he will be king there (080.0526-0548). Neither the author nor a printed title has been identified, and no text from outside the diary is linked here. The label "de Caen" is Marie's own and is not explained in the diary.

%% GLO_MELODIE_DE_CAEN.0004 %%
## Marie and "Si j'étais reine"

%% GLO_MELODIE_DE_CAEN.0005 %%
**9 June 1878, Paris.** "Pour me calmer je chante 'la mélodie de Caen', 'Si j'étais reine au beau pays de France !' mélodie charmante tout à fait vieille et d'une sensibilité parfaite pourvu de ne pas rire : non, moi j'aime ces vieilles chansons et c'est si bête de s'en moquer. Lisez plutôt c'est un bijou" (080.0525). She then copies the whole song, one verse line per paragraph (080.0526-0548), keeping the oddities noted by the Linguistic Annotator, such as the doubled "sceptre" in 080.0528 and the two verses run together in 080.0542, and closes: "C'est bête, c'est bête mais c'est bien bon" (080.0549). This is a rare case of Marie preserving a whole popular song in the diary, and one of her defences of "ces vieilles chansons".

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_MELODIE_DE_CAEN.0007 %%
- Marie's line "Si j'étais reine" occurs elsewhere as a daydream of her own (043.0119, 047.0066, 049.0280, 064.0417), unrelated to the song; for this reason the entry uses only the full opening line and the label "la mélodie de Caen" as aliases.
- Public domain status is unknown because the author is unknown; the text is quoted here only by incipit and paraphrase.
