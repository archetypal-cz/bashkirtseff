# L Italiana in Algeria

%% GLO_L_ITALIANA_IN_ALGERIA.0002 %%
## L'Italiana in Algeri

%% GLO_L_ITALIANA_IN_ALGERIA.0003 %%
*L'italiana in Algeri* (The Italian Girl in Algiers) is a dramma giocoso in two acts by Gioachino Rossini, to a libretto by Angelo Anelli, first performed at the Teatro San Benedetto, Venice, in May 1813 (Wikipedia, «L'italiana in Algeri»). Isabella, an Italian woman shipwrecked on the Algerian coast, outwits the Bey Mustafà and escapes with her lover Lindoro. It was a stock comic opera of the Italian houses.

%% GLO_L_ITALIANA_IN_ALGERIA.0004 %%
Marie writes the title as *L'Italiana in Algeria* and, once, *L'Italiano in Algeri*; both are her versions of the Italian title.

%% GLO_L_ITALIANA_IN_ALGERIA.0005 %%
## Marie and L'Italiana in Algeri

%% GLO_L_ITALIANA_IN_ALGERIA.0006 %%
**22 November 1874, Nice.** At the Opéra: "même robe de l'autre soir, ma coiffure, très bien. Je suis de bonne humeur et m'amuse bien qu'il n'y ait presque personne de bon, seulement le comte Arnim celui de l'année passée, Moreno avec sa famille et quatre autres Espagnoles" (026.0030). Count Arnim comes to their box and drives Galula away.

%% GLO_L_ITALIANA_IN_ALGERIA.0007 %%
**28 January 1875.** A word from the opera becomes the household's catchword. Marie writes of *muso* (Italian for muzzle, snout, familiarly face): "muso est un mot que nous disons à tout propos aujourd'hui, il vient de "L'Italiano in Algeri", le comique le dit très drôlement et qu'on nomme ainsi la Chestaskoff, l'Italien et tout ce qui est muso ou ne l'est pas" (029.0100). That the word comes from the opera is Marie's statement; the libretto has not been checked.
