# "God Save the Tsar!" (Russian imperial anthem)

%% GLO_HYMNE_RUSSE_LVOV.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_HYMNE_RUSSE_LVOV.0003 %%
"Bozhe, Tsarya khrani!" (Боже, Царя храни!, "God Save the Tsar!") was the national anthem of the Russian Empire. The tune was chosen in a competition held in 1833 and first performed on 18 December 1833. It was composed by the violinist Alexei Lvov, with words by the court poet Vasily Zhukovsky, and remained the anthem until the February Revolution of 1917 (Wikipedia, "God Save the Tsar!"). Marie writes "l'hymme russe" in the manuscript.

%% GLO_HYMNE_RUSSE_LVOV.0004 %%
## Marie and the Russian anthem

%% GLO_HYMNE_RUSSE_LVOV.0005 %%
**3 August 1878, Soden.** At the end of an evening party in Soden, the company discusses how to make its exit: "Le Docteur propose de faire jouer une polonaise et de l'exécuter à nous seuls; puis on veut faire jouer l'hymme russe et de défiler en triomphe" (081.0605). The Linguistic Annotator reads this as a nationalist display. In the end they leave two by two, to the sound of a galop, instead.

%% GLO_HYMNE_RUSSE_LVOV.0006 %%
**5 August 1878, Soden and Homburg.** "Pour être scrupuleuse il faut dire que mes princes ont joué l'hymme russe. On dit que l'on te marie et la Marseillaise" (081.0624): the young [Hohenlohe princes](../../people/mentioned/HOHENLOHE_PRINCES.md) played, among other pieces, the Russian anthem, [On dit que l'on te marie](ON_DIT_QUE_L_ON_TE_MARIE.md) and [La Marseillaise](LA_MARSEILLAISE.md). They then go on to [Homburg](../../places/cities/HOMBOURG.md), which she finds "ravissant", with "beaucoup d'Anglais comme il faut".
