# Le rossignol che vola

%% GLO_LE_ROSSIGNOL_CHE_VOLA.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LE_ROSSIGNOL_CHE_VOLA.0003 %%
*Lo rossignou que vola* ("The nightingale that flies") is a traditional song of Nice in the local Niçois dialect. Marie writes the dialect *que* as the Italianate *che*. Sources spell it "que vola" or "qué vola": the Phonothèque nationale (Musée de la Parole et du Geste) recorded a *ronde de mai* "Lou roussignol qué vola (Le rossignol qui vole)" sung by Jouan Nicola, president of the Ciamada Nissarda folklore society, in Nice on 27 August 1939 for Roger Dévigne's phonographic mission to the Alpes provençales and the pays niçois (BnF catalogue, notice cb38073115z), and Eugène Bozza (1905-1991) set "Lo rossignou que vola" as no. 4 of his *Cinq chansons niçoises*, op. 43 (LiederNet). Marie calls it "la chanson la plus populaire et la plus jolie de Nice" (059.0826).

%% GLO_LE_ROSSIGNOL_CHE_VOLA.0004 %%
## Marie and the song

%% GLO_LE_ROSSIGNOL_CHE_VOLA.0005 %%
**15 October 1875, Nice.** Outside the church of Sainte-Réparate in the old town, near the Audiffret cloth shop, the women of the market gather round: "j'ai chanté à demi-voix, le rossignol che vola cela les a enthousiasmés et les plus vieilles se mirent à danser, j'ai dit ce que je sais en niçois, en un mot triomphe populaire"; the apple-seller curtseys, crying "che bella regina !" (047.0064).

%% GLO_LE_ROSSIGNOL_CHE_VOLA.0006 %%
**The nickname.** The nightingale is also the girls' code name for [Émile d'Audiffret](../../people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md): Dina uses "rossignol" on 13 October (047.0012), and on 18 October Marie says he has been in Nice incognito "sous le nom du Rossignol che vola" (047.0151).

%% GLO_LE_ROSSIGNOL_CHE_VOLA.0007 %%
**13 February 1876, Rome.** Papers from Nice report that Audiffret gave 200 francs as a prize "pour des masques seuls" and named it "le prix du *Rossignol che vola*"; Walitsky writes that others call him nothing else, and Marie asks whether it is because of the caricature (054.0030). On 13 March, in Rome, Ricardo writes that he would like to send her the portrait of "le rossignoù che vaia en Hamlet", a costume at the Sabatier ball, and she replies that poor Ricardo "pense que le rossignoù che vola m'occupe encore" (055.0685, 055.0686).

%% GLO_LE_ROSSIGNOL_CHE_VOLA.0010 %%
**6 May 1876, Nice — the *Rossignou* in the rue de France.** Marie describes the old Niçois custom of "tourner le mai": a crown or lantern is hung up and people dance beneath it in rounds, singing; the custom has been dying out since Nice became French. She revives it in her own street: "je leur donne un Rossignou je nomme cela ainsi, parce que le Rossignou che vola est la chanson la plus populaire et la plus jolie de Nice" (059.0826) — a great leafy, flowery "machine" hung with Venetian lanterns in the middle of the street, fireworks and Bengal lights set off from the garden wall by Trifon, harp, flute and violin, wine in abundance, and Marie dancing in the street with Giro, Walitsky, Tchernichoff and Dina.
