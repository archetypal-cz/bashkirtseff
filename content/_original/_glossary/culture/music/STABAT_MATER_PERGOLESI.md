# Stabat Mater (Pergolesi)

## Overview

Giovanni Battista Pergolesi's setting of the medieval *Stabat Mater* sequence for soprano and alto soloists, strings and continuo (P.77), composed in 1736 during his last illness, in a Franciscan monastery at Pozzuoli near Naples; he died the same year, aged 26. It became one of the most widely performed and printed sacred works of the eighteenth century. (Source: Wikipedia, "Stabat Mater (Pergolesi)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabat_Mater_(Pergolesi).) The work is public domain.

## Marie and this work

Marie hears it in Naples in March-April 1877, in the city of its composer.

- **25 March 1877** (069.0519, 069.0527): the printed programme of the Società Filarmonica's sacred-music concert, pasted into her notebook, lists a Pergolesi solo and duet "nello Stabat" for soprano and contralto (item 5), alongside Mozart's *Lacrimosa* and Gounod's *Ave Maria*. At the concert the Duke of Melito says he prefers Les noces de Jeannette to "cette musique de Pergolèse et Mozart".
- **27 March 1877** (069.0555): "Les élèves du Conservatoire chantent *Le Stabat* de Pergolèse dans l'église de San Pietro à Mayella" (San Pietro a Majella, the church beside the Naples Conservatory); M. Hamontoff keeps chairs for the family. The footnote there calls it the celebrated sacred choral work of Pergolesi (1710-1736).
- **9 April 1877** (070.0298, 070.0427, 070.0442): it is sung again for Princess Margherita; its "accents divins" fill Marie's ears on the day of her crisis over Larderel; at dawn she writes that she would like to be rich enough to have the Stabat sung to her every day.
