# Giuditta (Paolo Giacometti)

%% GLO_GIUDITTA_GIACOMETTI.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_GIUDITTA_GIACOMETTI.0003 %%
*Giuditta* is a biblical tragedy in five acts by the Italian dramatist Paolo Giacometti (1816-1882), written for Adelaide Ristori, who played the title role. It retells the Book of Judith: the widow who leaves besieged Bethulia, wins the confidence of the Assyrian general Holofernes and cuts off his head while he sleeps. In her memoirs Ristori speaks of "Judith in the Biblical tragedy written expressly for me by my friend and favorite author, the lamented Paolo Giacometti", and recalls a night when she seized the slave Azraele, who has just found her lover's body, and threw her to the ground to end the act (Ristori, *Studies and Memoirs*, 1888). The drama was in print by 1857, when a Spanish edition appeared in Madrid, and an Italian edition with the author's preface and notes followed in 1859 (Internet Archive catalogue records). A Dutch magazine of 1860 describes how Giacometti reworked the Bible story into a five-act drama that lets Ristori show the full range of the role (Nederlandsch Magazijn, no. 26, 1860). An English version was printed in New York in 1866 for her American tour. The date of the first performance is not established here.

%% GLO_GIUDITTA_GIACOMETTI.0004 %%
The actress was among the leading tragediennes of the century. Born in 1822, she made her Paris debut in 1855 and rivalled Rachel there (Wikipedia, "Adelaide Ristori"). Giacometti wrote several other pieces for her, among them *Elisabetta, regina d'Inghilterra* and *Maria Antonietta* (Wikipedia, "Paolo Giacometti"). See [Adelaide Ristori](../../people/artists/ADELAIDE_RISTORI.md).

## Marie and Giuditta

%% GLO_GIUDITTA_GIACOMETTI.0006 %%
%% [#Adelaide_Ristori](../../people/artists/ADELAIDE_RISTORI.md) %%
- **5 April 1876, Rome.** "La célèbre Ristori jouait Judith au profit de l'instruction publique du quartier de Trastevere" (056.0424). The house was so full that Marie saw nobody but the Russians from the Borghese races; her mother was ill and the baroness chaperoned them. Marie does not name the author. The play is identified as Giacometti's because it is the only Judith in Ristori's repertoire that she herself describes as written for her.

%% GLO_GIUDITTA_GIACOMETTI.0007 %%
- **Same entry (056.0425).** Marie has heard that Ristori is sixty (she was fifty-four) and calls it a prodigy: "Elle joue divinement." She finds the subject, the role and the whole period "si loin de nous que nous ne pouvons plus comprendre les situations", yet Ristori, "affectée, enthousiaste et inspirée" in a role that is hard to grasp, kept so much naturalness and feeling that "on est émerveillé". She contrasts her with the "guindage ridicule et empesé" of the tragediennes one hears everywhere: "C'est la première tragédienne qui ait l'air d'une femme et non d'un mannequin affublé d'un costume."

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_GIUDITTA_GIACOMETTI.0009 %%
The bare word "Judith" is not used as an alias because it would also catch Cazin's painting of Judith leaving the city for Holofernes' camp, which Marie mentions at the Salon of 1883 (099.0163), and other biblical allusions. Her only other Ristori performances in the diary are in Schiller's *Maria Stuart* (see [Maria Stuarda](MARIA_STUARDA_SCHILLER.md)).
