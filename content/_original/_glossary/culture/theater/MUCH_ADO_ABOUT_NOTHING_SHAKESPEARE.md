# Much Ado About Nothing (Shakespeare)

%% GLO_MUCH_ADO_ABOUT_NOTHING_SHAKESPEARE.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_MUCH_ADO_ABOUT_NOTHING_SHAKESPEARE.0003 %%
*Much Ado About Nothing* is a comedy by William Shakespeare ([Shakespeare](../../people/mentioned/SHAKESPEARE.md)), thought to have been written in 1598-99 and printed in a quarto of 1600 and in the First Folio of 1623. It is set in Messina and joins two love plots: Claudio and Hero, whose wedding is wrecked by slander, and the quarrelling wits Beatrice and Benedick, whose friends contrive to make them fall in love by letting each overhear the other's supposed passion (Wikipedia, "Much Ado About Nothing"). The title was already an English proverbial phrase for a fuss over something trivial, and that is how Marie uses it.

## Marie and Much Ado About Nothing

%% GLO_MUCH_ADO_ABOUT_NOTHING_SHAKESPEARE.0005 %%
- **19 June 1875, Nice (034.0406).** Marie and Collignon study a white figure with a borrowed spyglass: "Much ado about nothing. C'est une porte dans un des créneaux de la tour gauche qui depuis douze jusqu'à quatre heures paraît blanche parce que les rayons du soleil ... l'éclairent. Voilà tout." The title is a ready phrase for an anticlimax.

%% GLO_MUCH_ADO_ABOUT_NOTHING_SHAKESPEARE.0006 %%
- **29 October 1876, Tchernakowka (067.0374).** Having listened to all the details of the family lawsuit: "J'écoutai tous les détails: «Much ado about nothing» et c'est tout. J'aurai préféré un péril réel et pas de scandale." Again the phrase, in English, in the sense of a great stir over nothing.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_MUCH_ADO_ABOUT_NOTHING_SHAKESPEARE.0008 %%
Neither mention shows that Marie has read or seen the play; both use the proverbial title. Two nearby passages may refer to the play without naming it. On 3 May 1876 at Nice she writes "J'ai lu une comédie de Shakespeare" and finds nothing as amusing as Dumas's novels or Plutarch (059.0686). On 21 December 1879 in Paris she asks "Comment s'appelle déjà la comédie de Shakespeare dans laquelle deux jeunes gens s'aiment parce qu'on leur monte réciproquement la tête de cette manière ?" (086.0762); that description fits Beatrice and Benedick, but Marie does not give the title and the identification is the researchers'.
