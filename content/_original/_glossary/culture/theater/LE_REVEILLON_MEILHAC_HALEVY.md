# Le Réveillon (Meilhac and Halévy)

%% GLO_LE_REVEILLON_MEILHAC_HALEVY.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LE_REVEILLON_MEILHAC_HALEVY.0003 %%
*Le Réveillon* is a comedy in three acts by Henri Meilhac and Ludovic Halévy, first performed at the Théâtre du Palais-Royal, Paris, on 10 September 1872 (Wikipedia, "Henri Meilhac"). It became the basis of Johann Strauss's operetta *Die Fledermaus* (1874), whose librettists Richard Genée and Karl Haffner took its plot (Wikipedia, "Henri Meilhac"). W. S. Gilbert adapted it for the London stage as *Committed for Trial* (1874) and *On Bail* (1877) (same source).

%% GLO_LE_REVEILLON_MEILHAC_HALEVY.0004 %%
Not to be confused with a réveillon in the ordinary sense, the late supper of Christmas or New Year's Eve, as in Marie's account of a supper at the London House in Paris (051.0463).

## Marie and Le Réveillon

%% GLO_LE_REVEILLON_MEILHAC_HALEVY.0006 %%
- **17 January 1874, Nice (015.0224-0225).** "Je voulais voir «Le Réveillon» par Ravel." She had expected the play to open the evening, but "on nous a maltraité par «Lo Specchio Infernale»" ([Lo Specchio Infernale](LO_SPECCHIO_INFERNALE_BALLET.md)). Afterwards she writes "«Le Réveillon» m'a plu": [Gioia](../../people/recurring/GIOIA.md) was in her box, in the same bronze outfit as at Monaco that morning, "si belle, si belle, si belle que la respiration m'a manqué". Marie says nothing about the play itself. "Par Ravel" refers to the actor [Pierre-Alfred Ravel](../../people/artists/PIERRE_ALFRED_RAVEL.md) of the Palais-Royal, who had played in Nice in January 1873 (see his entry); the diary does not give his role.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LE_REVEILLON_MEILHAC_HALEVY.0008 %%
The text calls the piece only "Le Réveillon" and credits Ravel, so the identification with Meilhac and Halévy's comedy rests on the title, the date and Ravel's presence at Nice. The diary does not say which Nice theatre gave it.
