# Giroflé-Girofla

## Overview

*Giroflé-Girofla* is an opéra bouffe in three acts with music by Charles Lecocq and a libretto by Albert Vanloo and Eugène Leterrier. It was first given at the Théâtre des Fantaisies-Parisiennes, Brussels, on 21 March 1874, and in Paris at the Théâtre de la Renaissance on 11 November 1874 (Wikipedia, "Giroflé-Girofla"; IMSLP). Set in 13th-century Spain, it tells of twin brides, one of whom is abducted by pirates; the other twin must pose as both until the first is rescued. Lecocq wrote it as a deliberately far-fetched follow-up to *La Fille de Madame Angot* (Wikipedia).

The Paris production at the Renaissance ran for some 220 performances and was one of the most profitable musical productions of the 1870s; it was played across Europe, the Americas and Australia (earlier glossary note, unsourced).

## Marie and this work

*Giroflé-Girofla* is the "family play" of Marie's Nice winter of 1875 and gives her a whole system of nicknames.

- **9 February 1875, Nice.** At the Théâtre Français (Nice) with her aunt; the second act is ending as they arrive (029.0262).
- **12 and 30 May 1875.** "Girofla" already serves as a name for the young Émile d'Audiffret (032.0408); on 30 May her relatives imagine her "enlevée par les pirates comme dans Giroflé-Girofla" (033.0357).
- **30 September 1875, Nice.** For the return of the popular actor Duplessis the theatre gives *Giroflé-Girofla*, "la pièce de famille comme dit Fiouloulou" (045.0319), because the group's nicknames come from it. Marie goes again on 4 October ("J'adore Giroflé-Girofla, plus on la voit, plus c'est amusant", 046.0101; the friends present a bouquet tied with blue and pink ribbons, "Giroflé-Girofla", 046.0094), on 12 October with the whole party (046.0368) and notes on 27 October that missing an evening is a first (048.0123). On 26 October she says it will become her favourite, like *Alice de Nevers* (048.0059).
- **Nicknames.** "Girofla" is [Émile d'Audiffret](../../people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md), the man who courts and then drops her; "Giroflé" is Olga Sapogenikoff (see [Olga Sapogenikoff](./OLGA_SAPOGENIKOFF.md)). The code name runs from 1875 to 1879 (e.g. 062.0549, 064.0460, 068.0102, 077.0038, 084.0270). In Russia she even calls her father's friend Pacha "presque un Girofla pour ma correspondance" (066.0530).
- **Girofla's lament.** She cites "la complainte de Girofla" (the abandoned heroine's plaintive song) as a prophecy of her own abandonment (055.1001, 056.0524 with footnote, 071.0185). In 038.0274 she sings "Ma belle Girofla" (LAN: a song from the operetta), and on 13 July 1876 she recites "les vers sur Girofla" (063.0207), verses of her own on the nickname.
- **9 December 1876, Nice.** A new performance, which she calls a "pièce historique", with Mme Anitchkoff bringing all her children; between acts the talk turns to Audiffret (067.1476-1477, footnote). On 11 December she watches Girofla arrive in the third act (067.1537).
- **19 January 1877.** "Tout en lisant 'Girofla'" (068.0731), probably the score or libretto.
- **5 May 1881, Paris.** She includes it in a list of operettas, "Barbe Bleue, la Belle Hélène, Orphée, Giroflé-Girofla", which she finds "insipide" (091.0564).

## Open points

- 084.0270 ("la tour de ce pauvre Girofla qui est en Chine", 21 February 1879) uses the nickname for a person; the "Chine" detail is unexplained. Compare the "Tour Audiffret" of 045.0434.
- 088.0153 (12 May 1880, Paris): Marie joins Rosita and her governess "à Giroflé-Girofla"; this most likely means a performance, but is not certain.
- Most later occurrences of "Girofla" are only Audiffret's nickname and not references to the operetta; this page records the work and the code, the person belongs to his own entry.
