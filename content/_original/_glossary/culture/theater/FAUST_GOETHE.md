# Faust (Goethe)

## Overview

*Faust* is the verse drama by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe (1749-1832) on the scholar who sells his soul to Mephistopheles. Part One (*Der Tragödie erster Teil*) was published in 1808 and contains the Gretchen (Marguerite) tragedy; Part Two appeared posthumously in 1832 (Wikipedia, "Faust, Part One"). In Marie's France the work was known largely through Gérard de Nerval's French translation and, above all, through Gounod's opera *Faust* (1859), which compresses the Gretchen story and which Marie knows far better than the drama.

## Marie and this work

- **19 July 1876.** After a poor performance of the opera, she is struck by the absurdity of Faust singing "Salut, demeure chaste et pure!" an hour before ruining the house, and concludes: "Il faudra lire le Faust de Goethe, peut-être la conduite du héros y est-elle motivée" (064.0003). The performance itself is Gounod's opera; the intention to read Goethe is hers.
- **28 July 1876.** "Me voilà en Faust": scholar's study, old German desk, books and papers (064.0287; source footnote glosses the allusion).
- **12 August 1876, Moscow.** Marie, writing of the nihilists, says "Méphistophélès pervertit Marguerite", casting the nihilists as the devil and the Russian people as Gretchen (064.0536; source footnote).
- **September-October 1877.** After reading the poem she was moved by Gabriel von Max's painting "Gretchen" (074.0370: the picture is "la personnification du poème de Goethe... que l'on ne peut l'admirer que si l'on a compris le poème qui aussi m'avait paru une... bizarrerie à la première lecture"), and thinks of Gretchen again on 18 September (074.0379) and 14 October, comparing Verdi's *Aida* to "la Gretchen de Max" (075.0305). Marie therefore did read the poem by September 1877, and found it strange at first.
