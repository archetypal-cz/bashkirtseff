# Simon Boccanegra (Verdi)

%% GLO_SIMON_BOCCANEGRA_VERDI.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_SIMON_BOCCANEGRA_VERDI.0003 %%
*Simon Boccanegra* is an opera with a prologue and three acts by Giuseppe Verdi to an Italian libretto by Francesco Maria Piave, based on Antonio García Gutiérrez's play *Simón Bocanegra* (1843). It was first performed at the Teatro La Fenice in Venice on 12 March 1857 and was a poor popular success; it fell out of favour after 1866. Twenty-three years later Verdi's publisher persuaded him to revise it, with text changes prepared by Arrigo Boito. The revised version, with the now famous Council Chamber scene, was first given at La Scala, Milan, on 24 March 1881 (Wikipedia, "Simon Boccanegra"). The diary's footnote adds that the revised version was first given in Paris on 27 November 1883 (French vocal score, record of the Bibliothèques de Paris, quoted in the source note to 102.0604).

## Marie and Simon Boccanegra

%% GLO_SIMON_BOCCANEGRA_VERDI.0005 %%
**29 December 1883, Paris.** After an evening in the company of Mlle de Pranuelos, the Darhas, de Luynes, the marquise d'Hervey de Saint-Denis and Cabanel, "la crème de Paris", at which she was neither at her best nor at ease, she writes: "On a chercher un nouvel opéra de Verdi, Simon Boccanegra et qui m'a paru ennuyeux" (102.0604). The sentence is loosely written; she evidently went to hear the new Verdi opera and found it boring. The theatre is not named.

%% GLO_SIMON_BOCCANEGRA_VERDI.0006 %%
**3 February 1884, Paris.** "Le Simon Boceanegra, dernier opéra de Verdi (nouvelle manière) est assommant et stupide. Il a chipé à l'allemand tous ses défauts…" (103.0281). "L'allemand" is Wagner (Linguistic Annotator), so the verdict is that Verdi, in his "new manner", has copied the German's faults. Since the revised version reached Paris only in November 1883, it was news to Parisian audiences that season.
