# Le Roi s'amuse (Victor Hugo)

%% GLO_LE_ROI_S_AMUSE.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LE_ROI_S_AMUSE.0003 %%
*Le Roi s'amuse* is a verse drama in five acts by [Victor Hugo](../../people/mentioned/VICTOR_HUGO.md), first performed at the Comédie-Française on 22 November 1832 and banned by the government after one evening. The ministry took the licentious king François I as an insult to Louis-Philippe, and Hugo's lawsuit for the right to continue performing it made him famous as a defender of freedom of expression (Wikipedia, "Le roi s'amuse"). Its hero is Triboulet, the hunchbacked court jester: the old Comte de Saint-Vallier, whose daughter the king has seduced, curses him for mocking him, and the curse falls on the jester's own daughter Blanche. Verdi's *Rigoletto* (1851) takes its plot directly from it (see [Rigoletto](../music/RIGOLETTO.md)).

%% GLO_LE_ROI_S_AMUSE.0004 %%
The play was not seen again at the Comédie-Française until the fiftieth anniversary in 1882, with Mounet-Sully as François I, Got as Triboulet and Julia Bartet as Blanche; it had 19 performances in 1882 and 28 in 1883 (Wikipedia, "Le roi s'amuse"). *La Vie moderne* devoted its issue of 25 November 1882 to the revival, and reproduced Gosselin's drawing of the act IV set by Rubé and Chaperon.

## Marie and Le Roi s'amuse

%% GLO_LE_ROI_S_AMUSE.0006 %%
%% [#Rigoletto](../music/RIGOLETTO.md) %%
- **23 November 1882, Paris.** "C'était hier la 2ème représentation du "Roi s'amuse" et en même temps le cinquantenaire. Naturellement on n'a pas pu trouver de loges" (097.0128). The anniversary date, 22 November, is the day she means by "hier".

%% GLO_LE_ROI_S_AMUSE.0007 %%
%% [#Coquelin](COQUELIN.md) [#Theatre_Francais](../../places/theaters/THEATRE_FRANCAIS.md) %%
- **24 November 1882, Paris.** She comes back "du Théâtre français, la 2ème du "Roi s'amuse"" (097.0141) and is severe: "Ce n'est pas bien joué, c'est même étonnant que ce soit si mal joué dans un tel théâtre." Got as Triboulet "n'est pas tragique, ni drôle, ni méchant"; the jokes of act I fall flat, and Marie thinks Coquelin would have been a perfect Triboulet. In act II the curse ("Ah ! la malédiction") should be fatal and superstitious, but Got "le chante comme dans les théâtres de drames inférieurs", and the cry "Je veux ma fille !" lacks the exhausted, frightening explosion she hears in the lines.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LE_ROI_S_AMUSE.0009 %%
Marie calls both evenings "la 2ème", which cannot be strictly true of two different nights. She may mean the second night of the revival's run on the first date and a later night on the second, or the second public performance of the play in fifty years (as Wikipedia counts it). The numbering is left as she writes it.
