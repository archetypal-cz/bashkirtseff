# Les Pattes de mouche (Sardou)

%% GLO_LES_PATTES_DE_MOUCHE.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LES_PATTES_DE_MOUCHE.0003 %%
*Les Pattes de mouche* ("Fly's Legs", i.e. scrawls) is a comedy in three acts by Victorien Sardou ([Sardou](../../people/mentioned/SARDOU.md)), first performed at the Théâtre du Gymnase, Paris, on 15 May 1860 (Wikipedia, "Victorien Sardou"). It is a comedy of intrigue built round a compromising letter that passes from hand to hand, and is known in English as *A Scrap of Paper*, in an adaptation by J. Palgrave Simpson (1861) (Britannica, "A Scrap of Paper").

%% GLO_LES_PATTES_DE_MOUCHE.0004 %%
## Marie and Les Pattes de mouche

%% GLO_LES_PATTES_DE_MOUCHE.0005 %%
- **6 August 1874, Spa (022.0429).** "Je m'habille avec peine, robe rose, cheveux pendants pour le théâtre «Les pattes de mouches»." Paul, "ce garnement", has won four hundred francs at play and takes a box; they sit in a stage box (avant-scène). She feels so ill that she loosens her bodice and sits behind the curtain.

%% GLO_LES_PATTES_DE_MOUCHE.0006 %%
- **7 August 1874, Spa (022.0458).** "Je me reprochais Boreel mais dans «Les pattes de mouche» j'ai entendu une phrase qui me rassure." She retells the line: when making tea one first pours a few drops of hot water and throws them away to remove the bitterness, and the first cup keeps all the flavour without it. It reassures her about her self-reproach over Boreel; she does not say more.

%% GLO_LES_PATTES_DE_MOUCHE.0007 %%
The same expression turns up in 013.0130, where "pattes de mouche" means Marie's own scrawl; that is the idiom, not the play.
