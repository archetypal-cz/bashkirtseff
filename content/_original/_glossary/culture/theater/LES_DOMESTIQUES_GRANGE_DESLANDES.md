# Les Domestiques (Grangé and Deslandes)

%% GLO_LES_DOMESTIQUES_GRANGE_DESLANDES.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LES_DOMESTIQUES_GRANGE_DESLANDES.0003 %%
*Les Domestiques* is a comedy in three acts "mêlée de chant" by Eugène Grangé and Raymond Deslandes, first performed at the Théâtre des Variétés, Paris, on 18 June 1861 (Center for Research Libraries catalogue record; Wikipedia, "Raymond Deslandes"). Printed editions appeared from 1861 (Internet Archive) and Calmann Lévy reissued it in 1880 (CRL catalogue). Marie's text gives only the title and the length, so this identification is probable and not certain.

%% GLO_LES_DOMESTIQUES_GRANGE_DESLANDES.0004 %%
## Marie and Les Domestiques

%% GLO_LES_DOMESTIQUES_GRANGE_DESLANDES.0005 %%
- **22 February 1873, Nice (002.0064).** At the Français she sees three pieces: "Ismène", "Le joueur de flûte" (an opéra bouffe in one act) and "Les domestiques" "en trois actes, la dernière pièce était très bien". The house is thin. In a box on the ground floor sit Mme and Mlle Bravura, and behind the daughter a prince pays court to her; Marie envies them and wishes that the Duke of Hamilton loved her.

%% GLO_LES_DOMESTIQUES_GRANGE_DESLANDES.0006 %%
Other occurrences of "les domestiques" in the diary mean household servants, not the play. The mention in 007.0335 (the waiters at the Russian restaurant of the Vienna exhibition) is one of them.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LES_DOMESTIQUES_GRANGE_DESLANDES.0008 %%
Only the title, the three acts and the French theatre in Nice point to Grangé and Deslandes's comedy; "Les Domestiques" is a plain title and could cover another piece. The authors of the other two pieces on the bill have not been checked.
