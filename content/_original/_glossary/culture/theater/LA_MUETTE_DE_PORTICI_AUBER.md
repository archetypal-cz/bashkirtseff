# La Muette de Portici (Auber)

%% GLO_LA_MUETTE_DE_PORTICI_AUBER.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LA_MUETTE_DE_PORTICI_AUBER.0003 %%
*La Muette de Portici* ("The Mute Girl of Portici", also *Masaniello* in some versions) is an opera in five acts by Daniel-François-Esprit Auber (1782-1871), with a libretto by Germain Delavigne revised by Eugène Scribe. It was first given at the Académie royale de musique, the Paris Opéra, on 29 February 1828 (title page of the libretto on Wikisource). The scene is Naples in the first and third acts and Portici, on the bay between Naples and Vesuvius, in the second and fourth; the central roles are the Neapolitan fisherman Masaniello and his mute sister Fenella (Wikisource, libretto, list of characters).

%% GLO_LA_MUETTE_DE_PORTICI_AUBER.0004 %%
It is regarded as the earliest French grand opera and is known for its alleged role in the Belgian Revolution of 1830 (Wikipedia, "La muette de Portici"). The diary's own footnote gives the date: a performance in Brussels on 25 August 1830 set off the revolution (note 086.0224.1).

## Marie and La Muette

%% GLO_LA_MUETTE_DE_PORTICI_AUBER.0006 %%
**8 September 1879, Paris.** "Ce soir Hecht nous a offert une avant-scène à l'Opéra. La première de 'la Muette' et une salle très élégante pour septembre." Marie, who is writing at half-past two in the morning, says she dressed with affected simplicity and tight-fitting lines; she had hoped that Joseph would be at the theatre, but his seat, which Hecht had sent him, stayed empty (086.0224). The paragraph is also the one that mentions Gambetta's absence from Paris. "La première" presumably means the first night of the season's revival at the Opéra; the paragraph does not give a cast.

%% GLO_LA_MUETTE_DE_PORTICI_AUBER.0007 %%
**22 March 1880, Paris.** Longing for Italy and Naples, she adds: "C'est tellement vrai que les décors de la Muette me causent une sorte d'émotion" (087.0684). The opera's Neapolitan settings stand for Italy; the line follows from a passage about Naples "le soir".
