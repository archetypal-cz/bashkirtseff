# La Pluie et le beau temps (Léon Gozlan)

%% GLO_LA_PLUIE_ET_LE_BEAU_TEMPS.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LA_PLUIE_ET_LE_BEAU_TEMPS.0003 %%
*La Pluie et le beau temps* is a one-act prose comedy by Léon Gozlan (1803-1866). Its preface explains its odd career. It began as one line on the programme of an evening that Jules Sandeau gave for friends from the Institut and the press, then was called to the Tuileries two days later and to Compiègne, and only afterwards reached the Théâtre-Français, where it was first performed "par les comédiens ordinaires de l'Empereur" in October 1861 (Calmann Lévy edition, 1892, title page and preface). It has four parts: a young widow, the baronne de Gontran (Mme Arnould-Plessy in the first cast), an unknown man, her maid Victorine and the servant Anselme (played by a Coquelin).

## Marie and La Pluie et le beau temps

%% GLO_LA_PLUIE_ET_LE_BEAU_TEMPS.0005 %%
%% [#Soutzo](../../people/mentioned/SOUTZO.md) [#Dina](../../people/core/DINA.md) %%
- **9 January 1880, Paris.** "Nous jouons "la Pluie et le beau temps" de L. Gazlan." Cast: the baronne de Gontran, Marie; the unknown man, Prince Soutzo; Victorine, Dina; Anselme, the Comte de Courtès; prompter, Gabriel; stage manager, the Marquis de Tarente (087.0158). Courtès and Marie play piano and mandolin duets between rehearsals (087.0160).

%% GLO_LA_PLUIE_ET_LE_BEAU_TEMPS.0006 %%
- **12 January 1880, Paris.** At her evening party, with sixty or seventy guests, the comedy "a été vraiment bien jouée pour avoir distribué les rôles mercredi et avoir eu quatre représentations" (she means four rehearsals). Marie is a revelation to herself: "je n'ai jamais eu moins peur de ma vie"; the larger the audience, the less she is intimidated. There are sincere murmurs and applause "à certains mots, gestes, inflexions", and Soutzo makes people laugh near the end "quand il parle de galions" (087.0188). The party falls on the Russian New Year's Eve.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LA_PLUIE_ET_LE_BEAU_TEMPS.0008 %%
Marie spells the author "Gazlan" in 087.0158. The first performance at the Théâtre-Français was in 1861, as the edition of 1892 states.
