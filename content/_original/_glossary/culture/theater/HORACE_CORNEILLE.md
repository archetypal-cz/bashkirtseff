# Horace (Corneille)

%% GLO_HORACE_CORNEILLE.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_HORACE_CORNEILLE.0003 %%
*Horace* is a tragedy in five acts and verse by Pierre Corneille (1606-1684), first performed at the Théâtre du Marais on 19 May 1640 and dedicated to Cardinal Richelieu. It was his second great success after *Le Cid*. Drawn from Livy's account of the combat between the Roman Horatii and the Alban Curiatii (see [Ab urbe condita](../literature/AB_URBE_CONDITA.md)), it sets the patriot Horace against his sister Camille, who curses Rome and is killed by him (Wikipédia, "Horace (Corneille)"). It stayed in the repertory of the Comédie-Française, and it was the part of Camille that the tragedienne Agar played at the Odéon in 1869 (Wikipédia, "Agar (actrice)"). Marie usually calls it by the plural of the title, *Les Horaces*.

## Marie and Horace

%% GLO_HORACE_CORNEILLE.0005 %%
%% [#Agar](../../people/artists/AGAR.md) [#French_Theater_Nice](../../places/social/FRENCH_THEATER_NICE.md) [#Tite_Live](../../people/writers/TITE_LIVE.md) %%
- **1 February 1877, Nice.** She goes to the ground-floor stage box on the left at the French theatre, where "Agar, de la Comédie française", is giving a performance: "J'ai entendu "Les Horaces"" (068.0915). The role Agar played is not stated. Marie reads Livy (*Tite-Live*) afterwards, and her list of Roman images runs on from the Horatii to the Capitol and the Pincio of her own Rome. The source footnote identifies the play as Corneille's tragedy of 1640. See [Agar](../../people/artists/AGAR.md).

%% GLO_HORACE_CORNEILLE.0006 %%
- **30 December 1875.** Earlier she had parodied Camille's imprecation from act IV: "Voir le dernier Romain à son dernier soupir, / Moi seule en être cause, et mourir de plaisir", with "Niçois" in place of "Romain" (051.0179).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_HORACE_CORNEILLE.0008 %%
073.0154 is a different allusion. In a letter to Marcuard of 25 July 1877 Marie writes "Les Horaces, le Serment de Grutli ou la réconciliation que vous êtes appelé à opérer" and asks why "ces grands amis" are cold, since "la corde qui les lie sur mon tableau était solide". The letter is headed by her drawing of Alexandre, Marcuard and Melissano dressed as William Tell (073.0153). Here "Les Horaces" most probably refers to Jacques-Louis David's painting *The Oath of the Horatii* (1784), paired with the Swiss Oath of the Rütli as images of a solemn pact between friends, rather than to Corneille's play. This is an inference; no entry for the painting exists yet.
