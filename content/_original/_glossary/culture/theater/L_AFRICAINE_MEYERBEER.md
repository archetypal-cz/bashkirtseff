# L'Africaine (Meyerbeer)

%% GLO_L_AFRICAINE_MEYERBEER.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_L_AFRICAINE_MEYERBEER.0003 %%
*L'Africaine* ("The African Woman") is a French grand opéra in five acts by Giacomo Meyerbeer (1791-1864) to a libretto by Eugène Scribe. Meyerbeer and Scribe began work in 1837; around 1852 the plot was changed to fictitious events in the life of the Portuguese explorer Vasco da Gama. The copying of the full score was completed the day before Meyerbeer died in 1864, and the Paris Opéra premiered the work in 1865 in a version prepared by François-Joseph Fétis, who restored the earlier title *L'Africaine*; that version was used until 2013 (Wikipedia, "L'Africaine"). The Researcher's note on the paragraph calls it a standard of the grand-opera repertoire.

## Marie and L'Africaine

%% GLO_L_AFRICAINE_MEYERBEER.0005 %%
**9 January 1878, Paris.** "J'ai enfin pris ma première leçon de guitare et nous sommes allés à l'Opéra pour Paul. Blanc et Zurlo. Je me suis ennuyée, on donnait 'L'Africaine' que j'entendais pour la première fois" (077.0140). The outing is for the benefit of her brother Paul, with the friends Blanc and Zurlo; the work leaves her bored, though it was a staple of the repertoire (see [Opéra](../../places/theaters/OPERA.md)).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_L_AFRICAINE_MEYERBEER.0007 %%
On 8 February 1877 (068.1105), in Nice, a quarrel with a friend ends with "ne fais pas l'Africaine", a figure of speech that calls back to the "tu es africaine" of the same scene (068.1073). The Linguistic Annotator's note suggests it may also pun on Meyerbeer's title, with low confidence; it is not a reference to the opera.
