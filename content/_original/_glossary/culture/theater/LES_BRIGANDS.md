# Les Brigands (Offenbach)

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0003 %%
*Les Brigands* is an opéra-bouffe in three acts by Jacques Offenbach ([Offenbach](../../people/mentioned/OFFENBACH.md)), to a libretto by Henri Meilhac and Ludovic Halévy. It was first performed at the Théâtre des Variétés, Paris, on 10 December 1869 (poster of the creation, Bibliothèque nationale de France; Wikipedia, "Les brigands"). A four-act version followed at the Théâtre de la Gaîté on 25 December 1878 (same source). It burlesques Italian brigand opera and the courts that live off it, and it also satirises financiers; its bumbling carabinieri, who always arrive too late, delighted the Paris audience and gave French the saying "arriver comme les carabiniers" (Wikipedia, "Les brigands"; RTBF, "Saviez-vous que l'expression arriver comme les carabiniers d'Offenbach avait un opéra pour origine"). The piece had a great success as the Second Empire ended; the outbreak of the Franco-Prussian War in 1870 cut its first run short (Wikipedia).

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0004 %%
The poster for the creation lists Mlle Aimée as Fiorella, the female lead (reproduced here). Marie's "nouvelle actrice, Mlle Aimée" at Nice in December 1875 may be the same performer; the diary does not say, and this has not been checked beyond the name.

## Marie and Les Brigands

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0006 %%
- **14 January 1874, Nice (015.0185).** A one-line paragraph reading only "Les brigands...". Nothing around it says whether she saw, heard or wanted to see the piece, so this is the least certain mention.

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0007 %%
- **22 July 1874, Spa (021.0483).** After seeing Basilévitch off at the station, the party meets the marquis, the count, Papa and Gericke halfway up the hill. Marie writes "Elle est partie, nous sommes les carabiniers": they have arrived too late, like Offenbach's carabinieri. Marie does not name the opera; the allusion is identified by the editorial footnote.

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0008 %%
- **21 November 1874, Nice (026.0023).** "On donne «Les brigands», je m'habille de blanc et argent et nous allons, mais oh déception !" An indisposed singer means that *Barbe-bleue* ([Barbe-bleue](BARBE_BLEUE_OFFENBACH.md)) is given instead, and Marie goes home to undress and write.

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0009 %%
- **7 June 1875, Nice (034.0047-0052).** Girofla is Marie's nickname for d'Audiffret, taken from the operetta *Giroflé-Girofla* (research note on 043). "Girofla en chantant tous les couplets du monde a chanté celui des Brigands et qui commence ainsi: / Il eut un jour un prince / Joli comme le jour." Marie summarises the rest of the verse, in which ladies die of love for the prince, and pinches her aunt so hard that she keeps quiet. The lines are as Marie writes them; the diary does not say which number of the score she means.

%% GLO_LES_BRIGANDS.0010 %%
- **23 December 1875, Nice (050.1162).** "Ce soir «Les Brigands» avec une nouvelle actrice, Mlle Aimée. J'y vais avec mes Grâces, dans notre vieille avant-scène."
