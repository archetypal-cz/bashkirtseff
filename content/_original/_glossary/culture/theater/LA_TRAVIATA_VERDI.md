# La traviata (Verdi)

## Overview

*La traviata* is an opera in three acts by Giuseppe Verdi to an Italian libretto by Francesco Maria Piave, based on Alexandre Dumas fils's play *La Dame aux camélias* (1852), itself adapted from his 1848 novel. It was first performed at La Fenice, Venice, on 6 March 1853 and was originally titled *Violetta* (Wikipedia, "La traviata"). The title means "the woman led astray".

## Marie and this work

*La Traviata* is the opera that displaces *Mignon* for her in 1876: the one in which someone dies.

- **20 April 1876, Naples.** She hears it for the first time in a box on the left (059.0064): "Je pensais qu'il n'y avait que Mignon et Faust ; il y a aussi La Traviata, sujet et musique tout est vivant et naturel. On s'attaque à Verdi comme on s'attaque à Dumas, pourquoi ? parce que ces deux hommes amusent, émeuvent, transportent leur public" (059.0065). "J'en ai presque pleuré" (059.0067); it makes her dream of Pietro (059.0074).
- **18-19 May 1876, Rome.** She buys the score and sings it constantly (060.0513); "Avant je pleurais sur Mignon, à présent je pleure sur la musique de La Traviata. Mignon me paraît une bourgeoisie. J'explique cela tout naïvement parce que personne ne meurt" (060.0581).
- **27 May 1876, Nice.** *La Traviata* sung in French at the Théâtre Français, from a small ground-floor box left by Barnola (061.0218).
- **12 February 1878, Paris.** With Albani, Capoul and Pandolfini in the cast: "Grands artistes, mais cela ne m'a pas plu", though in the last act she feels she will suffer and die (078.0126).
- **3 March 1878.** The street noise of the carnival recalls the last act: "il va sans dire que je suis Violette" (078.0349).
- **24 February 1879, Nice.** Patti with Nicolini, who is separated from his wife as she is from the Marquis de Caux (084.0293).
- **17 February 1880, Paris.** "Encore une soirée à sensation. Patti dans La Traviata": they hold a Tuesday subscription (087.0503). On 16 March she lists the roles she has seen Patti in (087.0623).

## Nickname

"La Traviata" is also Marie's name for La Righi, the mistress of Larderel (059.0192, 059.0445 with footnote, 068.1505); these are not references to the opera.
