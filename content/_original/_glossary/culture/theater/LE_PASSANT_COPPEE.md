# Le Passant (François Coppée)

%% GLO_LE_PASSANT_COPPEE.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_LE_PASSANT_COPPEE.0003 %%
*Le Passant* is a one-act verse comedy by [François Coppée](../../people/writers/FRANCOIS_COPPEE.md), his first play, first performed at the Odéon on 14 January 1869. It has two characters: Silvia (Agar) and the young wandering minstrel Zanetto, played in breeches by [Sarah Bernhardt](../../people/artists/SARAH_BERNHARDT.md). It was a success for both actresses and for the author, who dedicated it to Agar (Wikipédia, "Agar (actrice)"; "François Coppée"). The diary footnote adds that it was the piece in which Bernhardt first became famous.

## Marie and Le Passant

%% GLO_LE_PASSANT_COPPEE.0005 %%
%% [#Bojidar](../../people/recurring/BOJIDAR.md) [#Sarah_Bernhardt](../../people/artists/SARAH_BERNHARDT.md) %%
- **28 August 1881, Paris.** Marie and Bojidar spend the day together while Sarah is in Lille. She humours his "manie" and they talk of nothing but "la femme célèbre". Bojidar has invented a whole novel: at the age of eight he saw "Le passant" and fainted when he heard Sarah, and has adored her since. Marie notes that not two years earlier he said "des horreurs" about her. He now lives at her house, as an intimate of her son, and gets theatre tickets (093.0035).

%% GLO_LE_PASSANT_COPPEE.0006 %%
- **24 July 1884, Paris.** The same story is told to ease his way to a friend of Jules: "lorsque ses parents l'ont mené à l'Odéon voir "Le passant", il s'était évanoui. A l'âge de douze ans ! Quelle canaille" (105.0303). The footnote observes that Bernhardt made her name in the play in 1869, when Bojidar, born in 1862, was seven, not twelve.

%% GLO_LE_PASSANT_COPPEE.0007 %%
## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LE_PASSANT_COPPEE.0008 %%
Marie does not say she saw the play. In both entries it is the setting of Bojidar's tale, which she suspects of being invented. The two versions differ on his age (eight in 1881, twelve in 1884); on the footnote's reckoning both are wrong.
