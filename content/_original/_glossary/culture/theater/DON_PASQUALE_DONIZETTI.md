# Don Pasquale (Donizetti)

%% GLO_DON_PASQUALE_DONIZETTI.0002 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_DON_PASQUALE_DONIZETTI.0003 %%
*Don Pasquale* is a comic opera (opera buffa) in three acts by Gaetano Donizetti (1797-1848). The Italian libretto was completed largely by Giovanni Ruffini together with the composer, after Angelo Anelli's libretto for Stefano Pavesi's *Ser Marcantonio* (1810). It was first performed on 3 January 1843 by the Théâtre-Italien at the Salle Ventadour in Paris, with great success, and is generally regarded as the high point, and the end, of the nineteenth-century opera buffa tradition (Wikipedia, "Don Pasquale").

%% GLO_DON_PASQUALE_DONIZETTI.0004 %%
The serenade Marie sings is Ernesto's "Com'è gentil" in Act III, sung to the lovers' chorus from within ("Com'è gentil la notte a mezzo april…"), as printed in the libretto of the Paris edition (Ruffini, *Don Pasquale*, Internet Archive "donpasqualedramm00ruff"). Marie does not name the number, so the identification with this number is an inference from "la sérénade de Don Pasquale".

%% GLO_DON_PASQUALE_DONIZETTI.0005 %%
## Marie and Don Pasquale

%% GLO_DON_PASQUALE_DONIZETTI.0006 %%
**20 June 1876, Nice.** Describing an evening's singing, Marie lists her repertoire: "J'ai chanté la sérénade de Don Pasquale, trois romances russes, la célèbre Povera Maria de Forti… La Riconosenza, un superbe air qui me permet de faire mes vingt notes" (062.0512). The serenade is written for a tenor; she sings it regardless, and claims for her voice "trois octaves moins deux notes". The paragraph belongs to her running argument that a marriage would "couper court à toutes ces idées de scène et de gloire" (062.0513).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_DON_PASQUALE_DONIZETTI.0008 %%
The only other "Don Pasquale" in the diary (printed race card of 10 May 1877 in Florence, 071.0226-0231) is a racehorse; it has nothing to do with the opera. A bare alias "Don Pasquale" would therefore mis-tag it, which is why the aliases above are the longer forms.
