## Overview

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_VICTOR_HUGO_BONNAT.0002 %%
Léon Bonnat's portrait of the poet Victor Hugo (1802-1885), painted in 1879 and shown at the [Salon](./SALON.md) of that year; the etching after it by Paul-Adolphe Rajon is lettered «Salon de 1879» (pubhist.com, work w77588). It is oil on canvas, about 137.2 × 109.2 cm (pubhist.com). It was given in 1919 by Hugo's granddaughter Jeanne Hugo, deposited in 1948 at the Musée national du château de Versailles, and is managed by the Musée d'Orsay (Wikimedia Commons record; the Bayonne Bonnat exhibition dossier of 2022 also calls it an Orsay deposit at Versailles). Bonnat (1833-1922), the leading portraitist of the Third Republic, began in 1875 the series of portraits of prominent men that includes Thiers, Hugo, Taine and Pasteur (Britannica, «Léon Bonnat»).

## Marie and this painting

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_VICTOR_HUGO_BONNAT.0004 %%
On **12 May 1879**, at the Salon, Marie finds almost nothing she likes: «Rien de *vrai,* rien qui vibre, qui chante, qui empoigne, qui donne le frisson, qui fasse pleurer.» She continues: «Pour aujourd'hui je n'ai trouvé de bien que le portrait de Victor Hugo par Bonnat, mais Bonnat a déjà tout donné, il n'y a plus rien à attendre de lui; et puis et peut-être même d'abord, la toile de Breslau.» (085.0071). It is the one picture she praises that day, apart from [Louise Breslau](../../people/artists/LOUISE_BRESLAU.md)'s; her reservation is that Bonnat has already given everything. In May 1878 she had called Bonnat the painter she likes best «comme savoir» (080.0012).
