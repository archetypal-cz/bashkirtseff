# Apotheosis of Venice (Veronese)

## Overview

*The Apotheosis of Venice* (c. 1585) is the great oval canvas by **Paolo Veronese** (1528-1588) set into the ceiling of the **Sala del Maggior Consiglio**, the Great Council Hall of the Doge's Palace (Palazzo Ducale) in Venice. Oil on canvas, about 904 × 579 cm (Wikimedia Commons / Web Gallery of Art record). The central figure is Venice personified: a tall, fair, richly dressed woman enthroned on clouds between spiral columns, crowned by a flying Victory, with allegorical figures about her and Venetian citizens and soldiers below. The hall was redecorated after the fires of 1574 and 1577; the Doge's Palace guide on Wikipedia dates Veronese's ceiling canvas to 1585 and places it in the same programme as the Tintoretto workshop's *Paradiso* (Wikipedia, "Doge's Palace").

The painting is the "Venus-Venice" Marie has in mind when she measures herself or others against the figure of Venice. It is not the same work as Veronese's portrait of his wife (see below).

## Marie and this work

Marie had seen the ceiling on her Italian travels and uses the allegorical Venice as a standard of blond, fresh, full-bodied beauty:

- **1875-01-07 (028.0180, Nice)**: her chambermaid, also called Marie, "la première qui sut me plaire, grande belle, blonde, toujours gaie", "ressemble, en laid et en chambrière, à Venise peinte par Paul Véronèse sur le plafond de la grande salle du palazzo ducale à Venise".
- **1875-08-18 (039.0109, Schlangenbad)**: she recalls the ceiling again: Venice "sous les traits d'une femme, grande, blonde, fraîche. Je rappelle cette peinture"; her photographic portraits cannot render her colour, and her freshness and whiteness are her chief beauty.

Both passages were already glossed by the researcher in 028 as the *Apotheosis of Venice* (RSR 2026-01-30). Marie names no title, only "Venise peinte par Paul Véronèse sur le plafond de la grande salle", which fits the Sala del Maggior Consiglio ceiling.

### Related mentions of Veronese (not this painting)

- **1875-09-14 (043.0167, Florence)**: "J'aime beaucoup la grasse et fraîche figure de la femme de Paolo Véronèse, peinte par lui." A painted portrait labelled as Veronese's wife in a Florentine gallery is probably meant; the inventory could not identify it, and it is not the Apotheosis. Left unidentified (RSR 2026-10-10).
- **1882-04-22 (094.0428, Paris)**: at the Louvre, Veronese "m'a apparu dans toute sa splendeur"; no work named.
