## Overview

%% GLO_SORROW.0002 %%
%% [#Art_Practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
*Горе* ("Sorrow") is one of the three Bashkirtseff paintings still in Ukraine, and the only one outside Kharkiv and Dnipro: it belongs to the **Sumy Regional Art Museum** named after Nykanor Onatsky. The museum dates it **1882** and also says it was painted during her stay at home in **1881** — the two statements are not reconciled anywhere published. Its dimensions have not been published, and **no photograph of it could be located** in any language, on Commons or elsewhere.

%% GLO_SORROW.0003 %%
It reached Sumy the same way the Kharkiv pictures reached Kharkiv: the works Marie's mother gave to the Museum of Alexander III in St Petersburg were sent on to Soviet Ukraine in 1930–1932 and distributed among Ukrainian museums. See *[The Kharkiv Lost Works](./KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.md)* for the provenance chain and what happened to the rest of it.

%% GLO_SORROW.0004 %%
## Description

%% GLO_SORROW.0005 %%
Only the museum's own text describes it, and this entry can do no better than paraphrase: a woman at twilight, her face hidden in her palm, painted *contre-jour* — against the light — and left somewhat sketchy in handling.

%% GLO_SORROW.0006 %%
That description is worth holding beside *[La Douleur de Nausicaa](./DOULEUR_DE_NAUSICAA.md)*, the sculpture Marie began in 1883: «une femme debout qui pleure la tête dans ses mains» — a standing woman weeping with her head in her hands. The hidden face, the grief legible only from posture, is a motif she came back to. No source connects the two works, and none is claimed here; but if *Горе* is really of 1881–82 it is the earlier statement of the same idea.

%% GLO_SORROW.0007 %%
## Making of the Work

%% GLO_SORROW.0008 %%
**No passage in the diary has been matched to it.** The sweep of carnets 087–096, which covers the summer of 1881 at Gavronzi in detail, found one substantial picture from that stay: a life-size peasant woman standing against a wattle fence with water buckets at her feet, painted outdoors under a portable two-room pavilion (092.0104, 092.0110, 092.0143, 092.0198, and Julian's demolition of it at 092.0362). That canvas has no hidden face and no *contre-jour* effect, and the identification with *Горе* was explicitly rejected by the sweep. If the Sumy picture is the 1881 one the museum says it is, Marie did not describe it — or described it in terms nobody has yet recognised.

%% GLO_SORROW.0009 %%
**The picture is at risk, and this should be said plainly.** The Sumy museum's roof was damaged in 2023 and its windows were lost in the ballistic strike of **13 April 2025**. Sumy is thirty kilometres from the Russian border. The collection is **not** fully evacuated, and the acting director treats the question of what has been moved as classified. Nothing published states where *Горе* is or what condition it is in. Any claim that three Bashkirtseff paintings survive in Ukraine should carry the date on which it was made.
