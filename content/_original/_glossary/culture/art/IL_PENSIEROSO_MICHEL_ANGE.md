## Overview

%% GLO_IL_PENSIEROSO_MICHEL_ANGE.0002 %%
The seated marble figure of **Lorenzo de' Medici, Duke of Urbino** (1492-1519) on his tomb in the Medici Chapel (the New Sacristy of San Lorenzo, Florence), carved by [Michelangelo](../../people/mentioned/MICHEL_ANGE.md) in the 1520s and early 1530s (a Commons file title dates it 1524-34; other sources 1526-34). It faces the companion tomb of Giuliano, Duke of Nemours. The statue is called *Il Pensieroso*, «the thoughtful one», because the hand rises towards the face and the helmet casts the eyes in shadow; it is a deliberately idealised figure rather than a portrait, and the tomb is often mistaken for that of Lorenzo's grandfather, Lorenzo the Magnificent, who is buried nearby (Britannica, «Medici Chapel»; Wikipedia, «Lorenzo de' Medici, Duke of Urbino»).

## Marie and this statue

%% GLO_IL_PENSIEROSO_MICHEL_ANGE.0004 %%
On **15 July 1884**, three months before her death, Marie sets down her theory that great art lies in repose and not in agitation: «Comparez le penseur de Michel-Ange à tous les groupes les plus mouvementés» (105.0197). A few paragraphs on, with the *Moses* of San Pietro in Vincoli beside it ([Moïse de Michel-Ange](./MOISE_MICHEL_ANGE.md)): «Son Penseur ne remue pas, ne parle pas, mais c'est parce qu'il ne le veut pas; c'est un homme vivant qui est absorbé par ses pensées» (105.0199). The identification with the Medici Chapel figure is recorded in the reader's note to the entry; Marie herself gives no place name.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_IL_PENSIEROSO_MICHEL_ANGE.0008 %%
Marie writes only «le penseur de Michel-Ange»; the Medici Chapel figure is the only Michelangelo statue conventionally called *il Penseur* («Pensieroso»), so the identification is firm (about 0.9) though not stated by her. There is no Michelangelo «Thinker» in the sense of Rodin's.
