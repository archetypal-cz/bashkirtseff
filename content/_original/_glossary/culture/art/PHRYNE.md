# Phryne

**Research Status**: Basic
**Last Updated**: 2025-07-05
**Diary Coverage**: Up to 1874-01-17

## Historical Figure
Famous Greek courtesan (hetaera) of 4th century BCE Athens, model for Praxiteles.

## Marie's References

### 1874
- **January 17**: Compares "La Saxe" to Phryne when describing her classical beauty at the theater.

### 1875
- **March 1875** (030.0143): Marie compares her own figure, seen in the bathroom mirror, with «Phryné de l'Exposition universelle de Vienne», a statue she saw at the 1873 Weltausstellung. That is probably Francesco Barzaghi's marble *Phryne* (Milan), photographed at the exhibition (MAK Vienna photograph «Skulptur der Phryne», inscribed «Phryne von Franz Ritter v. Barzaghi») (0.7), not James Pradier's *Phryné* (Salon of 1845, London 1851). The exhibition catalogue has not been checked.

## Cultural Significance
- Symbol of beauty and courtesanship in classical world
- Famous for trial where she bared her breasts to win acquittal
- Model for Aphrodite of Knidos sculpture
- Represents power through beauty in Marie's imagination

## Literary Context
- Common reference in 19th century literature
- Part of Marie's classical education
- Shows her scholarly approach to understanding courtesans
- Links modern demimonde to ancient traditions
