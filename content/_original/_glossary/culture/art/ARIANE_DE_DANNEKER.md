## Overview

%% GLO_ARIANE_DE_DANNEKER.0002 %%
*Ariadne on the Panther* (*Ariadne auf dem Panther*) is the marble group by the Stuttgart court sculptor Johann Heinrich Dannecker (1758-1841), the most popular German sculpture of the nineteenth century (Liebieghaus, «The Ariadne file»). Dannecker began it in 1803 without a commission and showed a plaster model in 1805. The finished marble (height 146 cm) was completed on 7 June 1814 for the Frankfurt banker Simon Moritz von Bethmann, who had bought it in 1810 for 11,000 guilders. It showed Ariadne, the Cretan princess and bride of Dionysus, seated in a relaxed pose on a panther. The motto Dannecker is said to have devised for it was «wildness tamed by beauty» (Liebieghaus, «Ariadne on the Panther»).

%% GLO_ARIANE_DE_DANNEKER.0003 %%
In Frankfurt it went on view in 1816 in the Odeon, the city's first public museum, and from 1856 in a specially built **Ariadneum** in the Bethmann garden, where it was shown under rose-coloured light; Bethmann spoke of a «positive pilgrimage» to see it every day (Liebieghaus, «Ariadne on the Panther»). It is now in the Liebieghaus Skulpturensammlung, Frankfurt. In the century after its completion it was reproduced in hundreds of copies in bronze, porcelain, plaster, ivory and marble (Liebieghaus, «The Ariadne file»). Marie spells the sculptor's name «Danneker».

## Marie and this statue

%% GLO_ARIANE_DE_DANNEKER.0005 %%
In July 1878 Marie is in Soden, in the Taunus, and, with «toute la société», goes over to Frankfurt for Eduard Strauss's concert in the zoological garden, and to see the group: «J'y vais aussi pour voir la fameuse Ariâne. Elle est dans une espèce de chapelle qui est éclairée en rose. C'est superbe... comme moi.» (081.0457, 15 July 1878). The «chapelle» lit in rose is the Ariadneum of the Bethmann garden, as the reader's note to the paragraph says.

%% GLO_ARIANE_DE_DANNEKER.0006 %%
Two weeks later, on **31 July 1878**, she compares her own body with the statue's: «Ce soir je me suis posée devant la glace comme Ariane de Danneker à Francfort... si vous voulez savoir comment je suis faite allez à Francfort et regardez l'Ariane.» Even the toes, whose second one does not outreach the big toe, «sont comme les miens», which pleases her because it vexed her in the antique statues; her hips are fuller and her neck less round and classical than the sculptor's (081.0566). Her later plan for an *Ariadne* of her own is a different matter (see below).
