## Overview

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_FRANCOIS_I_TITIEN.0002 %%
The portrait of King Francis I of France (1494-1547) in the Louvre, painted by [Titian](../../people/artists/TITIAN.md) around 1538-1539 (Wikimedia Commons gives 1538; other catalogues 1538-1539 or 1539). It is oil on canvas, about 109 × 89 cm, Louvre inventory INV 753. The king is shown in profile to the right, in a black plumed hat and a black cloak over a red-pink satin doublet with a pendant on a chain. Titian never saw the king while painting it: it is generally explained that he worked from a profile medal of Francis I made by Benvenuto Cellini in 1537 (Fitzwilliam Museum, Cambridge, note on the Cellini medal; National Art Center, Tokyo, guide to the Louvre 2026 exhibition). The picture is thought to have been commissioned from Titian by the poet Pietro Aretino as a gift for the king (same Tokyo guide).

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_FRANCOIS_I_TITIEN.0003 %%
It is the best-known painted image of the Valois king, a bold, easy, worldly prince, and was in the Louvre in Marie's day (she knew the museum well).

## Marie and this painting

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_FRANCOIS_I_TITIEN.0005 %%
On **11 October 1881**, in Madrid, Marie goes to the opera in a box on the stage (*Guillaume Tell*) and meets the baritone Pandolini. She finds him «un être superbe, un Titien, la désinvolture du portrait de François 1er et une tête à la Don Carlos moderne» (093.0211): the king's carefree, swaggering air is her yardstick for a handsome man. She does not name the Louvre, but the phrase «un Titien … le portrait de François 1er» points at Titian's picture, which she could have seen in Paris. (Titian is her «peintre favori» in the Roman and Florentine galleries of 1875-76; see his entry.)

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_FRANCOIS_I_TITIEN.0006 %%
On **28 October 1881**, Marie compares her colleagues' behaviour towards her to that of «François 1er avec le Titien» (093.0309). This second mention is not about the portrait but about the well-known anecdote of a monarch stooping to pick up the painter's fallen brush. The reader's note in the diary points out that the story is normally told of the Emperor Charles V, so Marie has given it to the wrong king; it is kept here because it is the other place where Francis I and Titian are named together.
