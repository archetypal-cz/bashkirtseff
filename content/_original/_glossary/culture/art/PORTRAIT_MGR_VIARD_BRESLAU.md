## Overview

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_MGR_VIARD_BRESLAU.0002 %%
A portrait of a prelate, «Mgr. Viard», painted by [Louise Breslau](../../people/artists/LOUISE_BRESLAU.md) (1856-1927), Marie's fellow student at the Académie Julian, and sent to the [Salon](./SALON.md) of 1880. Marie's two paragraphs date it unambiguously: it is being painted «pour le salon» in January 1880 and is on the Salon walls on 30 April 1880. (An earlier inventory gave 1881; that is not supported by the diary.) The sitter is called only «Mgr. Viard»; he has not been identified, and the painting itself has not been traced: no image, catalogue entry or present location was found in the sources checked (museum and auction databases, Wikimedia Commons).

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_MGR_VIARD_BRESLAU.0003 %%
## Marie and this painting

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_MGR_VIARD_BRESLAU.0004 %%
On **23 January 1880**, in the studio, the classes are suspended for three weeks because Tony Robert-Fleury's mother is ill, and she notes: «Breslau fait pour le salon le portrait de Mgr. Viard.» (087.0300).

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_MGR_VIARD_BRESLAU.0005 %%
On **30 April 1880**, at the Salon, she finds her rival hung like herself in the galerie and on the cimaise (the eye-level line), but with «un bien mauvais morceau de peinture ou tout au moins une chose aussi désagréable à voir que possible. C'est le portrait de Mgr. Viard. Je crois que ce qui l'a perdue c'est une trop grande recherche de finesse de tons, tout est gris, le fond qui représente des panneaux de bois grisâtre, des décorations de chapelle, d'oratoire, le fauteuil, tout est sale, la tête est aussi très sale.» (088.0043). That is the only description of the picture: the prelate in an armchair against grey wood panelling, like a chapel or oratory.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_MGR_VIARD_BRESLAU.0009 %%
The sitter's identity is open (a bishop or monsignor named Viard); the Salon catalogue of 1880 (the *livret*) should give the full name and title. The Breslau entry already records the same two paragraphs.
