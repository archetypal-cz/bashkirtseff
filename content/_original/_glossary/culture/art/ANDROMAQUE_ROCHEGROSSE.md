## Overview

%% GLO_ANDROMAQUE_ROCHEGROSSE.0002 %%
*Andromaque* (1883) is the large history painting by [Georges Rochegrosse](../../people/mentioned/ROCHEGROSSE.md) (1859-1938) that made his name at the [Salon](./SALON.md) of 1883. It shows the sack of Troy: Andromache, half-stripped, struggles in the grip of Greek soldiers to save her son Astyanax, whom the Greeks are about to hurl from the ramparts; the city burns, corpses and severed heads litter the ground, and the walls are hung with the hanged. At the top of the stairs stands an impassive Greek leader, identified as Ulysses or Neoptolemus (French Wikipedia, «Andromaque (Rochegrosse)»). The State bought it at the 1883 Salon and it has been in the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Rouen since 1884 (same article). Rochegrosse received the Prix du Salon for it that year (English Wikipedia, «Georges Rochegrosse»).

%% GLO_ANDROMAQUE_ROCHEGROSSE.0003 %%
The picture belongs to the late-nineteenth-century wave of violent, sensational history painting. French Wikipedia presents Rochegrosse as the artist who tried to «desiccate» David's classical tradition and set the antique against the "most vile realism", for a public used to the strong emotions of the illustrated press and the boulevard theatre.

## Marie and this painting

%% GLO_ANDROMAQUE_ROCHEGROSSE.0005 %%
Marie writes about it twice, in the spring of 1883. On **6 May 1883**, at the dinner with the princess, Alexis and the priest, the talk is of the picture: «On fait un tapage énorme de la grande toile du jeune Rochegrosse. On arrache à Andromaque son fils Astyanax pour le précipiter du haut des remparts. C'est de l'antique original et moderne. Il ne suit personne et ne s'inspire de personne. La couleur et la peinture sont d'une vigueur sans pareille.» She adds, with a sideways glance at the press, that he is the stepson of Théodore de Banville, and still calls him «un tempérament prodigieux»: he is twenty-four, it is only his second exhibition, «Voilà comment il faudrait être, composition, dessin, couleur, tout est d'une fougue incomparable» (099.0367). Rochegrosse's Salon debut was in 1882 and his mother had married the poet Banville in 1875 (French Wikipedia, «Georges-Antoine Rochegrosse»), so the details match.

%% GLO_ANDROMAQUE_ROCHEGROSSE.0006 %%
On **10 June 1883**, having now seen it at the Salon, she is cooler: «Il y a toujours foule devant le jeune tableau du jeune Rochegrosse. C'est très puissant, c'est incontestable mais ça me laisse froide. Mais qu'est-ce qui ne me laisse pas froide ?» (100.0122), a remark that leads straight into her exaltation over Bastien-Lepage's [Jeanne d'Arc](./JEANNE_D_ARC_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.md). Both paragraphs are about this picture; the neighbouring mentions of Rochegrosse (the talk of the great medals on 21 May, 099.0513; his age, 100.0017; the medals again, 101.0224) concern the painter and the Salon prizes and are recorded in his own entry.
