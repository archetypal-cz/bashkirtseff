## Overview

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0002 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) [#Marie](../../people/family/MARIE_BASHKIRTSEFF.md) %%
*Autoportrait à la palette* (oil on canvas) is **the image by which Marie Bashkirtseff is known**. The painter stands with palette and brush, a harp behind her, looking directly out at the viewer. It hangs in the **Musée des Beaux-Arts Jules Chéret in Nice**, inv. **N.Mba 1982**, given to the city by the artist's mother in **1920**, and is on permanent display. Nice holds the largest surviving group of her work.

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0003 %%
The museum's own text on the picture is worth quoting: "Her posture and determined gaze, challenging the viewer, nevertheless reveal the profound melancholy of this young woman." That double reading — the challenge and the melancholy in the same face — is exactly what makes the picture the standing portrait of the diarist.

## Description

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0005 %%
**Both the date and the dimensions are disputed, and this entry does not resolve them.**

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0006 %%
- **Date.** Wikidata gives **1880**; the museum's own label gives **1882–1883**. Unresolved. "c. 1880–1883" is the honest form.
- **Dimensions.** The museum gives **92,3 × 73,5 cm**; Wikidata gives **114 × 95,2 cm**. The 1885 posthumous exhibition catalogue lists as its very first item a *Portrait de Mlle Bashkirtseff* measuring **0,90 × 0,75 m** — which matches the museum's figure almost exactly and argues strongly against the Wikidata measurement. This entry follows the museum.

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0007 %%
**Image gap.** The only reproductions on Wikimedia Commons are **466 × 600 px**. For her single most iconic image this is the most conspicuous illustration gap in the whole project, and it is not one this entry can close: the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Nice has digitised essentially none of its Bashkirtseff holdings. The picture can be seen on the city's own [Trésors de Nice](https://tresors.nice.fr/oeuvre/autoportrait-a-la-palette) page.

## Making of the Work

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0009 %%
**The diary does not record the making of this canvas.** No entry in the carnets has been matched to it — which is consistent with the disputed 1880–1883 dating, since the years in question are densely documented and a picture of this importance would be hard to miss. What the diary does preserve is the **lineage**: two earlier attempts at the same subject, both of them revealing.

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0010 %%
%% [#Nice](../../places/cities/NICE.md) %%
The first is at Nice on **4 May 1877** (¶ 071.0031), and she is caught in the act: "Marcuard m'a trouvée barbouillant mon propre portrait. Si je réussis à faire une esquisse ce sera déjà bien, les ombres et les couleurs par cœur, ce n'est pas une plaisanterie." She is working from memory, without a mirror sitting, five months before she ever enters the Académie Julian.

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0011 %%
The second, on **23 March 1879** (¶ 084.0422), is the fully self-dramatising version: with thirty minutes of daylight left she sketched herself "sur une toile de 4, debout retournée violemment sur moi-même, la traîne longue m'entourant et me dessinant, mise en Robespierre, en révolutionnaire, la tête haute et l'air de braver l'univers…" — working from mirror sketches in her album, and wanting, she says, to *immortaliser* her habitual black bodice, batiste chemisette and ruffled jabot. She notes it will stay unfinished: "Demain je l'arrangerai sans le finir." A *toile de 4* is roughly 33 × 24 cm, so this is a small panel and not the Nice canvas — but "l'air de braver l'univers" is precisely the expression the Nice museum describes three or four years later.

## References in Diary

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0013 %%
The Nice canvas itself has **no confirmed mention** in the diary. The two documented antecedents are:

%% GLO_AUTOPORTRAIT_A_LA_PALETTE.0014 %%
- **1877-05-04, ¶ 071.0031** — the first self-portrait attempt recorded anywhere in the diary, at Nice, painted from memory
- **1879-03-23, ¶ 084.0422** — the "Robespierre" self-portrait sketched in half an hour of failing light, from mirror studies, deliberately left unfinished
