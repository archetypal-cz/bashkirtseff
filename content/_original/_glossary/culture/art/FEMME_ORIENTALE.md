## Overview

%% GLO_FEMME_ORIENTALE.0002 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
*Femme orientale* (oil on canvas, 1882) is in the **Musée des Beaux-Arts Jules Chéret, Nice** — one of three Bashkirtseff paintings there, alongside the *Autoportrait à la palette* and the portrait of her brother Paul. The Nice group came to the city through the artist's mother.

%% GLO_FEMME_ORIENTALE.0003 %%
The picture belongs to the *orientalisme* that ran through French Salon painting for most of the century: a European model in Eastern dress, studio-made, the subject a costume rather than a place. Marie never travelled east of Vienna. 1882 was the year she was working hard at heads and studies between her two big Salon efforts, and this is best understood as one of them.

%% GLO_FEMME_ORIENTALE.0004 %%
## Description

%% GLO_FEMME_ORIENTALE.0005 %%
Oil on canvas; dimensions not published by the museum. No title in the 1885 posthumous exhibition catalogue matches it, though that catalogue contains a great many untitled *Étude*s and *Tête*s among which it could easily sit — no. 100, *Japonaise*, shows she was working in this costume-portrait vein at least once more.

%% GLO_FEMME_ORIENTALE.0006 %%
## Making of the Work

%% GLO_FEMME_ORIENTALE.0007 %%
Nothing is documented about the making of this picture. It is not identifiable in the diary under this or any related title, and the museum publishes no object record for it. What can be said is contextual: in 1882 Marie was working at Nice in the spring and in Paris from May, producing the great pastel of Dina, the head of Irma that became *La Parisienne*, *Thérèse*, and a run of studies — a productive year in which an undated costume study would be entirely at home.
