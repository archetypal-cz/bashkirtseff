## Overview

%% GLO_PENITENT_MAGDALENE_TITIAN.0002 %%
A half-length picture of the penitent Mary Magdalene by [Titian](../../people/artists/TITIAN.md), now in the Galleria Palatina of the Palazzo Pitti in Florence (Sala di Apollo). It is signed «TITIANUS» on the ointment jar at the lower left and is dated c. 1531 by Wikipedia and c. 1533-35 on the Commons photograph label. The saint looks upward with tears in her eyes, her loosened red-gold hair covering her nakedness and one hand pressed to her breast. (Wikipedia, «Penitent Magdalene (Titian, 1531)».)

%% GLO_PENITENT_MAGDALENE_TITIAN.0003 %%
According to the same source it was commissioned by Federico Gonzaga, Duke of Mantua, as a devotional image for the poet Vittoria Colonna, and was recorded in the collection of the Duke of Urbino in 1631. Titian and his workshop repeated the composition several times, so that Magdalenes «by Titian» hang in several galleries; the Pitti picture is described as the earliest and best known. (Wikipedia, as above; Sotheby's, Old Master Paintings sale, 2008, lot 117.)

## Marie and the Magdalene

%% GLO_PENITENT_MAGDALENE_TITIAN.0005 %%
Marie meets a Titian Magdalene twice. In Florence on **14 September 1875**, at the Palazzo Pitti, she writes: «Oh ! mais il y a une Madeleine du Titien qui m'a ravie» (043.0164), then at once qualifies her delight: «elle a des poignets trop gros et des mains trop grasses, de belles mains d'une femme de cinquante ans» (043.0165). The description fits the Pitti picture, where the saint's wrist and the hand laid on her breast are full and heavy.

%% GLO_PENITENT_MAGDALENE_TITIAN.0006 %%
In Rome on **4 February 1876**, in the gallery of the Palazzo Doria on the Corso, she praises «Une Madeleine du Titien, mon peintre favori, une vraie Madeleine belle et avec l'expression qu'il faut» (053.0378). This is probably a different picture from the Pitti one (see the notes below). The two mentions belong to the months in which Titian's women, with their red hair and heavy wrists, dominate her gallery notes (see [Titian](../../people/artists/TITIAN.md)).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_PENITENT_MAGDALENE_TITIAN.0008 %%
The identification of the first Magdalene (Palazzo Pitti, 1875) with the Titian now in the Galleria Palatina is secure on the evidence of the text. The second (Palazzo Doria Pamphilj, 1876) is not confirmed: no Titian Magdalene is recorded there in the sources consulted. The gallery does show a *Maddalena penitente* catalogued as a copy after Titian, after the version in Capodimonte, Naples (Wikimedia Commons, file «Copia da Tiziano - Maddalena penitente (Galleria Pamphilj, Roma).jpg»), which may be what Marie saw under Titian's name in 1876. The Doria gallery's celebrated Magdalene, by Caravaggio, is another painter's work and unlikely to be hers. No image of the Doria picture is given here because the available photograph is not freely reusable.
