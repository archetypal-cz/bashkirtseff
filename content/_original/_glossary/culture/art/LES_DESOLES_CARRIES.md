## Overview

%% GLO_LES_DESOLES_CARRIES.0002 %%
*Les Désolés* is Marie's name for a series of twenty-four busts of outcasts by the young sculptor **Jean Carriès** (1855-1894), shown at a « Cercle » (100.0280). The title is not found in the sources consulted: French Wikipedia says that in **1882** Carriès exhibited at the Cercle des arts libéraux (the rue de Sèze venue that Marie visited in December 1882) his plaster busts « issus de la série des Déshérités », portraying marginals and the poor, mixing naturalism and symbolism; it also lists a *Mendiant russe, ou Le Déshérité* (c. 1881) and a *Désespéré au grand chapeau* (1882). Whether Marie's « Désolés » is the same series under a variant title is the editors' inference from the date, the venue and the subject. (Source: French Wikipedia, « Jean Carriès »; Petit Palais record for PPS1209.)

## Marie and Carriès

%% GLO_LES_DESOLES_CARRIES.0004 %%
On **3 July 1883** Marie and her family go with the Russian Tchoumakoff to the studio in the rue Boissonnade, behind Montparnasse, of « un M. Carriès sculpteur, annoncé comme futur homme de génie, artiste du XVIe siècle, sauvage, primitif, désenchanté » (100.0279). She is struck by how he talks of himself « avec la plus vive admiration », finds his talent « très original, fantastique même et qui fatigue » (100.0283), and reports the series of twenty-four busts called *Les Désolés* (« Ça sent Rollinat », 100.0280-0281). He sells busts at 100 francs each and she buys **two** (100.0284). On **25 July 1883** he delivers them and stays to dinner; she finds him ill at ease and extremely poor (according to Émile Bastien-Lepage) and is « honteuse d'avoir payé deux œuvres d'art, le prix d'un chapeau » (100.0431-0436), wishing she could send him another hundred francs. Which two busts she bought is not stated, and their present whereabouts are unknown.
