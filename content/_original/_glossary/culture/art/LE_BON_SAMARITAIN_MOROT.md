## Overview

%% GLO_LE_BON_SAMARITAIN_MOROT.0002 %%
*Le Bon Samaritain* (1880) is the large canvas by [Aimé Morot](../../people/artists/MOROT.md) (1850-1913) with which he won the **medal of honour** at the [Salon](./SALON.md) of 1880. It is oil on canvas, 268.5 × 198 cm, in the **Petit Palais** (Musée des Beaux-Arts de la Ville de Paris), Paris. The Petit Palais describes its debt to Spanish seventeenth-century painting and its sober realism in treating the parable; the canvas was cut down from a larger original to concentrate on the life-size figures. (Sources: Petit Palais, collections page for the painting; Wikipedia, « The Good Samaritan (Morot) »; Commons record of the Petit Palais file.)

## Marie and this painting

%% GLO_LE_BON_SAMARITAIN_MOROT.0004 %%
On **5 May 1880** Marie goes to the Salon: « Le bon Samaritain de Morot est un morceau de peinture épatante. Le Job de Bonnat qui est large, dessiné et bien peint paraît une mièvrerie après le Morot » (088.0105). On **16 May 1880** she goes alone to the Salon early, studies it from a seat opposite with a lorgnette, and declares it « le tableau qui m'a fait le plus complètement plaisir depuis que j'existe » (088.0204, 088.0205); she hurries between it and Bastien-Lepage's *Jeanne d'Arc* in two neighbouring rooms (088.0208). On **20 June 1880**, the last day of the Salon, she notes that it has won the medal of honour -- and, though « la peinture de M. Morot a beau être épatante », she thinks the medal should be harder to win and should not simply go to the best work in the Salon; the picture « m'a presque ennuyée aujourd'hui », while Bastien-Lepage's head of Joan still moves her (089.0005, 089.0006).
