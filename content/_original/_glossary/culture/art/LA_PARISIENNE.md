## Overview

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0002 %%
%% [#Salon](./SALON.md) [#Art_practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
*La Parisienne, portrait d'Irma, modèle à l'Académie Julian* (1882, oil on canvas, 55,5 × 46 cm) is Marie Bashkirtseff's portrait of [Irma](../../people/mentioned/IRMA.md), a professional model at the [Académie Julian](../../places/schools/JULIAN.md). It was shown at the [Salon](./SALON.md) of 1883 simply as *Parisienne*, alongside *Jean et Jacques* and the pastel portrait of [Dina](../../people/core/DINA.md) -- the year Marie received her *mention honorable*. It is signed and dated at the upper left, "Bashkirtseff 1882".

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0003 %%
The picture is in the **Petit Palais, Musée des Beaux-Arts de la Ville de Paris**, inv. **PPP129**, given by the artist's mother, Maria Stepanovna Bashkirtseva née Babanina, in **1902**, at the museum's opening. It is not currently on display, but Paris Musées has released a museum-grade photograph of it into the public domain, which makes it one of the best-reproduced of all her works.

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0004 %%
## Description

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0005 %%
A round-faced young woman, seen close and half-length against a plain ground, in a simple straw hat trimmed with a garland of flowers. It is a small canvas and an unpretentious one -- Marie's own verdict at the Salon was that it was "une chose sans prétention" -- and its interest is that it is a portrait of a working model treated as a portrait of a Parisian woman: the title generalises the sitter into a type without prettifying her.

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0006 %%
Marie had described Irma in December 1881 as the vanished "type de la grisette", drôle and sentimental with naïvetés of vice, and had noted exactly the quality that makes this painting possible: "Elle pose avec intelligence, on peut en faire ce qu'on veut car elle est avec sa pâleur étonnante aussi bien une candide jeune fille qu'un abîme de dépravations" (094.0028).

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0007 %%
## Making of the Work

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0008 %%
Irma was sitting for her from **December 1881**, and the head was finished on **18 July 1882**: "Fin de la tête d'Irma" (095.0277). [Tony Robert-Fleury](../../people/artists/ROBERT_FLEURY.md) and [Rodolphe Julian](../../people/mentioned/RODOLPHE_JULIAN.md) arrived at seven that evening with great compliments -- "c'est gras, c'est souple, c'est d'un ton fin... Enfin vous êtes sortie des sécheresses. C'est reposé" (095.0278). The praise mattered: dryness was the standing charge against her painting, and this small head was the first work that answered it. She dated her subsequent decline from it: "depuis Irma j'ai pataugé dans la pluie avec le père Charles" (097.0207).

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0009 %%
%% [#Salon](./SALON.md) %%
Marie sent it to the Salon of 1883 as a second *envoi* that nobody knew about, and on jury day her terror was that the little head would be admitted and the big picture refused: "Non mais la tête d'irma." (098.0571–0573). Both were taken -- "il ne pouvait y avoir de doute pour Irma" (098.0603) -- and Irma got the better hanging: "La tète d'irma est sur la cimaise et dans un angle, par conséquent place d'honneur" (099.0177), while *Jean et Jacques* went above the line. Her own Salon verdict on it was cool and accurate: "la tête d'irma est agréable et d'une peinture assez franche. Mais c'est une chose sans prétention" (099.0311). A year later, arguing with herself about the reception of *[Un meeting](./UN_MEETING.md)*, she used it as the measure of how far she had come: "j'y étais l'année dernière avec une étude d'irma ? Un tableau entier, six personnages et dont chacun vaut trois fois la tête d'irma ?" (103.0622).

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0010 %%
**A note on the title.** Marie never calls this picture *La Parisienne* in the diary. From the studio to the Salon it is "la tête d'Irma", occasionally "une étude d'irma"; the generalising title belongs to the Salon *livret* and to the 1885 catalogue (no. 64, *Parisienne*, 0,54 × 0,45 m, with a separate study listed at no. 66 as *Irma*). Readers looking for the painting in the diary should look for the sitter's name.

%% GLO_LA_PARISIENNE.0013 %%
Irma also recurs throughout the diary as a person and a working model, notably at 101.0524, 102.0057, 102.0076, 102.0087, 103.0002, 103.0138 and 105.0014 -- including as a model for the *Douleur de Nausicaa* and as a companion on the wrestler-hunting expeditions of July 1884.
