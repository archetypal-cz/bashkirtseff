## Overview

%% GLO_TETE_JEUNE_FEMME_PLUME_BLEUE.0002 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
*Tête de jeune femme*, also circulated as *Jeune femme à la plume bleue* (oil on canvas, 55 × 45 cm, c. 1878), is in the **Musée Ziem at Martigues**, inv. **ZP 147**. It was given by the artist's mother for the museum's founding in 1908 — one of a series of gifts by which Maria Stepanovna placed her daughter's work in French public collections between 1902 and 1920.

%% GLO_TETE_JEUNE_FEMME_PLUME_BLEUE.0003 %%
If the c. 1878 date holds, this is a picture from Marie's **first year at the Académie Julian** — she entered on 3 October 1877 — and one of the earliest surviving oils by her.

%% GLO_TETE_JEUNE_FEMME_PLUME_BLEUE.0004 %%
## Description

%% GLO_TETE_JEUNE_FEMME_PLUME_BLEUE.0005 %%
Oil on canvas, 55 × 45 cm: a head of a young woman in a hat trimmed with a blue feather. The two titles in circulation describe the same object from two directions — *Tête de jeune femme* is the studio designation, *Jeune femme à la plume bleue* the descriptive one that took hold later.

**Image gap.** The only file on Wikimedia Commons is a **460 × 605 px** blog scan, below the resolution this project publishes, so this entry carries no illustration.

## Making of the Work

%% GLO_TETE_JEUNE_FEMME_PLUME_BLEUE.0008 %%
%% [#Julian](../../places/schools/JULIAN.md) %%
**No diary passage can be attributed to this picture, and the attempt to find one is instructive.** Carnets 076–086 (November 1877 – December 1879) were swept for it and contain no mention by title. What they contain in abundance is *têtes* — but these are the weekly concours heads painted from a set model in the atelier, judged and ranked, not autonomous pictures: "On a trouvé du très bon dans ma tête et du pas mal dans l'académie" (¶ 076.0407, 15 December 1877); "Ma tête est mieux que les dernières que j'ai peintes" (¶ 086.0321, 18 October 1879).

%% GLO_TETE_JEUNE_FEMME_PLUME_BLEUE.0009 %%
A hat with a blue feather is not atelier costume, which argues that this was a picture of a friend or a paid sitter rather than one of the concours heads — but that is an inference, not evidence. The honest position is that the work is **not attributable from the diary**.
