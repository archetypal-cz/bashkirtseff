## Overview

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BELLE_SOEUR.0002 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) [#Nini](../../people/mentioned/NINI.md) [#Paul](../../people/mentioned/PAUL.md) %%
*Portrait de sa belle-sœur* (oil on canvas, 92,5 × 73 cm, 1881) is Marie's portrait of **Alexandrine Patchenko**, the wife of her brother Paul. It is in the **Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam**, inv. **SK-A-1988**, given to the museum by the artist's mother, Maria Stepanovna Bashkirtseva née Babanina, in **June 1902**. It is monogrammed "MB / 1881" at lower right and was painted in Paris.

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BELLE_SOEUR.0003 %%
Of all Marie's surviving works this is the one the public can see best: the Rijksmuseum has released a **2756 × 3490 px** reproduction under **CC0**, museum-grade open access. For an artist whose most famous pictures survive only in poor scans, that is not a small thing.

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BELLE_SOEUR.0004 %%
**A name to watch.** The museum and the catalogues call the sitter Alexandrine Patchenko. **Marie never once calls her that.** In the diary she is "Nini" or, more often, "la femme à Paul". Anyone searching the French text for "Alexandrine" will find nothing.

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BELLE_SOEUR.0005 %%
## Description

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BELLE_SOEUR.0006 %%
Oil on canvas, 92,5 × 73 cm. Marie leaves no description of the composition, but she does leave a description of her sitter's colouring, and it is the best thing in the file: "Elle est d'une couleur… ravissante, fraîche sans crudité, veloutée, jeune" (¶ 094.0060). She thought the finished picture fell well short of what she could do.

## Making of the Work

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BELLE_SOEUR.0008 %%
%% [#Tony_Robert_Fleury](../../people/mentioned/TONY_ROBERT_FLEURY.md) [#Health](../themes/HEALTH.md) %%
The picture was painted over about a fortnight at the turn of 1881–82, in a burst of energy and then in illness. It begins on **29 December 1881** (¶ 094.0028): "Je fais le portrait de la femme à Paul, hier c'était une telle reprise de forces que je voulais faire à la fois Dina, Nini et Irma." Two days later she took the *esquisse* to Tony Robert-Fleury, who found it "très originale, très original d'arrangement et bien parti" (¶ 094.0029).

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BELLE_SOEUR.0009 %%
It was finished on **11 January 1882**, and her own verdict is characteristically merciless: "Le portrait est fini, ce n'est pas encore ça !! Ce n'est même pas ce que je puis faire… C'est moins ! C'est si difficile !" (¶ 094.0060). The conditions were wretched — she was working in a draught between two doors, running a temperature of 41°, and the thick January fogs ruined the light (¶¶ 094.0085, 094.0094). She was twenty-three, and already deaf in one ear and consumptive.
