## Overview

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0002 %%
%% [#Art_practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) [#Rodolphe_Julian](../../people/mentioned/RODOLPHE_JULIAN.md) [#Julian](../../places/schools/JULIAN.md) %%
*Caricature de Rodolphe Julian* (oil on panel, 85,5 × 38 cm, c. 1878) is Marie's caricature of **her own teacher** — the founder of the Académie Julian, the man who ran the studio where she trained, commissioned pictures from her, bullied and flattered her, and let her down badly over the Cercle exhibition of 1883.

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0003 %%
It is in a private collection; it was sold at **Drouot, Paris, on 24 April 2025**, and its present whereabouts is not public. It is little known, and it is one of the most immediately likeable things she made: an artist barely twenty, three months or so into art school, painting a full-length joke at the expense of the man in charge, and painting it in oils on a tall narrow panel rather than dashing it off in a sketchbook.

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0004 %%
## Description

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0005 %%
Oil on panel, 85,5 × 38 cm — a format more than twice as tall as it is wide, which is itself part of the joke: the proportions stretch the sitter. Working a caricature up in oil, at nearly life-scale in height, is a considerable amount of effort to spend on a gag, and suggests it was made to be seen and hung, not passed around.

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0006 %%
## Making of the Work

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0007 %%
**The finished panel is not described in the diary**, but the diary brackets it closely on both sides, and the two bracketing passages between them explain how such a picture came to exist.

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0008 %%
The first is **10 November 1877** (¶ 076.0099), five weeks after she entered the atelier: "Ensuite nous avons fait des croquis, un d'eux un peu en caricature a eu du succès, Julian me l'a fait signer et l'a mis dans son album." A caricatural sketch of hers is a hit; Julian makes her *sign* it and files it in his own album. This is the earliest documented drawing of hers that Julian kept — and it establishes that caricature was the register in which he first noticed her.

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0009 %%
The second is **19 May 1879** (¶ 085.0091). After a two-hour confessional in Julian's cabinet in which he tells her she is "son esclave, sa chose", she answers: "Alors je lui dis que je ferai son portrait." The promise of a portrait of Julian comes straight out of him needling her — which is exactly the affectionate-mocking register a caricature belongs to.

%% GLO_CARICATURE_RODOLPHE_JULIAN.0010 %%
Caricature was a constant habit through these years, not an isolated experiment. She posts a caricature of the Gans brothers to them by mail (¶¶ 082.0055–082.0056, 12 August 1878) and finally lets them in to see it (¶¶ 082.0123–082.0124, 21 August); she sketches Schwarz in a notebook the same month (¶ 082.0060); she paints a caricatural watercolour *pochade* of Mme de Fayet singing at a fleeing cupid with Paul de Cassagnac's head on it (¶ 078.0050, 8 February 1878); she caricatures "Popaul" Cassagnac and his brother-in-law with captions (¶ 086.0028, 8 August 1879), and reports "j'ai fait des caricatures et un tas de Brésiliens" (¶ 086.0157, 29 August 1879). As late as March 1883 she is still making *charges à la plume* (¶ 098.0483).
