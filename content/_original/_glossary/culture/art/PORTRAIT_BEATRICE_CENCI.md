## Overview

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BEATRICE_CENCI.0002 %%
The portrait of a young woman in a white turban, turned over her shoulder and looking out with a grave, sorrowful face, which a long tradition identified as **Beatrice Cenci** (1577-1599), the Roman noblewoman beheaded for the murder of her violent father (en.wikipedia «Beatrice Cenci»). It hangs in the Galleria Nazionale d'Arte Antica in the Palazzo Barberini, Rome. For centuries it was attributed to Guido Reni and dated c. 1600; the Barberini Corsini gallery now attributes it to Ginevra Cantofoli, c. 1650, and the identification of the sitter is itself only traditional (Commons file description citing barberinicorsini.org).

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BEATRICE_CENCI.0003 %%
The portrait and the legend were widely known in the nineteenth century: Shelley wrote the verse drama *The Cenci* (1819-20), and the supposed Reni portrait figures prominently in Hawthorne's *The Marble Faun* (1860) and in Melville's *Pierre* (1852) (en.wikipedia «Beatrice Cenci»). For Marie the picture is an emblem of tragic beauty and a costume to copy. The picture is Beatrice's portrait; the painter Guido Reni, to whom it was ascribed, is a separate person whose name she never writes.

## Marie and Beatrice Cenci

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BEATRICE_CENCI.0005 %%
Marie uses the picture first as a costume model and then as a mirror image. On **22 January 1876** she sets about «un vêtement comme celui de la Béatrice de Dante et un turban comme chez Béatrice Cenci» for a sitting (052.0377). On **3 February 1876**, in a gallery, the head of the Cenci irritates her as much as the many martyrdoms of Saint Sebastian: «il m'agace comme la tête de la Cenci» (053.0357). In Naples, on **13 April 1876**, she writes before a large mirror: «j'ai l'air de Béatrice Cenci moins le turban. C'est beau, une robe blanche et les cheveux épars» (057.0214). On **6 May 1876**, alone at home: «Et me voilà seule chez moi, en Béatrice Cenci, moins le turban» (059.0849). The two last lines echo each other, and the second is written in a lonely, unhappy mood (she is waiting for news from Pietro Antonelli).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_PORTRAIT_BEATRICE_CENCI.0007 %%
In 057.0214 and 059.0849 Marie compares herself to the portrait's sitter, and the footnotes in the diary rightly connect this to the white-turbaned portrait. The attribution to Guido Reni, which the diary's footnotes still state, is superseded. Where Marie saw the picture is not stated; Rome in early 1876 is the obvious occasion. In 053.0357 the head of the Cenci might be the same portrait or a copy; the paragraph does not say which gallery she is in.
