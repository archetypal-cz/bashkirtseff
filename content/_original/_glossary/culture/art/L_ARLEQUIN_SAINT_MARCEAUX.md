## Overview

%% GLO_L_ARLEQUIN_SAINT_MARCEAUX.0002 %%
*L'Arlequin* is the full-length figure of the commedia dell'arte Harlequin by the sculptor [René de Saint-Marceaux](../../people/religious/SAINT_MARCEAUX.md) (1845-1915): a masked, long-limbed figure with his arms folded on his chest and a wooden bat in his hand, head tilted in sceptical amusement. It was shown at the Paris Salon of 1880, and the plaster is in the **Musée des Beaux-Arts, Reims** (given by the artist in 1880; 175 × 65.7 × 62 cm); bronze reductions were made and sold. French Wikipedia quotes a judgment of the day: « Après l'œuvre de noble majesté, un poème de spirituelle malice », the *Arlequin* being a break with the neoclassical manner of his earlier *Génie* (see [Le Génie gardant le secret de la tombe](./LE_GENIE_GARDANT_LE_SECRET_DE_LA_TOMBE.md)). (Sources: French Wikipedia, « René de Saint-Marceaux »; Musée des Beaux-Arts de Reims record on Wikimedia Commons. Inventory dates the work 1879; the plaster record says 1879, the Salon showing was 1880.)

## Marie and the Arlequin

%% GLO_L_ARLEQUIN_SAINT_MARCEAUX.0004 %%
Marie is a fanatical admirer of Saint-Marceaux, and the *Arlequin* is the work that she returns to most. On **16 May 1880** at the Salon: after a year she had begun to think she had overrated him (the previous Salon's honour, « la médaille d'honneur », had gone to her head); « cet Arlequin me rouvre les yeux... Une chose si ingrate mais que de talent, c'est plus que du talent. C'est un vrai artiste » (088.0211).

%% GLO_L_ARLEQUIN_SAINT_MARCEAUX.0005 %%
On **11 April 1883**, at the Arts Décoratifs, she sees a reduction: « Jamais encore l'art ne m'a émue à ce point... La peinture ? Qu'est-ce que la peinture ! » She calls the figure « une œuvre de haute philosophie... la plus grandiose image d'un esprit supérieur qui voit défiler devant lui les vices, les ridicules, les infamies de ce monde » and wonders where the critic is who has written about it as a philosopher (099.0103, with a reader footnote; 099.0106: « Tout paraît flasque après l'Arlequin »). Within a month she owns one: on **9 May 1883** she mentions that she has had an *Arlequin* in bronze for three or four days (099.0386) and on the same day rages that it is « débiné » as an « ouvrage d'homme du monde » (099.0394). On **2 June 1883** she cites it as proof that « le monde est méprisable » (100.0057); on **14 September 1883** she goes to see « Saint-Marceaux, l'Arlequin, le Génie et le portrait de M. Renan » (101.0346).

## Other Saint-Marceaux works in the diary

%% GLO_L_ARLEQUIN_SAINT_MARCEAUX.0007 %%
Marie's other references: the busts that « saisie » her at the exhibition of the rue de Sèze (Cercle des arts libéraux) on 19 December 1882, where she spoke to him (« je bégayais de peur », 097.0268, 097.0322-0323); the bust of Bastien-Lepage he is making in April 1883 (099.0139); a statue of a dancing woman barely begun in his studio on 29 May 1883 (« la danse... une rêverie de la danse », 100.0035-0037); the bust of Ernest Renan seen at his studio on 16 June 1883 (100.0170) and again « à travers le buste » when Renan speaks at Turgenev's departure on 1 October 1883 (101.0471). French Wikipedia lists a plaster *Buste d'Ernest Renan* (1883) in the musée de la Vie romantique, Paris. A bust of « le père Hecht » is also mentioned; these works have no separate entries yet.
