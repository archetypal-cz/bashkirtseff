%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0001 %%
## Overview

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0002 %%
%% [#Art_Practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) %%
Marie left roughly 230 works. Most of them ended up in one building in **Kharkiv**, and that building was emptied and then burned in the Second World War. Sixty-five of her canvases are itemised as lost there. This entry carries the destruction history for the whole œuvre, because almost every "lost" and "whereabouts unknown" in the other artwork entries leads back to it.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0003 %%
The sentence found nearly everywhere — that her works "were destroyed by the Nazis during World War II" and "at least 60 survive" — is true in outline and wrong in three specifics, each of which matters:

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0004 %%
1. **The losses were concentrated in one place.** Not scattered across Europe, not "in Russia": her Ukrainian holdings had been consolidated into a single museum in 1932, and that museum was destroyed.
2. **"Destroyed" both understates and overstates.** The Kharkiv Art Museum's own filings with the German Lost Art Foundation classify the Bashkirtseff losses as ***looted* property** (*kriegsbedingt verbrachtes Kulturgut*), not as destroyed property. Part of the collection was removed westward by the Einsatzstab Reichsleiter Rosenberg in 1941–43; the rest burned when the occupiers torched the building. Some of the sixty-five may still physically exist somewhere.
3. **The claim that her paintings perished in the bombing of Gavrontsi is unsupported** and should simply be dropped. See below.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0005 %%
## Description

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0006 %%
The baseline against which any loss must be measured is the posthumous exhibition catalogue of **9 February 1885** — 224 numbered paintings, pastels and drawings plus five sculptures, probably compiled from Marie's own notebook, and therefore as close to an authorial checklist as exists. Against that baseline:

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0007 %%
| | Count | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Works in the 1885 catalogue | 229 (224 + 5 sculptures) | High — primary source |
| Works given by her mother to the Museum of Alexander III, St Petersburg | 141 | Medium — the museum's own unfootnoted figure; the date is disputed |
| Works transferred to Soviet Ukraine, 1930–1932 | 129 (2 + 127) | Medium — same source |
| Canvases itemised as lost at Kharkiv | **65** loss records ("66" in the Russian Museum's formula) | High for 65 — filed by the museum itself |
| Paintings surviving in Ukraine (as of August 2026) | 3 | High, but see the war note below |
| Works locatable today in public collections worldwide | 14 | High |

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0008 %%
**The arithmetic does not close, and honest public text should say so.** 127 + 2 sent onward, plus 8 paintings and 13 drawings retained in Leningrad, comes to 150 against a stated inflow of 141 — and at least one work (the *Parapluie*, inv. Ж-2740) entered the Russian Museum in 1904, before the supposed donation, so the museum evidently held Bashkirtseff material from more than one accession. The usable formulation is: *roughly 140 works went to St Petersburg, most were sent on to Kharkiv in 1932, and about 65 were lost there in the war* — with the precision flagged as unsettled.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0009 %%
## History

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0010 %%
### The provenance chain

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0011 %%
Everything begins in her studio in the rue Ampère and passes, after the 1885 memorial exhibition, to her mother, **Maria Stepanovna Bashkirtseva née Babanina**, who spent the rest of her life placing her daughter's work. Some went to French and Dutch museums and is still there: the Petit Palais (1902 and 1907), the Rijksmuseum (June 1902), the Musée Ziem at Martigues, the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Nice (1920). The great bulk — about **141 works** — went to the **Museum of Alexander III** in St Petersburg, today the **State Russian Museum**.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0012 %%
**When** is genuinely unresolved. The Russian Museum's artist page says **1908**; S. I. Bocharova of the Poltava Art Museum, the best-credentialed Ukrainian specialist, says **1902**; and the museum's own object record for *[Le Parapluie](./LE_PARAPLUIE.md)* says «Пост. в 1904 г. от М. С. Башкирцевой» — received in **1904**. The safe phrasing is "in the first decade of the twentieth century."

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0013 %%
From St Petersburg the collection was broken up: two works to Krasnoyarsk in **1928**; two paintings to Dnipropetrovsk in **1930**; and, at the request of the Ukrainian People's Commissariat of Education, **127 works to Soviet Ukraine in 1932**, where they were concentrated in the **Kharkiv State Art-Historical Museum**. The collector Tetiana Shvets reports that the transfer was initiated in 1931 by the academician **Novytsky**, petitioning on behalf of Ukrainian cultural figures on the grounds that Marie was born near Poltava — a detail that rests on Shvets's testimony and is attributed to her here. Eight paintings and thirteen drawings stayed in Leningrad.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0014 %%
### Kharkiv, 1941–1943

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0015 %%
The Kharkiv gallery was **not on the Soviet evacuation priority list**. In October 1941 only **4,711 items out of some 75,000** were taken east, to Novosibirsk; nothing places any Bashkirtseff among them. Under German occupation the building housed officers and functioned as an open warehouse, with consignments shipped to Germany. **A few days before the liberation of 23 August 1943, the occupiers burned the five-storey museum with everything left inside.**

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0016 %%
The Russian Museum's formula for what happened — «во время эвакуации Харьковской картинной галереи бесследно пропали 66 её полотен», sixty-six of her canvases vanished without trace *during the evacuation* — is a Soviet-era euphemism. There was no evacuation of these works. The museum's own postwar claims are filed against Germany.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0017 %%
### The itemised evidence: lostart.de

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0018 %%
The German Lost Art Database holds a contiguous block of loss records filed by the Kharkiv Art Museum and published since 2003, IDs **293222–293786**, indexed under the transliteration **"Baskirceva, Marija Konstantinovna"** — which is why searching for *Bashkirtseff*, *Bashkirtseva* or *Baschkirzew* finds nothing at all. The count is **65**. A specimen record:

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0019 %%
> *Sketch of a woman* — Baskirceva, Marija Konstantinovna (1860–1884) — oil on canvas — **116 × 93 cm** — inv. `1784 kleines russisches sh` — Kharkiv Art Museum — published since 2003 — **looted property**, report type *Suchmeldung* (search request). — [lostart.de record 293511](https://www.lostart.de/en/lost/object/sketch-woman/293511)

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0020 %%
Titles recoverable from the block include *Woman Model and Skeleton*, *Rendezvous*, *Portrait Pelican* (almost certainly the *Portrait du général Pélikan*, no. 13 in the 1885 catalogue), *Moon Light*, *Sea Shore*, *Forest*, *Two Women*, *Boats*, *Old Woman*, *Girl* and the *Sketch of a woman* above. **This is the single best documentary source on the losses and it is essentially unused in the English-language literature.** It has never been cross-referenced against the 1885 catalogue; doing so would let anyone say, work by work, what was lost, and would test whether "65" or "66" is right.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0021 %%
Shvets additionally reports having found ERR inventories in Kyiv listing works removed from the Kharkiv museum in 1941–42 with titles and dimensions, and that eight wagons of cultural property left Kharkiv undocumented in 1943. The digitised ERR archive is public at [err.tsdavo.gov.ua](https://err.tsdavo.gov.ua/); a full-text search for «Башкирцева» returns nothing, but the index appears to cover file-level descriptions rather than page images, and ERR inventories list titles in German rather than the artist's Cyrillic surname.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0022 %%
### Gavrontsi: the story to drop

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0023 %%
%% [#Gavronzi](../../places/residences/GAVRONZI.md) %%
The Russian Museum's text states that most of her works «погибла во время Второй мировой войны при бомбёжке Гавронцев» — perished in the bombing of Gavrontsi — and both Wikipedias repeat it onward into English sources. **No source in any language supports "bombed," and nothing places any Bashkirtseff work at Gavrontsi in the twentieth century.** The family left for abroad in 1870; the estate was sold to Count Sheremetev in 1900 and wrecked in 1917–19. Ukrainian Wikipedia's actual sentence says only that *by* the Second World War no trace of the estate remained — which is not a claim about artworks at all. The provenance chain runs Paris → Nice → St Petersburg → Kharkiv, and any claim that her work perished with the family estate is a conflation with the Kharkiv loss.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0024 %%
A second confusion worth avoiding: the **Poltava Local Lore Museum** burned in September 1943, with documented losses that do *not* include Bashkirtseff. The **Poltava Art Museum** is a different institution and holds genuine Bashkirtseff material — the 1885 exhibition catalogue, a magazine page, and an album of her early drawings dated 1877 — acquired in 1970 as a gift from Dr Armand Yon of Canada.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0025 %%
### What survives in Ukraine, and what is happening to it

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0026 %%
Three paintings: *[За книгою / At a Book](./AT_A_BOOK.md)* at the Kharkiv Art Museum, *[L'Atelier Julian / У студії](./L_ATELIER_JULIAN.md)* at the Dnipro Art Museum, and *[Горе / Sorrow](./SORROW.md)* at the Sumy Regional Art Museum. All three museums have been hit since 2022:

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0027 %%
- **Kharkiv** — shelled on 2 and 8 March 2022 (windows, roof, cracked walls) and again on 31 December 2023. On **14 June 2026** a Shahed drone struck the building directly: roof fire, second-floor ceilings collapsed, some 1,200 m² alight, roughly **a thousand works damaged** by blast, fire or firefighting water, several canvases burned. The best-known works had been evacuated by the state in 2022 to undisclosed destinations, but the collection exceeds 26,000 items and much remained in the building. **The status of *За книгою* is not established by any source.**
- **Dnipro** — façade and windows damaged on 30 September 2025 and again on 16 April 2026; anniversary displays restored on 6 June 2026. The condition of *У студії* has not been individually reported.
- **Sumy** — roof damaged in 2023, windows lost in the ballistic strike of 13 April 2025. The collection is **not** fully evacuated and the acting director treats the matter as classified. Sumy is thirty kilometres from the Russian border.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0028 %%
**Any statement that three Bashkirtseff paintings survive in Ukraine should be dated.** Two of the three are in museums with partly unevacuated collections under active bombardment.

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0029 %%
## References in Diary

%% GLO_KHARKIV_LOST_WORKS.0030 %%
None: all of this happened after Marie's death on 31 October 1884. The connection runs the other way. Because so much of the œuvre is gone, the diary is in many cases the **only surviving description** of a picture — the life-size peasant woman at the wattle fence painted at Gavronzi in the summer of 1881 (092.0104, 092.0198), the *Thérèse* of 1882 (090.0492), the whole Nice campaign inventoried at 090.0492–0493, the six or seven laughing heads of *[Les trois Rires](./LES_TROIS_RIRES.md)* (102.0072). For those works Marie's own account is the record, and the loss is what makes it one.
