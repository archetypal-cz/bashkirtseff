## Overview

%% GLO_SAMSON_FOUNTAIN_PETERHOF.0002 %%
The Samson Fountain is the central fountain of the Grand Cascade at **Peterhof**, the imperial palace on the Gulf of Finland west of Saint Petersburg ([Peterhof](../../places/cities/PETERHOF.md)). A bronze Samson tears open the jaws of a lion, from whose mouth a jet of water rises some twenty metres, down the axis of the Marine Canal that runs from the cascade to the sea. The group commemorates the Russian victory over the Swedes at Poltava on 27 June 1709, the feast of Saint Samson, won by [Peter the Great](../../people/historical/PETER_THE_GREAT.md) (Peterhof State Museum, «Fountain Samson»; Wikipedia, «Peterhof Palace»; the 20-metre jet and the lion are from the museum page).

%% GLO_SAMSON_FOUNTAIN_PETERHOF.0003 %%
The first Samson, in lead, was made by Bartolomeo Carlo Rastrelli and set up in 1735 for the 25th anniversary of the battle. Because the lead had deteriorated, it was replaced in 1802 by a bronze statue by **Mikhail Kozlovsky**, who kept the composition and meaning. This is the figure Marie saw. The Kozlovsky statue was lost in the Second World War (sources differ on whether it was carried off in 1941 or lost in 1944), and the Samson on the cascade today is a copy made in 1947 by the sculptors Simonov and Mikhailov from pre-war documents and photographs (Peterhof State Museum, as above).

## Marie and the Samson

%% GLO_SAMSON_FOUNTAIN_PETERHOF.0005 %%
On **9 August 1876**, during her stay in Russia, Marie watches the fountains of Peterhof set going at six o'clock: «A six heures les mille fontaines de Péterhof furent lancées. On ne peut s'imaginer rien de plus beau» (064.0473). She then describes the canal with its gigantic jets «qui semblent petits en comparaison du Samson terrassant un tigre, de la gueule béante de laquelle l'eau s'élance presque aussi haut que les deux terrasses superposées au sommet desquelles se trouve le palais de l'Empereur» (064.0475). «Ce canal Samson et le palais en perspective sont d'un effet rare.» The paragraph goes on with praise for the Emperor and the imperial house, who spend the summer at Peterhof.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_SAMSON_FOUNTAIN_PETERHOF.0007 %%
Marie calls the beast a tiger; it is a lion (the Peterhof museum and the diary's own footnote say lion). The date 1735 in the diary footnote belongs to Rastrelli's original, not to the statue she saw. The photograph shows the central jet from above; the statue itself, which stands in the middle of the basin, is hidden by the water. No free photograph of the Kozlovsky statue itself was found.
