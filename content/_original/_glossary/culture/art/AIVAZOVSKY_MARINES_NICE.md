## Overview

%% GLO_AIVAZOVSKY_MARINES_NICE.0002 %%
A charity exhibition of seascapes by the Russian-Armenian marine painter [Ivan Aivazovsky](../../people/mentioned/AIVAZOVSKY.md) (1817-1900), held in the [Hôtel Chauvain](../../places/social/HOTEL_CHAUVAIN.md) in Nice in early 1874, with a franc admission «au profit des pauvres». The diary is the only source found for the exhibition; the pictures shown are not listed, and no catalogue or press notice has been located. No image is given because no work can be tied to the exhibition.

%% GLO_AIVAZOVSKY_MARINES_NICE.0003 %%
Aivazovsky was the most celebrated Russian marine painter, based in Feodosia in the Crimea and known for luminous seas, storms and mists (Wikipedia, «Ivan Aivazovsky»). Wikipedia says that in 1872 he travelled to Nice and Florence to exhibit his paintings (citing Rogachevsky); Marie's dated visit is 1 March 1874, so either he showed pictures in Nice on more than one occasion or the Wikipedia date is approximate.

%% GLO_AIVAZOVSKY_MARINES_NICE.0004 %%
## Marie and the exhibition

%% GLO_AIVAZOVSKY_MARINES_NICE.0005 %%
On **1 March 1874**, in Nice, the family goes to the Hôtel Chauvain «voir les tableaux d'Aïvazowsky, l'entrée un franc au profit des pauvres. Les effets de la lumière, l'eau, les vagues, les brouillards sont d'une finesse, d'un naturel, d'une transparence surprenants» (017.0090). She is fifteen, and this is her first recorded response to a marine painter; what she praises is light, water, waves and mist.

%% GLO_AIVAZOVSKY_MARINES_NICE.0006 %%
The pictures stay in her eye. On **12 April 1874**, watching a furious storm at the spot Nice calls «enlève-chapeau», she sees «l'eau [...] transparente, verte, claire, vaporeuse comme chez Aïvazowsky» (018.0244). This is a comparison with his seas, not a second visit.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_AIVAZOVSKY_MARINES_NICE.0008 %%
Which paintings were exhibited, and whether the artist was present in Nice in 1874, remain open. The artist's own biography is in [Aivazovsky](../../people/mentioned/AIVAZOVSKY.md); a separate glossary page [Aïvazowsky](../history/AIVAZOWSKY.md) also exists for the 1874 comparison.
