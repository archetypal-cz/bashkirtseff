## Overview

%% GLO_VENUS_DE_MEDICIS.0002 %%
The Venus de' Medici is a Hellenistic marble sculpture of Aphrodite, 1.53 m tall, in the Uffizi Gallery in Florence. It is a 1st-century BC marble copy of a bronze original of the Praxitelean tradition, following the type of the Aphrodite of Knidos. The goddess is shown in a momentary, modest pose as if surprised on emerging from the sea, which the dolphin at her feet alludes to. The signature «Kleomenes, son of Apollodoros, Athenian» on the base is not original. The arms were restored by Ercole Ferrata. (Wikipedia, «Venus de' Medici».)

%% GLO_VENUS_DE_MEDICIS.0003 %%
Published in the collection of the Villa Medici in Rome in 1638, it was sent to Florence in 1677 and placed in the Tribuna of the Uffizi, where it became a high point of the Grand Tour and was reckoned one of the half-dozen finest antique statues. It was taken to Paris in 1803 and returned to Florence on 27 December 1815. From the nineteenth century onward a few dissenting voices began to qualify the admiration (Haskell and Penny, cited by Wikipedia, as above). Marie's verdict belongs to that dissent.

## Marie and the Venus de' Medici

%% GLO_VENUS_DE_MEDICIS.0005 %%
On **15 September 1875**, in the Uffizi, she finally stands before it: «Enfin me voilà devant la fameuse *Venere Medicea* ! Cette petite poupée est une déception nouvelle» (043.0191). She dislikes the prominent ankles, the head «trop petite», the features «communs à toutes les statues grecques», the cold mouth and expressionless eyes. She grants that the proportions are admirable, but adds: «Qu'on me nomme barbare, ignorante, arrogante, stupide, mais c'est mon avis. La Vénus de Milo est beaucoup plus Vénus» (see [Venus de Milo](./VENUS_DE_MILO.md)).

%% GLO_VENUS_DE_MEDICIS.0006 %%
A few paragraphs later, at the close of the same day's gallery visit, she writes: «La Vénus de Médicis m'a rendue joliment fière !» (043.0200). She does not say why. The remark most likely refers to her own figure set beside the statue, but the text does not state it.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_VENUS_DE_MEDICIS.0008 %%
The image is a period photograph (Alinari, c. 1875-1900) of the statue as it stood in the Uffizi in Marie's years. The reason for her pride in 043.0200 is open.
