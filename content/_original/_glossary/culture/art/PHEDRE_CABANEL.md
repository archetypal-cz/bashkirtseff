## Overview

%% GLO_PHEDRE_CABANEL.0002 %%
*Phèdre* is a large history painting of 1880 by [Alexandre Cabanel](../../people/artists/CABANEL.md), shown at the Salon of 1880 and then given by the painter to the museum of his native Montpellier, the Musée Fabre, where it hangs (Wikipedia, «Phaedra (Cabanel)»). The Musée Fabre's teaching file lists it as 194 x 286 cm, «don de l'auteur, 1880» (a dealer listing gives other dimensions). Phaedra, Theseus's wife in Euripides and Racine, lies on her bed in her sleeplessness, consumed by her guilty passion for her stepson Hippolytus. Two attendants watch her, one anxious, one asleep. Wikipedia reads the picture as one of Cabanel's «theatrical heroines»; Sarah Bernhardt had played Racine's Phèdre in 1879 (Musée Fabre teaching file).

## Marie and Cabanel's Phèdre

%% GLO_PHEDRE_CABANEL.0004 %%
Marie names the picture only as a symbol of official taste. On **16 January 1884**, on the public success going to the wrong painters, she is indignant: «est-ce possible que le succès aille au Phèdre et au Aurore de Cabanel, Bouguereau, Lefèbvre et compagnie !» (103.0046). The «Phèdre» is Cabanel's, the «Aurore» is Bouguereau's (she writes in the same entry, «une Aurore de Bouguereau ou un tableau historique de Cabanel», 103.0058). On **29 January 1884**, praising Munkácsy's *Golgotha*, she says that no «fabricant d'Aurore et de Phèdre de Pris» can be compared to him (103.0178; see [Golgotha](./GOLGOTHA_MUNKACSY.md)).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_PHEDRE_CABANEL.0006 %%
Marie does not describe the painting; she only uses its title with that of Bouguereau's *Aurore* as shorthand for the academic mythological picture that wins prizes. The *Aurore* is not Cabanel's work. «de Pris» in 103.0178 is probably a slip for «de prix».
