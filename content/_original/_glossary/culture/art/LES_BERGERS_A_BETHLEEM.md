## Overview

%% GLO_LES_BERGERS_A_BETHLEEM.0002 %%
*Les Bergers à Bethléem* is a **project**, not a finished picture: a Nativity-night subject (the shepherds and the star) that [Jules Bastien-Lepage](../../people/recurring/BASTIEN_LEPAGE.md) was planning in the winter of 1883-84, and which Marie knew only through his brother [Émile Bastien-Lepage](../../people/mentioned/EMILE_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.md), the architect. Bastien-Lepage died on 10 December 1884 without painting it; there is no work to illustrate, so this entry carries no image.

%% GLO_LES_BERGERS_A_BETHLEEM.0003 %%
The subject was not new to him: in 1875 his *Annonciation aux bergers* (« Angels appearing to the Shepherds ») won him second place in the Prix de Rome competition. (Source: English and French Wikipedia, « Jules Bastien-Lepage ».) Whether the project of 1884 reworked that earlier picture is not stated in the diary or in the sources consulted.

## Marie and the project

%% GLO_LES_BERGERS_A_BETHLEEM.0005 %%
On **16 January 1884** Émile tells her that among Bastien-Lepage's many projects is *Les Bergers à Bethléem* (103.0039). Marie spends the evening on it: « Sujet sublime, et qu'il rendra plus sublime encore » (103.0040); she has a « vision très nette » of it, as an evening scene dominated by « la fameuse Étoile », tender, grand and mysterious, and she connects it with *Jeanne d'Arc* and *Le Soir au village* (103.0041). She compares its effect to that of her own [Saintes femmes](./LES_SAINTES_FEMMES.md) -- also an evening picture, but his will be « grand, fort, tendre, radieux » whereas hers is « désolé, terrible » (103.0042).

%% GLO_LES_BERGERS_A_BETHLEEM.0006 %%
On **2 February 1884** she cannot sleep for thinking of it: « Rien sur la terre n'est comparable aux divins Bergers. Je n'ai été aussi folle que pour mes Saintes femmes » (103.0237), and she writes in bed that « ces maudits Bergers me troublent » (103.0253). Her private catalogue of feelings in that entry matches the project to the opera *Hamlet* (103.0249). She is excited about a picture « que je n'ai pas vus et qui n'existent pas encore » and knows that most people would find her absurd (103.0041).
