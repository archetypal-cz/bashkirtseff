## Overview

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0002 %%
%% [#Art_Practice](../themes/ART_PRACTICE.md) [#Salon](./SALON.md) %%
*Jean et Jacques* (1883, oil on canvas, 155 × 115 cm) is the picture that made Marie a prize-winning painter. Two small, deliberately unhandsome boys from the Paris outskirts walk hand in hand along a wall on their way to school. It was accepted at the **Salon of 1883** — where she showed it beside *[La Parisienne](./LA_PARISIENNE.md)* and the pastel portrait of her cousin Dina — and the same year it took a ***mention honorable* at the Exposition internationale de Nice**, a detail recorded in the 1885 catalogue and missing from almost every account of her since.

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0003 %%
It is also the canvas that shows what she had learned from **[Bastien-Lepage](../../people/recurring/BASTIEN_LEPAGE.md)**: a naturalist subject taken from the street, painted outdoors, at life scale, with none of the prettiness the Salon expected of a woman painter. The anxiety runs through the whole making of it — «Si mon tableau allait lui ressembler…» (098.0418). Its direct descendant is *[Un meeting](./UN_MEETING.md)*, conceived five weeks after this one was hung.

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0004 %%
## Description

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0005 %%
Marie's own account, 27 February 1883: «deux gamins qui marchent le long d'un troittoir, en se tenant par la main, l'aîné a [huit ans] et regarde dans le vague devant lui, une feuille entre les lèvres, le petit regarde le public, une main dans la poche de son pantalon» (098.0419) — two boys walking along a pavement holding hands, the elder staring into the middle distance with a leaf between his lips, the younger looking straight out at the viewer with one hand in his trouser pocket. Sixteen days earlier she had put it more plainly still: two small boys «qui vont a l'asile ou a l'ecole», one not yet six and the other just turned four, painted «dans la cour de l'asile, en plein air bien enveloppee» (098.0318–0319) — in the yard of the infant school, outdoors, well wrapped up. She was consumptive and knew it.

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0006 %%
Her own verdict at the Salon was harsh: «le tableau m'a paru sombre et quoique fait en plein air, il n'en a pas l'air. Le mur n'a pas l'air d'un mur» (099.0311) — dark, and not looking painted outdoors even though it was; the wall does not look like a wall.

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0007 %%
**Where the picture is now is genuinely unresolved, and this entry asserts nothing.** Three locations circulate in the literature: the **Newberry Library** in Chicago (repeated by several secondary sources, but not confirmed in the Newberry's own collections database), the **Musée d'Orsay** via the Louvre (per the Dom Russkogo Zarubezhya), and an unnamed **private collection**. None could be verified. There is likewise **no free reproduction** — nothing on Wikimedia Commons — which for one of her two or three most discussed canvases is among the largest image gaps in her œuvre.

## Making of the Work

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0009 %%
%% [#Tony_Robert-Fleury](../../people/artists/ROBERT_FLEURY.md) [#Julian](../../people/mentioned/RODOLPHE_JULIAN.md) %%
The conception comes on **30 January 1883**, out of a bad night: «Je ne sais quel coup de vent passe… Je renais, j'ai trouvé un tableau» (098.0218), written immediately after a bout of despair over the Bastien-Lepage gossip. She begins on 7 February (098.0302), scraps the first canvas and restarts on the 11th (098.0318). With thirty days to the Salon deadline, Tony Robert-Fleury's first look is «très très bien» (098.0324, 098.0326). On 28 February the Canroberts come to see it and she counts the cost: nineteen days, where fifteen would have done if she had not had to repaint one of the boys, who «paraissait trop âgé» (098.0433). Then the verdicts: Tony again (098.0442), Émile Bastien-Lepage — «il sera bien vu au Salon», and she is ashamed to be showing him something so close to his brother's manner (098.0465) — and Julian (098.0489). On 27 March, after a day of confusion over the admission lists: «Je suis archi-reçue» (098.0603).

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0010 %%
At the Salon it is not on the *cimaise*, though Tony insists it can still be seen (099.0129, 099.0177), and Émile Bastien-Lepage walks the critic Fourcaud past it with an article promised (099.0339). Then the peculiar morning of **23 May 1883**: the picture is moved *higher* on the wall, above Benner's tulips, on the very day the *mention honorable* is announced — and the award label is attached to the pastel instead. Marie's letter to Tony and his reply are both copied into the entry (099.0393).

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0011 %%
The picture went on working for her afterwards. In November 1883 the St Petersburg *Illustration universelle* put a drawing after it on its front page (102.0054); in March 1884 she sent it to Mme Bertaux's exhibition of the Union des Femmes Peintres et Sculpteurs with the Grande-Jatte landscape and the Dina pastel (103.0412, 103.0420); in August 1884 Périvier of *Le Figaro illustré* took note of "Jean-Jacques" and she promised a drawing after it for the Christmas number (105.0713, 105.0741). The prize itself is reported flatly, on 14 May 1884: «Mon tableau *Jean et Jacques* a obtenu une mention honorable à Nice. Tout le monde est fou de joie, excepté moi.» (104.0165)

%% GLO_JEAN_ET_JACQUES.0012 %%
**Careful with the name.** Marie never calls the picture *Jean et Jacques* while she is painting it — throughout 1883 it is simply «mon tableau» or «les deux gamins», and the title surfaces only when the Nice prize is announced. And the «portrait de Jacques à l'huile» she works on in 1884 (103.0132, 103.0136, 103.0169 «Fini Jacques !», 103.0649, 104.0443, 104.0625–0626) is a *separate, later* picture of the same child model, not this canvas.
