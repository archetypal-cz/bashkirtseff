## Overview

%% GLO_LE_MENDIANT_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.0002 %%
*Le Mendiant* (The Beggar) is a large figure painting by [Jules Bastien-Lepage](../../people/recurring/BASTIEN_LEPAGE.md): an old, bearded tramp with a stick and a sack stands on the doorstep of a village house, while a small girl in a blue dress peeps round the half-open door. The canvas is signed «Bastien-Lepage, Damvillers, 1880» (his native village in the Meuse) and is dated 1880 in the Copenhagen record, while biographies give 1881; it is now in the Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek, Copenhagen (Wikimedia Commons photograph taken in the museum; artrenewal.org record «Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek»; en.wikipedia «Jules Bastien-Lepage», cited in the diary's footnote at 091.0548). A drawing of an old beggar on a house step, related to the Copenhagen picture, is in the Louvre (Louvre collections, cl020026119).

%% GLO_LE_MENDIANT_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.0003 %%
It belongs with the village subjects (*Les Foins*, *Le Père Jacques*, *Le Soir au village*) on which Bastien-Lepage's reputation as the painter of peasant truth rested in the early 1880s.

%% GLO_LE_MENDIANT_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.0004 %%
## Marie and Le Mendiant

%% GLO_LE_MENDIANT_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.0005 %%
On **1 May 1881**, at the Paris exhibition (the Salon), Marie looks at three heads hung together, «entre le portrait de Wolff et le mendiant de Bastien-Lepage», and finds in the mediocre «barbouillages» around them at least something good (091.0548). The footnote there identifies both pictures as Bastien-Lepage's, the portrait of the critic Albert Wolff and *Le Mendiant*, of 1881. The two are named as neighbours, which is how she places the beggar in the Salon.

%% GLO_LE_MENDIANT_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.0006 %%
On **27 April 1883**, running through Bastien-Lepage's pictures in her mind, she grants him only «peut-être les pommes de terre et le mendiant... Et encore...» (099.0228). The «pommes de terre» are the *Saison d'octobre* (see [Saison d'octobre](./SAISON_D_OCTOBRE.md)); the «mendiant» is probably this picture, in a paragraph about the painter's critics. The next paragraph praises *Le Père Jacques* (099.0229).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LE_MENDIANT_BASTIEN_LEPAGE.0008 %%
The year differs between the canvas (1880) and the biographies (1881, the year of the Salon where Marie saw it, per the diary footnote). The 1883 mention (099.0228) is not tied to a title; *Le Mendiant* is the likely reading because the painter has no other well-known picture of this name. Whether the Copenhagen picture is the one exhibited in the Salon of 1881 rests on the title and date, not on a catalogue entry that was consulted.
