## Overview

%% GLO_HONORINE_BALZAC.0002 %%
*Honorine* is a novella by [Honoré de Balzac](./BALZAC.md), published in 1843 and later placed among the *Scènes de la vie privée* of *La Comédie humaine* (Wikipedia, «Honorine (novel)»). It is dedicated to Achille Devéria (dedication on French Wikisource). A story within a story: in 1836, at Genoa, the consul Maurice tells a dinner of Parisian guests how, as a young secretary, he served Count Octave, whose wife Honorine had left him. She had gone off with an adventurer who abandoned her, and she now lives in remorse and refuses every approach. Octave secretly helps her in her work with flowers, and Maurice is sent, disguised, to the house next door to plead his case. In the end she agrees to see her husband and returns to him, and the story ends sadly for both (same Wikipedia article).

%% GLO_HONORINE_BALZAC.0003 %%
The book opens with a preamble on France and Paris: «Retrouver Paris ! savez-vous ce que c'est, ô Parisiens !» The opening praises the quick conversation and the genius of the unspoken that, Balzac says, exist nowhere outside Paris (text, French Wikisource).

## Marie and Honorine

%% GLO_HONORINE_BALZAC.0005 %%
- **5 December 1882 (097.0191).** «Je sors de lire d'un trait "Honorine" et je voudrais posséder cette sublime éloquence de la plume afin qu'en me lisant on s'intéresse à ma plate existence.» She asks whether she must not first «devenir quelqu'un» if anyone is to read through her thousands of pages.
- **9 December 1882 (097.0221).** In the same breath as her reading of [*Modeste Mignon*](./MODESTE_MIGNON_BALZAC.md): «Balzac est le plus grand génie... Et puis relisez les premières pages d'"Honorine" sur Paris et sur ce qui fait aimer Paris, ce que j'ai essayé si souvent de dire sans y arriver...» The opening passage described above is what she means.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_HONORINE_BALZAC.0007 %%
- **Another «Honorine» in the glossary.** The entry HONORINE (culture/theater) is a different person, an actress seen at the Nice theatre in March 1874; it does not concern this novella. Because both carry the bare name, this entry's aliases use the full form.
- Marie does not say which edition she read.
