## Overview

%% GLO_L_HOMME_FEMME_DUMAS_FILS.0002 %%
*L'Homme-femme* is a short polemical book by Alexandre Dumas fils (1824-1895), published in Paris by Michel Lévy frères in 1872 under the full title *L'Homme-femme, réponse à M. Henri d'Ideville* (Internet Archive record of the Michel Lévy edition). It takes the form of an open letter to the writer Henri d'Ideville, who had published an article in *Le Soir* on the question "Faut-il tuer la femme adultère ? Faut-il lui pardonner ?" and had concluded for forgiveness. Dumas answers that the guilty wife must be killed, and he announces that this question will be the basis of his next play, *La Femme de Claude* (text of the Michel Lévy edition, pp. 1-2 of the pamphlet).

%% GLO_L_HOMME_FEMME_DUMAS_FILS.0003 %%
The best-known piece of the argument is the division of women into three classes, "les vestales... les matrones... les hétaïres", in plainer words "les femmes de temple ; les femmes de foyer ; les femmes de rue": virgins belong to the temple, wives and mothers to the hearth, courtesans to the street. The temple, he says, is defined by "le mystère et l'impénétrabilité". The pamphlet ends with the imagined verdict on the unfaithful wife: "c'est la femelle de Caïn ; -- tue-la." (same edition). The slogan "Tue-la !" became attached to Dumas's name. See also [#Alexandre_Dumas_fils](../../people/mentioned/ALEXANDRE_DUMAS_FILS.md).

## Marie and L'Homme-femme

%% GLO_L_HOMME_FEMME_DUMAS_FILS.0005 %%
%% [#Alexandre_Dumas_fils](../../people/mentioned/ALEXANDRE_DUMAS_FILS.md) %%
**16 January 1879, Paris** (084.0043). Marie reads the book and records "la sensation la plus violente de ma vie", stronger than the fright after her "rencontre scaligère et royale" with the King of Italy at Naples. Her admiration for Dumas made her believe for a moment in "de l'amour, de la passion, du délire pour cet homme de près de [cinquante-cinq ans, the words blacked out in the manuscript] et que je n'ai jamais vu. J'ai compris Bettina et Goethe." The comparison is with Bettina von Arnim's passion for the aged Goethe. Dumas fils was then 54.

%% GLO_L_HOMME_FEMME_DUMAS_FILS.0006 %%
**18 May 1879, Paris** (085.0096). Of her friend Jeanne de Ballore, trapped in a marriage she hates, Marie writes that Jeanne "ne pourrait aimer personne... c'est une femme du Temple, si vous avez lu *L'Homme-Femme*". The diary's footnote glosses the term from Dumas's three classes. Marie's use fits his definition of the temple as the woman who stays closed and impenetrable.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_L_HOMME_FEMME_DUMAS_FILS.0008 %%
- The date 1872 is that of the Michel Lévy edition. Marie calls it only "l'Homme-femme", with the article, and reads it in 1879, years after the first Parisian debate.
- Dumas fils's age in the blacked-out words is the diary's; Marie's "cinquante-cinq ans" is her own estimate (he was born in July 1824).
