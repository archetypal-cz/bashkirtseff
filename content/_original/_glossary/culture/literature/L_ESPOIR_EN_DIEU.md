## Overview

%% GLO_L_ESPOIR_EN_DIEU.0002 %%
%% [#Musset](../../people/mentioned/MUSSET.md) %%
"L'espoir en Dieu" ("Hope in God") is a long philosophical poem by Alfred de Musset (1810–1857), first published February 15, 1838 in the *Revue des Deux Mondes* and later collected in *Poésies nouvelles (1836–1852)*. It is one of Musset's major meditative works, written in the tradition of the philosophical/religious poem practiced by contemporaries such as Lamartine and Vigny.

%% GLO_L_ESPOIR_EN_DIEU.0003 %%
The poem stages an unresolved argument between reason and faith. Musset weighs the pagan/skeptical position — that the world is governed by chance or indifferent gods, and the only wisdom is to enjoy life while it lasts — against the Christian claim that suffering has meaning under a just and watchful God. Rather than resolving the dilemma philosophically, the poem ends in a prayer: an appeal to God to make Himself known despite the impossibility of proving His justice by reason alone. Its central question — why a good and just God permits evil and suffering — is the classical problem of theodicy.

## Why Marie Invokes It

%% GLO_L_ESPOIR_EN_DIEU.0005 %%
%% [#Health](../themes/HEALTH.md) [#Religion](../themes/RELIGION.md) %%
Marie refers to the poem in her entry of November 16, 1882 (paragraph 097.0081), immediately after a hospital surgeon confirms that her progressive deafness is incurable. Grappling with "Pourquoi Dieu fait-il souffrir ?" ("Why does God make us suffer?"), she tells her reader to consult Musset's poem rather than have her restate its argument, and asks her editor to insert it "tout au long" (in full) at this point in the text — a request Marie's actual editors never honored, in either the censored 1887 edition or, as far as this project's sources show, any later one. The invocation is both a literary shorthand (Musset's poem was widely known to educated 19th-century readers as *the* text on this exact question) and a deeply personal one: Marie is 22, going deaf, and quarreling with the God she was raised to believe in.

## Reading the Full Text

%% GLO_L_ESPOIR_EN_DIEU.0007 %%
The poem is long (several hundred lines in multiple movements) and is in the public domain (Musset died in 1857). Rather than reproduce it here, readers wanting to honor Marie's request and read it "tout au long" can consult the validated French text on Wikisource: [L'espoir en Dieu — French Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/L%E2%80%99espoir_en_Dieu).
