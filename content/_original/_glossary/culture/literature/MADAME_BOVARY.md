## Overview

%% GLO_MADAME_BOVARY.0002 %%
*Madame Bovary: Mœurs de province* is the first novel of [Gustave Flaubert](./FLAUBERT.md). It appeared in the *Revue de Paris* between 1 October and 15 December 1856, when public prosecutors attacked it as obscene. The trial in January 1857 made it notorious, Flaubert was acquitted on 7 February 1857, and the book came out in two volumes in April 1857 and became a bestseller (Wikipedia, «Madame Bovary»). Its heroine, Emma Bovary, lives beyond her means to escape the boredom of provincial life (same article). It is often counted a cornerstone of realism in the French novel.

%% GLO_MADAME_BOVARY.0003 %%
One scene matters for the diary: in the third part Emma's lover Léon has a cab fetched, and the fiacre, urged on by the voice inside («Marchez donc !»), wanders for hours through Rouen «sans parti pris ni direction, au hasard», while the driver, near tears with thirst and fatigue, cannot understand what makes his fares unwilling to stop (text, Project Gutenberg #14155).

## Marie and Madame Bovary

%% GLO_MADAME_BOVARY.0005 %%
- **14 July 1880 (089.0156).** On the night of the national holiday she and her party go by cab to the Buttes-Chaumont fireworks; the cabman is «un peu gris» and the streets are nearly empty of carriages. She writes: «Je crois qu'à cette heure-ci il n'y a pas eu beaucoup plus d'une voiture dans les rues, notre fiacre me faisait songer à celui de "Mme Bovary" (de Flaubert).» The allusion is to the Rouen cab ride above.
- **24 March 1884 (103.0566).** In a fog of low spirits: «Je viens de relire un livre que j'avais peu admiré il y a quelques années et qui est admirable: Mme Bovary.» The next paragraph reduces literary form to execution: «La forme littéraire... le style... oui... en somme ce n'est que de l'exécution.» (103.0567), and she turns at once to her own hard realities: ten years of struggle and work, and a mortal illness (103.0568-0570).
