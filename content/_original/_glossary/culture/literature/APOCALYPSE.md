## Overview

%% GLO_APOCALYPSE.0002 %%
%% [#Bible](./BIBLE.md) %%
The Apocalypse of John, called Revelation in English, is the last book of the New Testament: a series of visions of the end of the world, with beasts, seals, trumpets and a seven-headed dragon, traditionally ascribed to John of Patmos. In French it is *l'Apocalypse*, and its images (the beast, the seven heads, "l'alpha et l'oméga") passed into everyday speech as a byword for the obscure and the monstrous. For the Bible as a whole see [Bible](./BIBLE.md).

## Marie and the Apocalypse

%% GLO_APOCALYPSE.0004 %%
%% [#Reading](../themes/READING.md) %%
**27 December 1873, Nice (014.0240).** At half past ten in the evening: «Je viens de lire "L'Apocalypse", et je n'y comprends pas plus que les autres.» She dozes several times and catches herself with her nose on the book (the word for dozing was written in Russian), though she is not sleepy when she looks away. The phrase «les autres» is left unexplained.

%% GLO_APOCALYPSE.0005 %%
%% [#Walitsky](../../people/recurring/WALITSKY.md) %%
**27 December 1873, margin (014.0259).** A note in the margin says that the Apocalypse reminds her of the letter Walitsky wrote to Makaroff, whose gist is that "a horse without a tail and rails without an embankment are not a bird". She means it as an example of nonsense, in which she finds the book comparable.

%% GLO_APOCALYPSE.0006 %%
**30 December 1873 (014.0291).** Another margin note: «J'ai lu l'Apocalypse, je n'ai pas dormi cette fois.»

## Later allusions

%% GLO_APOCALYPSE.0008 %%
She later uses the book only as a figure of speech: the "sept têtes de l'hydre de l'Apocalypse" (033.0146, 23 May 1875); something "plus incompréhensible que l'Apocalypse" (034.0363, 17 June 1875); Zucchini, who "peut dire comme dans l'Apocalypse: Je suis l'alpha et l'omega" (056.0305, 1 April 1876); and a creature that "ressemblait à un animal de l'Apocalypse" (084.0489, 16 March 1879). They show that she knew its imagery well, but they are not readings of the book.
