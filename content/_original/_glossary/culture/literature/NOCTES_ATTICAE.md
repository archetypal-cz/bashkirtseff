## Overview

%% GLO_NOCTES_ATTICAE.0002 %%
%% [#Aulu_Gelle](./AULU_GELLE.md) %%
The *Noctes Atticae* ("Attic Nights") is a Latin commonplace book in twenty books by [Aulus Gellius](./AULU_GELLE.md) (c. 125 - after 180 AD). It collects notes on grammar, philosophy, history, antiquarian matters and other subjects, and it preserves fragments of many earlier authors who would otherwise be unknown (Wikipedia, "Aulus Gellius"; the Latin text on la.wikisource). The title alludes to Attica: Gellius was educated in Athens and lived there for a considerable period (Wikipedia, "Aulus Gellius").

%% GLO_NOCTES_ATTICAE.0003 %%
Because its chapters are short, self-contained and anecdotal, the book was a standard Latin reader. The author has his own entry; this page is the work.

## Marie and the Noctes Atticae

%% GLO_NOCTES_ATTICAE.0005 %%
Marie meets Gellius in the Latin lessons she gave herself in 1875. The two paragraphs are both from that summer.

%% GLO_NOCTES_ATTICAE.0006 %%
- **17 June 1875** (034.0363): in a tirade about a mystery "plus incompréhensible que l'Apocalypse, plus incompréhensible que cette pièce d'Aulu-Gelle - qu'on n'a jamais pu comprendre". Here *pièce* is a piece of text, not a play. The passage she means is not identified.
- **15 July 1875** (037.0009): "Je traduisais très tranquillement un passage d'Aulu-Gelle quand ma tante vient me demander avec un air malicieux si je ne suis pas à la fenêtre car il y est" -- she is supposed to be watching at the window for a young man, and the aunt teases her. Again no chapter is named.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_NOCTES_ATTICAE.0008 %%
- Neither paragraph says which edition or which chapters Marie read; the French translations linked above are the ones available to her generation, but the diary does not tell us which she used.
- Only 034.0363 names the author in running text with the work in mind ("pièce d'Aulu-Gelle"); in 037.0009 the diary says only that she translated "un passage".
