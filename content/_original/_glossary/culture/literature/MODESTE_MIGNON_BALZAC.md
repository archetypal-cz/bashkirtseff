## Overview

%% GLO_MODESTE_MIGNON_BALZAC.0002 %%
*Modeste Mignon* is a novel by [Balzac](./BALZAC.md), part of the *Scènes de la vie privée* of *La Comédie humaine*. A provincial girl of Le Havre, living on her imagination, begins a secret correspondence with a famous Paris poet she has never met (Wikipedia «Modeste Mignon»; fr.wikipedia «Modeste Mignon»). Its first part appeared in a toned-down version in the *Journal des débats* in April, May and July 1844; an expanded version was later published by Chlenowski, and a third edition came in 1846 in Furne's complete *Comédie humaine* (Wikipedia, «Modeste Mignon»). Balzac wrote it after his stay in Saint Petersburg in the summer of 1843 with Ewelina Hańska, his future wife, and dedicated it to her («À une étrangère», French Wikisource; Wikipedia).

## Marie and Modeste Mignon

%% GLO_MODESTE_MIGNON_BALZAC.0004 %%
- **9 December 1882 (097.0220).** Reading it she finds «des choses frappantes»: «Cette jeune fille qui vit par l'imagination, qui s'arrange une double vie et qui est continuellement l'héroïne d'une quantité de romans, de drames inventés par elle; qui arrive à éprouver des sensations presque physiques, qui a usé tout par l'imagination...» The next paragraph (097.0221) goes on: «Mais c'est moi, et puis elle s'est fait un idéal de bonheur et d'amour... Il faudrait tout citer.» She says that if one could guess what goes on in her head at the theatre, at a ball or in conversation, one would be surprised, herself included.
- **28 June 1883 (100.0255).** Having just written «Je demande un grand homme à aimer ! Il n'y en a pas.», she adds: «Je rêve d'entamer une correspondance à la modeste Mignon...»

%% GLO_MODESTE_MIGNON_BALZAC.0005 %%
In March 1884 she did write anonymously to a famous author, Guy de Maupassant ([Maupassant](./GUY_DE_MAUPASSANT.md)), which is the same move as Modeste's.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_MODESTE_MIGNON_BALZAC.0007 %%
- Not to be confused with the opera *Mignon* (Ambroise Thomas), which is a different work.
- The 1883 allusion is only a short phrase; the reading of December 1882 is the first sign that Marie knew the book.
