## Overview

%% GLO_COURTISANES_DU_MONDE.0002 %%
*Les Courtisanes du monde* (Paris, E. Dentu, 1870, four volumes) is the last of three linked series of society novels by [Arsène Houssaye](./ARSENE_HOUSSAYE.md): *Les Grandes Dames* (1868, four volumes), *Les Parisiennes* (1869, four volumes) and *Les Courtisanes du monde* (1870, four volumes), twelve volumes in all by the author's own count in the preface to *Les Grandes Dames* (Project Gutenberg 9261). The cycle follows, among others, the fictional duc de Parisis, "surnommé don Juan", through the Paris of the Second Empire, the racecourses and watering-places, and the world of the courtesans (*les grandes dames*, *les parisiennes*, *les courtisanes*, *les femmes démasquées*). The fr.wikisource author page lists *Les Courtisanes du grand monde* as the "3e et dernière série" of the *Grandes Dames* (1870); the BnF/Gallica scans on the Internet Archive are titled *Les Courtisanes du monde*, volumes 1-4, 1870.

## Marie and this work

%% GLO_COURTISANES_DU_MONDE.0004 %%
It is the forbidden book of her fourteenth year and one of her first acts of reading in defiance.

%% GLO_COURTISANES_DU_MONDE.0005 %%
- **21 July 1873 (007.0097).** "Il y a longtemps, plus d'un an que maman a acheté ce livre d'Arsène Houssaye *Les courtisanes du monde*. Il m'a été défendu." She finds it on the table where the painter is drawing her, opens it, reads a few pages standing, then two hours the next morning: "c'est très bien écrit, et je partage tout à fait l'opinion de l'auteur ; que c'est loin d'être immoral". Then comes a sentence that hits her like a blow: "Toute l'écurie du duc de Parisis a été vendue au duc de Hamilton !..." This sentence exists in the volume she has, the second: Houssaye's text (t. 2, checked on the Gallica scan) reads "Toute l'écurie du duc de Parisis avait été vendue au duc de Hamilton pour lord Sommerson". To Marie, who is in love with [the Duke of Hamilton](../../people/core/DUKE_OF_HAMILTON.md), it is "ce nom toujours cher".
- **21 July 1873 (007.0098).** She finds in it "bien des historiettes" of the duke and Lord Hamilton, of Mme Paskevitch in Venice, and of the worlds of Nice and Baden.
- **22 July 1873 (007.0105).** "Quel dommage de n'avoir que le deuxième volume": the work is in "quatre divisions, chacune en quatre volumes: Les courtisanes, Les grandes dames, Les Parisiennes" (a fourth division, *Les femmes déchues*, is crossed out in the manuscript). This matches the three four-volume series.
- **16 January 1874 (015.0209).** She asks her mother openly for the book "en lui disant que j'ai lu un volume. Et on me l'accorde."
- **1 February 1874 (016.0072).** She plans to read it after *Nickleby* so that Houssaye should not bore her after a book on La Vallière.

The inventory also lists 008.0116 ("le reste des *courtisanes*"): this uses the ordinary word for courtesans, not the book.
