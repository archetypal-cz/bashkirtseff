# Gustave Flaubert

## Overview

Gustave Flaubert (1821-1880), French novelist, author of *Madame Bovary* (1857) and *Bouvard et Pécuchet* (published posthumously, 1881), and literary mentor ("patron") to [Guy de Maupassant](GUY_DE_MAUPASSANT.md) — Maupassant met Zola, Turgenev, and other realist/naturalist writers at Flaubert's home, and is often described as Flaubert's protégé.

## Relevance to Marie

On 15 July 1884 (para 105.0214), amid a long meditation on Maupassant's writing and its obsessive focus on sex, Marie writes: "Si je meurs jamais ce sera d'indignation devant la bêtise humaine qui est infinie comme dit Flaubert, feu le patron de cette vache de, non de ce taureau de Maupassant" — "If I ever die it will be of indignation at human stupidity, which is infinite, as Flaubert says — the late master/mentor of that ... bull of a Maupassant." Marie is invoking Flaubert's famously scathing, exhausted view of human stupidity (*la bêtise humaine*) — a recurring preoccupation of his later work, above all the unfinished *Bouvard et Pécuchet*, an encyclopedic satire of human folly, and his *Dictionnaire des idées reçues*. By 1884 Flaubert had been dead four years; Marie's phrase "feu le patron" (the late master/patron) marks both his death and his role as literary godfather to Maupassant's generation of naturalists.
