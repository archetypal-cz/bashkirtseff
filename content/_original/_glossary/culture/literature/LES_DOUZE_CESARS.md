## Overview

%% GLO_LES_DOUZE_CESARS.0002 %%
*De vita Caesarum* (in French *Les Douze Césars* or *Vie des douze Césars*) is a set of twelve biographies by the Roman writer Suetonius: Julius Caesar and the first eleven emperors, Augustus, Tiberius, Caligula, Claudius, Nero, Galba, Otho, Vitellius, Vespasian, Titus and Domitian. It was written about AD 121 (Wikipedia, «Lives of the Twelve Caesars»). The French Wikisource author page for Suétone lists three French versions of the *Vie des douze Césars* (Baudement, La Harpe, La Pause), and the Internet Archive holds volume 2 of La Harpe's translation (1770).

%% GLO_LES_DOUZE_CESARS.0003 %%
The life of Julius Caesar recounts the assassination in chapter LXXXII. Suetonius tells it in dry detail: Caesar is struck first below the throat, wraps his toga about his head and draws its skirt round his legs «that he might fall more decently», receives «three and twenty wounds», and utters only a groan at the first. Some authors, he adds, report that he said to Brutus, «Thou, my son!»; the body is then carried home on a litter with one arm hanging over the side (Project Gutenberg #6386, Thomson's translation).

## Marie and Suétone

%% GLO_LES_DOUZE_CESARS.0005 %%
On **28 November 1883** (carnet 102) Marie finds an old sketch of the murder of Caesar in her albums, a sight that «m'a empoigné» (102.0343). She goes home at once and, in her words, «saute sur Suétone et Plutarque» (102.0345), reading the two accounts of the murder against each other. Plutarch's, she finds, is «arrangé et éloquent» (and she remarks that Montesquieu loved it), whereas «dans Suétone ça fait frémir, c'est un procès verbal qui fait froid dans le dos» (102.0346). Her verdict matches the plain, reportlike telling of chapter LXXXII. She goes on: «Quel prestige étonnant ont donc les grands hommes pour qu'au bout de plusieurs siècles leur vie et leur mort nous fasse frémir et pleurer.» That evening she resolves to paint the scene; see [Le Meurtre de César](../art/MEURTRE_DE_CESAR.md).

%% GLO_LES_DOUZE_CESARS.0006 %%
Plutarch's account is in his [*Parallel Lives*](./PARALLEL_LIVES.md) (the *Life of Caesar*); Marie does not name the *Life*. The glossary has an entry for [Julius Caesar](../../people/historical/CAESAR.md).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LES_DOUZE_CESARS.0008 %%
- Marie names only «Suétone», never the title, and does not say which translation she read. The identification with the *Vie des douze Césars* rests on the account of Caesar's murder, which is in the first of the twelve lives.
- Her reference to Montesquieu's liking for Plutarch's version of the murder has not been traced.
