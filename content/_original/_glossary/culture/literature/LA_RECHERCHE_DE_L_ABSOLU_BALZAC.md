## Overview

%% GLO_LA_RECHERCHE_DE_L_ABSOLU_BALZAC.0002 %%
%% [#Balzac](./BALZAC.md) %%
*La Recherche de l'absolu* (*The Quest of the Absolute*) is a novel by [Honoré de Balzac](./BALZAC.md). It first appeared in 1834 as a *Scène de la vie privée*, was published on its own by Charpentier in 1839, and took its final place in *La Comédie humaine* in 1845 (Wikipedia, "The Quest of the Absolute"). It tells of the Flemish nobleman Balthazar Claës, who ruins his family in a chemist's search for the Absolute, and of his wife Joséphine and his daughter Marguerite, who try to save the house (the diary's footnote at 079.0338; text on fr.wikisource).

## Marie and the novel

%% GLO_LA_RECHERCHE_DE_L_ABSOLU_BALZAC.0004 %%
**12 April 1878, Paris** (079.0338). "J'ai cru que *la Recherche de l'Absolu* était tout autrement parce que moi aussi je cherche l'Absolu... mais l'absolu des sentiments et l'absolu en toutes choses, c'est ce qui me fait penser et écrire quarante mille tâtonnements à la suite desquels j'arrive bien mais à côté et jamais juste." The title seemed to promise a book about her own kind of seeking; the novel's Absolute is a chemist's.

%% GLO_LA_RECHERCHE_DE_L_ABSOLU_BALZAC.0005 %%
**15 April 1878** (079.0395). "Balzac a dit, dans *La Recherche de l'Absolu* quelque chose qui ferait plaisir aux impérialistes et qui me fait plaisir parce que cela les justifie !!... Elle se sentait criminelle à la manière des grands hommes qui violent les libertés pour sauver la patrie." The sentence is verbatim from the novel, where it describes Marguerite Claës arriving at her father's house: "Elle se sentait criminelle à la manière des grands hommes qui violent les libertés pour sauver la patrie" (fr.wikisource, Houssiaux edition). Marie, who in these years follows the Bonapartist press (Paul de Cassagnac, *Le Pays*), means by "les impérialistes" the Bonapartists, and apparently reads the line as a justification of the coup of 2 December 1851 that gave Napoléon III power.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LA_RECHERCHE_DE_L_ABSOLU_BALZAC.0007 %%
- The reading of "impérialistes" as Bonapartists and of the sentence as a reference to the coup of 1851 is an inference from context; Marie does not name the event.
- On the same days Marie reads *Le Lys dans la vallée*; see [#Le_Lys_dans_la_vallee_Balzac](./LE_LYS_DANS_LA_VALLEE_BALZAC.md).
