# Nana (Émile Zola)

## Overview

*Nana* (1880) is the ninth novel in Émile Zola's twenty-volume *Rougon-Macquart* cycle (Wikipedia, "Nana (novel)"). It was serialised in the daily *Le Voltaire* from 16 October 1879 to 5 February 1880 under an advertising campaign that covered the walls of Paris with posters, and appeared in book form with Charpentier in February 1880 (Wikipedia). It follows the rise of a young woman of the working-class Parisian demi-monde, Anna Coupeau, "Nana", who becomes a courtesan and destroys the men around her. The novel opens at the Théâtre des Variétés with the first night of an invented opéra-bouffe, **La Blonde Vénus** (a pastiche of Offenbach's *La Belle Hélène*), in which the talentless Nana, cast as Venus, takes the house by her body rather than her voice (Wikipedia).

## Marie and Nana

The book was the scandal of her first winter in the Paris art world, and she reads it, rejects it and uses it as a measure of other people.

- **23 January 1880** (087.0303): among "trente-six mille choses à la fois" she lists "une [composition] sur Nana", next to Taine's *Ancien Régime* and the *Nouvelle Revue*; what that composition is (a picture or a written piece) is not stated.
- **25 February 1880** (087.0535): chasing a model, Léonie, and meeting her family, she calls it "du Zola pur, du Zola de Nana, du reste c'est ce nom-là que je donne à Léonie".
- **10 March 1880** (087.0585): at the studio, [Julian](../../people/mentioned/RODOLPHE_JULIAN.md) "me compare à l'héroïne 'de certains romans que nous avons lu, quand elle paraît au second acte de la Blonde Vénus'" (Marie adds, for readers who have not read "l'infecte Nana"): "entre nous... vous comprenez jusqu'où va la comparaison, du reste entre artistes, ne vous récriez pas, vous êtes flattée". The comparison is to Nana's entrance as Venus.
- **31 July 1880** (089.0260): she says she has read "un mauvais livre", Belot's *Mademoiselle Giraud, ma femme*, "pour m'étourdir, m'oublier sans fatigue"; "Vous savez bien j'ai lu 'Nana', du reste il n'y a pas de mauvais livres; pourtant il paraît qu'il y a des intelligences qui s'en ressentent."

## Notes and doubts

- Marie writes "Nana" and "la Blonde Vénus" (the play within the novel); the identification is clear from both the title and Julian's reference.
- She reads Zola in French as a topical reader of 1880, within weeks of the book's release; the diary does not say how she got it (she was later "apportée Zola complet", 104.0116, May 1884).
- For Zola as an author, see [Émile Zola](./EMILE_ZOLA.md).
