## Overview

%% GLO_LES_ROIS_EN_EXIL.0002 %%
*Les Rois en exil* is a novel by [Alphonse Daudet](./ALPHONSE_DAUDET.md), published in 1879 and subtitled «roman parisien» in the Dentu printings (French Wikisource; Internet Archive records of the Dentu editions). Daudet dedicated it to Edmond de Goncourt, «à l'historien des reines et des favorites, au romancier de *Germinie Lacerteux* et des *Frères Zemganno*», calling it «ce roman d'histoire moderne» (dedication, Wikisource). It is the story of a deposed royal family in Paris. The first chapter opens with the exiled and deposed Queen Frédérique waking in a Paris hotel after a siege of two months; she asks first for the comte de Zara and for the king, and her servants speak a Dalmatian patois (chapter I, «Le premier jour»). Chapter II, «Un royaliste», introduces the devoted royalist Élisée Méraut, whom a party of Franciscans calls on in his cheap lodging. A later chapter is titled «Joies populaires» and another «À l'Académie» (table of contents, Wikisource). The Internet Archive catalogues a Dentu printing of 1884 as the 61st edition, which suggests how widely it was read.

## Marie and Les Rois en exil

%% GLO_LES_ROIS_EN_EXIL.0004 %%
- **8 August 1882, Paris (096.0038-0039).** After another sitting («J'ai encore posé»), with her aunt in and out on errands, she writes: «j'ai la tête un peu troublée par "Les rois en exil" de Daudet, je l'avais déjà lu mais ça se recommence, il y a là de si ravissantes pages, une finesse d'analyse, une netteté d'expression qui me ravit, des choses qui font pleurer. Des choses de sentiment...» The book makes her think of Tony ([Robert-Fleury](../../people/artists/ROBERT_FLEURY.md)), who is alone and says he may marry, and she follows other people's feelings as, she supposes, novelists do: «On entre presque dans la peau des gens et on pense avec ou pour eux».
- **Same evening (096.0039).** «"Les rois en exil". Ah ! les ravissantes et attendrissantes choses. L'amour de Méraut pour la reine. C'est beau l'amour. Mais où vais-je trouver cet homme extraordinaire, ce héros... Car ce doit être un héros, le mot tourné en ridicule est juste.»

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LES_ROIS_EN_EXIL.0006 %%
- The August 1882 entry is a rereading. The first reading is not recorded in the diary.
- Marie's «la reine» is Queen Frédérique. How the love of Méraut for her develops in the book has not been checked here beyond the opening chapters.
