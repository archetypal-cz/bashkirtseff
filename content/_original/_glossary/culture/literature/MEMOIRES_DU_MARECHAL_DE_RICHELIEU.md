## Overview

%% GLO_MEMOIRES_DU_MARECHAL_DE_RICHELIEU.0002 %%
The *Mémoires du maréchal duc de Richelieu* were published in 1790–1793 under the name of Louis-François-Armand de Vignerot du Plessis, duc de Richelieu (1696–1788), Marshal of France and a famous libertine of the courts of Louis XIV, the Regency and Louis XV. The compiler was the abbé Jean-Louis Giraud-Soulavie, who presents them as composed "dans la bibliothèque et sous les yeux du maréchal" (title page of the Internet Archive copies, 1790 and 1793); their authenticity is usually doubted (BnF and Ossolineum library records; Wikipedia, «Louis François Armand de Vignerot du Plessis, duc de Richelieu»). They were reissued in the nineteenth century, for example with notes by F. Barrière in 1858.

## Marie and the Mémoires

%% GLO_MEMOIRES_DU_MARECHAL_DE_RICHELIEU.0004 %%
%% [#Emile_d_Audiffret](../../people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md) %%
**22 September 1875, Nice (044.0059).** While Girofla and others walk through a room examining it with candles, Girofla opens a cupboard and reads the title of the first book he finds: «Mémoires du maréchal duc de Richelieu». Marie's reaction: «Bigre ! voilà ce que je ne voulais pas.» A footnote explains that the memoirs' tales of gallantry were no reading for a young girl. The paragraph does not say whose cupboard it was.

%% GLO_MEMOIRES_DU_MARECHAL_DE_RICHELIEU.0005 %%
%% [#Love](../themes/LOVE.md) %%
**24 September 1875 (044.0182).** Two days later she gives her verdict on [Émile d'Audiffret](../../people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md): he is «un jeune homme qui a lu "Les mémoires du maréchal duc de Richelieu", dont il veut suivre les traces. Il vise et atteint à la don juanerie. Il n'a pas encore aimé.» The book becomes her shorthand for his pose as a seducer. Whether he owned the copy found on the 22nd is not stated.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_MEMOIRES_DU_MARECHAL_DE_RICHELIEU.0007 %%
In July 1877 (carnet 073, entry of 18 July) Marie also recalls the poor spelling of "le maréchal de Richelieu"; that is the marshal as a person, not the book. The two mentions above do not say which edition she means.
