## Overview

%% GLO_HOMER_ODYSSEY.0002 %%
%% [#Homere](../../people/mentioned/HOMERE.md) %%
The *Odyssey* is one of the two Greek epics attributed to Homer (usually dated to the 8th century BC), telling Odysseus's ten-year return from Troy. Together with the *Iliad* it was the centre of the classical reading Marie pursued (see [Homer's Iliad](HOMER_ILIAD.md)).

## Marie and this work

%% GLO_HOMER_ODYSSEY.0004 %%
%% [#Homere](../../people/mentioned/HOMERE.md) [#Grand_papa](../../people/family/GRAND_PAPA.md) [#Reading](../themes/READING.md) %%
9 April 1875 (031.0140–0145): at dinner the talk turns to Homer and her father tells her she does not know what the *Iliad* is. She flares up and challenges him to say what is left of Homer besides the *Iliad*. "Rien, il n'y a que l'Iliade !" he answers, and she nearly chokes with triumph: "Et l'Odyssée ? ... qu'en faites-vous ?" He replies "il n'y a pas d'Odyssée", and she adds the "poème héroï-comique", the *Batrachomyomachia* (see [Batrachomyomachia](BATRACHOMYOMACHIA.md)). She laughs so hard that her cousins laugh too and "grand-papa s'est fâché"; the girls run away. The RSR note on 031.0140 adds that in the same entry she weeps over historical figures (Alexander, Napoleon, Nero, Alcibiades).

%% GLO_HOMER_ODYSSEY.0005 %%
%% [#Homere](../../people/mentioned/HOMERE.md) %%
In 082.0442 (1878-09-23) she lists Homer among the Greeks: "Homère, je le connais très bien".

%% GLO_HOMER_ODYSSEY.0012 %%
%% [#Homere](../../people/mentioned/HOMERE.md) %%
12 February 1880 (087.0471): among the curiosities in a friend's room she notes "un livre de Homère en grec avec une préface latine et sa photographie avec ces mots: souvenir bien respectueux et dévoué du souffleur à la baronne" (a Greek Homer with a Latin preface; the work, Iliad or Odyssey, is not specified).

%% GLO_HOMER_ODYSSEY.0013 %%
%% [#Homere](../../people/mentioned/HOMERE.md) [#Nausicaa](../art/DOULEUR_DE_NAUSICAA.md) %%
22-28 March 1883 (098.0554-0555, 098.0594, 098.0609): while modelling her *Douleur de Nausicaa* she retells the parting of Nausicaa and Odysseus (Odyssey VI-VIII) and, on 22 March, sides with Ouida, who "voudrait étrangler la vieille Pénélope et marier Ulysse avec cette idéale jeune fille appuyée à la colonne de marbre rose du palais de son père". The line is from Ouida's novel *Ariadnê* (1877), vol. 1, ch. X, p. 265: "For me, I always wish, sinfully perhaps, to strangle Penelope in her own web, and wed Ulysses to the sweet Phaeacian maid" (see [Ariadnê](ARIADNE_OUIDA.md)). On 27 March she goes back to the poem itself: "Mais je viens de chercher dans l'Odyssée, Homère ne donne pas la scène que j'ai imaginée", though Ulysses's speech of praise on meeting Nausicaa "a dû inévitablement monter à la tête de celle-ci"; on 28 March "J'en reviens à Homère."
