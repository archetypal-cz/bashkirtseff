## Overview

%% GLO_LE_LYS_DANS_LA_VALLEE_BALZAC.0002 %%
%% [#Balzac](./BALZAC.md) %%
*Le Lys dans la vallée* is a novel by [Honoré de Balzac](./BALZAC.md), published in 1835-36 (Wikipedia gives 1835) and part of *La Comédie humaine*. It concerns the emotionally intense but never physically consummated affection between Félix de Vandenesse and Henriette de Mortsauf, the unhappily married châtelaine of a house in the Loire valley (Wikipedia, "Le Lys dans la Vallée"). Henriette was modelled on Balzac's friend Laure de Berny, 22 years older than he. Lady Dudley, the worldly Englishwoman who takes Félix from Henriette, was inspired by Frances Sarah Lovell, Countess Guidoboni-Visconti (same article).

## Marie and the novel

%% GLO_LE_LYS_DANS_LA_VALLEE_BALZAC.0004 %%
**8 April 1878, Paris** (079.0263). She reads it "pendant les repas" at the studio (the Académie Julian): "Je suis à la moitié du livre et je ne l'aime pas. Quand Mme de Mortsauf parle de sa tante il me prend des envies de rire." The fit of laughter at Henriette's talk is the only detail she gives; the passage is not located.

%% GLO_LE_LYS_DANS_LA_VALLEE_BALZAC.0005 %%
**9 April 1878** (079.0272). "J'ai fini *Le lys dans la vallée*. C'est un livre très fatigant malgré ses beautés. La lettre de Nathalie de Manerville qui termine le livre est charmante et vraie." Nathalie de Manerville is the woman to whom Félix recounts his story in a long letter; her reply is the last text in the book, and it is the part Marie praises. See also [#Nathalie_de_Manerville](../../people/mentioned/NATHALIE_DE_MANERVILLE.md) and [#Mme_de_Mortsauf](../../people/aristocracy/MME_DE_MORTSAUF.md).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LE_LYS_DANS_LA_VALLEE_BALZAC.0007 %%
- The date is given as 1836 in some references and as 1835 by Wikipedia; the novel's first appearance was in 1835 and the volume form followed in 1836.
- Within the same week she also mentions Balzac's *La Recherche de l'absolu* (12 and 15 April 1878); see [#La_Recherche_de_l_Absolu_Balzac](./LA_RECHERCHE_DE_L_ABSOLU_BALZAC.md).
