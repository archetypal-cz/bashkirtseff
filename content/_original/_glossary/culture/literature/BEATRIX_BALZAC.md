# Béatrix (Honoré de Balzac)

## Overview

*Béatrix* is a novel by [Honoré de Balzac](./BALZAC.md), first published in 1839 (it appeared in *Le Siècle* in August 1839 and in volume form the same year, under the sub-title *ou Les Amours forcés*) and later placed in the *Scènes de la vie privée* of *La Comédie humaine* (Wikipedia, "Béatrix"; Internet Archive record of the 1839 edition). In Brittany, the young Calyste du Guénic loves the older writer Félicité des Touches, who writes as **Camille Maupin** and is modelled on George Sand; she is unable to return his feelings and tries to help him win the blonde Marquise **Béatrix de Rochefide** (modelled on Marie d'Agoult), who is carried off by the musician Gennaro Conti (modelled on Liszt). The critic **Claude Vignon** (modelled on Gustave Planche) is a further rival (same Wikipedia article).

## Marie and Béatrix

The novel enters the diary twice as a gift and twice as a mirror.

- **8 and 10 June 1880** (088.0673, 088.0698): she makes [Soutzo](../../people/mentioned/SOUTZO.md) read Balzac's *Béatrix* ("ma tante et moi assises et lui à nos pieds"), but "il ne peut pas lire cela sérieusement. Il ne comprend pas Balzac... ce n'est pas encore son âme qui sera ma sœur de la mienne"; later: "ça ne l'amuse pas".
- **4 January 1883** (097.0351): [Julian](../../people/mentioned/RODOLPHE_JULIAN.md), speaking of Gambetta and the woman in his life, tells her she will never feel a true passion, that she is not woman enough: "presque la tirade de Claude Vignon à Camille Maupin (voir Béatrix de Balzac)". Marie reports him adding that she will always have a court of admirers: "on m'adorera sans m'aimer".
- **6 April 1884** (103.0746): "Merci, mais ce discours a déjà été tenu par Claude Vignon à Mlle des Touches et il n'y a là rien qui m'humilie." The footnote in the diary glosses the allusion: Vignon analyses the writer Camille Maupin as brilliant, formidable, but lacking the tender feminine qualities that inspire great love.

## Notes and doubts

- "Béatrix" is the title used by Marie; there is no doubt about the author (she writes "Béatrix de Balzac").
- The two allusions in 1883 and 1884 show she knew Claude Vignon's speech well enough to cite it by speaker and addressee; the project has not checked her wording against the text of Vignon's scene in the Houssiaux edition.
