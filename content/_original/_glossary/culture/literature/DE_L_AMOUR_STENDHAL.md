## Overview

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0002 %%
%% [#Stendhal](../../people/mentioned/STENDHAL.md) %%
*De l'amour* (1822) is an essay by [Stendhal](../../people/mentioned/STENDHAL.md) on how love is born and on its kinds, with the notion of *cristallisation* (see the Stendhal entry, which cites Britannica and en.wikipedia, "De l'amour (Stendhal)"). It is divided into short numbered chapters, among them chapter XXVI, "De la pudeur" (Project Gutenberg #60882).

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0003 %%
## Marie and De l'amour

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0004 %%
**14 June 1878, Paris** (080.0609-0612). Marie writes: "Je trouve cela dans un livre et comme cela se rapporte à certaines choses qui me sont arrivées je le copie *in extenso*. Combien de fois il m'est arrivé d'avoir honte comme cela... mais lisez." She then copies three paragraphs from *De l'amour*, chapter XXVI, "De la pudeur", without naming the book or the author (080.0610-0612). The passage says that for a shy and tender woman nothing is worse than having allowed herself, in a man's presence, something she believes she should blush for, and that a woman above the common run therefore has everything to gain from very reserved manners.

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0005 %%
The text matches the Gutenberg edition (chapter XXVI, "De la pudeur") closely but not exactly. The parenthesis "(Il ne faut même pas être timide et tendre pour cela)" is Marie's own, the italics are hers, she writes "de le blâmer" where Stendhal has "de la blâmer", and her last sentence says the lightest infractions are "permises par la honte la plus atroce" where Stendhal has "punies par la honte la plus atroce". The last change reverses the sense and is probably a copying slip.

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0006 %%
**14 July 1883, Paris** (100.0330). Marie names the book for the first time: «Avez-vous lu "L'amour" de Stendhal ? Je le lis à présent.»

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0007 %%
Over the following weeks she borrows its vocabulary to analyse her own feelings. On 14 July (100.0333-0356) she uses *cristallisation*, *amour-goût*, *amour-passion* and the «facilités à l'espérance». On 16 July she writes: «La cristallisation me préoccupe vivement et je suis convaincue qu'il y a un livre à faire sur les cristallisations innocentes, qui n'aboutissent pas» (100.0358), and reasons that for [Cassagnac](../../people/recurring/CASSAGNAC.md) «il y avait commencement de cristallisation» but she will not let it go further (100.0361-0366). She notes impressions «dit Stendhal», such as the heart stopping at the sight of a hat like the beloved's (100.0366), and on 17 July: «Stendhal dit que l'amour vit d'espérance et de craintes des plus affreux malheurs» (100.0378). Her complaint of the same day, «Toujours préoccupée des cristallisations, sans objet, hélas» (100.0373), plays on Stendhal's *objet*, the person loved. A last echo is on 26 July (100.0438).

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0008 %%
**12-13 August 1883** (101.0018, 101.0051, 101.0071). Before an awkward meeting she means to «suivre les préceptes de Stendhal, être gracieuse, lui faire sentir qu'il n'est pas Dieu sans le blesser»; afterwards: «ô cristallisations ! Stendhal tu es vrai», and «Stendhal y a aidé.»

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0009 %%
**13 September 1883** (101.0333). «Je lis dans Stendhal que les peines paraissent moins amères lorsqu'on les idéalise. C'est excessivement juste. Comment idéaliser les miennes ?»

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0010 %%
**24 October 1883** (102.0055). In a marginal note she calls her state «les cristallisations de Stendhal, les fameuses cristallisations»; the source footnote explains the term.

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0011 %%
**2 December 1883** (102.0408). «Je lis "L'amour" de Stendhal, il y a là des choses si vraies que c'est effrayant. Le chapitre de l'engouement est peut-être ce qui définit bien mes impressions.» She then copies a passage on souls «ardentes par excès, amoureuses à crédit», which throw themselves at objects and cover them in advance with imaginary charm, until «on découvre que l'objet adoré ne renvoie pas la balle». It is the chapter «De l'engouement» (chapter XXII in the Project Gutenberg edition, #60882) and her quotation follows that text closely, apart from a small slip («ni ardentes» for «ou ardentes»).

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0013 %%
- The author and work are identified in 1878 by comparing the passage with the text; Marie names neither there. In 1883 she names the book as «"L'amour" de Stendhal» and gives no edition.

%% GLO_DE_L_AMOUR_STENDHAL.0014 %%
- The Gutenberg edition is Garnier frères, with a study by Sainte-Beuve. Chapter numbers may differ in other editions.
