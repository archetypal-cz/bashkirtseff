## Overview

%% GLO_LA_JEUNE_CAPTIVE_CHENIER.0002 %%
"La Jeune Captive" ("L'épi naissant mûrit de la faux respecté") is an elegy by the poet André Chénier (1762-1794), written in 1794 in the prison of Saint-Lazare, where he was held before being guillotined during the Terror (fr.wikisource, header of the poem; the diary's own footnote at 053.0202). The girl of the title was, according to the note in the Moland edition (Garnier, 1889), Mlle Franquetot de Coigny, who had married the duc de Fleury in 1784 and was imprisoned at Saint-Lazare; the same note gives the first printing as *La Décade philosophique*, 30 nivôse an III (fr.wikisource, Moland edition).

%% GLO_LA_JEUNE_CAPTIVE_CHENIER.0003 %%
The poem is spoken by the captive herself, who refuses to die yet: "Je ne veux point mourir encore" closes both the first and the eighth stanza. Chénier's poems were collected and published by Henri de Latouche in 1819 (fr.wikisource lists the 1819 Latouche edition).

## Marie and the poem

%% GLO_LA_JEUNE_CAPTIVE_CHENIER.0005 %%
On 29 January 1876 in Rome, in a bitter mood about her isolation, Marie writes: "Vrai, je suis une misérable dans ce monde, je ne dirai pas que « ma coupe est encore pleine » comme *La Captive* de Chénier" (053.0202). She is quoting from memory the stanza "Au banquet de la vie à peine commencé, / Un instant seulement mes lèvres ont pressé / La coupe en mes mains encor pleine": the captive, young and with her life ahead of her, still holds a full cup. Marie says she cannot claim the same. Her title "La Captive" is the common short form of "La Jeune Captive".

%% GLO_LA_JEUNE_CAPTIVE_CHENIER.0006 %%
Paragraph 053.0207, in the same entry, is not a further reference to the poem; it continues the same page of the diary about Merjeewsky.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_LA_JEUNE_CAPTIVE_CHENIER.0008 %%
- The identification of "La Captive" with Chénier's "La Jeune Captive" rests on his name, the word *coupe* and the phrase "encor pleine" in the stanza quoted above; Marie does not give the full title.
- The diary's own footnote says the line means "my cup is still full" and calls it a declaration of life and hope.
