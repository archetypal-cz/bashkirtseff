## Overview

%% GLO_PREDICTION_DE_CASSANDRE.0002 %%
*Prédiction de Cassandre* is a comic poem in refrain form that Marie composed in Nice on **6 October 1875** and reworked on **1-2 December 1875**. It prophesies that [Émile d'Audiffret](../../people/recurring/EMILE_D_AUDIFFRET.md) -- the young man she calls Girofla and is fixed on that autumn -- will leave Nice for Paris, spend his money on restaurants, dance halls and women, make a scandal, and in the last line of every stanza "pourrira" (rot). The title borrows the myth of Cassandra, whose true prophecies nobody believed.

%% GLO_PREDICTION_DE_CASSANDRE.0003 %%
## Form and content

%% GLO_PREDICTION_DE_CASSANDRE.0004 %%
Each stanza is four lines of rhymed future tenses ending on the refrain "Et pourrira, et pourrira !" The first stanza (046.0156) sets it up: "Ton fils d'ici partira / Et très grand bruit mènera / Chantera, criera, rira, / Mais pourrira, mais pourrira !" The following stanzas name Bignon (a Paris restaurant), the Bois de Boulogne, "tous les sales lieux", a "Moulin Rouge" (an anachronism for 1875, as the RSR note at 046.0159 records), the Bal Mabille, yellow gloves and a crushed top hat, and so on through 26 stanzas. The addressee is the *father*: "Ton fils" speaks to [Léon d'Audiffret](../../people/mentioned/LEON_D_AUDIFFRET.md). Whether Émile really was Léon's son is not established by the diary (see that entry); the poem simply assumes it.

## Marie and this work

%% GLO_PREDICTION_DE_CASSANDRE.0006 %%
The first version has **26 numbered stanzas** (046.0156-0181) and was made on a terrace, with Olga, watching for a man in white seen coming down the lane to the château (046.0186-0188). That evening she keeps it to herself; on 11 October (046.0345) she lists her "chefs-d'œuvre" -- "La valse, la prédiction de Cassandre et la complainte du très malheureux Girofla" -- which she has refused to show although "Nina a demandé hautement: La valse !". The *Complainte* is its companion piece: [Complainte de Girofla](./COMPLAINTE_DE_GIROFLA.md).

%% GLO_PREDICTION_DE_CASSANDRE.0007 %%
On **1 December 1875** (050.0359) she reads it to [Collignon](../../people/mentioned/COLLIGNON.md), "qu'elle trouve très bien mais fort lancée" -- very good but rather bold. The next day she titles it "Prédiction de Cassandre à M. Léon d'Audiffret" and writes out an expanded text of **38 stanzas** (050.0380-0417): "J'ai comme on a pu le voir augmenté et revu ce poème de trente-huit couplets. Nous avons fait cela avec Collignon qui est une femme sérieuse. J'ai déjà commencé à copier et l'on recevra cette beauté sous peu de temps" (050.0418-0420). The last stanza (050.0417) closes: "Comme une pâle ombre errera / Chavirera et tournera / Mais pourrir toujours devra / Et pourrira et pourrira." The diary does not say whether the fair copy was in fact posted.

%% GLO_PREDICTION_DE_CASSANDRE.0008 %%
The poem is the best surviving example of the mock-letters and verses she aimed at the Audiffret family in the autumn of 1875; compare the [Rosière letter](./ROSIERE_LETTRE_AUDIFFRET.md) of the same weeks.
