## Overview

%% GLO_MRS_BODGERS_NICE_NOVEL.0002 %%
An English-language novel that Marie read in Nice in the winter of 1875-76 and that has not been identified. Its subject, as she summarises it, is the foreign colony of Nice and the rise of a woman named Mrs Bodgers. Neither the title nor the author can be established from the diary, and no novel with these names has been traced.

%% GLO_MRS_BODGERS_NICE_NOVEL.0003 %%
## Marie and the book

%% GLO_MRS_BODGERS_NICE_NOVEL.0004 %%
On 31 December 1875 Marie writes that there is an English book, whose title she gives as *Fair in the fearless old fashion*, which reviews the society of Nice and describes the origin of Mrs Bodgers: the daughter of a gentleman, married to Bodgers, the son of a gardener and himself a humble and wretched clergyman. All her life she dreamed of becoming somebody and, after every humiliation imaginable, "Mrs Bodgers attained the summit of her ambition in Nice" (051.0222; the English is in the original, the paragraph has no accents). The title as printed is garbled and clearly not complete, and the English quotation may be as unreliable as her Italian ones.

%% GLO_MRS_BODGERS_NICE_NOVEL.0005 %%
%% [#Prodgers](../../people/mentioned/PRODGERS.md) %%
The paragraph is tagged in the diary with [Mme Prodgers](../../people/mentioned/PRODGERS.md), the English hostess of Nice society, and the next paragraph (051.0223) is about her "bureau de mariage". Whether "Bodgers" is a disguise for "Prodgers" in the novel, or only a name Marie's memory has assimilated to the woman she knew, is open: the resemblance is striking but no source confirms it.

## Notes and doubts

%% GLO_MRS_BODGERS_NICE_NOVEL.0007 %%
- At 053.0264 (31 January 1876) Marie quotes "a phrase from an English book", a father asking his daughter Kate: "Bless my heart, Kate, are you mad? What, you, of all girls in the world, confess your love for a fellow who has not first said he loves you?" The diary does not say that it is the same book as the Nice novel, and it is quite possibly a different one; the quotation has not been traced either.
- The book is unidentified. A full-text search of English novels with a Nice setting (HathiTrust, Google Books) might settle it.
