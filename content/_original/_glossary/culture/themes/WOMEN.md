## Thematic Tag: Women's Condition

%% GLO_WOMEN.0002 %%


%% GLO_WOMEN.0003 %%
This thematic tag collects diary paragraphs where Marie writes about what women may and may not do: "si j'étais homme", doors closed to women artists, the double standard, women's rights and feminist journalism (including her *La Citoyenne* articles under the pseudonym Pauline Orell). It replaces the feminism material formerly filed under [Politics](POLITICS.md) and the misfiled Feminism stub.

%% GLO_WOMEN.0004 %%
## Tagging Criteria

%% GLO_WOMEN.0005 %%
- **Tag** a restriction or injustice felt as a woman ("si j'étais homme", barred from the Beaux-Arts, a woman alone in the street).
- **Tag** feminism and women's rights: Hubertine Auclert, *La Citoyenne*, the Pauline Orell project.
- **Tag** women's work, ambition and independence as a question.
- **Tag** the condition of women generally: marriage law as such goes to [Marriage](MARRIAGE.md), but its effect on women's freedom is tagged here.
- **Do not tag** a woman merely described.
- **Do not tag** women artists' careers with no gendered obstacle in view ([Her Art](ART_PRACTICE.md)).
- **Do not tag** a man's political opinion about women ([Politics](POLITICS.md)) unless Marie discusses the condition of women.
- **Do not tag** "femme" meaning wife.
- The bar is that the paragraph is substantively *about* the theme; a passing word never qualifies. A paragraph takes at most three themes.

%% GLO_WOMEN.0006 %%
## Common French Cues

%% GLO_WOMEN.0007 %%
- **Phrases**: "si j'étais homme", "nous autres femmes", "une femme ne peut pas", "les droits de la femme", "émancipation"
- **Names**: La Citoyenne, Hubertine Auclert, Pauline Orell, Académie Julian (the women's studio)
- **False friends**: *femme* = woman or wife; *féminin* used of a taste or a trait without a social point

%% GLO_WOMEN.0008 %%
## Notable Patterns

%% GLO_WOMEN.0009 %%
- Her most developed passages (1880) are the plan to write for *La Citoyenne* under a pseudonym.
- The wish to go where men go (the street, the Louvre, the Beaux-Arts) recurs from the first carnets.
