## Thematic Tag: Ambition & Fame

%% GLO_AMBITION.0002 %%


%% GLO_AMBITION.0003 %%
This thematic tag collects diary paragraphs where Marie writes about her hunger for glory, greatness and a name that will outlast her: wanting to be célèbre, measuring herself against the famous, fearing obscurity and a life without distinction. Ambition is the diary's engine from the first carnets (a singer's career, then a painter's), and it is the other pole of her despair: "à quoi bon vivre sans génie".

%% GLO_AMBITION.0004 %%
## Tagging Criteria

%% GLO_AMBITION.0005 %%
- **Tag** a stated desire for glory, fame or greatness ("je veux la gloire", "je serai célèbre", "être quelqu'un").
- **Tag** Marie ranking herself against the great, or against rivals, as a question of destiny and reputation.
- **Tag** fear of obscurity or of a wasted life; the price she would pay for a name.
- **Tag** what fame would mean to her: triumphs, applause, being known to posterity.
- **Do not tag** someone else described as famous, with no bearing on her own hopes.
- **Do not tag** the work itself (studio, lessons, the Salon): that is [Her Art](ART_PRACTICE.md), unless the paragraph is about the glory it should bring.
- **Do not tag** vanity about her looks or her dress ([Beauty & Appearance](APPEARANCE.md), [Clothing & Fashion](FASHION.md)).
- **Do not tag** an admiring remark about a celebrity she meets, unless it is turned on herself.
- The bar is that the paragraph is substantively *about* the theme; a passing word never qualifies. A paragraph takes at most three themes.

%% GLO_AMBITION.0006 %%
## Common French Cues

%% GLO_AMBITION.0007 %%
- **Nouns**: la gloire, la célébrité, la renommée, le nom, la postérité, le génie, le talent, le triomphe, l'immortalité, l'obscurité
- **Phrases**: "être quelqu'un", "devenir célèbre", "me faire un nom", "laisser un nom", "percer", "arriver"
- **False friends**: *gloire* in a religious sense ("gloire à Dieu"); *célèbre* as a plain epithet of another person

%% GLO_AMBITION.0008 %%
## Notable Patterns

%% GLO_AMBITION.0009 %%
- Early carnets tie fame to the voice and the stage; from 1877 it passes to painting and the Salon.
- Ambition often shares a paragraph with [Mortality](MORTALITY.md): the fear of dying before she has become anyone.
- Reader-addresses about her future fame also take [Addressing the Reader](FOURTH_WALL.md).
