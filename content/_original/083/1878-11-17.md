The "Patriote Nicois" article (a Nice newspaper) apparently questioned Marie's femininity, pushing her to doubt "whether I was a woman." The biological "proof" of menstruation arrives as ironic confirmation. The interplay of gender doubt (from yesterday's wish to be male), physical self-assessment, and bodily reality creates a layered psychological moment. She stays home, "rather ill-disposed." %%
%% 2026-10-21T11:12:21 RSR: CORRECTION — the Patriote niçois article was not about Marie: it was the «correspondance de Versailles» about Julia («une horreur possible vu le physique de l'étrange Juliette», 083.0199), part of the false report that Cassagnac was suing his wife, for which the papers were convicted («il est fait allusion à une prétendue instance civile introduite par le demandeur contre sa femme; que cette instance n'existe pas», judgment in Le Figaro, 28 December 1878, quoted in 083.0524). Marie's doubt about herself comes from comparing herself with the woman the article described. %%
Après avoir regardé Mme de Cassagnac je me suis crue fanée et après l'article du "Patriote Niçois" je me suis demandée si j'étais femme... comme je commençais à en douter il m'arriva une preuve... bref je suis restée chez moi assez mal disposée.
