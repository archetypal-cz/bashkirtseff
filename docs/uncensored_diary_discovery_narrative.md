# The Diary and Its Editions: How Marie Bashkirtseff's Uncensored Text Reached Readers

## What Marie wrote

Marie Bashkirtseff (born 1858 near Poltava, died in Paris on 31 October 1884, aged 25) kept her diary from January 1873 until days before her death. The manuscript is held at the Manuscripts Department of the Bibliothèque nationale de France. The project follows the Cercle des Amis edition and numbers 106 diary notebooks (carnets 001–106) plus Marie's retrospective preface of May 1884 (carnet 000), which is why the project speaks of 107 carnets while the diary itself has 106 notebooks. One line in `docs/research/marie-bashkirtseff-research.md` gives the BnF count as 105 numbered notebooks; that discrepancy has not been resolved against the BnF inventory.

In the preface Marie says plainly what she wanted done with the text:

> _"Si je ne meurs pas jeune, j'espère rester comme une grande artiste, mais si je meurs jeune je veux laisser publier mon journal qui ne peut pas être autre chose qu'intéressant."_
>
> (If I do not die young, I hope to remain as a great artist; but if I die young, I want my journal published, which cannot be anything other than interesting.) — Préface, May 1884 (000.0003)

and, a few lines on, that she is "absolutely sincere" precisely because she hopes to be read (000.0003). She did not write a prediction that she would be edited into someone acceptable; that sentence, quoted in earlier versions of this page, does not exist in the manuscript.

## The 1887 edition

Three years after her death, her mother had the diary published: _Journal de Marie Bashkirtseff_, Paris, G. Charpentier, 1887, two volumes, edited by the poet and novelist André Theuriet. Theuriet deleted, condensed and re-sequenced entries to produce a narrative arc. The project's glossary entry `CENSORED_1887` estimates that the edition contains roughly a quarter of the diary; the project tags the passages it does contain with `#Censored_1887`, so the cuts can be seen paragraph by paragraph.

The edition also moved Marie's birth year to 1860. The manuscript preface itself gives 1859; she was born in November 1858, and the family had concealed her age for years (footnote at 000.0003, after Kernberger 2013). The 1860 date was later engraved on her tomb.

## The manuscript was never lost

The notebooks were not rediscovered; they were in the BnF and were consulted and partly published for a century. Fragments appeared through the 1920s (Pierre Borel's editions, e.g. _Le Visage inconnu_, Garnier, 1925; the "Cahiers intimes inédits" digitised on Gallica, link in `docs/research`). What took a century was a **complete** text.

The 1980s brought the scholarly reassessment: Colette Cosnier's article "Pour une lecture féministe du Journal de Marie Bashkirtseff" (Toulouse, AFFER, 1984) and her book _Marie Bashkirtseff, un portrait sans retouches_ (Paris, Pierre Horay, 1985; reissued by Presses universitaires de Rennes, 2022). Cosnier worked from the manuscripts and documented how far the published text departed from them. She did not discover the notebooks, and the project has not verified the earlier page's claims about what exactly she found (the feminist journalism for _La Citoyenne_ and the family lawsuit are in the diary; the project does not have a verified account of which of these the 1887 editors removed, beyond the paragraph-level tags).

## The complete text

- **French**: _Mon Journal_, texte intégral, transcribed from the BnF notebooks by Ginette Apostolescu, Montesson, Cercle des Amis de Marie Bashkirtseff, 16 volumes, 1995–2005. `content/_original` follows this edition.
- **English**: Phyllis Howard Kernberger and Katherine Kernberger translated from the manuscripts; the two-volume Fonthill edition of 2013 (_I Am the Most Interesting Book of All_, _Lust for Glory_) covers all 106 notebooks but is a selection, roughly a quarter of the paragraphs by the project's own matching.

This project's aim is the rest: the complete text, in several languages, with the apparatus a reader needs to follow her world.
